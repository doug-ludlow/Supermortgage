### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Timing — 12 CFR 1002.9(a)(1) (CFPB eRegs; 00a-fed §3.1) — VERIFIED.** A creditor shall notify an applicant of action taken within: "(i) 30 days after receiving a completed application concerning the creditor's approval of, counteroffer to, or adverse action on the application; (ii) 30 days after taking adverse action on an incomplete application, unless notice is provided in accordance with paragraph (c) of this section; (iii) 30 days after taking adverse action on an existing account; or (iv) 90 days after notifying the applicant of a counteroffer if the applicant does not expressly accept or use the credit offered." Calendar days.

**Completed application — §1002.2(f) (eCFR up to date as of 9/08/2026) — VERIFIED.** "Application means an oral or written request for an extension of credit that is made in accordance with procedures used by a creditor for the type of credit requested." A completed application is one for which the creditor has "received all the information that the creditor regularly obtains and considers in evaluating applications" for the amount and type of credit requested, "including, but not limited to, credit reports, any additional information requested from the applicant, and any approvals or reports by governmental agencies or other persons that are necessary to guarantee, insure, or provide security for the credit or collateral" (as displayed), and "The creditor shall exercise reasonable diligence in obtaining such information." Comment 9(a)(1)-1 (VERIFIED, CFPB eRegs): "Once a creditor has obtained all the information it normally considers in making a credit decision, the application is complete and the creditor has 30 days in which to notify the applicant of the credit decision. (See also comment 2(f)-6.)" Comment 2(f)-6 (eCFR Supplement I to Part 1002, 9/03/2026): "The creditor must act with reasonable diligence to collect information needed to complete the application" and should "request information from third parties promptly".

**Content — §1002.9(a)(2), (b)(1), (b)(2) — VERIFIED.** (a)(2): a written notification must contain a statement of the action taken; the name and address of the creditor; the ECOA notice under (b)(1); the name and address of the Federal agency that administers compliance; and either "(i) A statement of specific reasons for the action taken; or (ii) A disclosure of the applicant's right to a statement of specific reasons within 30 days, if the statement is requested within 60 days of the creditor's notification". (b)(1) ECOA notice, "substantially similar to the following: The Federal Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibits creditors from discriminating against credit applicants on the basis of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant has the capacity to enter into a binding contract); because all or part of the applicant's income derives from any public assistance program; or because the applicant has in good faith exercised any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act. The Federal agency that administers compliance with this law concerning this creditor is [name and address as specified by the appropriate agency or agencies listed in appendix A of this part]." Appendix A (CFPB eRegs, VERIFIED): item 9 — "Retailers, finance companies, and all other creditors not listed above": "Federal Trade Commission, Consumer Response Center, 600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20580"; item 1 (banks/thrifts/credit unions over $10 billion and affiliates): "Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection, 1700 G Street NW, Washington, DC 20552". A non-bank partner names the FTC. (b)(2): "The statement of reasons for adverse action required by paragraph (a)(2)(i) of this section must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action. Statements that the adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies or that the applicant, joint applicant, or similar party failed to achieve a qualifying score on the creditor's credit scoring system are insufficient."

**Specific reasons for scoring/model-based decisions — comments 9(b)(2)-1 to -9 (CFPB eRegs) — VERIFIED (transcribed).** -1: "A creditor must disclose the principal reasons for denying an application or taking other adverse action. The regulation does not mandate that a specific number of reasons be disclosed, but disclosure of more than four reasons is not likely to be helpful to the applicant." -2: "The specific reasons disclosed under §§ 1002.9(a)(2) and (b)(2) must relate to and accurately describe the factors actually considered or scored by a creditor." -3: "A creditor need not describe how or why a factor adversely affected an applicant. For example, the notice may say 'length of residence' rather than 'too short a period of residence.'" -4: "If a creditor bases the denial or other adverse action on a credit scoring system, the reasons disclosed must relate only to those factors actually scored in the system. Moreover, no factor that was a principal reason for adverse action may be excluded from disclosure. The creditor must disclose the actual reasons for denial (for example, 'age of automobile') even if the relationship of that factor to predicting creditworthiness may not be clear to the applicant." -5: no single method is required for selecting reasons from a scoring system; two acceptable methods are identifying "the factors for which the applicant's score fell furthest below the average score for each of those factors achieved by applicants whose total score was at or slightly above the minimum passing score" or below the average for all applicants; "Any other method that produces results substantially similar to either of these methods is also acceptable". -6: judgmental systems — reasons "must relate to those factors in the applicant's record actually reviewed by the person making the decision." -7: combined systems — "the reasons for the denial must come from the component of the system that the applicant failed"; gray-band cases take reasons "from both components"; "disclosure of more than a combined total of four reasons is not likely to be helpful". -8: "When a creditor denies the credit request because of an automatic-denial factor, the creditor must disclose that specific factor." -9: "Disclosing that a credit report was obtained and used in the denial of the application, as the FCRA requires, does not satisfy the ECOA requirement to disclose specific reasons"; "Disclosing the key factors that adversely affected the consumer's credit score does not satisfy the ECOA requirement to disclose specific reasons"; sample forms C-1 through C-5 provide for both. Appendix C commentary (CFPB eRegs, VERIFIED): the forms "are illustrative and may not be appropriate for all creditors"; creditors should "modify the checklist by substituting or adding other reasons"; reasons must be the factors actually used — "'insufficient bank references,' not 'insufficient credit references.'" **Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 were withdrawn May 12, 2025 (90 FR, doc 2025-08286; "not necessarily final") — the rule and comments above are the binding standard (00a-fed §3.2, VERIFIED).**

**Incomplete applications — §1002.9(c) and comments — VERIFIED.** (c)(1): within 30 days after receiving an incomplete application, notify "Of action taken, in accordance with paragraph (a) of this section; or Of the incompleteness, in accordance with paragraph (c)(2)". (c)(2): "the creditor shall send a written notice to the applicant specifying the information needed, designating a reasonable period of time for the applicant to provide the information, and informing the applicant that failure to provide the information requested will result in no further consideration being given to the application. The creditor shall have no further obligation under this section if the applicant fails to respond within the designated time period. If the applicant supplies the requested information within the designated time period, the creditor shall take action on the application and notify the applicant in accordance with paragraph (a)." (c)(3): oral requests permitted; "If the application remains incomplete the creditor shall send a notice in accordance with paragraph (c)(1)". Comment 9(a)(1)-3: the creditor "may deny the application giving as the reason for denial that the application is incomplete" or, alternatively, send an NOIA; comment 9(a)(1)-4: when the file "provides sufficient data for a credit decision", "missing information or 'incomplete application' cannot be given as the reason for the denial"; comment 9(c)(2)-1: late-supplied information may require "a new application"; comment 9(c)(3)-1: after an unanswered oral request "a creditor must send a written notice to the applicant within the 30-day period".

**Counteroffers, withdrawal, multiple applicants — VERIFIED.** §1002.2(c)(1)(i): adverse action includes "A refusal to grant credit in substantially the amount or on substantially the terms" requested unless a counteroffer is made and accepted; comment 9(a)(1)-5: the regulation "does not require a creditor to hold a counteroffer open for 90 days or any other particular length of time"; comment 9(a)(1)-6: "A creditor that gives the applicant a combined counteroffer and adverse action notice that complies with § 1002.9(a)(2) need not send a second adverse action notice if the applicant does not accept the counteroffer" (form C-4). §1002.9(e) (withdrawal of approved application when the parties contemplate an inquiry and none comes within 30 days) — not this build's model. §1002.9(f): "notification need only be given to one of them but must be given to the primary applicant where one is readily apparent." Comment 9(a)(1)-7: telephone applications — the creditor "must request the applicant's name and address in order to provide written notification". §1002.12(b)(1): retain the application, notices, statement of reasons and related records for 25 months (00a-fed §3.6).

**Discouragement after the 2026 rule — §1002.4(b) as currently in force (CFPB eRegs; 91 FR 21620, effective July 21, 2026) — VERIFIED (text) / PARTIALLY VERIFIED (FR amendatory text not read).** "A creditor shall not make any oral or written statement, in advertising or otherwise, directed at applicants or prospective applicants that the creditor knows or should know would cause a reasonable person to believe that the creditor would deny, or would grant on less favorable terms, a credit application by the applicant or prospective applicant because of the applicant or prospective applicant's prohibited basis characteristic(s). For purposes of this paragraph (b), oral or written statements are spoken or written words, or visual images such as symbols, photographs, or videos." The rule also removed the "effects test" from §1002.6(a)/comment 6(a)-2 and added SPCP restrictions; §1002.9 was not amended (00a-fed §3.8). Consequence here: reason statements and every AI-generated explanation must never reference a prohibited basis, and the removal of the effects test does not relax §1002.9(b)(2) accuracy, state fair-lending law, Colorado SB 26-189 or Fannie Mae's contractual expectations (LL-2026-04, A3-2-01).

**FCRA §615(a) — 15 U.S.C. 1681m(a) (uscode.house.gov, current through Sept 8, 2026) — VERIFIED.** "If any person takes any adverse action with respect to any consumer that is based in whole or in part on any information contained in a consumer report, the person shall— (1) provide oral, written, or electronic notice of the adverse action to the consumer; (2) provide to the consumer written or electronic disclosure— (A) of a numerical credit score as defined in section 1681g(f)(2)(A) of this title used by such person in taking any adverse action based in whole or in part on any information in a consumer report; and (B) of the information set forth in subparagraphs (B) through (E) of section 1681g(f)(1) of this title; (3) provide to the consumer orally, in writing, or electronically— (A) the name, address, and telephone number of the consumer reporting agency (including a toll-free telephone number established by the agency if the agency compiles and maintains files on consumers on a nationwide basis) that furnished the report to the person; and (B) a statement that the consumer reporting agency did not make the decision to take the adverse action and is unable to provide the consumer the specific reasons why the adverse action was taken; and (4) provide to the consumer an oral, written, or electronic notice of the consumer's right— (A) to obtain, under section 1681j of this title, a free copy of a consumer report on the consumer from the consumer reporting agency referred to in paragraph (3), which notice shall include an indication of the 60-day period under that section for obtaining such a copy; and (B) to dispute, under section 1681i of this title, with a consumer reporting agency the accuracy or completeness of any information in a consumer report furnished by the agency." §1681g(f)(1) (current through Sept 7, 2026): "(B) the range of possible credit scores under the model used; (C) all of the key factors that adversely affected the credit score ... the total number of which shall not exceed 4" (five where the number of inquiries is a key factor — §1681g(f)(9), 00a-fed §7); "(D) the date on which the credit score was created; (E) the name of the person or entity that provided the credit score." A tri-merge report has three CRAs and, for the representative score used, one model; the notice lists every CRA whose report was used and the score(s) actually used in the decision (the representative score per B3-5.1-02; where VantageScore 4.0 is used, that model — LL-2026-06, 00a-fnma §2). FCRA duties run to *each* consumer subject to the adverse action, so each applicant receives a notice with only that applicant's score (Reg V §1022.75(c) analogue for RBP notices, 00a-fed §7).

**HMDA action taken — 12 CFR 1003.4(a)(8) (eCFR 9/03/2026) and comments (CFPB eRegs) — VERIFIED.** (a)(8)(i): "Whether a covered loan was originated or purchased; Whether an application for a covered loan that did not result in the origination of a covered loan was approved but not accepted, denied, withdrawn by the applicant, or closed for incompleteness" (plus preapproval outcomes); (a)(8)(ii): "The date of the action taken by the financial institution." 2026 FIG (Version 2026, October 2025) codes: 1 Loan originated; 2 Application approved but not accepted; 3 Application denied; 4 Application withdrawn by applicant; 5 File closed for incompleteness; 6 Purchased loan; 7 Preapproval request denied; 8 Preapproval request approved but not accepted; date "YYYYMMDD". Comments (restated with quoted phrases): 4(a)(8)(i)-3 approved-not-accepted when approval was "subject only to customary commitment or closing conditions" and the loan "was not otherwise consummated"; -4 denied when the institution "made a credit decision denying the application before the applicant withdraws" or the file is closed; -5 withdrawn when the applicant "expressly withdraws" before a decision or closure ("A preapproval request that is withdrawn is not reportable under HMDA"); -6 closed for incompleteness when the institution sent the §1002.9(c)(2) notice and the applicant did not respond within the period; -9 counteroffers: if the applicant declines or fails to respond, "report the action taken as a denial on the original terms"; if the applicant agrees, report "the disposition of the application based on the terms of the counteroffer"; -10 rescinded after closing but before submission → "approved but not accepted"; -13 conditional approvals: unmet customary commitment/closing conditions (clear title, survey, termite inspection, subordination agreement, settlement statement) → approved not accepted; unmet underwriting/creditworthiness conditions (counteroffer terms, DTI/LTV, PMI determination, appraisal, income/asset verification) → denied; express withdrawal before satisfying creditworthiness conditions → withdrawn; all creditworthiness conditions met then withdrawn → approved not accepted. Action-taken date comments 4(a)(8)(ii)-1 to -6: denied/closed → "the date the action was taken or the date the notice was sent"; withdrawn → "the date the express withdrawal was received or the date shown on the notification form"; approved not accepted → "any reasonable date, such as the approval date, the deadline for accepting the offer, or the date the file was closed"; originated → "generally reports the closing or account opening date". Denial reasons — §1003.4(a)(16): "The principal reason or reasons the financial institution denied the application, if applicable." FIG codes: 1 Debt-to-income ratio; 2 Employment history; 3 Credit history; 4 Collateral; 5 Insufficient cash (downpayment, closing costs); 6 Unverifiable information; 7 Credit application incomplete; 8 Mortgage insurance denied; 9 Other (free text ≤ 255 characters); 10 Not applicable; 1111 Exempt. §1003.4(f): LAR entries "within 30 calendar days after the end of the calendar quarter in which final action is taken" (28.3).

**Colorado SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026; enrolled act read on leg.colorado.gov) — VERIFIED.** Creates C.R.S. 6-1-1701 to 6-1-1709. Section 5(1): "this act takes effect January 1, 2027"; Section 5(3): "This act applies to consequential decisions made on or after January 1, 2027". "Adverse outcome" (6-1-1701(1)): a consequential decision that "denies, terminates, revokes, or materially reduces" access or eligibility, or imposes a "materially less favorable differentiated price, cost, compensation, or other material terms" — a counteroffer at a higher rate or lower amount is an adverse outcome, not only a denial. Deployer duties (6-1-1704): "prior to a deployer using a covered ADMT to materially influence a consequential decision, the deployer shall provide a clear and conspicuous notice" at the point of interaction; within 30 days after an adverse outcome, "a plain language description of the consequential decision and the role the covered ADMT played" plus instructions for obtaining additional information and the consumer's rights; 6-1-1705(1)(a)(i): the right to request "correcting factually incorrect or materially inaccurate personal data used in a consequential decision"; (1)(a)(ii): "an opportunity for meaningful human review and reconsideration of the consequential decision, to the extent commercially reasonable"; 6-1-1703: records "not less than three years after the date of a consequential decision"; 6-1-1704(6)(a): a creditor providing notice under the "Equal Credit Opportunity Act, 15 U.S.C. sec. 1691 et seq." and "Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. sec. 1681 et seq." complies to the extent that notice "also satisfies the notice or disclosure requirements of this section"; 6-1-1706: AG enforcement with a notice of violation and a cure period of "sixty days" before Jan 1, 2030, waivable for knowing or repeated violations; AG rulemaking by Jan 1, 2027. The impact-assessment regime, the AG 90-day self-report and the express algorithmic-discrimination duty of SB24-205 are gone (00a-fed §13.1).

**Fannie Mae — DU recommendations as reason sources.** B3-2-07 Refer with Caution (last updated Feb 01, 2023; VERIFIED): "The layering and degree of risk factors result in greater risk of serious delinquency than Approve recommendations"; "Any loan casefile receiving a Refer with Caution recommendation does not represent acceptable risk for DU loans" and is "not eligible for sale to Fannie Mae as a DU loan"; the lender should review the data for accuracy, "determine if information outside submitted data could have affected DU's recommendation", resubmit if warranted, and "may manually underwrite per Selling Guide requirements". B3-2-11 (Sept 7, 2022; VERIFIED): the findings report contains "the overall underwriting recommendation and eligibility component" and processing messages; "Neither the presence nor absence of these messages alters the lender's responsibility to ensure accurate information in all areas of the loan process or otherwise comply with applicable law." Consequence: "DU Refer with Caution" is a recommendation about eligibility for sale, not a principal reason; the reasons are the loan's actual risk factors (B3-2-03 risk factors — not fetched, **[UNVERIFIED]** — e.g., credit history, DTI, LTV, reserves) as evaluated by the partner's underwriting rules. Manual underwriting is out of scope by policy (inventory 23.2-Q1), so a Refer with Caution that survives data correction and resubmission is a denial on the actual factors or a counteroffer. LL-2026-04 (Apr 08, 2026; effective Aug 6, 2026; 00a-fnma §1): AI/ML governance flows to SM as vendor; disclosure on request.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. "Colorado AI Act (reasons, correction, human appeal)" is superseded: SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026) repealed and re-enacted the regime; the duties are pre-use notice, 30-day adverse-outcome explanation, correction, human review "to the extent commercially reasonable", 3-year records, and the ECOA/FCRA deemed-compliance clause; operative for consequential decisions made on or after Jan 1, 2027 (Section 5(3) of the act) — not June 30, 2026.
2. "CFPB Circulars 2022-03/2023-03 (status to verify)": both withdrawn May 12, 2025; comments 9(b)(2)-1 to -9 govern.
3. The row's "30-day clock from complete application" needs the definition: completion is the receipt of everything the creditor "regularly obtains and considers" (credit report, verifications, appraisal, MI approval where required) with reasonable diligence — the fixture example (completed Mon Oct 5 → due Wed Nov 4, 2026) is the *conservative operational* clock this build adopts (anchor = Reg B application date), not the legal minimum.
4. The row omits the FTC as the Federal agency for a non-bank creditor (Appendix A item 9); it also omits §1002.9(f) (one notice per application, to the primary applicant) versus FCRA's per-consumer duty.
5. The row omits the counteroffer-not-accepted mechanics: adverse action notice within 90 days of the counteroffer unless the combined C-4 notice was used, and HMDA "denied on the original terms".
6. The row's "withdrawal" trigger must be an *express* withdrawal for HMDA code 4; silence is closed-for-incompleteness (code 5) only after an NOIA, or "approved but not accepted" (code 2) after approval.
7. The row's "Fannie Mae DU findings as reason source" is wrong as stated — DU recommendations and messages are inputs, never reasons (B3-2-11: the lender remains responsible under applicable law).
8. Baseline `decisions.kind` lacks `file_closed_incomplete` and `approved_not_accepted`; added here.

### Operational prerequisites
- Partner: written underwriting standards (thresholds and automatic-denial factors) approved by partner credit policy — the reason taxonomy (below) is derived from them; the partner's name/address for notices; FTC address per Appendix A; designation of the `underwriting_reviewer` roster (partner or delegated SM staff; second-level reviewers for Colorado human review must differ from the first).

- SM: 28.3 HMDA store fields writable from this process; 31.2 fair-lending monitoring hooks (reason distribution by prohibited basis is monitored on the restricted `applicant_demographics` table, never used in decisions).
- Rule sets: `regb.2026` (post-July 21, 2026 text), `regc.hmda.2026` (FIG 2026), `co.sb26_189` (effective 2027-01-01), `fnma.selling.2026-09-02`, `fnma.du.12.1`.
- Records: retention classes `regb_25m`, `hmda_3y`, `co_admt_3y` (new), `fnma_loan_file_life_plus_4y`.

### Build spec

#### Inputs and triggers
- `application.received` (Reg B date, 21.1) — starts the conservative 30-day clock; `verification.received{kind}`, `credit.report.received`, `valuation.received`, `mi.certificate.issued`/MI decline, `du.findings.received`, `condition.opened/cleared` — feed completion and the decision factors (22.x, 23.x, 24.x).
- `underwriter` agent outputs (23.3): `decision_recommendation` with `decision_factors[]` (each factor: rule id, threshold, applicant value, source evidence).
- Borrower actions: express withdrawal (any channel; verbatim captured), counteroffer acceptance, NOIA responses, Colorado human-review or data-correction requests.
- Timers: `REGB_1002_9_DECISION_30`, `REGB_1002_9_NOIA`, `REGB_1002_9C2_NOIA_RESPONSE`, `REGB_1002_9_COUNTEROFFER_90`, `CO_SB26_189_1704_PREUSE_NOTICE_GATE` (asserted by 20.3/21.1 at the point of interaction), `CO_SB26_189_1704_ADVERSE_EXPLANATION_30`, `SM_UNDERWRITING_REVIEWER_SLA_2BD`.
- Downstream: 28.3 (HMDA), 21.4 (lock cancellation on denial), 29.1 (commitment fallout), 31.2 (monitoring), 24.2 (appraisal copy duty survives denial — §1002.14(a)(4)).

#### Data model

`applications` (baseline): add `completed_application_at timestamptz null` (legal completion per §1002.2(f), set when the last regularly-obtained item arrives), `decision_due_at` (conservative: `application_date + 30 calendar days`), `disposition` ∈ {open, conditional_approval, approved, counteroffer_pending, counteroffer_accepted, denied, withdrawn, closed_incomplete, approved_not_accepted, originated}.

`decisions` (baseline; enum extended): `kind` ∈ {conditional_approval, approval, counteroffer, denial, incomplete (NOIA), withdrawal, file_closed_incomplete, approved_not_accepted}; columns added: `decision_id`, `application_id`, `decided_at`, `basis_component` ∈ {rules, judgmental, combined, automatic_denial_factor}, `decision_factors jsonb[]` (`{rule_id, description, threshold, observed, applicant_ids[], evidence_document_ids[], failed boolean}`), `counteroffer_terms jsonb null` (`{loan_amount_cents, note_rate, product_code, ltv, conditions[], expires_on}`), `combined_notice boolean` (C-4 used), `reviewer_escalation_id null`, `reviewer_id null`, `reviewer_decided_at null`, `notice_id` (baseline), `hmda_action_taken int null`, `hmda_action_taken_date date null`.

`adverse_actions` (new): `adverse_action_id`, `application_id`, `decision_id`, `kind` ∈ {denial, denial_incomplete, counteroffer_not_accepted, denial_after_counteroffer, counteroffer (adverse outcome under CO)}, `principal_reasons jsonb[]` (ordered; ≤ 4; each `{reason_code, statement_text, factor_ref, source ∈ {rules, judgmental, credit_report, valuation, verification, mi}, hmda_denial_code}`), `per_applicant jsonb[]` (each `{applicant_id, notice_id, fcra_block{cra[]: {name, address, phone, toll_free}, score, score_range_low, score_range_high, key_factors[], score_date, score_provider, model}, delivery}`), `federal_agency_block` (FTC text), `state_overlays[]`, `co_admt jsonb null` (`{pre_use_notice_id, explanation_due_at, explanation_notice_id, explanation_sent_at, human_review_requested_at, human_review_reviewer_id, human_review_completed_at, human_review_outcome, correction_requested_at, correction_completed_at}`), `reviewer_escalation_id`, `approved_by_reviewer_at`, `sent_at`, `retention_class` = {`regb_25m`, `co_admt_3y`, `fnma_loan_file_life_plus_4y`}.

`noias` (new): `noia_id`, `application_id`, `decision_id`, `items_needed jsonb[]`, `designated_period_days int` (default 14), `sent_at`, `response_due_on`, `oral_request_at null`, `responded_at null`, `closed_at null`.

`withdrawals` (new): `withdrawal_id`, `application_id`, `received_at`, `channel`, `statement_text`, `evidence_document_id`, `express boolean` (must be true to use HMDA code 4), `after_decision boolean`.

`hmda_records` (baseline, 28.3 owns): this process writes `action_taken`, `action_taken_date`, `denial_reasons[1..4]`, `denial_reason_other_text`. Events: `decision.recommended{kind ∈ denial, counteroffer, incomplete}` (the agent's proposal that opens the `underwriting_reviewer` escalation), `decision.issued{kind}` (baseline), `notice.adverse_action.sent` (baseline), `noia.sent`, `noia.responded`, `application.closed_incomplete`, `application.withdrawn`, `counteroffer.accepted`, `counteroffer.expired`, `co_admt.preuse_notice.delivered` (emitted by 20.3/21.1 when the point-of-interaction notice is delivered), `co_admt.explanation.sent`, `co_admt.human_review.requested/completed`, `co_admt.correction.requested/completed`, `hmda.action_taken.recorded`.

Reason taxonomy (`rule_sets.ecoa_reasons`, partner-approved; each entry maps to an HMDA denial code): `dti_excessive` ("Excessive obligations in relation to income", HMDA 1); `income_insufficient` ("Income insufficient for amount of credit requested", 1); `income_unverifiable` ("Unable to verify income", 6); `employment_unverifiable` ("Unable to verify employment", 6); `employment_length` ("Length of employment", 2); `credit_delinquent` ("Delinquent past or present credit obligations with others", 3); `credit_bankruptcy` ("Bankruptcy", 3); `credit_foreclosure` ("Foreclosure or repossession", 3); `credit_no_file` ("No credit file", 3); `credit_limited` ("Limited credit experience", 3); `collateral_value` ("Value or type of collateral not sufficient", 4); `collateral_condition` ("Property condition does not meet requirements", 4); `collateral_ineligible_project` ("Condominium/PUD project not eligible", 4); `cash_insufficient` ("Insufficient funds for down payment, closing costs, or reserves", 5); `assets_unverifiable` ("Unable to verify assets or source of funds", 6); `mi_denied` ("Mortgage insurance denied", 8); `incomplete` ("Credit application incomplete", 7); `residency_status` ("Residency/eligibility requirements not met", 9 with text); `identity_unverified` ("Unable to verify identity", 6); `other_specific` (free text, 9). Automatic-denial factors (comment 9(b)(2)-8) are flagged `automatic=true` (e.g., OFAC match, fraud confirmed — 22.6/28.4). Prohibited: any reason referencing a prohibited basis, "DU Refer with Caution", "internal standards", "did not meet score cutoff".

#### State machine

`applications.disposition`: `open` → `conditional_approval` (creditworthiness conditions may remain) → `approved` (all creditworthiness conditions met; only customary closing conditions remain) → `originated` (26.3) | `approved_not_accepted` (approval/commitment expired or express withdrawal after all creditworthiness conditions met) ; `open`/`conditional_approval` → `counteroffer_pending` → `counteroffer_accepted` (express acceptance → continue as `approved`/`conditional_approval` on new terms) | `denied` (no acceptance within the counteroffer's own expiry or 90 days; adverse action notice unless C-4 was used) ; `open`/`conditional_approval` → `denied` (creditworthiness/collateral/eligibility failure) ; `open` → `incomplete_noia_sent` → `open` (response) | `closed_incomplete` ; any non-terminal → `withdrawn` (express). Terminal: `originated`, `denied`, `withdrawn`, `closed_incomplete`, `approved_not_accepted`. Guards: `denied`, `counteroffer_pending`, `incomplete_noia_sent` (i.e., every denial, counteroffer and NOIA) require `reviewer_decided_at` from an `underwriting_reviewer` escalation; `withdrawn` requires `withdrawals.express=true`; `closed_incomplete` requires an expired `noias.response_due_on`; every terminal state writes `hmda_action_taken` and `hmda_action_taken_date` in the same transaction.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Clocks.** `decision_due = application_date + 30 calendar days` (fixture: Mon Oct 5 + 30 = **Wed Nov 4, 2026**; purchase: Mon Oct 19 + 30 = **Wed Nov 18, 2026**). Legal completion (`completed_application_at`) is recorded when the last regularly-obtained item (credit report, income/asset/employment verification, valuation, title commitment, MI decision where LTV > 80 %, DU findings) arrives; the conservative clock never extends beyond the application-date clock, so it is the binding one. If an NOIA is sent on day d and answered on day r, the decision is due `r + 30`. Counteroffer: `adverse_notice_due = counteroffer_sent + 90 calendar days` (e.g., counteroffer notice Fri Oct 30, 2026 → **Thu Jan 28, 2027**); the counteroffer itself may expire earlier by its own terms (default 15 calendar days; comment 9(a)(1)-5) and the adverse action notice issues at that expiry.
2. **Disposition → HMDA mapping.** denial → code 3, `action_taken_date` = notice sent date (or decision date; policy: notice date), denial reasons from `principal_reasons[].hmda_denial_code` (≤ 4; code 9 with ≤ 255-character text); counteroffer not accepted → code 3 on the original terms, date = the adverse notice date (or the counteroffer expiry when C-4 was used); counteroffer accepted → disposition of the application on the new terms (originated → 1 with the closing date; later denial → 3); express withdrawal before decision → 4, date = withdrawal received (or the form's date); NOIA unanswered → 5, date = closure date; conditional approval failing creditworthiness conditions → 3; failing only customary closing conditions, or approved and not consummated → 2, date = "any reasonable date" — policy: the approval expiration date; originated → 1, date = consummation (28.3 finalises); rescinded after closing → 2.
3. **Principal reasons.** From `decision_factors` where `failed=true`, ordered by materiality (the rules component ranks by distance from threshold — comment 9(b)(2)-5 method 1 analogue for rules: the factors whose observed value fell furthest beyond the threshold relative to applicants at the margin); at most four; every principal factor included (comment 9(b)(2)-4); automatic-denial factors always disclosed (-8); judgmental additions by the reviewer are recorded as `source='judgmental'` and disclosed when they were the basis (-6/-7); the text follows the taxonomy (never a DU message, never a score cutoff); key factors from the credit score are disclosed in the FCRA block but never substitute for ECOA reasons (-9).
4. **DU-derived factors.** `refer_with_caution` → the agent (i) verifies data accuracy and resubmits if corrected (B3-2-07); (ii) if the recommendation stands, the denial reasons are the loan's actual factors under the partner's rules (e.g., `dti_excessive` at 51.3 % vs 50 % maximum; `credit_delinquent` — two 30-day mortgage lates in the last 12 months; `collateral_value` — LTV 97 % on a second home); `approve_ineligible` → the ineligibility factor is a reason only if the partner cannot cure it (e.g., loan amount above the 2026 limit $832,750 — `other_specific` "Loan amount exceeds the maximum for this program" — or a counteroffer at an eligible amount); `out_of_scope` → the reason is the product/eligibility factor, not "out of scope".
5. **FCRA block per applicant.** For each applicant whose report was used: every CRA that furnished a report (three for a tri-merge), the score used (the applicant's representative score under B3-5.1-02 — middle of three or lower of two), its range, ≤ 4 key factors (5 with inquiries), score date, model/provider (e.g., "Classic FICO — Experian/Fair Isaac Risk Model V2" or "VantageScore 4.0 — TransUnion"); the free-report (60 days) and dispute statements; each applicant receives only that applicant's score.
6. **Notice routing.** One Reg B notice per application to the primary applicant (§1002.9(f)) is the minimum; the design sends the combined notice to every applicant with the FCRA block individualised (FCRA duty per consumer) — the ECOA content is identical. Delivery: e-delivery under E-SIGN consent (FCRA permits electronic; Reg B "written notification" satisfied electronically with consent) else first-class mail; telephone/voice applications: name and address captured at intake (comment 9(a)(1)-7).
7. **Colorado content.** For a CO consumer, the adverse action/counteroffer notice embeds: the plain-language description of the decision and "the role the covered ADMT played" (e.g., "an automated underwriting system evaluated your application against [partner]'s credit standards; a licensed underwriter reviewed and approved the decision"); how to request additional information, human review and correction of inaccurate personal data; the deadline for the explanation (30 days) is satisfied at notice sending. Human review is performed by an `underwriting_reviewer` different from the one who approved the decision; a correction request triggers re-verification and, if the data changes, re-decision.
8. **Withdrawal hygiene.** "I'm going with another lender" recorded verbatim = express; "no response for 21 days" = not a withdrawal → NOIA (if incomplete) or approval expiry (if approved). A withdrawal after a denial decision but before the notice is sent is still a denial (comment 4(a)(8)(i)-4).
9. **Interaction with locks/commitments.** Denial or closure → 21.4 `lock.cancelled{lender_declination}`; withdrawal → `lock.cancelled{borrower_withdrawal}`; both without pair-off (C2-1.2-02).
10. **Appraisal copies.** §1002.14(a)(4): copies are due "whether credit is extended or denied or if the application is incomplete or withdrawn", within 30 days after the creditor determines consummation will not occur — 24.2 owns; this process emits the terminal-disposition event 24.2 listens to.

The `underwriter` agent's rules component fails `dti_max_50` (observed 51.3 %) and flags `credit_delinquent` (a 30-day late on a revolving account in June 2026, observed on the refreshed report) as a second factor; no judgmental component; recommendation `denial` Fri Oct 23 09:40 MST → `underwriting_reviewer` escalation (SLA 2 creditor business days → Tue Oct 27; cap: `decision_due − 3 days` = Nov 1, not binding). #### AI agent design (AI-first)

`underwriter` agent (decision-notice tools: `computeCompletion`, `trackDecisionClock`, `recommendDisposition`, `selectPrincipalReasons`, `assembleFcraBlock`, `assembleStateOverlays`, `openReviewerEscalation`, `renderNotice`, `deliverNotice`, `recordHmdaAction`, `cancelLock` (21.4), `writeDecision`; Colorado tools: `sendAdmtExplanation`, `routeHumanReview`, `processCorrectionRequest`). End-to-end: the agent watches completion, keeps the borrower informed of outstanding items (oral/app requests), recommends NOIAs at the day-20 checkpoint, derives the disposition from the rules component's factor results (and the reviewer's judgmental findings where any), selects and words the principal reasons from the taxonomy, assembles the FCRA and state blocks, files the `underwriting_reviewer` escalation with a one-screen package (factors with evidence, proposed reasons, proposed HMDA codes, any fair-lending flags from 31.2's pre-send check), and on approval sends the notices and writes HMDA. Decision record (`agent_decisions`): `{decision_id, application_id, disposition, basis_component, decision_factors[], principal_reasons[], hmda{code, date, denial_codes}, fcra_blocks[], state_overlays[], clocks{decision_due_at, counteroffer_due_at, co_explanation_due_at}, reviewer{escalation_id, reviewer_id, decided_at, changes[]}, rationale, model_version, prompt_version, confidence}`. Guardrails (never alone): never send a denial, counteroffer or NOIA without `reviewer_decided_at`; never state a reason that is not a `failed` factor in `decision_factors` (the renderer refuses free text outside the taxonomy except `other_specific` with reviewer sign-off); never use "DU Refer with Caution", "internal standards", a score cutoff, or a prohibited basis as a reason; never omit a principal factor; never disclose one applicant's score to another; never make a statement that "would cause a reasonable person to believe" the creditor would deny or grant less favorable terms because of a prohibited-basis characteristic (§1002.4(b)) — the LLM's borrower-facing text passes a discouragement classifier and a prohibited-basis lexicon check before send; never treat silence as withdrawal; never report HMDA code 4 without an express withdrawal; never let the 30-day clock lapse for want of review (auto-escalation to `officer` at `decision_due − 3 days`). Escalations: `underwriting_reviewer` (every adverse disposition and NOIA; second reviewer for Colorado human review), partner `officer` (clock at risk; disputes), `compliance-sentinel` (pre-send fair-lending check on reason patterns; any late notice), `human_agent` (borrower request). Borrower-facing: the AI explains the decision in plain language consistent with the notice, offers the human-review/correction routes (Colorado) and the appraisal copy; it never negotiates new terms (the MLO of record does — 21.4/20.4). Human path: the decision console shows the same package with edit rights for the reviewer; every edit is a `changes[]` entry with the reviewer's rationale.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Application withdrawn after conditional approval but before creditworthiness conditions are met: HMDA 4 (comment 4(a)(8)(i)-13); after all creditworthiness conditions are met: HMDA 2.
- Joint applicants where one is the reason (e.g., co-borrower's credit): reasons are stated for the application; the FCRA block is per applicant; §1002.9(f) primary applicant receives the notice (both receive it in this build).
- Counteroffer accepted, then denied later on a new factor: adverse action notice within the policy 5 business days; HMDA 3 with the new reasons.
- Denial for incompleteness when data is insufficient (comment 9(a)(1)-3): reason "Credit application incomplete" (HMDA 7) — allowed only when no credit decision could be made; where the file supports a decision, the real reasons must be given (comment 9(a)(1)-4).
- Applicant supplies NOIA items after the designated period: comment 9(c)(2)-1 permits a new application; policy: reopen without a new application if within 30 days of closure and no pricing/ eligibility change; HMDA: the closed file keeps code 5; the reopened file is a new record only if a new application is taken.
- Fraud confirmed (22.6/28.4): automatic-denial factor disclosed as the specific factor ("Unable to verify identity"/"Misrepresentation in application" per taxonomy) — the SAR is never referenced (31 CFR 1029.320(d) confidentiality).
- Colorado consumer, decision before Jan 1, 2027: no SB 26-189 duties; the ECOA/FCRA notice still carries the human-review statement by policy (uniform template).
- Score model differs across applicants (not permitted for Fannie Mae delivery — LL-2026-06: same model for all borrowers): the credit adapter enforces one model per loan; a mismatch blocks decisioning.
- No credit score (thin file): the FCRA block states no score was used; reasons come from the actual factors (e.g., `credit_no_file`).
- Returned mail / bounced e-delivery: re-send by mail to the last known address within 3 business days; the notice date remains the original send date; evidence retained.
- Appraisal-copy duty on denial (24.2) and appraisal-fee refund policy on denial (NY §38.6(b)(2) refunds lock fees and points — 21.4; the appraisal fee is refundable only where the partner's policy or state law says so).

#### Audit and evidence

Per application: the completion timeline (every regularly-obtained item with receipt date; `completed_application_at`; the conservative clock), the factor-level decision record with evidence, the reviewer escalation (who, when, what changed), each notice with content hash, per-applicant FCRA blocks, delivery evidence (e-delivery receipts or mailing proof), Colorado explanation/human-review/correction records, HMDA fields with the mapping rationale, and lock/commitment fallout events. Exam packs: CFPB/state ECOA adverse-action sample (timeliness, reason specificity vs factors, FTC agency block), FCRA §615 sample, HMDA action-taken reconciliation (28.3), Colorado AG (3-year records; notices; human-review log); Fannie Mae (LL-2026-04 AI use disclosure; B3-2-07 handling of Refer with Caution; fair-lending A3-2-01); litigation: reproducible decision (inputs + rule-set/model versions → same factors).

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Completion anchor.** Default: the conservative clock (application date + 30) governs operations; legal completion is recorded for defence. Alternative: anchor on legal completion only — riskier for an AI pipeline that completes files in days anyway.
2. **NOIA designated period.** Default 14 calendar days (never fewer than 10); confirm with partner compliance and any state rules.
3. **Counteroffer form.** Default: always the combined C-4 notice (no second notice; counteroffer expiry 15 calendar days). Alternative: plain counteroffer and a separate adverse action notice at expiry — more borrower-friendly but doubles notices.
4. **Colorado scope.** Default: apply SB 26-189 when the applicant resides in Colorado *or* the property is in Colorado (the act protects Colorado consumers — residence is the safer trigger); 31.2 to confirm the act's "consumer" definition.
5. **Reviewer identity.** Default: SM underwriting staff under the partner's delegated authority, with individual licensing per state where processing/underwriting by non-employees is a licensed activity (§1008.103(d); 31.1); partner staff as the second-level (Colorado human review) reviewer.
6. **Action-taken date for approved-not-accepted.** Default: the approval/commitment expiration date; alternative: the file-closure date — either is "reasonable"; consistency is required.
7. **Reason-selection method for any future scored component of the underwriting agent.** Default: comment 9(b)(2)-5 method 1 (furthest below the marginal-pass average) with quarterly validation by 31.2; no model-derived reason text is used without a taxonomy mapping.
