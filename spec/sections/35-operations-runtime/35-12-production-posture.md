### Blueprint row
Only nonprod exists and its own runbook forbids borrower data in it: docs/DEPLOY.md:374 ("Do not load borrower data. Nonprod has no data-classification controls, its access is a single shared bearer token, and its logs are readable by everyone with project Viewer"), with the path to production listed as prose at docs/DEPLOY.md:386 (private IP, REGIONAL, an identity provider, a narrowed deployer, CMEK on every store, Cloud Armor out of preview, real adapters each behind its own secret and egress rule, org policies). The tree confirms each item: Cloud SQL is `ZONAL` with a public IP (infra/terraform/sql.tf:18, :39); the runtime throws on any `INTEGRATIONS` value but `fake` and Terraform hard-codes `fake` (src/runtime/config.ts:36-37; infra/terraform/run.tf:10); every `/v1` route is guarded by one shared `API_TOKEN` and the acting human is whatever JSON the caller sends (src/runtime/server.ts:46-48, :145-149 `actorOf`); the deployer holds `roles/editor` (infra/bootstrap.sh:121); the WAF signatures are `preview = true` (infra/terraform/lb.tf:45, :57); the FAKE reviewers approve six roles from the sweep whenever `INTEGRATIONS=fake` (src/infra/integrations/reviewers.ts:40, :54-57). What already behaves differently under `ENVIRONMENT=production` is the seed: the demo clock is not constructed (src/runtime/main.ts:57), `/v1/demo/*` answers 403 (src/runtime/server.ts:354, :361), the partner-book demo offers are off (src/runtime/partner-book-offers.ts:90), and the two cipher keys are required (src/runtime/staff/repo.ts:32, src/infra/pii/tin.ts:17). This process turns the prose into measured controls: an environment manifest recorded at every deploy, a posture check whose failures are clocks with owners, a per-vendor integration switch that two people throw, a restore drill with observed RPO/RTO, a data scan that keeps real people out of nonprod, and the go-live checklist that the four-week parallel run against the incumbent servicer must satisfy before a real loan is serviced here. Inputs: the deploy's Terraform and runtime facts, the environment's own database, the incumbent's daily trial balance. Outputs: manifests, checks, findings, drills, scans, the parallel-run report and the attestation — each a hashed document 34.4's evidence pack exports. Today a person does none of it because there is nothing to do it against.

### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-15)
**FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314.4, verified in 19.2).** The program must include encryption of customer information in transit and at rest, multi-factor authentication for any individual accessing any information system, procedures for the secure disposal of customer information, change management, and monitoring and logging of authorized users' activity (314.4(c)(3), (c)(5), (c)(6), (c)(7), (c)(8)); the written risk assessment and the Qualified Individual approve exceptions (314.4(a), (b)). Operationally: no shared credential, a person behind every privileged act, encryption keys under Supermortgage's control, and a documented exception with an expiry for any control that is not met. 19.2 owns the program; this process measures the environments against it.

**23 NYCRR Part 500 (Second Amendment, Nov. 1, 2023; verified sections in 19.2).** 500.12 requires multi-factor authentication for any individual accessing any information system; 500.13(a) an asset inventory with classification, owner and location; 500.16(d) that the covered entity "periodically test its ability to restore its critical data and information systems from backups" **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — the row is 19.2's `NYDFS_500_16D_BACKUP_RESTORE_TEST_365`; the annual period is 19.2's policy reading, the quarterly automated drill here is Supermortgage policy]**. 500.7 limits privileged access and requires periodic review; 34.1's `SM_STAFF_ACCESS_REVIEW_90` and 19.2's `SM_PRIV_ACCESS_REVIEW_90` carry that.

**Fannie Mae Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement (Sept. 2, 2025; mandatory Aug. 12, 2025; verified in 19.2).** Encryption in transit and at rest for Fannie Mae confidential information, access management with least privilege and MFA, business resiliency with tested recovery, vendor oversight tiered by risk (19.3's `FNMA_SUPP_VENDOR_REASSESSMENT`), and incident notice. The parallel run produces the first two months of Form 496/496A the Guide expects (6.3, 35.4) against the incumbent's; the Supplement itself names no parallel run **[UNVERIFIED — the 28-day parallel run and its pass rule are Supermortgage policy, not a Fannie Mae or CFPB requirement; the transfer itself is governed by 1.x/17.x and Reg X §1024.33]**.

**Google Cloud controls as the terraform attributes state them.** Private IP only is `ip_configuration.ipv4_enabled = false` with Private Service Access and Direct VPC egress from Cloud Run; high availability is `availability_type = "REGIONAL"`; point-in-time recovery is `backup_configuration.point_in_time_recovery_enabled = true` with `transaction_log_retention_days` (7 today, sql.tf:27); CMEK is `encryption_key_name` on the instance (sql.tf:10) with the key's `rotation_period` (kms.tf:18, 90 days) **[UNVERIFIED against Google's current documentation — the attribute names are what infra/terraform uses at HEAD; the build re-checks them when the production `envs/` variables are written]**.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**: (1) DEPLOY.md:393 ("Path to prod" item 2, :386) lists "IAP or the identity provider" as an alternative; the build takes the identity provider (OIDC, the Workspace directory) behind 34.1's own sessions, with IAP not required, because 34.1's action log needs the `staff_user_id` the session resolves, not a header IAP injects. (2) DEPLOY.md:401 ("Path to prod" item 6) says "real `INTEGRATIONS` adapters"; the switch here is per vendor with `off` as the default, so a production environment can exist with no vendor real yet, and a vendor is never FAKE in production (35.7's `NO_FAKE_IN_PRODUCTION`). (3) config.ts:36-37 accepts only `fake`; the build accepts `fake` and `real`, and `real` reads `integration_switches`. (4) The gap assessment's "Real-loan configuration" finding (src/runtime/delinquency.ts:27 one loan-local zone; src/runtime/servicing.ts:46 fixture servicer contact blocks) is a go-live item here (GL-09), fixed by 35.5 and 35.9, not by this process. (5) The parallel run compares our figures with the incumbent's; where they differ the owning section's command corrects our book under its own roles — this process dispositions, never posts.

#### State machine
Per finding (`posture_findings`, latest row): `opened —(a ciso acknowledges; optional)→ acknowledged —(a later manifest's check passes)→ resolved` | `—(a 19.2 exception approved by the Qualified Individual)→ excepted —(exception `expires_at` passes without a passing check)→ opened` again as a new finding. Per switch (`integration_switches`): `off | fake —(ciso requests, compliance confirms ≤ 10 min)→ real(sandbox | live)`; `real —(same two people)→ off`; in production `→ fake` is refused for ever after `go_live.attested`, and before it only while no `parties` row exists. Per drill: `started → passed | failed`. Per parallel run: `opened —(daily reconcile)→ opened … —(28 days, last 7 days clean, no open diff; officer)→ closed(passed)` | `—(officer, reason)→ closed(abandoned)`; an `extended` row moves `planned_end_on` forward and resets the clean-week count. Per checklist: every item `open → satisfied | waived` (waived only for `GL-08`, `GL-10` by `compliance` with a reason; the money and identity items cannot be waived) → `GL-00 attested` once, by two people. Terminal: `resolved`, `closed`, `attested`. Who: the agent computes every check, scan, reconciliation and checklist status; `ciso` resolves drift, requests switches, runs drills and requests the attestation; `compliance` confirms switches and the attestation, closes real-data findings; `officer` opens, dispositions and closes the parallel run; `counsel` signs GL-07.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Two environments, one image, one migration set.** Production is a separate Google Cloud project (`supermortgage-prod`), its own Terraform state (`environment=production`), its own Cloud SQL, KMS ring, secrets, load balancer and service accounts; nonprod and staging are `environment ∈ {nonprod, staging}`. The image digest and `migration_head` deployed to production are the ones the manifest records; a production manifest whose `image_digest` was never recorded for `staging` first fails `PST-13` (rule 2). Every manifest is a `documents` row with a hash; `env_hash` lets two manifests be compared without re-reading them.
2. **The posture controls (seed `posture_controls`, version 1).** Each is a check the agent computes from the manifest and the environment's own database; `required` in the environments listed; a `fail` on a required control opens a finding at the control's severity.
   - `PST-01` private networking (production, staging): `terraform.sql.ipv4_enabled = false`, Private Service Access present, Cloud Run `vpc_egress` set — sev 1 (sql.tf:39 today).
   - `PST-02` high availability (production): `availability_type = "REGIONAL"` — sev 2 (sql.tf:18 today).
   - `PST-03` backups (all): `pitr = true`, `log_retention_days ≥ 7`, `retained_backups ≥ 14` — sev 1 (sql.tf:23-31 passes today).

3. **A failure is a clock with an owner, never a note.** `posture.check` writes one `posture_checks` row per control and, for each required control that fails and has no open finding, a `posture_findings{opened}` row and `posture.drift.detected` — one finding per (`environment`, `control_code`) until it resolves; a second run with the same failure appends a check row and nothing else. A finding resolves only by a later manifest's passing check (`cause = manifest`) or a 19.2 `control_exceptions` row approved by the Qualified Individual (`ciso`) with `expires_at ≤ 12 months` (`cause = exception`); a person cannot close a finding by hand. `unverifiable` (the manifest lacks the fact) is a `fail` in production and `not_applicable` elsewhere.
4. **`INTEGRATIONS` is a real switch per vendor.** `loadConfig` accepts `fake` and `real`. Under `fake` every vendor port is its in-repo FAKE (every build stage, docs/DEPLOY.md §7). Under `real` the runtime constructs, for each vendor, the adapter named by the latest `integration_switches` row for (`environment`, `vendor`): `real` → the adapter over its `secret_ref`; `off` or no row → an `OffPort` whose every call answers the typed refusal `VENDOR_OFF{vendor}` before any write (a tool that needs the vendor is refused, never served by a FAKE); `fake` → refused at start-up in production (`NO_FAKE_IN_PRODUCTION`, 35.7) and honoured elsewhere. A switch is two people (`TWO_PERSON_SWITCH`): the `ciso` requests, `compliance` confirms the same `request_id` within 10 minutes, the row's `effective_at` is the confirmation, and running instances re-read the table within one sweep (the demo-clock `follow` pattern, src/runtime/demo-clock.ts). In `nonprod` and `staging` a vendor that moves money or reports on a person — `lockbox_bai2`, `ach_nacha`, `eoscar`, `fnma_p360`, `fnma_smdu`, `fnma_lsdu`, `print_mail`, `mers`, `credit_bureau`, `irs_ives` — may be `real` only with `endpoint_class = sandbox` (`MONEY_VENDOR_SANDBOX_ONLY`); production is `live` only.
5. **Backups and the restore drill.** Production keeps PITR with 7 days of transaction logs and 14 retained daily backups (sql.tf:23-31, taken at 07:00 UTC = 03:00 America/New_York). Every 90 calendar days (and before go-live) the drill job clones the production instance to `supermortgage-drill-<yyyymmdd>` at a PITR target inside the last hour, connects the runtime in read-only mode, and computes: `rpo_observed_s = pitr_target_at − max(loan_events.created_at)` on the clone; `rto_observed_s = completed_at − started_at`; `row_checks` per spec table (count on the source as of the target instant via `created_at ≤ target` where the table has it, equal to the clone's count); `event_chain_ok` (per loan, `loan_events.sequence` runs 1..n without a gap); `ledger_balanced` (every `ledger_entry_sets` row's lines sum to zero). Targets: RPO ≤ 300 s, RTO ≤ 4 h (19.2's tier-1 `rpo_minutes = 5`, `rto_hours = 4` **[UNVERIFIED — policy values, to be set in 19.2's `assets` rows]**). A drill passes only when every check holds and both targets are met; the clone is destroyed within 24 hours and the destruction is part of the evidence. A failed drill is sev 1 to the `ciso` and does not satisfy either clock.
6. **No real borrower data in nonprod, no synthetic data in production.** The daily `nonprod_real_data` scan (nonprod, staging) examines every `parties` row (`synthetic = true` required), every `transfer_batches` row (`synthetic = true`), every `applications` row (its borrowers' identity verification event carries `vendor = "FAKE"`), every `integration_switches` row (rule 4's sandbox rule) and every `documents` row (`metadata.blob_store = "fake-blob"` or a nonprod bucket); each rule that fails contributes `{table, column, rule, count}` — counts only. A scan with `real_data_found = true` emits `posture.real_data.detected`, sev 1 to `compliance`, and arms the purge clock. The remedy is the environment, not the row (the tables are append-only): the database is dropped and rebuilt from `db/migrations` and re-seeded (`db/rebuild-nonprod.sh`), the new manifest is recorded, and a clean scan carrying the finding's `scan_id` emits `posture.real_data.purged`. At the door: `POST /v1/transfers/batches` in nonprod refuses a tape without the `X-Supermortgage-Synthetic: true` header (`REAL_DATA_REFUSED_IN_NONPROD`) and production refuses a tape with it (`SYNTHETIC_REFUSED_IN_PRODUCTION`); the borrower entry in nonprod accepts identity only from the FAKE vendor. The production scan (`production_synthetic`) is the inverse: any `parties.synthetic = true` row fails `PST-11`.
7. **The parallel run.** For 28 consecutive calendar days from `opened_on` the platform services the transferred book in production while the incumbent remains the system of record and the borrower-facing servicer (no notice, statement, draft, credit-report line or remittance leaves the platform: every outbound vendor stays `off` or `sandbox` until go-live, and 35.11's daily report proves it). Each evening the incumbent's trial balance for the day is loaded as a document and `parallel_run.reconcile` compares, per loan, the eight fields of `parallel_run_diffs.field` — ours from the typed rows (35.1: `loans`, `escrow_accounts`, `loan_installments`, `fees`, the 5.x/6.x period rows) at 21:00 America/New_York, theirs from the file. Tolerance is zero on every field: a money field matches only to the cent, a date only exactly, a count only exactly. A mismatch opens a diff (one per loan, field and day; a diff already open on the same loan and field for the same `ours`/`theirs` pair is not reopened). The day's row carries `comparisons = loans × 8`, `matched`, `mismatched` and `mismatch_cents = Σ |delta_cents|` over the day's mismatched money fields; the daily report is a hashed document.
8. **A diff is dispositioned by an officer and corrected by the owning section.** `parallel_run.disposition` requires `officer` (`ROLE_REQUIRED{officer}`) and a reason; the four dispositions are `ours_right`, `theirs_right`, `both_wrong`, `timing`. A disposition changes nothing on the book (`NO_MONEY_FIELD`): when ours is wrong the officer runs the owning section's own command (a 2.7 late-charge waiver, a 3.x escrow adjustment, a 2.1 reversal) under that section's roles, and the next day's reconciliation shows the field matched. The agent proposes a disposition with a confidence and a rationale; it never applies one.
9. **The pass rule and the close.** A run closes `passed` only when `as_of_date − opened_on ≥ 28` days, every one of the last 7 reconciled days has `mismatched = 0` on the six money fields (`next_due_date` and `days_delinquent` may differ by `timing` on those days if dispositioned), no diff is open, and no reconciliation day is missing (a breach of `SM_PROD_PARALLEL_RUN_DAILY` resets the clean-week count to 0 from the missed day). Otherwise `close` is refused with `PARALLEL_RUN_TOO_SHORT{days}` or `PARALLEL_RUN_OPEN_DIFFS{count}` or `PARALLEL_RUN_DIRTY_WEEK{days_clean}`; an officer may close `abandoned` with a reason at any time, which un-satisfies the go-live gate (a new run re-arms it).
10. **The go-live checklist (`GL-01`…`GL-12`), computed, never typed.** `go_live.check` derives each item's status from rows and answers the evidence reference: `GL-01` a production manifest recorded in the last 24 h; `GL-02` the latest production posture run has `failed = 0` and no open finding; `GL-03` a passed production restore drill in the last 90 days; `GL-04` 35.7's handover board for production shows every kernel role `staffed` (holders ≥ 1, `fake = false`) and the dual-control pairs for `officer`, `funding_approver`, `qc_officer` distinct; `GL-05` `INTEGRATIONS = real` and every vendor the servicing book needs (`lockbox_bai2`, `ach_nacha`, `print_mail`, `edelivery`, `telephony_sms_email`, `eoscar`, `fnma_p360`, `fnma_smdu`, `fnma_lsdu`, `mers`, `blob_store`) `real` and `live` with a canary call logged in the last 24 h; `GL-06` the parallel run closed `passed`; `GL-07` the retention matrix signed by `counsel` (35.2) and the bucket lock applied; `GL-08` a nonprod scan with `real_data_found = false` every day for the last 30 days (waivable by `compliance` with a reason when nonprod was rebuilt inside the window); `GL-09` per-loan time zone and the servicer identity blocks read from configuration, not constants (35.5/35.9; src/runtime/delinquency.ts:27, src/runtime/servicing.ts:46 replaced); `GL-10` two month-end closes attested in the parallel run (35.4) with the Form 496 figures reconciled to the incumbent's (waivable by `compliance` when the run spans one month-end, with the second close due within 30 days); `GL-11` 35.11's `ops_daily_reports` show `fake_approvals = 0` for the last 7 days in production; `GL-12` every `[UNVERIFIED]` item this process and 35.2/35.7 name has a signed confirmation document. `GL-00` is the attestation: `ciso` requests, `compliance` confirms within 10 minutes (`TWO_PERSON_GO_LIVE`), only after `SM_PROD_GO_LIVE_ATTEST_GATE`'s day and with every item `satisfied` or `waived` (`GO_LIVE_ITEM_OPEN{code}`); the attestation names the `manifest_id` it was made against, and a later manifest with a different `env_hash` re-opens `GL-01`/`GL-02` (the attestation stands; the report shows it was made against an older manifest).
11. **Nothing here touches a borrower's money or identity.** Every tool reads the environment and writes its own rows; no ledger line, no `loans` column, no notice; the events and documents carry ids, counts, hashes and the eight reconciliation figures only.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`compliance-sentinel` agent (tools: `posture.record`, `posture.check`, `posture.drift.resolve`, `integrations.switch`, `integrations.status`, `backup.drill`, `data.scan`, `parallel_run.open`, `parallel_run.reconcile`, `parallel_run.disposition`, `parallel_run.close`, `go_live.check`, `go_live.attest`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end: unprompted, the agent records every deploy's manifest, runs the posture check on every manifest and daily, opens and resolves findings from checks alone, runs the nonprod scan daily and the production inverse, runs the drill on its quarter day, reconciles every parallel-run day at 21:00 and proposes a disposition per diff with a confidence and a rationale, and computes the checklist on demand; it proposes switches (a vendor whose canary fails three days running → propose `off`) and never throws one; it never edits infrastructure (`POSTURE_IS_OBSERVED` — Terraform and IAM change only through the deploy workflow reviewed by people), never closes a finding by hand, never dispositions a diff, never attests. Decision record schema `{subject: {kind: manifest | check_run | finding | switch | drill | scan | parallel_run | diff | checklist, id}, environment, action, control_code?, vendor?, field?, proposed_disposition?, by, by_role, confirmed_by?, reason, rule_set_version: posture.v1, model_version: deterministic (the reconciliation and checks) | <model> (a proposed disposition's rationale), prompt_version: 35.12-v1, confidence}`. Escalations: `ciso` for every posture drift, a failed or missed drill, a secret past 90 days and a canary failing three days; `compliance` for real data in nonprod, a switch or attestation left unconfirmed, and any `go_live.attested` event without both people; `officer` for a missed reconciliation day, a diff open more than 2 business days and the parallel-run close; `counsel` for `GL-07` and `GL-12`.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- The deploy workflow cannot reach `/v1/posture/manifests` (the load balancer is not up yet) → the deploy fails before the smoke; no manifest, no check; the next day's `SM_PROD_POSTURE_DAILY` breach names the missing run.
- A manifest arrives for `production` while `INTEGRATIONS=fake` → `posture.check` fails `PST-10` sev 1, and the runtime itself refuses to start (`NO_FAKE_IN_PRODUCTION`), so the service is down and the deploy's smoke fails: the environment never serves a request with fakes.
- A vendor switch confirmed while a command holding that vendor's port is mid-flight → the command completes on the port it constructed; the next command sees the new mode; the `integration.switched` event carries `effective_at`.
- The PITR target falls inside a backup gap or the logs were pruned → the drill fails `failure_reason = target_unrecoverable` sev 1; the clock stays unsatisfied; the finding names the observed log retention.
- The incumbent's file names a loan the platform does not have → the day is reconciled for the loans it has and a diff of `field = upb_cents`, `ours = absent` opens for the missing loan; a file that is missing a loan we have does the same with `theirs = absent`.
- A day's file arrives late (after 21:00) → the reconciliation runs when it arrives, as that `as_of_date`; the daily clock breaches at 21:00 the next day if none ran; the clean-week count treats a late day as reconciled once it is.
- A parallel run extended (`extended` row) → `planned_end_on` moves, the gate re-anchors, the clean-week count resets; the report shows every extension with its reason.
- `go_live.attest` confirmed by the same person who requested → `TWO_PERSON_GO_LIVE` refused; logged with both ids.
- Nonprod rebuilt while a real-data finding is open → the new manifest and a clean scan close it; the old database's backup is deleted and the deletion is part of the purge evidence.
- A production scan finds `parties.synthetic = true` → `PST-11` fails sev 1; there is no purge in production (append-only): the finding is resolved only by a 19.2 exception naming the rows and the counsel-signed disposal, or by proving the marker was wrongly set (a new manifest cannot resolve it).
- The environment has no incumbent (a purchase-originated book only) → the parallel run is opened with `loan_count = 0` and `incumbent_servicer = none`; the gate still runs 28 days over the origination flows' output; `GL-06` reads the run's close.

#### Audit and evidence
What an examiner is shown: the manifests as recorded at every deploy with their hashes and the diff between any two `env_hash`es; the posture control catalogue with citations and every run's per-control result and the findings' full histories with clocks and who resolved or excepted each; the switch ledger with both people per throw and the canary log per vendor; every drill with observed RPO/RTO, row checks, chain and ledger results and the clone's lifecycle; every scan's counts; the parallel run day by day with the incumbent's hashed files, our figures, each diff's disposition and the section command that corrected our book; the go-live checklist with each item's evidence reference, the waivers with reasons, and the attestation with both people and the manifest it was made against — exported as a period-subject evidence pack (34.4 `controls.evidence.pack`) and, for 19.2, as `control_test_results` rows and `backup_restore_test.passed` events the safeguards program reports on.

### Open questions / decisions
1. Should staging be a third project or a second Terraform workspace in the nonprod project? **Default: a second workspace in the nonprod project (`environment=staging`), with the nonprod class rules (synthetic data only, sandbox vendors) and `PST-01`/`PST-05` required so the network and identity posture is rehearsed before production.**
2. Is 28 days the right parallel-run length, and must it span a month-end? **Default: 28 calendar days minimum, and `GL-10` asks for two month-end closes, so in practice the run starts in the last week of a month and spans two closes; `compliance` may waive to one with the second due within 30 days.**
3. Zero tolerance on every parallel-run field, or a cent tolerance on interest-bearing fields where the incumbent rounds differently? **Default: zero; a rounding difference is dispositioned `theirs_right` or `ours_right` with the rule cited, and a systematic one is a 2.x/3.x finding, not a tolerance.**
4. Who is the Qualified Individual for posture exceptions — the `ciso` role, or `compliance`? **Default: `ciso`, as 19.2's Qualified Individual; `compliance` confirms switches and the attestation so no one person both runs and approves the environment.**
5. Keep the 90-day machine-secret age rule (`PST-14`) or tie it to each vendor's contract? **Default: 90 days for Supermortgage's own secrets (database, cipher keys, the URL secret); a vendor secret follows the vendor's rotation term when longer, recorded on the switch row.**
6. Does the engine gain a keyed subject override (finding-keyed clocks) in 35.7 or here? **Default: 35.7 lands it (`SM_ROLE_QUEUE_UNSTAFFED_1BD` needs it first); this process's two deadline rows use it and, until it exists, arm on the global subject with the finding id in the payload.**
