### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**APR accuracy — §1026.22 (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026; 00a-fed §1.8).** §1026.22(a)(2): the APR "shall be considered accurate if it is not more than 1⁄8 of 1 percentage point above or below" the actual rate. §1026.22(a)(3): in an irregular transaction the tolerance is "not more than 1⁄4 of 1 percentage point above or below"; irregular transactions are those with "multiple advances, irregular payment periods, or irregular payment amounts (other than an irregular first period or an irregular first or final payment)". A 30-year fixed-rate loan with an odd-days first period is therefore a **regular** transaction and gets the 1/8 tolerance. §1026.22(a)(4) (mortgage loans): in addition to (a)(2)–(3), the disclosed APR "shall also be considered accurate if" it "results from the disclosed finance charge" and the disclosed finance charge "would be considered accurate under §1026.18(d)(1) or §1026.38(o)(2)" (or, for rescission, §1026.23(g)/(h)). §1026.22(a)(5): if the finance charge is calculated incorrectly but is still accurate under those sections, an understated APR is accurate if it "is closer to the actual annual percentage rate than the rate that would be considered accurate under paragraph (a)(4)" (and symmetrically for an overstated APR) [closely paraphrased — the fetch tool paraphrased (a)(4)–(5); the digest's reading is the same]. §1026.22(b): tables or any computation tool may be used if the result "equals the rate determined in accordance with appendix J" within the tolerance.

**Method — Appendix J (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026).** (b)(1) actuarial method; (b)(2) the unit-period is "the most frequently occurring common period" between payments, not exceeding one year. (b)(3)(i): "The term of the transaction begins on the date of its consummation, except that if the finance charge or any portion of it is earned beginning on a later date, the term begins on the later date." (b)(3)(ii): a period "includes the interval of time between the date the finance charge begins to be earned and the date of the first advance thereafter or the date of the first payment thereafter." Counting rule for a monthly unit-period: full unit-periods are counted from the later date (the first payment date) and the remaining fraction is "the number of days measured forward from the earlier date to the beginning of the first full unit-period, divided by 30" (365 for annual). The general equation discounts each payment by (1 + f·i)(1 + i)^t where f is the odd-days fraction and t the number of full unit-periods.

**Minor irregularities — §1026.17(c)(4) (eCFR current as of 9/04/2026).** "In making calculations and disclosures, the creditor may disregard" (iii) "For transactions in which the term is at least 10 years, a first period shorter than or not more than 32 days longer than a regular period," and (iv) "The occurrence of leap year." §1026.17(c)(1): "The disclosures shall reflect the terms of the legal obligation between the parties."

**Finance charge — §1026.4 (eCFR current as of 9/04/2026).** (a): "The finance charge is the cost of consumer credit as a dollar amount. It includes any charge payable directly or indirectly by the consumer and imposed directly or indirectly by the creditor as an incident to or a condition of the extension of credit." (a)(1): third-party charges are included if the creditor requires the service or retains a portion; (a)(2): "Fees charged by a third party that conducts the loan closing are finance charges only if the creditor requires the particular services, requires the imposition of the charge, or retains a portion"; (a)(3): mortgage-broker fees are finance charges "even if the creditor does not require the consumer to use a mortgage broker and does not retain any portion". Examples (b): interest; service/transaction/carrying charges; "points, loan fees, assumption fees, finder's fees"; "Appraisal, investigation, and credit report fees" (b)(4); "Premiums or other charges for any guarantee or insurance protecting the creditor against the consumer's default" (b)(5); debt-cancellation charges (b)(10). Exclusions (c): "Application fees charged to all applicants for credit, whether or not credit is actually extended" (c)(1); late/default charges (c)(2); "Seller's points" (c)(5); real-estate related fees "if the fees are bona fide and reasonable in amount" (c)(7): (i) "Fees for title examination, abstract of title, title insurance, property survey, and similar purposes"; (ii) "Fees for preparing loan-related documents, such as deeds, mortgages, and reconveyance or settlement documents"; (iii) "Notary and credit-report fees"; (iv) "Property appraisal fees or fees for inspections to assess the value or condition of the property if the service is performed prior to closing" (including pest and flood-hazard determinations per the rule text); (v) "Amounts required to be paid into escrow or trustee accounts if the amounts would not otherwise be included in the finance charge". (d): premiums for property insurance and voluntary credit insurance/debt cancellation excluded on the stated conditions (coverage not required or insurer freely chosen; disclosed; "consumer signs or initials an affirmative written request" for voluntary coverage). (e)(1): "Taxes and fees prescribed by law that actually are or will be paid to public officials for determining the existence of or perfecting, releasing, or satisfying a security interest"; (e)(3): "Any tax levied on security instruments or on documents evidencing indebtedness if the payment is a requirement for recording".

**CD accuracy standards consumed here — §1026.38(o)(2), (o)(4), (t)(4) (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026).** Finance charge: "The disclosed finance charge shall be treated as accurate if the amount disclosed as the finance charge: (i) Is understated by no more than $100; or (ii) Is greater than the amount required to be disclosed." APR: "expressed as a percentage" with the statement "Your costs over the loan term expressed as a rate. This is not your interest rate." Percentages are rounded to "three decimal places" ((t)(4) — phrase read; the trailing-zero sentence is in §1026.37(o)(4)(ii) for the LE and is applied to the CD by (t)(4) [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — (t)(4)(ii) full text not rendered by the fetch]).

**Redisclosure trigger that this engine feeds — §1026.19(f)(2)(ii)(A) (eCFR 9/08/2026):** a new three-business-day waiting period is required when "The annual percentage rate disclosed under §1026.38(o)(4) becomes inaccurate, as defined in §1026.22." (25.2 owns the waiting period; 25.1 produces the accuracy verdict.)

**TRID tolerances — §1026.19(e)(3) (00a-fed §1.5).** Zero tolerance (e)(3)(i); 10 percent aggregate for third-party services the consumer may shop for and recording fees (e)(3)(ii); unlimited for prepaid interest, property insurance, escrow deposits, consumer-selected providers not on the list, and property taxes/non-required services (e)(3)(iii). 21.5 owns `tolerance_tests`, `tolerance_cures`, `runToleranceTest` and `REGZ_1026_19F2V_TOLERANCE_REFUND_60`; 25.1 invokes `runToleranceTest` at every checkpoint and gates on its result.

**QM/ATR, HPML, HOEPA (00a-fed §5; 23.4 owns).** General QM APR thresholds 2.25 / 3.5 / 6.5 with 2026 loan-amount tiers $137,958 / $82,775; 2026 points-and-fees cap "3 percent of the total loan amount for loans ≥ $137,958"; safe harbor if the QM is "not a higher-priced covered transaction" (APR < APOR + 1.5 for first liens); HPML at APOR + 1.5 (≤ conforming limit) / 2.5 (above) / 3.5 (subordinate); HOEPA APR trigger "more than 6.5 percentage points" (first lien), 2026 loan-amount threshold $27,592 and dollar trigger $1,380; §1026.35(b)(1) escrow "established before consummation" for a first-lien HPML with the five-year cancellation floor. These are determinations, and 25.1 re-executes them on CD and consummation figures; it does not redefine the tables (`qm_determinations`, `hpml_determinations`, `high_cost_determinations` are 23.4's).

**State high-cost / predatory statutes — representative (verified today where noted).** New York Banking Law §6-l (nysenate.gov, most recent revision shown 2014-09-22): a home loan is high-cost where "the annual percentage rate of the home loan at consummation of the transaction exceeds eight percentage points over the yield on treasury securities" (first lien; "equals or exceeds nine percentage points" subordinate) or "The total points and fees exceed: five percent of the total loan amount if the total loan amount is fifty thousand dollars or more"; scope: "The principal amount of the loan at origination does not exceed the conforming loan size limit (including any applicable special limit for jumbo mortgages) for a comparable dwelling as established from time to time by the federal national mortgage association"; among the consequences, "a lender shall not, directly or indirectly, finance any points and fees … in an amount that exceeds three percent of the principal amount" and no balloon "unless such balloon payment becomes due and payable at least fifteen years after the loan's origination". North Carolina G.S. 24-1.1E (ncleg.gov; last amendment shown 2024-28, s. 6(a)): high-cost where the APR "is such that the loan is considered a 'mortgage' under section 152 of the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act of 1994", or "the total points and fees exceed five percent (5%) of the total loan amount if the total loan amount is twenty thousand dollars ($20,000) or more", or the loan permits prepayment penalties "more than 30 months after the loan closing or which exceed, in the aggregate, more than two percent (2%) of the amount prepaid"; scope "does not exceed the conforming loan size limit for a single-family dwelling as established from time to time by Fannie Mae"; consequences include "A lender may not make a high-cost home loan without first receiving certification from a counselor approved by the North Carolina Housing Finance Agency" and no financing of points and fees. Both statutes set points-and-fees triggers below the federal HOEPA test and use their own counting rules, so a loan that passes §1026.32 can still fail a state test. Title 6 mortgage-banker rules), Texas (Finance Code ch. 343 high-cost home loans), Colorado (C.R.S. 5-3.5 Consumer Equity Protection Act), Kentucky (KRS 360.100), Maryland (Com. Law §12-127 et seq.), Indiana (IC 24-9), Arkansas (Home Loan Protection Act). The engine treats every state row as its own test code with its own rule-set version.

**Fee reasonableness and RESPA §8 — §1024.14 (CFPB eRegs; 00a-fed §6.6) and the CFPB RESPA Section 8 FAQs (page last modified March 15, 2024, fetched 2026-09-10).** §1024.14(b): "No person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback or other thing of value" for referrals; (c): no split of charges "other than for services actually performed"; (g)(1) permits bona fide compensation "for goods or facilities actually furnished or for services actually performed" and "normal promotional and educational activities that are not conditioned on the referral of business"; (g)(2): "The Bureau may investigate high prices"; whether the payment raises charges is "irrelevant". FAQs on MSAs: "A lawful MSA is an agreement for the performance of marketing services where the payments under the MSA are reasonably related to the value of services actually performed"; unlawful where there is "An agreement to pay for referrals", or "the payment is in excess of the reasonable market value for the services performed", or "the services are not actually performed, the services are nominal, or the payments are duplicative". Gifts/promotional activity: "The activities are not conditioned on referral of business; and The activities do not involve defraying expenses that otherwise would be incurred by the referral source." The FAQs are the current CFPB posture (Compliance Bulletin 2015-05 was rescinded when the FAQs issued in October 2020 — the FAQ page does not restate this [PARTIALLY VERIFIED]); the May 12, 2025 withdrawal notice did not touch the FAQs or the February 2023 digital comparison-shopping advisory opinion (00a-fed §6.6). AfBA (§1024.15): disclosure at referral/application, no required use (except attorney, credit reporting agency, appraiser), only a return on ownership interest; retained 5 years. §1026.4(c)(7) makes excluded real-estate fees conditional on being "bona fide and reasonable in amount", which is the hook for the fee-reasonableness test.

**Loan-originator rules — §1026.36 (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026; 00a-fed §12).** (d)(1)(i): "no loan originator shall receive and no person shall pay to a loan originator … compensation in an amount that is based on a term of a transaction"; proxy: "A factor that is not itself a term of a transaction is a proxy for a term of the transaction if the factor consistently varies with that term" over a significant number of transactions; compensation "based on a fixed percentage of the amount of credit extended" is permitted (subject to a minimum/maximum dollar amount); non-deferred profits-based compensation only if it "does not … in the aggregate, exceed 10 percent of the individual loan originator's total compensation". (d)(2)(i)(A) dual compensation: "if any loan originator receives compensation directly from a consumer … No loan originator shall receive compensation … from any person other than the consumer"; (d)(2)(i)(C): the organization may still pay its individual originators. (d)(3): affiliates "shall be treated as a single 'person'". (e)(1): "a loan originator shall not direct or 'steer' a consumer to consummate a transaction based on the fact that the originator will receive greater compensation … unless the consummated transaction is in the consumer's interest"; (e)(2)–(3) safe harbor: present, for each transaction type of interest, "The loan with the lowest interest rate", "The loan with the lowest interest rate without negative amortization, a prepayment penalty … or balloon payment in the first 7 years", and "The loan with the lowest total dollar amount of discount points, origination points or origination fees", obtained "from a significant number of the creditors with which the originator regularly does business". (f)(2): individuals licensed/registered "to the extent the individual is required to be licensed or registered under the SAFE Act"; (f)(3)(ii): background, credit and fitness ("demonstrated financial responsibility, character, and general fitness"). (g)(1)–(2): the organization's "name and NMLSR ID … and the name of the individual loan originator … with primary responsibility for the origination and … that NMLSR ID" on (i) the credit application, (ii) the §1026.19(e) and (f) disclosures, (iii) the note, and (iv) the security instrument. (h): no mandatory arbitration; (i): no financing of credit-insurance premiums. §1026.25(c)(2): compensation records retained "for three years after the date of receipt or payment".

**SAFE Act / Reg H (00a-fed §12).** §1008.103(b): an individual "engages in the business of a loan originator" by taking an application and offering or negotiating terms for compensation; (d): independent-contractor processors/underwriters must be licensed; (e)(3)–(4): clerical exemption only "at the direction of and subject to the supervision and instruction of" a licensed originator. No regulator statement on AI "taking an application" was located (UNVERIFIED-negative). NMLS license data: NMLS B2B Access (FAQ updated 5/2017) provides license/registration records for individuals, companies and branches, sponsorship relationships and state authorizations ("you can ask the B2B Data to tell you in which states they are authorized to conduct business"), with phone/e-mail "intentionally left out"; delivery mechanism, refresh cadence and fees are not on the public FAQ [PARTIALLY VERIFIED]; NMLS Consumer Access remains the manual fallback.

**E-SIGN (00a-fed §8).** 15 U.S.C. 7001(c)(1)(A)–(D): affirmative consent not withdrawn; prior clear and conspicuous statement of paper rights, scope, withdrawal procedure and paper-copy fees; hardware/software statement and consent "in a manner that reasonably demonstrates that the consumer can access information in the electronic form"; re-disclosure on material change. **TCPA (00a-fed §13.7).** FCC 24-17: AI-generated voices are "artificial or prerecorded voice"; "callers must obtain prior express consent from the called party" before such calls; 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10): revocation "by using any reasonable method", honored "within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days". **Fair lending.** Reg B §1002.4 (discrimination on a prohibited basis) and the July 21, 2026 final rule removing the effects test (00a-fed §3.8, §17.2) — the statutes, state laws and Fannie Mae's contractual fair-lending expectations are unchanged; LL-2026-04 requires fair-lending monitoring of AI pricing outputs (00a-fnma §1).

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. The blueprint lists §1026.19(e)/(f) as a source for 25.1's "TRID tolerances"; the tolerance engine (`tolerance_tests`, `tolerance_cures`, `runToleranceTest`, `REGZ_1026_19F2V_TOLERANCE_REFUND_60`) is 21.5's. 25.1 calls it and gates on it.
2. "1/8% / 1/4%": the 1/4 tolerance applies only to irregular transactions, and §1026.22(a)(3) expressly carves out "an irregular first period" — the fixture (odd days) is a regular transaction; the blueprint's pairing suggests otherwise. The blueprint also omits the (a)(4)/(a)(5) finance-charge-derived tolerances, the $100 finance-charge tolerance of §1026.38(o)(2), and the §1026.17(c)(4)(iii) option to disregard a first period up to 32 days longer than regular.
3. "Every test must pass before CD issuance and again before funding" is too coarse: §1026.36(g) (NMLSR ID) and licensing bind the LE; §1026.36(e) steering options bind the lock; APR accuracy must hold at consummation (it triggers a new waiting period, not merely a funding hold); Fannie Mae eligibility (HOEPA ineligible, QM/points-and-fees) gates delivery. Five gates, not two.
4. "Licensing" is the partner's, the branch's and the individual MLO's, per property state; an AI cannot be licensed, so the test attaches to `mlo_of_record` and the partner's NMLS records (31.1 owns the licensing program; 25.1 owns the per-loan check).
5. The blueprint omits E-SIGN consent validity, TCPA consent for outbound AI-voice contact, and fair-lending pricing-exception review, all of which are in the assignment and are implemented here.
6. Fixture arithmetic: the brief's P&I "$3,402.63" computes to **$3,402.62** ($560,000 × 0.0051041667 / (1 − 1.0051041667^−360) = $3,402.6190…, rounded half-up); every worked example in §25 uses $3,402.62.
7. Fannie Mae A3-2-01 and B2-1.5-02 are cited by the blueprint but were not verifiable today **[UNVERIFIED]**; NMLS B2B Access delivery mechanics **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**; state high-cost statutes other than NY and NC **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### Build spec

#### Inputs and triggers
- Checkpoint commands: `issueLE` (21.2), `lock` (21.4), `issueRevisedLE` (21.5), `issueCD` / `issueCorrectedCD` (25.2), `consummate` (26.2), `disburse` (26.3), `submitDelivery` (29.4). Each calls `assertGateOpen(application_id, <gate code>)`, which runs (or re-uses an unexpired) `compliance_test_runs` row for that checkpoint.
- Events that invalidate a run: `fee_items` change, `locks` change, `changed_circumstance.recorded`, `du.findings.received`, `application_borrowers`/`application_properties` change, `mi.certificate.issued`, `valuation.received`, `disclosure.cd.delivered`, `closing.scheduled` (date change moves the odd days), `fundings` disbursement-date change, `apor` table refresh, `rule_sets` version change, `license_checks` expiry.
- Reference data: FFIEC APOR tables (weekly; keyed by rate-set date and term), NMLS B2B Access records, `fee_benchmarks`, `holiday_calendars`.
- Borrower actions: E-SIGN consent grant/withdrawal (`consents`), TCPA consent grant/revocation (`consents`), requests for a human (`human_agent`).

#### State machine

Per `compliance_test_runs` row: `queued` → `running` → {`passed`, `passed_with_warnings`, `failed`, `errored`}; any terminal run becomes `superseded` when an invalidating event arrives. Gate state is derived: `open` iff the latest non-superseded run for the checkpoint has `overall_result ∈ {pass, pass_with_warnings}` and `completed_at` is within the freshness window (24 h for LE/lock; 4 h for CD/consummation/disbursement/delivery; recomputed at any invalidating event regardless). `blocked` otherwise. Transitions are performed by the `compliance-tester` agent; only `officer` can attach a `compliance_waivers` row (policy tests), which re-derives the gate.

**APR engine (`computeApr`).** Inputs: loan amount, note rate, amortization schedule from `loan_terms` (the legal obligation, §1026.17(c)(1)), MI premium stream if any, `finance_charge_classifications`, `term_start_date`, `first_payment_date`. Steps: (1) prepaid finance charges PFC = Σ `fee_items` classified `prepaid_finance_charge` (includes prepaid interest, creditor-retained fees, MERS fee, tax-service fee, upfront MI, escrow-waiver fee); (2) amount financed A = loan amount − PFC (comment 18(b): amount financed = principal − prepaid finance charges); (3) payment stream P_k = scheduled P&I (+ MI while payable); escrow deposits excluded ((c)(7)(v)); (4) counting per Appendix J (b)(3): full unit-periods counted back from `first_payment_date`; odd days measured forward from `term_start_date` to the start of the first full unit-period; f = odd_days / 30; (5) solve Σ_k P_k / ((1 + f·i)(1 + i)^k) = A for the monthly rate i by bisection to |Δ| < 10⁻¹² (200 iterations); APR = 12·i, stored to six decimals, disclosed rounded half-up to three decimals; (6) if `method = appendix_j_disregard_17c4` (permitted when the first period is ≤ 32 days longer than a regular period for terms ≥ 10 years), set f = 0 and k = 1…n unchanged — PFC and A do not change; (7) finance charge = Σ P_k + PFC − loan amount; total of payments (for the APR) = Σ P_k; TIP = (Σ interest in P_k + prepaid interest) / loan amount (comment 37(l)(3) includes prepaid interest [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — comment not re-read]). Default method: `appendix_j_exact` (decision 25.1-Q1).

**Worked example 1 — the refinance fixture, CD issued Mon Nov 2, 2026.** Loan $560,000.00; 6.125%; 360 months; monthly rate i = 0.06125/12 = 0.0051041667; P&I = 560,000 × i / (1 − (1+i)^−360) = $3,402.6190… → **$3,402.62**. Consummation Fri Nov 6, 2026; disbursement Thu Nov 12, 2026 (interest begins on disbursement — the later date under Appendix J (b)(3)(i), so `term_start_date = 2026-11-12`); first payment Fri Jan 1, 2027. Counting back one full month from Jan 1, 2027 lands on Dec 1, 2026; odd days measured forward from Nov 12 to Dec 1 = **19 days**; f = 19/30 = 0.6333333333. Per-diem interest at 365 days (26.3 owns the convention `365_rounded_per_diem` — rule 2 there: the per diem is rounded to the cent *before* multiplying so the CD's "per day × days" reproduces the total) = round(560,000 × 0.06125 / 365) = round($93.9726…) = **$93.97**; prepaid interest = 19 × 93.97 = **$1,785.43** (the unrounded product $1,785.48 is not used — 25.1-Q2's earlier "round once at the end" proposal is superseded by 26.3). Prepaid finance charges total **$3,849.95** = prepaid interest $1,785.43 + underwriting fee (creditor-retained) $1,950.00 + tax-service fee $84.00 + MERS registration fee $24.95 + flood life-of-loan monitoring $5.57. Amount financed A = 560,000.00 − 3,849.95 = **$556,150.05**. Payment stream: 360 × $3,402.62 = $1,224,943.20; finance charge = 1,224,943.20 + 3,849.95 − 560,000.00 = **$668,793.15**; total interest = 664,943.20 + 1,785.43 = 666,728.63; TIP = 666,728.63 / 560,000 = **119.059%**. Solving Σ 3,402.62 / ((1 + 0.6333·i)(1+i)^k) = 556,150.05 gives i = 0.005132802455, **APR = 6.159363% → disclosed 6.159%**. Under the §1026.17(c)(4)(iii) disregard option (first period 19 days longer than a month, ≤ 32): i = 0.005158290686, APR = 6.189949% → 6.190%. The two permitted results differ by 0.0306 percentage points (< 1/8), so a CD showing either is accurate against an actual computed by the other method. Check: with PFC = 0 and f = 0 the engine returns 6.1250%, the note rate. Tolerance test at consummation (Fri Nov 6): disclosed 6.159 vs actual 6.159 → |Δ| = 0 ≤ 0.125 → `APR_1026_22_ACCURACY = pass`, basis `a2`.

**Worked example 2 — the APR becomes inaccurate before consummation.** Same fixture, but on Wed Nov 4, 2026 the borrower elects to buy the rate down: 0.750 discount points ($4,200.00, creditor-retained → prepaid finance charge) for a 5.875% rate; P&I becomes **$3,312.61** (560,000 × 0.0048958333 / (1 − 1.0048958333^−360), rounded half-up), prepaid interest 19 × (560,000 × 0.05875/365 = $90.1370) = $1,712.60, PFC = 1,712.60 + 1,950.00 + 84.00 + 24.95 + 5.57 + 4,200.00 = $7,977.12, A = $552,022.88, finance charge = 360 × 3,312.61 + 7,977.12 − 560,000.00 = $640,516.72. The engine recomputes the exact-Appendix-J APR: i = 0.004982460, **APR = 5.978952% → 5.979%**. |5.979 − 6.159| = 0.180 > 0.125 → `APR_1026_22_ACCURACY = fail` against the delivered CD → 25.2 must issue a corrected CD and restart `REGZ_1026_19F1_CD_3SBD_GATE` (§1026.19(f)(2)(ii)(A)); e-signed Wed Nov 4 → consummation no earlier than Sat Nov 7; because the borrower prefers Monday, closing moves to Mon Nov 9. Note the direction: the APR *decreased*; a decrease beyond tolerance is still "inaccurate, as defined in §1026.22" (the TRID FAQ's "overstated APR" relief applies only when the overstated APR is accurate under §1026.22(a)(4), i.e., results from a finance charge that is itself accurate — here the finance charge changed by more than $100, so (a)(4) does not rescue it).

**Worked example 3 — finance-charge-derived tolerance (§1026.22(a)(4)–(5), §1026.38(o)(2)).** Fixture CD discloses finance charge $668,793.15 and APR 6.159%. At consummation the settlement agent adds a $60.00 courier fee the creditor required ((a)(2) → finance charge). Actual finance charge $668,853.15; disclosed is understated by $60.00 ≤ $100 → finance charge accurate under (o)(2)(i); the disclosed APR "results from the disclosed finance charge" → accurate under (a)(4) regardless of the (a)(2) arithmetic (the exact recomputation, ~6.160%, is within 1/8 anyway). Corrected CD at or before consummation under (f)(2)(i); no new waiting period (25.2).

**Fee reasonableness (`FEE_REASONABLENESS`).** For each fee classified under (c)(7): `reasonable = low_cents ≤ amount ≤ high_cents` in `fee_benchmarks` for the property state/county; outside the band → the exclusion is withdrawn (fee re-classified `finance_charge`, APR recomputed) and a warning is raised to `officer`. **LO compensation (`LOCOMP_1026_36D`).** The MLO of record's plan components are enumerated (`salary`, `flat_per_loan`, `fixed_pct_of_amount` with min/max, `deferred_plan`, `nondeferred_profit ≤ 10%`); any component keyed to rate, price, margin, points, fees, product, term, prepayment or lock period fails. **Steering (`STEERING_1026_36E_OPTIONS`).** Before `lock`, the `pricing` agent must have presented and logged, for the transaction type of interest, the three (e)(2) options from the partner's product set (the only creditor); with a single creditor, "a significant number of the creditors with which the originator regularly does business" is that creditor (decision 25.1-Q4). The record includes the options, the consumer's choice, and the reason if the chosen loan is not the lowest-rate option.

**Fair-lending pricing exceptions (`FAIR_LENDING_PRICING_EXCEPTION`).** `deviation_bps = quoted_or_locked_price − rate_sheet_price(product, attributes, as_of)`; any non-zero deviation must have a `pricing_exception_reviews` row with `discretionary = false` and a reason code; `discretionary = true` is not a legal value the agent can write. Aggregate monitoring (deviation by prohibited-basis proxy) is 31.2's; 25.1 supplies the per-loan rows.

**E-SIGN (`ESIGN_7001C_CONSENT`)** — `consents{kind=esign}` row with `granted_at < delivery time`, `withdrawn_at null`, `scope ⊇ disclosure class`, `hw_sw_statement_version = current`, `access_demonstrated = true` (the consumer opened a test document in the same format). **TCPA (`TCPA_CONSENT_OUTBOUND`)** — `consents{kind=tcpa_express | tcpa_express_written}` covering the number and purpose; no `revoked_at`; DNC scrub ≤ 31 days old (20.2); the AI discloses automation at the start of the call (baseline §8 item 10).

**Gate composition.** `gate_open(checkpoint) = ∀ t ∈ tests(checkpoint): t.result ∈ {pass, warn, not_applicable} ∨ (t.blocking = false) ∨ (t.waiver_id ≠ null ∧ definition.waivable)`; an `error` result (missing APOR, NMLS unreachable) blocks like a failure.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`compliance-tester` agent (tools: `runTestSuite(application_id, checkpoint)`, `computeApr`, `classifyFinanceCharges`, `runToleranceTest` (21.5), `determineQm`/`determineHpml`/`determineHoepa` (23.4), `evaluateStateHighCost`, `checkLicenses`, `checkEsignConsent`, `checkTcpaConsent`, `reviewPricingException`, `checkRespa8`, `assertGateOpen`, `openEscalation`, `writeDecision`). Behavior: on any checkpoint command or invalidating event it assembles the canonical input snapshot, hashes it, runs every applicable test deterministically (no model in the arithmetic path — the LLM is used only to draft the human-readable failure explanation and the cure plan), writes the run, derives the gate, and, on a blocking failure, drafts the cure (e.g., "issue corrected CD; new waiting period ends Sat Nov 7") and files an `escalation` to `officer` with `SM_O61_BLOCKING_FAILURE_REVIEW_1BD`. Decision record (`agent_decisions`): `{run_id, checkpoint, rule_set_versions, inputs_hash, tests[], overall_result, gate, cure_plan, rationale, model_version, prompt_version, confidence}`. Guardrails: the agent may never (i) waive a legal test, (ii) change a rule-set version, threshold or APOR value, (iii) reclassify a fee to make a test pass without a cited basis, (iv) mark `discretionary=false` on a deviation that has no reason code, (v) suppress a state test by editing `jurisdiction_rules`. Escalations: `officer` (partner) for blocking failures and policy waivers; `mlo_of_record` for an individual licensing failure; `licensed_specialist` where a state requires a licensed individual to perform processing/underwriting; `human_agent` on borrower request. Borrower-facing: the agent never contacts the borrower directly from this process; cures that need borrower action (new E-SIGN consent, waiver statement) go through the `disclosure` agent with the automation disclosure. Human path: the ops console shows the same run with "re-run", "attach waiver (policy tests only)" and "open cure task"; there is no "force gate open".

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Disbursement date moves after the CD (dry-funding state, recording delay): odd days change (e.g., Nov 12 → Nov 13 = 18 days), prepaid interest and APR change; re-run; if within 1/8 the CD is corrected at/before consummation or within 30 days post-consummation under (f)(2)(iii) (25.2).
- First period shorter than a month (disbursement Dec 20, first payment Feb 1 → counting back one full month from Feb 1 gives Jan 1; odd days Dec 20–Jan 1 = 12): still a regular transaction; f = 12/30.
- Leap year (2028 first payments): disregarded under §1026.17(c)(4)(iv); the engine uses 365-day per diem consistently (26.3 convention).
- MI stream that drops at 78% LTV (Ohio fixture): the APR payment stream reflects the scheduled MI termination date per the HPA computation (24.6); the engine consumes the schedule, never assumes level MI.
- Missing APOR for the rate-set week (FFIEC late): tests `error` → gates block; `officer` may not waive; wait for the table.
- NMLS feed outage: last good `license_checks` within 30 days stands; beyond that, manual lookup path.
- Fee classified (c)(7) but outside the benchmark band: reclassified as finance charge, APR recomputed — this can itself push the APR out of tolerance and trigger 25.2's new waiting period.
- Affiliate detected late (title agent is a partner affiliate): `RESPA_8_AFBA_DISCLOSURE` fails at CD; cure is not possible retroactively for the referral date — escalate to `officer`; the fee is re-evaluated under (a)(2) and the 10-percent tolerance category (affiliate → zero tolerance per 21.5).
- E-SIGN consent withdrawn after CD e-delivery: the delivered CD stands (consent was valid at delivery); further disclosures go to paper with the mailbox rule (25.2).
- Borrower revokes TCPA consent by "stop" text: `TCPA_CONSENT_OUTBOUND` fails immediately for that number; honored well within the 10-business-day maximum.
- Rule-set version change mid-file (new HOEPA thresholds on Jan 1, 2027): the checkpoint uses the version in force on the checkpoint date; a loan consummated Dec 30, 2026 and delivered Jan 8, 2027 keeps the 2026 thresholds for HOEPA (determination "at consummation") — the delivery re-run uses `as_of = consummation_at`.
- Manual-underwriting loans are out of scope (DU Approve/Eligible gate, 23.2-Q1); a `refer_with_caution` casefile never reaches `issueCD`.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Default APR method** (`appendix_j_exact` vs `appendix_j_disregard_17c4`). Default: exact Appendix J (odd-days fraction), because it is always permitted, is what most compliance vendors compute, and yields the lower APR (6.159 vs 6.190 on the fixture); the disregard option is retained as a secondary computation for tolerance comparisons against third-party CDs.
2. **Per-diem interest convention** (365 vs 360; where to round). Decided by 26.3 rule 2 (`sm.funding.v1`, `365_rounded_per_diem`): 365-day year, per diem rounded half-up to the cent *before* multiplying by the day count, so the CD's per-day amount × days equals the total (fixture $93.97 × 19 = $1,785.43). 25.1's earlier proposal ("exact per diem × days, round once") is withdrawn; the APR engine consumes whatever 26.3 stores, and 26.3 must confirm the convention matches the note and the settlement agent's practice.
3. **SM's origination-fulfillment compensation under §1026.36(d).** Is SM a "loan originator" organization for the partner's loans (its AI takes the application and presents terms on the partner's behalf)? Default: treat SM as a loan originator organization; SM's origination fee from the partner is a flat per-loan amount (or fixed percentage of the loan amount with a cap), never varying with rate/price/product; the 12.5 bps/yr platform fee is contractually labeled subservicing compensation and starts at boarding; legal opinion requested via 31.1.
4. **Steering safe harbor with a single creditor.** Default: present the three (e)(2) options from the partner's product menu and log them; since SM/MLO compensation does not vary by transaction, (e)(1) cannot be violated in practice, but the record is kept for exams.
5. **Fee-benchmark source and band width.** Default: SM-maintained bands from its own transaction history plus vendor fee schedules, refreshed quarterly; a fee outside ±25% of the median withdraws the (c)(7) exclusion pending `officer` review.
6. **State high-cost matrix scope.** Default: block only in states with a verified statute row; states listed as [UNVERIFIED] are researched by 31.1 before the platform originates there; NY and NC rows are live from day one.
7. **NMLS B2B Access vs manual lookups.** Default: subscribe to B2B Access; until the feed is live, a 30-day manual cadence with screenshot evidence.
