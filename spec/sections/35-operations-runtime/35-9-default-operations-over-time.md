### Blueprint row
Sections 11–15 exist as compute tools, case tables and timer registrations, and nothing on the hosted runtime moves a case forward over time (gap assessment, gap 13: "no process '13.'/'14.'/'15.' execution in src/runtime"). The borrower flows react to a handful of events only — src/runtime/borrower/flows/10-hardship.ts runs 11.3 `contact.log` / `qrpc.capture` / `lossmit.request.create`, reacts to 12.2's offers and 12.4's plans, and paints cards on `bankruptcy.petition.filed` and the 13.3 referral advice notice; src/runtime/borrower/flows/12-exits.ts is the payoff and transfer side. The sweep breaches every due timer and opens one escalation per breach — src/runtime/app.ts:270-291, `escalations.open({kind: sev<n>, ownerRole: escalateTo[0] ?? "ops_analyst", payload: {timer_code, breach: breachText}})` — but never executes the action the registry row names: a late firm acknowledgment (`FNMA_E3205_FIRM_ACK_2BD`, "sev 2 → firm call; 13.6 scorecard") opens a sev2 row and no firm is called; an uncertified sale at −7 days (`FNMA_E3302_SALE_CERT_WINDOW_7_15`, "postpone instruction auto-issued at −7") opens a sev1 row and no postponement is instructed. The console's views are `dashboard`, `loans`, `book`, `controls`, `ai`, `agents`, `directory`, `staff`, `access_review` (35.8's inventory of src/console/ui/index.html) — no case view. The counterparties' fakes exist without a caller: `FakePacer.docket` (src/infra/integrations/legal.ts:20-30) returns docket entries no pass reads, `FakeFnmaP360` (fnma.ts:165) and `FakeFnmaSmdu` (fnma.ts:133) are constructed per Runtime and never called by a pass (gap 12), and there is no fake law firm at all. This process is the case engine: one timeline per case folded from the sections' events, the next expected milestone per case with its clock, the attorney firm as an outbox counterparty with a FAKE that acknowledges and reports, docket entries classified and reacted to, every registry breach action executed by a cited executor (escalation remains the default action, never the only one), and the 15.x claim candidates opened, packaged and filed on their milestones — all run by 35.3's cycle as loan-scoped units and shown on 35.8's screens.

### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-15)
**Reg X §1024.41(f)(1)–(2), (g) (12 CFR; the 2014 Mortgage Servicing Rules as amended 2016, in force) — verified through 13.1/13.2's verification, not re-read today [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — cited via 13.1:REGX_1024_41F1_120_DAY_GATE and 13.2:REGX_1024_41G_DUAL_TRACK_GATE].** The first notice or filing may not be made before the loan is more than 120 days delinquent, nor while a complete loss-mitigation application received before the first notice is pending; after the first notice, a complete application received more than 37 days before a sale stops the servicer from moving for judgment or order of sale or conducting the sale until an (g)(1)–(3) exit. Operationally: every foreclosure act this process executes or proposes runs the owning section's gate evaluation (13.1 `foreclosure.gates.evaluate`) inside the command; a gate closed by a hold row (`foreclosure_holds`, db/migrations/0014_lossmit.sql) refuses the act with the 13.x refusal, and this process records the refusal on the timeline and never retries around it.

**Fannie Mae Servicing Guide E-1.2-02 (referral timing) and E-3.2-05 (firm acknowledgment within 2 business days) [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — cited via the 13.3 rows `FNMA_E1202_REFER_NO_EARLIER_121`, `FNMA_E1202_NONPR_REFER_BY_120`, `FNMA_E3205_FIRM_ACK_2BD`, `FNMA_E3205_MISSING_DOCS_3BD`].** Referral of a principal-residence loan no earlier than day 121 and, for a non-principal-residence loan, by day 120 after the earliest unpaid due date; the firm acknowledges the referral within two business days and a missing-document request is answered within three. The build: the referral package is 13.3's (`attorney_referrals.package_manifest`, `data_snapshot`); this process only proposes, dispatches and tracks it.

**Fannie Mae Servicing Guide A1-4.2-02 (02/13/2019) and F-2-03 (07/09/2025) — verified by 13.5 on its verification date; the formula and F-2-03's Example 1 re-run here.** "UPB × (Daily PTR/365) × Number of Days Delayed", days counted from the LPI due date through the sale date against the state's allowable time frame plus credited delays. The build tracks exposure daily (13.5 rule 5: today as the provisional sale date, plus the firm's forecast) and raises the 70% warning to a firm status demand (`FNMA_E3215_TIMEFRAME_WARNING_70`), which this process executes.

**FRBP 3002.1 and 11 U.S.C. §362 [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — cited via 14.1/14.2's rows `FRBP_3002C_POC_BAR_70`, `FRBP_4001A3_ORDER_STAY_14`, `BK_3002_1_PAYMENT_CHANGE_21`, `FRBP_3002_1F_STATUS_RESPONSE_28`].** The stay attaches on the petition; relief takes effect 14 days after the order absent waiver; a proof of claim is due 70 days after the petition; a 410C13 status motion or the trustee's final-cure notice must be answered within 28 days (+3 if mailed). The build: a docket entry is classified by 14.1's `docket.classify` (LLM, with `classifier_confidence`) and the deterministic reaction runs through 14.1/14.2's own tools; a low-confidence or counsel-class entry becomes an `attorney` work item, never a silent guess.

**Fannie Mae Servicing Guide E-5 / F-1-05 (expense reimbursement: final claim within 60 days of the milestone) and F-1-06 (MI claim: file within 30 days on MI-insured liquidations; MICP participants' documents 10 business days before the filing deadline) [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — cited via 15.2's `FNMA_E501_EXPENSE_FINAL_60`, `FNMA_F106_MI_EXPENSE_FINAL_30` and 15.3's `FNMA_F106_MICP_DOCS_10BD`, `MI_MP_CLAIM_FILE_60`].** The build opens a claim candidate on the milestone event, builds the package by its own 5-business-day clock (well inside the legal clocks, which the 15.x rows keep), and files through 15.2/15.3's own tools and the P360 / insurer ports.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**: (1) The blueprint imagines the breach action as the sweep's escalation; here every breach runs the `breach_action_registry` row for its code in the breach transaction, with `escalate` as the default action for a code without a row — the escalation the sweep opens today is kept as the `escalated_only` outcome, so no existing test changes. (2) The registry's breach column is prose parsed only for a severity and backticked roles (src/kernel/timers/registry.ts:61-67 `parseSeverity`); the executor never parses it — a row in `breach_action_registry` quotes the column verbatim (`cited_text`) and names the action a person registered against it, so the executable set is explicit and reviewable. (3) The sections' own case tables (`foreclosure_cases`, `foreclosure_milestones`, `attorney_referrals`, `sale_events`, `fc_timeframe_tracking` — db/migrations/0015_foreclosure.sql; `bankruptcy_cases`, `bankruptcy_docket_events` — 0016; `mi_claims`, `mi_claim_calculations`, `expense_claims`, `expense_claim_lines` — 0017) stay theirs and are written only by their tools; this process projects a timeline and expectations beside them and reads them through 35.1's typed record. (4) The attorney firm has no port in the tree; this process adds one (`law-firm`) with a FAKE, on the CLAUDE.md rule that every vendor is an in-repo fake named FAKE in every build stage. (5) 35.8 already owns the `foreclosure_case` and `bankruptcy_case` screens and consumes `case.milestone.due`; this process emits that event and does not add a screen — the claims work a person does (bulk upload, PSA response, IRT) stays a `fnma_portal_operator` escalation that 35.8's rule 8 routes to the 34.4 escalation view (open question 3).

#### State machine
Per case timeline (derived, never stored as a status of its own): the owning row's `status` is the state — `foreclosure_cases.status` (13.3: `prereferral → referred → acknowledged → pre_first_legal → first_legal → in_process → judgment → sale_scheduled → sale_held → post_sale → closed_*`, holds overlaid), `bankruptcy_cases.status` (14.1's chapter sub-states), `mi_claims.status` (15.3), `expense_claims.status` (15.2), `lossmit_applications.status` (12.1), `regx_ei_windows` (11.1). The timeline row records `status_before` / `status_after` per event; a transition this process did not expect (rule 3's expectation map has no edge for it) is logged `case.status.unexpected` and opens an `ops_analyst` work item — the section's row is right by definition, the expectation map is what gets fixed.

Per milestone expectation (`case_milestone_expectations.status`): `expected` —(the daily unit sees `due_on` < today, or `expected_on` < today for a `docket_order` basis)→ `due` (emits `case.milestone.due`, opens the 35.8 `case_milestone` work item, arms `SM_CASE_MILESTONE_OVERDUE_5BD`) —(the owning section's event for the code)→ `satisfied` (from `expected` or `due`; emits `case.milestone.satisfied`, closes the work item); `expected | due` —(`attorney` or `officer` on the screen with a reason)→ `waived`; any —(the case reaches a terminal or on-hold status that makes the milestone moot)→ `cancelled`. Terminal: `satisfied`, `waived`, `cancelled`.

Per breach action (`breach_actions.outcome`, written once): `executed` (the owning tool ran and its event is `command_event_id`), `deferred` (`needs_human` or a derivation gap — a work item exists), `escalated_only` (no registry row or `action_kind = escalate`: today's behaviour), `refused` (a 13.x/14.x gate refused the act — the refusal code is recorded and an `attorney` escalation opened, never a retry), `failed` (the tool threw — the breach pass still commits the breach and the escalation; the row says so; 35.11 counts it).

Per claim candidate (`claim_candidates.status`): `opened` —(`claims.package`)→ `package_building` → `package_built` (`case.claim.package_built`; the document stored through 35.2) —(15.2 `submitted` / 15.3 `filed` or `filed_by_fnma` observed)→ `filed` —(15.2 `paid` / 15.3 `settled`)→ `settled` → `closed`; `opened | package_building` —(15.x reports the milestone reversed, e.g. `foreclosure.sale.rescinded`)→ `withdrawn`. The `fnma_portal_operator` performs the P360 bulk upload, the PSA response and the IRT (15.2's actors); `officer` decides a write-off (15.2 rule 10) — both through their sections' commands.

Per docket reaction: `bankruptcy_docket_events.applied_at` is 14.1's own mark; a `docket_reactions` row exists for every docket event with `applied_at` set, and for every one deferred to a person.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **The timeline is a fold, not a status.** `case.timeline` returns the rows of `case_timelines` for a loan (and case) in `event_sequence` order with the owning rows' current status; the fold runs in the seam's post-commit hook for every event in the consumed list and in the daily unit for anything missed (`events_folded` counts the catch-up); a row per event per case, `event_id` unique, so folding twice writes nothing. The owning section's status is read after the event and stored as `status_after`; this process never writes a section's status.
2. **The daily unit progresses a loan in one transaction under 35.1's lease.** `case.progress{loan_id, as_of_date}` runs, in order: (a) fold; (b) 11.x — for an open `regx_ei_windows` row, the day's planned `contact_attempt_plans` step through 11.1's contact tool on the FAKE telephony/e-mail ports, and 12.1's diligence follow-up when the last contact is 7 days old (REGX_1024_41B1_DILIGENCE_FOLLOWUP_7's cadence — the row is 12.1's, this is its caller); (c) 12.x — 12.2's deemed-rejection check for an offer past its accept-by plus grace, and the workout plan's end-of-term check (12.4/12.5) through their own tools; (d) 13.x — when `prereferral.review.due` has fired and no `prereferral_reviews` row is complete, run 13.4's checklist tools (`dmdc.verify`, `bk.scrub`, `title.status.get`, `mi.status.get`, `insurance.status.get`, `disaster.lookup`) and record the review; when `foreclosure.referral.eligible` is on the timeline with no open proposal, propose the referral (rule 3); recompute the timeframe exposure (rule 5); mark expectations due (rule 4); (e) 14.x — react to every docket entry with `applied_at` null (rule 6); (f) 15.x — run 15.4's `recomputeAdvancePosition` for an S/S or S/A loan in delinquency (referenced from SM_DELADV_POSITION_DAILY, 15.4's row), and `claims.sweep` (rule 9). Each step's command runs as the owning agent (`default-collections`, `lossmit-underwriter`, `foreclosure-ops`, `bankruptcy-ops`, `claims-reo`) with a decision record naming `job_id` and `as_of_date`; a step that throws is recorded on the unit (35.3 `job_events.failed`) and the later steps still run; a gate refusal is a timeline row (`source: cycle`, `event_type: foreclosure.gate.refused`) and never a retry.
3. **Referral is proposed by the engine and decided by a person.** When 13.1's gates are all open (`foreclosure.gates.evaluate` run inside the command, never asserted), 13.4's review is complete with outcome `refer`, 13.6's retained-firm gate is open for the state and no hold row is open, `case.refer{op: propose}` writes `case.referral.proposed{case_id, firm_id, gates: [...], review_id, proposed_at}` and a 35.8 `work.action.propose` on `foreclosure_case.refer` for `officer`; `case.refer{op: decide, decision ∈ {approve, decline}, reason}` needs an `officer` (a FAKE officer under 35.7's handover when the plan says so) and writes `case.referral.decided`; on `approve` the same command runs 13.3's referral (`attorney.message.send{kind: refer}` with the package 13.3 assembles), which emits `foreclosure.referral.sent` and arms 13.3's `FNMA_E3205_FIRM_ACK_2BD`; `firm.dispatch{kind: referral_package}` writes the outbox row on the `law-firm` adapter and the `firm_dispatches` row. A proposal on a case whose gates close again before the decision is cancelled (`case.referral.decided{decision: cancelled, cause: gate_closed}`) — the person never approves a stale referral.
4. **Expectations come from the firm, the court or the default, in that order.** On `foreclosure.referral.sent` the engine writes the expectation `referral_ack` (`expected_on` = sent + 2 servicer business days, `basis: section_clock`, `basis_ref` = the FNMA_E3205_FIRM_ACK_2BD instance) and `first_legal` (basis `firm_forecast` when the acknowledgment carries a forecast date, else `jurisdiction_default` from `jurisdiction_rules.fc_milestone_defaults`); each milestone recorded (`foreclosure.milestone.recorded`, from the firm, the DRA import or the docket) satisfies its expectation and writes the next one; a docket order with a date (`docket_order`) replaces a default. `due_on` = `expected_on` + 3 calendar days for `firm_forecast` and `jurisdiction_default`, `expected_on` itself for `docket_order` and `section_clock`. On `due`, `case.milestone.due{case_id, milestone_code, due_on, basis}` opens the 35.8 item (`source_kind: case_milestone`, `screen_code: foreclosure_case` or `bankruptcy_case`, `required_role: attorney`) and arms `SM_CASE_MILESTONE_OVERDUE_5BD`.
5. **The exposure projection is 13.5's formula run daily.** For every open foreclosure case with `fc_timeframe_tracking.status ∈ {tracking, at_risk_70pct, over_allowable}`: `actual_days` = days from `lpi_due_date` through the provisional sale date (today) and, separately, through the firm's forecast sale date when one exists; `credited` = Σ `fc_delay_credits` (13.5 rule 2, caps applied there); `excess` = max(0, actual − allowable − credited); `exposure_cents` = round_half_up(UPB × PTR ÷ 365 × excess) with `Decimal` at scale 30 rounded once to the cent; written through 13.5's `comp_fee.exposure.updated` (its tool, its row); when `actual_days` first reaches 70% of (allowable + credited) the engine emits nothing of its own — 13.5's `foreclosure.timeframe.at_risk` fires and its `FNMA_E3215_TIMEFRAME_WARNING_70` breach action (rule 8) sends the status demand.

   **Worked example A (loan L-A, Florida judicial, allowable 720 days — the F-2-03 Example 1 jurisdiction).** UPB $187,450.00 at LPI; PTR 5.125%; LPI due date 2025-03-01; a Chapter 13 filed 2026-02-10 and dismissed 2026-05-05, code 67 reported and accepted → 84 actual days, cap 125 → 84 credited. Daily run of 2027-09-15 (provisional sale = today): actual = 928 days; excess = 928 − 720 − 84 = **124** days; per-diem = 187,450.00 × 0.05125 ÷ 365 = 26.32003…; exposure = 26.32003… × 124 = 3,263.6842… → **$3,263.68**. The firm's forecast sale 2027-11-02: actual = 976; excess = 172; exposure = **$4,527.05**. The 70% mark: 0.7 × (720 + 84) = 562.8 → day 563 = 2026-09-15, the day `at_risk_70pct` was set; allowable + credits exhausted on 2027-05-14, so `over_allowable` from 2027-05-15. Check against F-2-03 Example 1 with the same function: 100,000 × 0.0475 ÷ 365 × 266 = **$3,461.64** (13.5's figure, reproduced).
6. **Docket reactions are deterministic where the section is.** For each `bankruptcy_docket_events` row with `applied_at` null: run 14.1's `docket.classify` (LLM; `classifier_confidence` stored) unless `event_type` is already a structured class (EBN/PCL feeds are); then, when confidence ≥ 0.85 and the class is in the deterministic set — `petition`, `meeting_341_scheduled`, `bar_date_notice`, `plan_confirmed`, `plan_modified`, `relief_order_entered`, `dismissal`, `discharge`, `conversion`, `case_closed`, `reaffirmation_filed`, `trustee_notice_410c13_n`, `motion_410c13_m1`, `motion_410c13_m2`, `mfr_filed` — run the owning reaction through 14.1/14.2's tools (`bk.case.write` for the status and stay-gate transition, `timer.compute_frbp` and the section's clock for a bar date or a 28-day response, `bk.status_response.compute` for a 410C13 motion, `bk.statement_mode.set` for 14.3's mode) and write `docket_reactions{needs_human: false}` with the command event; otherwise (confidence < 0.85, a class outside the set — objections, adversary proceedings, cramdown, in rem, agreed orders, anything naming money) write `docket_reactions{needs_human: true}` and a 35.8 item on `bankruptcy_case.docket` for `attorney`, who decides through the screen; either path emits `case.docket.reacted{docket_event_id, reaction_kind, needs_human}` and 14.1 sets `applied_at`. The two money applications (`bk.ledger.apply_trustee`, `bk.ledger.apply_postpetition`) are never a reaction — they are 35.8's officer-approved acts (35.8-T14).
7. **Every breach runs its registered action in the breach transaction.** The sweep's breach pass (src/runtime/app.ts:270-291) calls `breach.execute{timer_id}` for each evaluated breach after opening the escalation it opens today: the executor reads `breach_action_registry` for the code; no row or `action_kind = escalate` → `breach_actions{outcome: escalated_only, escalation_id}`; `refuse_gate` → `escalated_only` with `action_kind` recorded (a gate refuses at command time — there is nothing to execute); `message_firm` / `instruct_firm` → 13.6 `attorney.message.send` / 13.2 `attorney.instruction.send` with the input the named deriver builds from the case row (never from the breach payload) → `executed{command_event_id}` and a `firm_dispatches` row; `open_work_item` → 35.8 `work.item.open{screen_code, required_role}` → `deferred{work_item_id}`; `set_flag` → the owning section's flag tool (13.5's `fc_timeframe_tracking.status`, 13.3's `sale_results`) → `executed`; `run_tool` → the named tool with its deriver → `executed`; `inform` → an `escalations` row per `inform_roles` entry beyond the first, `executed`; `cancel_clock` is refused at registration (34.4 rule 1 — no clock is edited from a breach). A 13.x/14.x gate refusal inside an action → `refused{refusal_code}` plus an `attorney` escalation; a throw → `failed{error_class}` and the breach pass still commits. `breach.recon{as_of_date}` (daily, before the receipt) counts the day's `timer.breached` events without a `breach_actions` row and the `failed` rows, writes `breach_action.recon.run_completed{as_of_date, breaches, executed, deferred, escalated_only, refused, failed, missing}` and opens a `compliance` escalation when `missing + failed > 0`. Seed rows (registered by the build with `cited_text` from spec/registry/timers.json, reviewable in `breach_action_registry`): `FNMA_E3205_FIRM_ACK_2BD` → `message_firm{kind: ack_demand}` + 13.6 scorecard event; `FNMA_E3302_SALE_CERT_WINDOW_7_15` → `instruct_firm{kind: postpone_sale}` ("postpone instruction auto-issued at −7 if not certified"); `FNMA_E3215_TIMEFRAME_WARNING_70` → `message_firm{kind: status_demand}` + `set_flag{fc_timeframe_tracking.status = at_risk_70pct}`; `REGX_1024_41G_INSTRUCT_COUNSEL_1BD` → `escalate` to `attorney` + `inform{officer}`; `FNMA_E3205_MISSING_DOCS_3BD` → `open_work_item{foreclosure_case, attorney}`; `SM_BK_DOCKET_SYNC_1BD` → `run_tool{docket.sync}`; `FNMA_E2_1_04_LAWFIRM_ACK_2BD` → `message_firm{kind: ack_demand}` (re-send, 14.1's "re-send; firm scorecard"); `SM_MICP_DOCS_TARGET_15` → `open_work_item{escalation view, fnma_portal_operator}`; `SM_CASE_MILESTONE_OVERDUE_5BD` (own) → `message_firm{kind: status_demand}`; every other 11.x–15.x code → `escalate` (today's behaviour, now recorded). A registry row is added or re-versioned only by `compliance` with `officer` confirmation (dual control through 35.7), and never with an action the `cited_text` does not name.
8. **A firm is an outbox counterparty.** `firm.dispatch` writes one `integration_messages` row on the `law-firm` adapter (idempotency key `firm:<case_id>:<kind>:<owning_event_id>`, 35.3's drain retry policy) and a `firm_dispatches` row; the FAKE firm (`FakeLawFirm`, src/infra/integrations/legal.ts) acknowledges a referral 1 servicer business day after delivery with a forecast first-legal date = referral + `jurisdiction_rules.fc_milestone_defaults.<state>.<method>.first_legal` days, answers a document request in 2, reports each milestone on its forecast date, schedules the sale per the state's default, posts a DRA snapshot daily and invoices at the 15.2 milestone percentages (30/65/75/85/95/100) against 13.6's fee schedule; `firm.inbound` turns each into the owning section's event with `source: firm` (`ack_source: fake` on the dispatch row). In production the same port is the vendor feed **[UNVERIFIED — protocol]**; nothing in the domain knows which.
9. **Claims open on the milestone, package on the clock, file through the sections.** `claims.sweep{loan_id}` runs 15.2's `sweepClaimCandidates` and 15.3's `routeMiClaim` against the loan's timeline: a liquidation or completion milestone (rule 2's list) with no candidate of that kind → `claim_candidates{opened}` and `case.claim.candidate_opened{candidate_id, claim_kind, milestone_kind, milestone_date, legal_due_on}`, where `legal_due_on` is read from the 15.x clock the milestone armed (`FNMA_E501_EXPENSE_FINAL_60`, `FNMA_F106_MI_EXPENSE_FINAL_30`, `MI_MP_CLAIM_FILE_60`, `FNMA_F106_MICP_DOCS_10BD`) — the earliest of them for the kind. `claims.package{candidate_id}` runs 15.2's `validateLine` per `advances` row then `assembleClaim` / `buildBulkPackage`, or 15.3's `computeShadowClaim` then `assembleMiPackage`, or 15.4's `buildIrrPackage`, stores the package through 35.2 `documents.store` (retention `fnma_reporting_7y`, an existing `retention_class` value) and emits `case.claim.package_built{candidate_id, claim_id, document_id, sha256, gross_cents | claim_amount_cents}`; filing is 15.2's submission (API channel by the agent when the fixture's `submission_channel = api`; a `fnma_portal_operator` escalation for `bulk_upload`) or 15.3's `fileDirectClaim` / the MICP upload; the candidate follows the claim's status from then on (state machine). Every figure is the 15.x tool's; this process asserts them, it does not compute them.

   **Worked example B (loan L-B, Texas non-judicial, sale held Tue 2027-07-06 — Fannie Mae acquired; MI 30% BPMI, `servicer_direct` filer).** The shadow claim (15.3 rule 4) at candidate open: UPB $163,920.44; note rate 5.750%; interest paid to 2026-10-01; anchor 2027-07-06 → 9 whole months (2026-10-01 → 2027-07-01) + 5 stub days. Claimable advances: taxes $2,914.60; hazard premium $1,062.00; inspections 6 × $30.00 = $180.00; preservation $385.00; attorney fee $2,300.00 + costs $565.00 = $2,865.00 (cap: UPB < $200,000 → min($6,000.00, 5% × 163,920.44 = $8,196.02) = $6,000.00 ✓) → advances = **$7,406.60**; MI premiums and technology fees excluded; credits none (escrow negative, no rents). `claim_amount` = 163,920.44 + 7,198.17 + 7,406.60 = **$178,525.21**; `benefit` = round_half_up(178,525.21 × 0.30) = 53,557.563 → **$53,557.56** (`mi_claim_calculations.benefit_cents = 5355756`). Clocks read for `legal_due_on`: `MI_MP_CLAIM_FILE_60` → 2027-09-04; `FNMA_F106_MI_DIRECT_FILE_30` → 2027-08-05 (the earlier governs the MI candidate); `SM_CLAIM_PACKAGE_5BD` from the candidate opened Wed 2027-07-07 → `package_due_on` 2027-07-14.

   **Worked example C (the same loan's 571 expense claim, 15.2 rule 9's method).** Lines: taxes $2,914.60; hazard premium $1,062.00 (paid 2027-03-15; term 2027-03-20 → 2028-03-20); MI premiums Nov 2026–Jul 2027 = 9 × $98.17 = **$883.53**; inspections 6 × $30.00 = $180.00; preservation $385.00; attorney fee $2,300.00; costs $565.00; technology + e-invoice $30.00. 10. **No money field, no legal act, from this process.** The engine proposes referral, expects milestones, reacts to structured docket classes, executes registered actions and builds packages; it never files, never instructs a sale or bid on its own initiative (only a registered breach action does, with its citation), never applies a payment, never waives, writes off or changes an amount — those are 35.8 acts with the sections' role gates (`officer` for money, `attorney` for filings), and a tool input that carries a money field this process did not derive is refused `NO_MONEY_FIELD`.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`foreclosure-ops` agent (tools: `case.timeline`, `case.progress`, `case.refer`, `case.milestone.expect`, `case.milestone.record`, `docket.sync`, `docket.react`, `firm.dispatch`, `firm.inbound`, `breach.execute`, `breach.recon`, `claims.sweep`, `claims.package`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end: unprompted, every sweep, the agent executes each breach's registered action inside the breach pass and, once a day as 35.3's units, folds the timelines, runs the 11.x contact cadence and 12.x follow-ups through their sections, runs the 13.4 review when due, proposes eligible referrals, dispatches what the sections send to the firm and folds what the firm answers, expects and satisfies milestones, syncs and reacts to the docket, recomputes timeframe exposure, opens claim candidates and builds their packages, reconciles the day's breaches and writes the receipt; the steps that belong to another section run as that section's agent (`default-collections`, `lossmit-underwriter`, `bankruptcy-ops`, `claims-reo`) with its decision record. It proposes for a human: every referral (`officer` decides), every docket entry below 0.85 confidence or outside the deterministic set and every milestone overdue (`attorney`), every claim needing a portal act (`fnma_portal_operator`), every write-off (`officer`), every unexpected transition (`ops_analyst`). It never files, instructs a sale or a bid except as a registered breach action with its citation, never applies money, never edits a clock, never registers a breach action itself, and never fabricates a milestone — a milestone comes from the firm, the DRA, the docket or a person. Decision record schema `{subject: {kind: case | breach | docket_event | candidate | run, id}, loan_id, action, inputs: {event_ids, through_sequence, registry_version, jurisdiction_rule_version, classifier_confidence?}, outcome, figures: {exposure_cents?, claim_amount_cents?, benefit_cents?, gross_cents?, net_cents?} as strings of cents, rule_set_version: default-ops.v1, model_version: deterministic | the 14.1 classifier's, prompt_version: 35.9-v1, confidence: 1 for deterministic steps, the classifier's otherwise, rationale}`. Guardrails: `NO_MONEY_FIELD`, `NO_LEGAL_ACT_UNREGISTERED`, `NO_CLOCK_EDIT` (34.4 rule 1), `GATES_RUN_NOT_ASSERTED`, `ACTION_MATCHES_CITED_TEXT`, `DOCKET_CONFIDENCE_0_85`, `ONE_ACTION_PER_BREACH`, `SECTION_STATUS_READ_ONLY`. Escalations: `officer` for referral decisions, write-offs and deferred breach actions; `attorney` for docket entries needing counsel, milestones overdue, gate refusals inside an action and every filing; `fnma_portal_operator` for P360 bulk uploads, PSA responses, IRT and MICP uploads; `compliance` for a reconciliation with missing or failed actions and for registry changes; `counsel` for a `[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]` state dual-tracking rule the gate evaluation flags; `ops_analyst` for unexpected transitions and manual `case.progress` runs.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Two docket entries on one loan in one day → one `SM_DOCKET_REACTION_1BD` instance (engine keying), two `docket_reactions` rows; the reconciliation counts entries with `applied_at` null older than 1 business day as missing, so the second entry cannot hide behind the first's satisfaction.
- The firm acknowledges through the DRA snapshot before its message arrives → `firm.dispatch.acknowledged{ack_source: dra}` on the DRA import; the later message is folded as a duplicate (`event_id` unique on the timeline) and changes nothing.
- A referral proposal approved after a new hold row opened (12.x application received meanwhile) → the referral command re-runs the gates and is refused; `case.referral.decided{decision: cancelled, cause: gate_closed}`; nothing is sent; the person sees the refusal on the screen.
- A registered `instruct_firm` action on a case in bankruptcy → the 13.x instruction is refused by `BK_362_STAY_GATE`; `breach_actions{outcome: refused, refusal_code: BK_362_STAY_GATE}`; `attorney` escalation; no retry on later sweeps (one action per breach instance).
- The `law-firm` adapter is down for three days → dispatches stay `queued` and are delivered on recovery in order; the sections' acknowledgment clocks breach on their own terms and their registered actions run (an ack demand queued behind the package — the reconciliation lists both as undelivered).
- A milestone recorded with `occurred_on` earlier than an already-satisfied predecessor (the firm corrects its report) → recorded as 13.3 records it; the timeline shows both; the expectation map re-derives the next expectation from the latest `occurred_on`; `case.status.unexpected` only when the section's status moved backwards.
- The 14.1 classifier is tripped by 34.4's kill switch → every docket entry is `needs_human` until reset; the reconciliation reports the deferred count; the 1-business-day clock still runs.
- A claim milestone reversed (`foreclosure.sale.rescinded`) after the package was built → `case.claim.withdrawn{cause: milestone_reversed}`; the 15.x claim row follows its own void path; a new candidate opens on the new milestone.
- A demo-clock advance of 30 days → one `default_case_daily` run per crossed day in order (35.3 T9), receipts per day, expectations marked due on the day they fell due, the FAKE firm's reports folded on their forecast dates — the same rows a 30-day hosted run writes.
- `loan_installments` absent for a loan (35.5 not yet run for it) → no delinquency window opens (11.x's counter selects nothing, gap 3); the daily unit skips the loan with `skipped: no_installments` and 35.11's report lists it — never a synthesized delinquency.
- A registry row whose `cited_text` no longer matches spec/registry/timers.json after a spec re-register → the executor treats the code as `escalate` (`escalated_only`) and opens a `compliance` escalation `REGISTRY_STALE{timer_code}`; the row is re-versioned by hand.

#### Audit and evidence
What an examiner is shown: the timeline of a case — every event with the status before and after, who or what caused it (section, firm, DRA, docket, court, screen, cycle, sweep) and the decision record behind each engine step; the expectations with their basis (the firm's forecast, the court's order, the default rule and its version, the section's clock) and how each was satisfied, waived or cancelled; every breach with its registry row (`cited_text` verbatim from the timer registry), the action taken, the owning tool's event and the escalation; the docket entries with the classifier's confidence and the reaction or the counsel's decision; the firm dispatches with outbox delivery and acknowledgment evidence; the claim candidates with their legal due dates read from the 15.x clocks, the package documents and hashes, and the 15.x figures they assert; the daily runs with their reports. ### Open questions / decisions
1. Should a referral be automated when every gate is open (13.3's class), with `officer` decision only on exceptions? **Default: proposed and decided by an `officer` everywhere until 35.7's handover plan lists `officer` as FAKE-filled for the environment; the decision clock is 2 business days inside 13.3's 5-calendar-day referral window.**
2. Should breach actions execute for every section's code from the first commit, or only the seeded 11.x–15.x rows? **Default: only the seeded rows execute; every other code is `escalated_only`, recorded — a later process (35.11) registers further rows section by section with `compliance` review.**
3. Should claims have their own 35.8 screen (`claims_case`)? **Default: no new screen in this process — portal acts are `fnma_portal_operator` escalations in the 34.4 view with the package document attached; a `claims_case` screen version is 35.8's to add when the queue shows the need.**
4. Should the FAKE firm's forecast dates come from the 13.5 exhibit's allowable-days shares or from fixed per-state constants? **Default: fixed constants in `jurisdiction_rules.fc_milestone_defaults` for the demo states, marked [UNVERIFIED], so a demo advance is reproducible; the exhibit stays 13.5's.**
5. Should the docket confidence threshold be 0.85 or the 14.1 build's own? **Default: 0.85 here, read from `ai_system_versions` for the 14.1 classifier when 18.1 records one, so the threshold is versioned with the model.**
