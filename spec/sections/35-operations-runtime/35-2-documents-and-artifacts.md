### Blueprint row
Every section that produces paper — LEs and CDs (21.2, 25.2), closing documents (26.1), notices and statements (7.x, 10.x, 16.1), the 1098 (7.1), the evidence pack (34.4) — assumes a document layer beneath it: bytes that outlive the process that made them, a hash that is the bytes, a place the borrower can open them and a signature that a court and Fannie Mae will accept. The tree at HEAD has none of it. The only blob store is in-memory and per process (`src/runtime/borrower/vendors/fake-blob-store.ts:1-12` — "a second Cloud Run instance does not see it"); rendered notices live in `Runtime.noticeMemory` (`src/runtime/app.ts:159`) beside the `notices` table; the renderer produces `html`, `text` and layout `blocks`, never a file (`src/notices/render.ts:17`); LEs, CDs and closing documents are text renders whose hash is hashed into JSONB (`src/domain/closing/ops-26-1.ts:383, 398` — `render_hash: sha256(text)`); 16.3 and 15.1 write `documents` rows whose `storage_uri` is `store://documents/<id>` with no store behind it (`src/app/tools/section16-3.ts:75`, `src/app/tools/section15-1.ts:62`); the evidence pack writes `evidence://packs/<id>` (`src/runtime/controls/evidence.ts:274`); the borrower's `/doc/[id]` page is an explicit stub (`apps/borrower/app/doc/[id]/page.tsx:8`); `package.json` carries no PDF library and `infra/terraform/` no `google_storage_bucket`. A person cannot view or sign an LE, a CD or a note, even with every vendor FAKE (gap "Documents, PDF, e-sign and RON artifact layer", blocking). This process is that layer: a `BlobStorePort` with a Postgres-backed FAKE in nonprod and Cloud Storage in production, an in-repo PDF writer, `documents` rows whose stored bytes are what integrity re-hashes, legal holds with their own log, an e-sign envelope model with signature evidence, the viewer that serves stored bytes, and print/mail manifests in and out. The section owners keep their content rules and checklists; 35.2 owns the bytes.

### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-15)
**Integrity, WORM and the daily check (19.1 Integrations and rule 7).** "WORM object store (S3 Object Lock compliance mode or equivalent; US regions only): every `documents`, recording and audit-log copy; integrity job verifies hashes daily; failure → sev-1 (19.2 CTL-SEC-22)"; "WORM outage: writes queue in the transactional store with `worm_pending`; disposal and productions requiring WORM verification pause; sev-1 after 4 hours"; disposal "requires an `officer` attestation before execution" and "never delete without verified WORM copy existence check passing first"; 19.1-T12: "Given the WORM integrity check fails for one object, then that object's disposal is blocked and a sev-1 incident (19.2) is opened." Legal hold: `holds.place` is an agent tool, `holds.release` is "**not** allowed — human only" (19.1 AI agent design).

**Electronic records and signatures (Selling Guide A2-4.1-03, 05/06/2026, as quoted by 19.1 and 26.2).** Electronic records are acceptable "provided they are capable of reproduction in paper format"; systems must "be sufficiently secure to preserve the integrity and authenticity of the records"; electronic signatures must comply with ESIGN/UETA with retained evidence of "the authenticated identity of the signer," attribution, the signer's express or implied agreement to use an electronic signature, the date, and the method (and, if applicable, the eSign vendor) of signing; a lender "may not require a borrower to use remote notarization and must have other notary options available for borrowers upon request" (the borrower's right to paper records is E-SIGN's, 15 U.S.C. 7001(c), via 7.4 — not Guide text); paper notes "may not be converted into eNotes". The eNote itself, its tamper seal, eVault and eRegistry are 26.2's and are not redefined here.

**E-SIGN consent before an electronic record (15 U.S.C. 7001(c), 7.4).** An electronic record satisfies a writing requirement only after the consumer has affirmatively consented in a manner that reasonably demonstrates access; 7.4's `ESIGN_7001C_CONSENT_GATE` refuses an electronic channel without `consents{kind=esign, scope∋class, status=active}`. An envelope is an electronic channel: no consent, no envelope.

**Layout facts (7.1 Data model; 10.4-T5).** 7.1: "Layout rules are evaluated against the rendered DOM's bounding boxes from headless Chromium (page, y-fraction, font size ratio)"; 10.4-T5: "the rendered PDF's body font size is ≥ 12 pt (template metadata check)". 32.1: "PDFs have text layers (baseline: Handlebars → HTML → PDF)"; 32.2: "Documents are served through signed, short-lived URLs bound to the session; downloads are logged to `ui_events{document_opened}`."

**Print/mail (7.1 Integrations).** "SFTP batch (JSON/XML per vendor spec, **[vendor-specific, UNVERIFIED]**) + API for singles; inbound manifest (proof of mailing), IMb scan events, return-mail images. Idempotency key = `notice_id`; daily reconciliation of file counts vs manifest; failure → in-house print fallback ≥ 2 days before `statement_due_by`."

**Form 1098 substitute statements (IRS Pub. 1179, Rev. 07-2026 — Rev. Proc. 2026-18, IRB 2026-30, §4.1.3 and §4.4.1; verified 2026-09-15).** The Copy B furnished to the payer may be a substitute if the tax year, form number and form name are displayed prominently together in one area; it must carry the same information as the official form (the name, address and TIN of the filer and of the recipient; the box numbers and captions titled substantially as on the official form), the direct-access telephone number of an individual who can answer questions about the statement, instructions to the recipient similar to the official form's, and the two §4.4.1 legends verbatim (rule 11); the payer's TIN may be truncated to its last four (§2.2.1), the filer's may not. The e-file transmittal (7.1: IRIS/FIRE) is 35.4's, not this process's.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**: (1) 7.1 measures layout from headless Chromium bounding boxes; the build measures them from the writer's own page projection (every block's page, y-fraction and point size are inputs to the writer, so they are facts, not measurements) — recorded as a deviation for counsel, not a change of rule. (2) 32.1's "Handlebars → HTML → PDF" baseline becomes block model → PDF directly; the text layer 32.1 requires is preserved. (3) 19.1's "S3 Object Lock compliance mode" becomes a Cloud Storage bucket with versioning, per-object temporary holds for legal holds and a retention policy that is *not locked* until counsel signs the retention matrix (a locked policy is irreversible) — until then `document_blobs` in Postgres is the durable copy and the FAKE store is the default. (4) The per-process `FakeBlobStore` is replaced by a Postgres-backed FAKE (`fake-blob://` resolves to `document_blobs`) so two runtime instances and the sweep job see the same bytes. (5) 26.2 relies on the RON platform's tamper-sealed audit trail; for the envelopes Supermortgage itself hosts (disclosure acknowledgments, consents, servicing agreements, payoff authorizations, the ancillary closing documents in an esign_only session) Supermortgage is the platform of record, so the signature evidence is its own append-only table and its own audit-trail PDF.

#### State machine
Per document (`documents.storage_status`): `staged` —(`documents.store` with the object store reachable, or the drain; sha256 of the object equals the row)→ `stored`; `staged` —(object store down)→ stays `staged` with `document_blobs.drain_attempts` counting; `stored` —(19.1 disposal run with `officer` attestation, no hold, `verify_status = verified`)→ `disposed`. Terminal: `disposed`. Overlay `legal_hold`: false —(`documents.hold{place}`, agent or human)→ true —(`documents.hold{release}`, human `compliance` or `counsel` only)→ false. Overlay `verify_status`: `unverified` → `verified` or `mismatch`/`missing` per run; a `mismatch` never returns to `verified` by a later run — only a `ciso`-closed escalation and a new row (the corrected object is a new document with `supersedes_document_id`).
Per envelope (`esign_envelopes.status`): `draft` —(`esign.envelope.send`; every signer has an active E-SIGN consent whose scope covers the kind)→ `sent` —(first `viewed`)→ `in_progress` —(every required field of every signer signed)→ `completed`; `sent`/`in_progress` —(a signer declines)→ `declined`; —(`esign.envelope.void` by the owning process, or `consent.esign.withdrawn` for a signer)→ `voided`; —(`SM_ESIGN_ENVELOPE_EXPIRY_30` breach)→ `expired`. Terminal: `completed`, `declined`, `voided`, `expired`. A `completed` envelope is never voided; a correction is a new envelope.
Per outbound mail manifest: `submitted` —(vendor acknowledgement)→ `acknowledged` —(inbound manifest ingested, counts reconciled)→ `mailed`; —(adapter down two sweeps or `mail.fallback`)→ `in_house`. Pieces carry their `notice_deliveries` status.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **The hash is the bytes.** `documents.sha256` is SHA-256 of the exact bytes stored; `byte_size` their length. A row is written in the same transaction as its bytes reach `document_blobs` (staged) or the object store (stored); a store that returns a different hash than the caller computed is refused and nothing is written. Integrity (`documents.verify`) re-reads the stored object and hashes it; it never re-renders, so a change of the writer, of `node:zlib` or of a template can never open a sev 1 on an old document.
2. **Deterministic per build.** The writer (`src/infra/files/pdf.ts`, in-repo on the `src/infra/files/xlsx.ts` precedent: PDF 1.4, the standard 14 fonts with embedded AFM width tables for line breaking, WinAnsi encoding, `node:zlib` FlateDecode content streams, a fixed `/ID` derived from the payload hash, `CreationDate` from the command clock) produces identical bytes for identical (template version, payload, locale, clock, build). A payload character outside WinAnsi is refused `GLYPH_UNSUPPORTED` naming the character and the block; nothing is silently substituted. Every page has a text layer: the content stream's text operators carry the rendered text in reading order so the borrower's viewer, screen readers and the presence rules read the same words the block model held.
3. **Layout facts come from the projection.** Each `{{#block}}` of the block model (`src/notices/render.ts:7-13`) is placed on a Letter page at its `page`, `y` fraction and `pt` size; the writer returns those placements with the bytes and the caller's checklist evaluates its layout rules against them (10.4's "≥ 12 pt", 7.1's page-1 rules). The measurement is the placement, and the placement is what was drawn.
4. **Staging first, always.** `documents.store` writes `document_blobs` and the `documents` row with `storage_uri = worm_pending:<document_id>` and `storage_status = staged` in the command's transaction, and emits `document.staged`; the drain (a 35.3 job, every sweep, through the outbox with the adapter `blob-store`) puts the object with `x-goog-if-generation-match: 0`, re-reads it, compares the hash and only then swaps `storage_uri` to `gs://<bucket>/<retention_class>/<document_id>#<generation>` (`fake-blob://<document_id>#1` in nonprod) — the one permitted swap — and emits `document.stored`. When the store is reachable at write time the drain runs inline in the same command, so the common case stores in one transaction and the clock never arms for long.
5. **Holds are two-sided and logged.** `documents.hold{place}` sets `legal_hold = true`, writes a `document_holds{placed}` row and places the object store's temporary hold in the same command (the FAKE records `FAKE:hold:<n>`); an agent may place a hold (19.1 `holds.place` allowed) and never release one (`HOLD_RELEASE_HUMAN_ONLY`); release needs a human `compliance` or `counsel` actor and a reason, and writes the `released` row before the flag changes. A held document is never disposed (`HOLD_ACTIVE`) and never leaves the `records_requests` scope it was held for.
6. **Disposal is 19.1's act.** `documents.dispose{disposal_run_id}` runs only inside a 19.1 disposal run that carries the `officer` attestation event; it refuses a held row, a `mismatch`/`missing` row (19.1-T12) and an unverified row (the WORM existence check), deletes the object (crypto-shred where the bucket is CMEK), nulls nothing on the `documents` row except the permitted status columns, and leaves the tombstone.
7. **The viewer serves stored bytes.** `documents.open` resolves the row, refuses a subject the session does not own (`NOT_YOUR_DOCUMENT` → 404, never 403 — the id must not leak existence), refuses an expired or mis-signed URL (401), streams the object (or the staged blob while `storage_status = staged`), sets `Content-Type` from the row, `Content-Disposition: inline`, `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, and writes `document_access_log` plus `ui_events{document_opened}` for a borrower session. The bytes served are hashed on the way out and the hash on the log row equals the row's `sha256` — a served mismatch is refused (`INTEGRITY_FAILED`) and raises the same sev 1 as the daily run.
8. **An envelope is an electronic channel.** `esign.envelope.send` requires, per signer, `consents{kind=esign, status=active}` whose scope covers the envelope kind (7.4's gate), records `sent` with the consent ids, and arms `SM_ESIGN_ENVELOPE_EXPIRY_30`. `esign.envelope.sign` requires an authenticated borrower session at L2 or above (32.14) or the FAKE signer in tests; each field signed writes one `field_signed` event with `auth_method`, `ip`, `user_agent`, `typed_name`, the document's hash at that moment and the chained `event_hash`; the last required field completes the envelope, renders the signed document (unsigned bytes + a signature page listing every field, signer, time and auth method + the per-field stamps), stores it as a new `documents` row with `supersedes_document_id = unsigned id`, writes `signed_document_id` once, renders the audit-trail PDF (every `esign_signature_events` row and the hash chain's head) as `evidence_document_id`, and emits `esign.envelope.completed`. No agent ever signs (`NO_AGENT_SIGNS`); a signature field's actor is always a human party.
9. **Mail is a manifest in and a manifest out.** `mail.batch` renders nothing — every piece's PDF already exists — and writes one outbound `mail_manifests` row with one `mail_manifest_pieces` row per notice delivery, `sheets = ceil(page_count ÷ 2)`, the notice's `separate_document` rule as `separate_envelope`, the address snapshot from the notice, the manifest file (JSON lines: piece id, notice id, document sha256, mail class, address) stored as a document and hashed, one `integration_messages` row per file to the `print-mail` adapter (idempotency key = the batch id; per piece `notice_id`+`attempt_no`, 7.1), and arms `SM_MAIL_MANIFEST_2BD`. `mail.manifest.ingest` parses the vendor's proof-of-mailing file, matches pieces by `notice_id`+`attempt_no`, writes inbound rows, sets `notice_deliveries.mailed_at`, `imb`, `mail_manifest_id` (`manifest_id` already holds the `notice_batches` id from submission), marks the manifest `reconciled` when counts match, emits `mail.manifest.ingested` and `mail.piece.mailed` per piece, and opens an `ops_analyst` escalation naming every piece the vendor did not mail.
10. **In-house fallback.** `mail.fallback` builds the same manifest with `vendor = in_house`, produces one merged PDF per mail class in page order with a cover sheet per piece, emits `mail.batch.submitted{vendor=in_house}` and escalates to `ops_analyst` to print and post; the inbound manifest is the analyst's `mail.manifest.ingest` with `mailed_on` per piece. 7.1's "≥ 2 days before `statement_due_by`" is the owning process's clock; this process only makes the fallback possible.
11. **The 1098 Copy B.** `documents.render{document_kind=irs_1098_copy_b}` takes 7.1's `tax_forms_1098` boxes (cents) and renders the substitute statement per Pub. 1179 (Rev. 07-2026) §4.1.3 and §4.4.1: the tax year, form number (1098) and form name displayed prominently together in one area; the official box numbers and captions; the recipient/lender block with Supermortgage's name, address, telephone number and TIN in full (the filer's TIN is never truncated); the payer block with the payer's TIN truncated to its last four (`XXX-XX-1234`); the direct-access telephone number of an individual who can answer questions about the statement; the two required legends verbatim — (1) "The information in boxes 1 through 9 and 11 is important tax information and is being furnished to the IRS. If you are required to file a return, a negligence penalty or other sanction may be imposed on you if the IRS determines that an underpayment of tax results because you overstated a deduction for the mortgage interest or for these points, reported in boxes 1 and 6; or because you did not report the refund of interest (box 4); or because you claimed a nondeductible item." and (2) "*Caution: The amount shown may not be fully deductible by you. Limits based on the loan amount and the cost and value of the secured property may apply. Also, you may only deduct interest to the extent it was incurred by you, actually paid by you, and not reimbursed by another person."; the Instructions for Payer/Borrower as on the official Copy B; money as `$1,234.56`. The bytes are the furnished copy: `tax_forms_1098.furnished_at` is written by 35.4's unit only after the `documents` row exists.
12. **No money moves here.** No ledger set is posted by any 35.2 tool; the figures on a rendered page are the owning process's, reproduced to the cent and never recomputed. The worked examples below prove the reproduction, not the arithmetic that produced them.

**Worked example A (statement PDF — 7.1's own figures).** Loan $400,000 5/6 SOFR ARM at 5.750%; 7.1's worked example gives P&I **$2,334.29**, escrow **$612.50**, total payment 2,334.29 + 612.50 = **$2,946.79**, late charge 5% of P&I = 2,334.29 × 0.05 = 116.7145 → **$116.71**, and, with the Sept 1, 2026 payment unreceived, the Oct 1 statement's amount due 2,946.79 + 2,946.79 + 116.71 = **$6,010.29**. `documents.render{template_code=NTC_REGZ_41_STMT_STD}` on that payload at clock 2026-09-17T05:00:00Z produces a PDF whose text layer contains each of those five strings exactly once in the amount-due box, whose `payload_hash` equals `payloadHash(payload)` (`src/notices/render.ts`), and whose `sha256` is stable across two renders.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`security-records` agent (tools: `documents.render`, `documents.store`, `documents.open`, `documents.verify`, `documents.hold`, `documents.dispose`, `esign.envelope.create`, `esign.envelope.send`, `esign.envelope.sign`, `esign.envelope.void`, `mail.batch`, `mail.manifest.ingest`, `mail.fallback`). End-to-end: the agent renders and stores unprompted whenever an owning process hands it a payload, drains staged blobs, runs and reports the daily integrity check, batches the day's mail and ingests the vendor's manifests, places a hold the moment a `records_requests` row or a litigation flag names a document, and voids envelopes whose signer withdrew consent; it proposes for a human: every hold release (`compliance`/`counsel`), every disposal (`officer` attestation, 19.1), the in-house fallback when the vendor is down (`ops_analyst` prints), and the ciso's response to a mismatch; it never signs, never releases a hold, never disposes on its own, never alters a stored byte and never changes a figure on a page. Decision record schema `{document_id | envelope_id | manifest_id, action, sha256, retention_class, hold: boolean, subject: {loan_id? application_id? party_id?}, rule_set_version: docs.v1, model_version: deterministic, prompt_version: 35.2-v1, confidence: 1, rationale}` — no rendered text, address or TIN in a decision (`NO_PII_IN_DECISION`). Guardrails: `BYTES_ARE_WRITE_ONCE`, `URI_SWAP_ONCE`, `VERIFY_STORED_BYTES_ONLY`, `HOLD_RELEASE_HUMAN_ONLY`, `DISPOSE_NEEDS_OFFICER_ATTESTATION`, `NO_ENVELOPE_WITHOUT_CONSENT`, `NO_AGENT_SIGNS`, `NO_MONEY_FIELD`, `NO_PII_IN_DECISION`. Escalations: `ciso` sev 1 on any integrity mismatch or a missed daily run; `ops_analyst` on a blob not stored within a day, a manifest missing after two business days, an expired envelope and the in-house fallback; `compliance` on a hold-release request or a disposal blocked by a hold; `officer` for the disposal attestation.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- The same payload rendered twice in one command (a retry) → one `documents` row: `documents.store` is idempotent on (`template_code`, `template_version`, `payload_hash`, `subject`) and returns the existing id; a different clock is a different document.
- A borrower upload larger than 25 MB or with a MIME type outside {application/pdf, image/jpeg, image/png, image/tiff} → refused `UNSUPPORTED_ARTIFACT` before any row is written (22.1's classifier runs after storage, never on rejected bytes).
- The object store returns a generation whose re-read hash differs from the staged hash → the drain does not swap, writes `last_drain_error = HASH_MISMATCH_ON_PUT`, retries with a new object name, and after 5 attempts raises `ops_analyst`; the staged copy remains the served copy.
- A hold placed while the document is `staged` → the flag and the log row are written now; the object hold is placed by the drain when the object exists.
- A hold release for a document under a second, open matter → refused `HOLD_STILL_REQUIRED` naming the other `matter_ref`; each matter's hold is its own placed/released pair.
- A signer's session drops mid-envelope → the fields already signed stand (their events are chained), the envelope stays `in_progress`, and a fresh L2 session resumes at the next field; nothing is re-signed.
- A signer declines → `declined`, an audit-trail PDF is still produced (the evidence of the decline), and the owning process is told by event to mail.
- A vendor manifest names a piece the outbound manifest does not → the inbound row is written with `notice_id = null` and an `ops_analyst` escalation; nothing on `notice_deliveries` changes.
- The integrity run finds an object the row says is `stored` but the store says is missing → finding `missing`, sev 1 to `ciso`, disposal blocked; if the staged blob still exists (nonprod, or a production row drained under 7 days ago) the run says so in the finding.
- More than 250,000 stored rows → the run switches to the rolling scope and says so in `document_integrity_runs.scope`; the clock is satisfied by either scope.
- A request to open a `disposed` document → 410 with the tombstone's hash and disposal run id in the staff view, and the borrower copy "This document is no longer available" (32.13's copy library key); logged.
- A production runtime with `INTEGRATIONS=fake` → refuses to start (35.12); the FAKE blob store, e-sign signer and print vendor exist only where `ENVIRONMENT != production`.

1. Lock the bucket retention policy at go-live or after the parallel run? **Default: after counsel signs the retention matrix and the parallel run ends; `document_blobs` plus an unlocked policy is the durable copy until then.**
2. In-house envelope or a vendor for non-closing signatures? **Default: in-house, with the evidence table as the record; the eNote and every notarial act stay with 26.2's platform.**
3. Keep the staged bytes in Postgres after a production drain? **Default: null `content` after the drain verifies the hash and 7 days pass (the integrity run's rolling window), keeping the row.**
4. PDF/A for anything? **Default: only 14.2's filing package, and only when 14.2 asks; everything else is PDF 1.4 with a text layer.**
5. Resolved (Pub. 1179 §4.1.3, §2.2.1): the Copy B must carry the filer's (Supermortgage's) TIN in full — only the payer's TIN may be truncated; no copy this process renders carries a full payer TIN, and the e-file record (35.4) carries it encrypted.
