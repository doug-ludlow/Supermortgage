### Blueprint row
The portal as built on 2026-09-15 refuses its own first account and cannot show who signed up. The bootstrap creates one row holding `admin` (src/runtime/staff/auth.ts:465), every borrower view is gated on the three ops roles (src/console/server.ts:349-350; src/runtime/staff/roles.ts:20), the picker's choice is sent with every request and a held-but-not-accepted role is refused instead of falling back (src/console/ui/index.html:69-70; roles.ts:51), the landing page is one of the refusing views (index.html:56) and no one can fix it from inside (`NO_SELF_ROLE_CHANGE`, auth.ts:392-394) — study-console.md §0.1, study-roles.md §1, study-jobs.md §1.1. The directory is a search box: three characters or more, fifty results or fewer, sorted by name, no list, no newest-first, no count, no stage (src/runtime/directory/routes.ts:5-9; search.ts:23-24, :166) — study-accounts.md §0.2 and §3, study-console.md §0.2. Four kinds of people are invisible in every role (leads that never authenticated, interview applications with no party, the boarded demo borrowers, un-named video visitors — study-accounts.md §3 "What the directory does not see"), the funnel exists as an API with no page (server.ts:370), the action log has no view and no column for the role that acted (server.ts:348; db/migrations/0127_staff.sql:77-91), the Evidence tab is shown to an admin the route refuses (index.html:422 against src/runtime/controls/routes.ts:48), and no test loads the screen (study-console.md §4). This process is the portal's information architecture — eight areas, each with a landing that works for every account that can see it, the role chosen per read and per act — and the accounts list: a browse over every person from every door, newest first, with counts, filters and paging, masked and logged exactly as the search is, beside a tab for the people who are not yet an account. Design of record: /root/gate-logs/ops-portal/proposal.md §3–§5, accepted 2026-09-15 with the owner's answers to its questions 1, 3 and 5 (Open questions below).

### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-15)
**Roles are the only authority; admin touches no borrower; the role on every row (34.1 rules 2–4).** A person acts under a role their account holds and the kernel knows; `admin` manages staff and roles and nothing that touches a borrower; on a `GET` the route falls back to the least-privileged accepted role the account holds in the order ops_analyst < officer < compliance < admin and names it (`acted_as`); on a `POST` the preferred role must be accepted or the answer is 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, act_as}` before any write; every `staff_actions` row carries the role that acted. **[VERIFIED — spec/sections/34-operator-portal/34-1-*.md:58-60 as amended 2026-09-15; the tree before the amendment: src/runtime/staff/roles.ts:47-58 (`chooseRole`; :51 the refusal of a held-but-not-accepted role; :55 the no-preference branch that takes the route's first role), src/console/server.ts:421 (the silent re-role on the bus route, retired for acts), db/migrations/0127_staff.sql:77-91 (`staff_actions` without a role column; migration 0139 adds it).]**

**One directory for every person, masked by role, never enumerable (34.2 rules 1–2 and 4).** A homegrown party and a partner-book party are the same row kind, `parties{borrower}`, with origin and doors; the default view is names and masked contact with last-four identifiers, unmasked only per person for 15 minutes with a reason by `compliance` or `officer`; the search is logged as a hash, refuses fewer than three characters and asks to narrow above 50 matches. The list shows what the search shows and nothing more, is open to the same three roles, and its paging is guarded by `LIST_VOLUME` where the search's cap was the control. **[VERIFIED — spec/sections/34-operator-portal/34-2-*.md:52-53, :55 as amended 2026-09-15; src/runtime/directory/search.ts:23-24, :126-138 (`originsOf`), :163-166; src/runtime/directory/account.ts:120-130; src/runtime/directory/mask.ts:19, :79-84.]**

**The doors and the funnel (32.14, 32.16, 32.17).** The account comes first: a web visitor either creates an account — e-mail and password, or Google — which makes a borrower party at once, or leaves nothing behind; the anonymous minute survives as an API (`POST /v1/borrower/lead`) whose lead has `party_id = null` until it authenticates; the funnel is a read model of events per window (`lead.created` … `lock.executed`), not of people. The video door opens a provisional party (`Borrower (video)`) that becomes a person only when `video.identify` writes the name and the e-mail; a visitor who leaves before naming themselves leaves nothing that is shown to anyone else. **[VERIFIED — spec/sections/32-borrower-experience/32-16-*.md:78, :95; 32-14-*.md:210 (T18), :223; 32-17-*.md:74-75 (rules 11–12), :111 (the edge case); src/runtime/borrower/lead-routes.ts:172-173; src/infra/db/borrower-parties.ts:62-72; src/console/pg-store.ts:121-124; db/migrations/0132_journey_progress.sql:55-68 (the four stage events this process reads: `application.trid_received`, `decision.issued`, `clear_to_close.issued`, `loan.funded`).]**

**Partner-book parties (33.1).** Every homeowner on a monitored book is a real borrower party from the first load with the partner's contact on file, one per homeowner, linked to a homegrown party only on e-mail and name; the loan is `monitored`, never serviced, and the partner stays servicer of record; the invitation and its one reminder are 33.1's. The boarded transfer book (1.x) creates no party at all, so its borrowers are "no account yet" until a party exists. **[VERIFIED — spec/sections/33-partner-book/33-1-*.md:14, :17, :46, :62; src/runtime/partner-book.ts:292; src/runtime/transfers.ts:143-144.]**

**The boards and screens the areas frame (35.3, 35.7, 35.8).** The Cycles and Jobs boards are 35.3's; a session's actable set is `roles ∪ reviewer_roles` minus `admin` and `chooseRole` is reused over it (35.7); the work screens' codes, the `ROLE_REQUIRED` before deriving, "never admin", the money-field two-person rule and the widened queue with its role filter kept are 35.8's. This process frames them — Operations, Oversight and the application and loan pages are the shells they fill — and asserts nothing about their availability: whether a screen is available is 35.8's own T-ids' business. **[VERIFIED as authored, not built — spec/sections/35-operations-runtime/35-3-*.md:135; 35-7-*.md:75-76; 35-8-*.md:46, :86-87, :90; docs/audit/COVERAGE.md:257, :261-262 read 35.3 0/40, 35.7 2/44, 35.8 0/63 at the time of writing.]**

**Least privilege and the monitoring of authorized users (16 CFR §314.4(c)(1), (c)(8)).** Customer information is accessed only by authorized users under controls that limit access to what each needs, and the activity of authorized users is monitored and logged so that unauthorized use of customer information by such users is detected. Here: the list is open to the three ops roles only and admin sees no borrower-derived figure anywhere; every page of a list is one logged look with the filters hashed; `LIST_VOLUME` is the detector for a screen-scrape. **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — (c)(1) as 34.1 verified it against the Safeguards Rule as amended 2021; (c)(8)'s operative text was not re-read against the primary source in this session, and the threshold of 20 pages or 1,000 rows a day is this process's own policy, not the rule's.]**

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**: (1) A list was never a unit of 34.2 (its rule 4 shapes the search the other way); this process adds the browse as its own counted units and amends 34.2's prose rather than rewriting a done process's T-ids. (2) The bus route's silent re-role (server.ts:421) already does for acts what 34.1 rule 3 now forbids; it is retired for acts, and reads fall back in one declared order instead of the route's own order (roles.ts:55). (3) The "not yet an account" rows are not persons under 34.2 rule 1; they are listed on a second tab and never counted as people. (4) The proposal's default for a lead on that tab was ids only; the owner decided on 2026-09-15 that a lead shows its name if given and its e-mail and phone in full, to the three ops roles, every look logged and never exported (T4); the accounts list itself stays masked as 34.2 rule 2 says. (5) The proposal's T-ids for the Loans list assumed servicing figures on every row; every figure is read from the ledger and the owning sections' rows and asserted by them, never computed here — this process has no worked figure.

### Build spec
#### Inputs and triggers
- `GET /ops/api/portal/home?kind=` (every held role; `?kind=` keeps the queue's filter) → `portal.home` (the clocks due within 24 hours and breached, the open escalations counted by role, the work waiting for every role the session holds as one list with the role on each row, the dead-letter and held-notice counts, the roles a FAKE reviewer fills in this environment; for an `admin`-only session the staff, access-review and controls tiles and one line naming the roles the other areas need).
- `GET /ops/api/directory/list?tab=accounts|not_yet_an_account&origin=&door=&status=&verified=&stage=&partner=&created_since=&created_until=&last_seen_since=&has_application=&has_loan=&signed_in_today=&sort=newest|last_seen|name&cursor=` (`ops_analyst`, `officer`, `compliance`) → `directory.list` (one page of 50 in creation order, newest first by default, with a `next` cursor; the second tab's rows and the "video visits, not identified" count).
- `GET /ops/api/directory/counts?<the same filters>` (the three ops roles) → `directory.counts` (people in total; new today, this week, this month; by origin; by status; by stage as a funnel of people; by created day — no cap, no row, exempt from `LIST_VOLUME`); the existing event funnel (`GET /api/funnel?from&to`, 32.14) is shown beside it, labelled as events.
- `GET /ops/api/pipeline?stage=&partner=&mlo=&channel=&since=&until=&cursor=` (the three ops roles) → `pipeline.list` (every application by stage from lead to funded, counts per stage, a page of 50 with a `next` cursor).
- `GET /ops/api/loans?status=&delinquency=&case=&partner=&q=&cursor=` (the three ops roles; this process's route replaces the legacy `GET /api/loans?q=` search, which `q` keeps) → `loans.list` (the serviced book with the servicing columns, monitored partner-book loans as `monitored` rows, a page of 50 with a `next` cursor).
- `GET /ops/api/notices/{id}/text` (the three ops roles) → `notice.read` (the rendered text as delivered, contact fields masked by role, unmasked under an open 34.2 unmask of the same session).
- The header search box on every page → 34.2's `directory.search`, unchanged; the account page's actions → 33.1 `account.invite{kind: reminder}`, 34.2 `directory.unmask` and `directory.export`; the officer waiver action on the loan and application pages → the owning section's officer tool through the existing bus route `POST /ops/api/tools/{process}/{name}` with a `rationale` field (rule 11).
- Events (global; ids and hashes only, never a filter text, a name or a figure): `directory.listed{staff_user_id, filters_hash, results}`, `directory.counted{staff_user_id, filters_hash}`, `pipeline.listed{staff_user_id, filters_hash, results}`, `loans.listed{staff_user_id, filters_hash, results}`, `notice.read{staff_user_id, notice_id}`, `portal.home.viewed{staff_user_id, roles}`.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Eight areas, one order, a landing that never refuses.** Home (every account); People & accounts, Pipeline, Loans and Partner book (`ops_analyst`, `officer`, `compliance`); Operations (the ops roles; `admin` reads); Oversight (all four read the controls; the action log is `admin` and `compliance`, with a filtered self-view for analysts and officers; `compliance` and `admin` act); Staff (`admin`; the access review also `compliance`). The sidebar shows only the areas the session's roles open — nothing on the screen is a link to a refusal; an area reached by address answers 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, held, act_as}` naming the role. One search box in the header on every page, routed to `directory.search`, whose hits open the account, application or loan page. Compliance Sentinel has one home, Oversight; Home's first section is titled "My queue" and keeps the `?kind=` filter.
2. **Home is My queue for every role held (Q5, decided).** `portal.home` returns the clocks due within 24 hours and the breached clocks with code, subject and breach role; the open escalations counted by role; the work waiting for every role the session holds as one merged list where each row names the role it needs (escalations, held notices, dead letters, portal tasks, breached timers), with "Act as" as a filter and never as a gate; the dead-letter and held-notice counts; the roles a FAKE reviewer fills in this environment; and, once 35.3 exists, today's cycle receipts and dead units. For an account holding `admin` only it returns the staff, access-review and controls tiles and one line naming the roles the other areas need — and no count or field derived from a borrower party, application or loan (34.1 rule 2 is not blurred for a tile).
3. **The role on a read, the role on an act (34.1 rule 3).** Every request carries the picker's role as a preference. On a `GET`, a preferred role the account holds but the route does not accept falls back to the least-privileged role the route accepts that the account holds, in the order ops_analyst < officer < compliance < admin, and the answer names it (`acted_as`); on a `POST`, the preferred role must be accepted or the answer is 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, act_as: [the accepted roles the account holds]}` before any write, and the screen offers "act as <role>" for each role in `act_as` and re-sends with that role named; a role the account does not hold is refused on either method. No page ever substitutes a greater authority on an act; no page renders a refusal string as its body.
4. **The accounts list is a browse over borrower parties.** One row per `parties{borrower}` from every door, newest first by creation. Columns: person (name; e-mail masked as `m…@example.com`; phone masked as `···0101`); origin (`front door` · `video door` · `partner book: <partner>`, both when a partner-book party also used the front door); doors used (`password` · `Google` · `e-mail code` · `SMS code` · `passkey` · `video`); status (`invited` — provisioned, never signed in · `active`); verified (the e-mail verified instant, or unverified); stage (`lead` · `account` · `application` · `decision` · `closing` · `funded` · `boarded` · `monitored`, with `activated` on a monitored row); subjects (application id and loan number, last four only); partner (the lender or servicer of record: the application's, else the loan's, else the configured entry partner); created, first seen, last seen; open work (the count of open escalations and cases on the person's subjects — from increment 2). A video-door party appears only once `video.identify` has run; there is no `provisional` status in the list (rule 9). The stage is derived from the subject's events exactly as the `journey_progress` view reads them: the furthest of `application.trid_received`, `decision.issued`, `clear_to_close.issued` and `loan.funded` on the record; an application with none of them is `application`; a served loan is `boarded`; a partner-book loan is `monitored`.
5. **Filters, sort, paging.** Filters: origin; door; status; verified or not; stage; partner; created since and until; last seen since; has an application; has a loan; signed in today. Sort: newest, last seen, name. A page is a cursor of 50 in creation order with the id as the tiebreak; `next` returns the remainder and no party appears twice across pages; more than 50 matches is a page, never `NARROW_QUERY` (that refusal is the search's).
6. **Counts are a separate read.** Header tiles: people in total; new today, this week, this month; by origin; by status; by stage as a funnel of people. Beside it, 32.14's event funnel for a chosen window (lead created → disclosure → goal → range → authenticated → application started → soft pull → terms → application received → TRID → DU → intent → lock), labelled as events, because the two answer different questions: how many people are where, versus how many times each step happened. The counts have no cap and return no row; the ops roles see them; `admin` sees none of them.
7. **Privacy and masking.** The list shows what the search shows and nothing more: names and masked contact; last-four identifiers; never an SSN, a date of birth, a credential hash, a token or a vendor payload (34.2-T6's contract holds for every response of this process). Unmasking stays per person, per case, for 15 minutes with a reason, on the account page — never on a list. Every page of a list is one logged look (`LOG_EVERY_LOOK`) with the filters hashed: a name filter is personal data, so it is never logged in clear. There is no export of any list; the one-person export exists and is `compliance`-only. The 20-unmasks-a-day escalation to `compliance` stays, and the person it names cannot complete it (`SELF_SUBJECT`, 34.4). Nothing here changes who may see what: the lists are open to the same three roles as the search, and `admin` sees no borrower-derived figure anywhere.
8. **`LIST_VOLUME` watches the paging.** 34.2 rule 4's fifty-result cap was the anti-enumeration control; a list that pages without limit is a screen-scrape export unless someone is watching. More than 20 pages or more than 1,000 rows read by one person in one day across `directory.list`, `pipeline.list` and `loans.list` opens one `compliance` escalation with the person id, the page and row counts and the day, on the pattern of the 20-unmasks rule; a second day's paging opens a second; `directory.counts` never counts toward it; the person named cannot complete it (`SELF_SUBJECT`). The lists keep answering — the control is the escalation, not a lock-out — and the access review lists the quarter's escalations (34.1 rule 6).
9. **People who are not yet an account.** Three kinds of people-shaped rows exist without a party and one kind must stay hidden; they live on a second tab, "Not yet an account", labelled `no account yet` as the directory already labels party-less loans, open to the three ops roles, and are not counted as people. (a) A *lead* from the API-only anonymous minute (SMS, voice, a direct call to `POST /v1/borrower/lead`) that never authenticated — shown as `lead` with its name if one was given, its e-mail and phone in full, its channel, its created date and its assurance level (Q3, decided 2026-09-15: full contact, not ids only); every look is logged (`LOG_EVERY_LOOK`, `directory.listed` with no contact in the row) and no route exports the tab or a lead. (b) An *interview* application (21.1) with no linked party — shown as `interview` with the application id's last four, the MLO of record and the date, ids only. (c) A *boarded borrower* from a transfer tape with no party — shown as `boarded loan` with the loan number's last four and the partner, ids only. (d) A *video visitor who has not given a name* — open session or closed — appears only as the count "video visits, not identified" and never as a row or a subject (32.17: nothing of it is shown to anyone else); a placeholder application on such a party is no subject of any row. When a lead verifies and becomes a party it leaves the tab and appears in the accounts list with origin `front door` and its lead date as first contact. This is a spec decision — 34.2 rule 1 defines a person as a borrower party — made explicit here rather than by stretching rule 1.
10. **Pipeline.** Every application by stage from lead to funded, the stage derived from the furthest of the four `journey_progress` events on its record (rule 4); each row carries the borrower's masked name, the partner and its MLO of record, the channel, the application date and the next clock due; counts per stage sum to the number of applications; filters by stage, partner, MLO, channel and date. A row opens the application page, which frames the record, the clocks, the escalations and the last ten acts and lists the stage's screen codes (`le_review`, `cd_review`, `conditions`, `closing_schedule`, `funding_release`) with the role each needs, from a static list shaped like 35.8's `work_screen_versions` registry; whether a screen is available is 35.8's business, never this process's.
11. **Loans and the officer waiver action.** The serviced book with status, UPB, next due date, delinquency bucket, escrow flag, open cases (loss mitigation, foreclosure, bankruptcy), an open payoff request, transfer status and the partner — every figure read from the ledger and the owning sections' rows and never computed by the list; filters by status, delinquency bucket and case kind. Monitored partner-book loans appear as `monitored` rows whose link opens the Partner book loan page (34.3 is the record view; Loans is the index). A loan row links to its borrower's account page and the account page's subject links back to the loan page. The loan and application pages carry one officer waiver action that dispatches the owning section's own officer tool (2.7's courtesy waiver, 16.1's payoff override, 3.5's refund) through the existing bus route with a `rationale` field: taken by a session not acting as `officer` it is refused 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role: officer, act_as}` (with `act_as: [officer]` when the account holds it, `[]` when it does not) and nothing is written; taken as `officer` it runs on the bus with `actor = {human, <staff_user_id>, officer}`, the ledger set it writes balances with its own `rule_ref`, the decision record carries the rationale and the `staff_actions` row records `officer`. Money needs a second pair of eyes everywhere else: whatever an analyst proposes waits for a different officer (35.8 rule 5), and above the sections' dual-control thresholds two distinct people are required regardless of how many roles one person holds.
12. **Operations, Oversight, Staff.** Operations: the outbox with dead letters and the bounded requeue (34.4); two boards from day one, "Portal tasks" and "Held notices", listing every open row regardless of the session's roles with `needs: <role>` on each (every `human_portal_tasks` row today is `fnma_portal_operator`'s); their completion is 34.4's `controls.portal_task.complete` and `controls.notice.supersede` (increment 2), never a direct write; the Cycles, Jobs, Close and Closing boards fill the area as 35.3, 35.4 and 35.6 land. Oversight: Compliance Sentinel; Controls (clocks, escalations with disposition, outbox, AI systems and the two-person kill switch); the access review; the action-log page over `GET /ops/api/staff/actions` filtered by person, subject and date, showing `staff_actions.role`, with a self-view of their own rows for analysts and officers; the Evidence view shown only to `compliance` (34.4 rule 5); one completion path for an escalation (the queue row calls the same 34.4 command the Controls tab does); the Agents page dispatches `controls.ai.kill` request and confirm only. Staff: 34.1's invite, roles, disable, re-invite and passkeys; the access review moves here (`compliance` still sees it) with its `self_attested` rows, the sole-officer count and the "open until a second person" items; role bundles (rule 14). Retirements are 34.4's units in increment 2 (`POST /api/agents/ai-off`, the direct `UPDATE`s of the queue's completions).
13. **The notice look.** `notice.read` returns the rendered text of a notice the person was sent, as delivered, with contact fields masked by role and unmasked only under an open 34.2 unmask of the same session; it is logged (`notice.read{staff_user_id, notice_id}`), appears in the account's activity stream as `staff_look`, and changes no notice row. It is opened from the account page and from the loan page — an operator on a "did you get our letter" call sees what was sent; until now the rendered text reached a person only inside a compliance evidence pack.
14. **Role bundles.** A bundle is a named preset on the staff page that expands to roles when a person is invited or changed: `analyst` = [ops_analyst], `servicing officer` = [ops_analyst, officer], `compliance officer` = [compliance], `administrator` = [admin]. A bundle never combines `compliance` with `officer` or `ops_analyst` (`BUNDLE_SEPARATION`): first-line work, the approval authority and the second-line control are three seats. A bundle is sugar — the same `staff.invite` or `staff.role.set` runs with the expanded roles, the bundle name goes in the rationale, `NO_SELF_ROLE_CHANGE` applies to one's own row, a role outside `STAFF_ROLES ∪ HUMAN_ROLES` is `UNKNOWN_ROLE`, and 35.7's disjointness checks validate the expansion when they land.
15. **Nothing computed, nothing written, nothing sent.** Every tool of this process is a read; the only rows it writes are 34.1's log row, its own look events and the `LIST_VOLUME` escalation. It edits no clock, sends no notice, posts no ledger line and holds no state of its own; the partner is named as lender or servicer of record on every subject; the FAKE marker appears wherever a FAKE acted or will act.

No money figure is computed here.

- None. No vendor; no adapter. The Playwright harness of section 32 drives T8 (Chromium from `/opt/pw-browsers` through `playwright-core`), a test fixture, not an integration.

#### Outputs and artifacts
- Rows: `staff_actions` (34.1; with the role that acted), `loan_events` (the look events), `escalations` (`LIST_VOLUME`).
- Events: `directory.listed`, `directory.counted`, `pipeline.listed`, `loans.listed`, `notice.read`, `portal.home.viewed`.

Presentation follows the borrower app's tokens (one monospace family, no panels, no radius, no shadow, colour only for state and the FAKE marker; dark by default) — a rule of the product, not a unit of this spec.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`security-records` agent (tools: `portal.home`, `directory.list`, `directory.counts`, `pipeline.list`, `loans.list`, `notice.read`). End-to-end: every tool is a read that projects the record for the caller's role, writes one `staff_actions` row and its own look event, and answers; `portal.home` merges the held roles' work; `directory.list` and `directory.counts` browse and count borrower parties and the "not yet an account" rows; `pipeline.list` and `loans.list` index applications and loans from the sections' own rows; `notice.read` returns a delivered notice's text masked by role. The agent proposes nothing and never acts; the acts a page offers (the re-send, the unmask, the export, the officer waiver, an escalation's completion) are the owning sections' tools with the session's actor. Decision record schema: none of its own — no tool writes a decision; the log row and the look event are the record, and `LIST_VOLUME`'s escalation carries `{staff_user_id, day, pages, rows, tools}`. Guardrails: `ROLE_MASK` (the projection is masked for the role before it leaves the tool), `LOG_EVERY_LOOK` (one log row and one event per page, filters hashed), `LIST_VOLUME` (rule 8), `NO_FULL_SSN`, `NO_SECRETS` (hashes, tokens, vendor payloads never projected), `READ_ONLY` (the tools write only their own log rows and events), `NO_MONEY_FIELD` (no figure is computed or written; the waiver is the owning section's officer tool), `NO_PII_IN_LOG` (ids, hashes and the role only). Escalations: `compliance` on `LIST_VOLUME`; nothing else automatic.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- A partner-book party that also used the front door → one row, origin names both doors (34.2's edge case); counted once.
- A video-door party that identified → a row with origin `video door` and the doors it used since; one that did not → the "video visits, not identified" count only, whether its session is open or closed; its placeholder application is no subject of any row.
- A lead that verifies and becomes a party → leaves the "Not yet an account" tab; its lead date is the person's first contact in the accounts list.
- A household sharing an e-mail → two rows, never merged; the household's loans appear under each.
- More than 50 matches → a page and a `next` cursor, never `NARROW_QUERY`; the cursor is creation-ordered with the id as the tiebreak, so a party created while paging cannot appear twice.
- A person pages past 20 pages or 1,000 rows in a day → one `compliance` escalation (`LIST_VOLUME`) naming them; the lists keep answering; a second day opens a second; the counts read never counts.
- An unmask expires while a notice is open → the next `notice.read` is masked again; the page asks for the reason again (34.2).
- A session holding `admin` only → Home answers the admin tiles with no borrower-derived figure; a list reached by address answers 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role: ops_analyst, held: [admin], act_as: []}` and the sidebar never showed the link.
- A `GET` asked for with an `x-staff-role` the account holds but the route does not accept → the least-privileged accepted held role acts and the response names it; a `POST` so asked → 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, act_as}` and the "act as" offer, nothing written, never a silent substitution.
- The officer waiver action taken by a session not acting as `officer` → refused with `act_as: [officer]` when the account holds it, `[]` when it does not; no ledger, event or decision row is written.
- A loan with no party (the boarded demo book) → a Loans row with no borrower link and a `boarded loan` row on the "Not yet an account" tab.
- A monitored loan → a `monitored` row whose link opens the Partner book loan page; no servicing figure is computed for it.
- A notice held or superseded → `notice.read` shows the text as it stands with its status; it never releases or re-sends it.
- The browser lock is held by a section 32 shell suite → T8 waits on `acquireBrowserLock` and runs after it, in the same CI shard.
- A page whose route table has no route for an area a role opens → a contract test fails (T8: every link shown answers 200 for that session); nothing ships with a link to a refusal.

### Open questions / decisions
1. How should the platform owner's account get its roles on the running nonprod? **Decided 2026-09-15: the nonprod-only bootstrap roles setting `STAFF_BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_ROLES` (34.1 operational prerequisites, 34.1-T9), including the one-row upgrade of the bootstrap e-mail's row through `staff.role.set` when its roles are a strict subset of the setting; a real second admin before production; never a second account for the same person and never a `DELETE` on the staff tables.**
2. When 35.7 lands, which reviewer roles should the owner hold, and which of the paired ones? **Decided 2026-09-15: for testing, every role the platform can grant to one person. Increment 1 grants the four staff roles (question 1). When 35.7 lands, the same nonprod-only `STAFF_BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_ROLES` setting carries reviewer roles into `staff_users.reviewer_roles` under 35.7's own constraints, so the owner's row holds every kernel role its disjointness rules allow beside `officer`: all twenty-two except `qc_officer` and `funding_approver` (paired against `officer`), one of the `mlo_of_record` / `underwriting_reviewer` pair (default `underwriting_reviewer`; `mlo_of_record` only after 24.1's `AIR_RESTRICTED_PARTY` T-id lands), and never the external-party roles (`appraiser`, `property_data_collector`, `notary`, `closing_attorney`, `settlement_agent`), which are never staff. The roles the owner cannot hold stay with the FAKE reviewer on nonprod, and the confirmations a second admin or compliance member must give (the kill switch, an independence-role grant, the FAKE handover, a break-glass review) are given on nonprod by a FAKE confirmer — a `FAKE:admin` / `FAKE:compliance` actor on src/runtime/reviewers.ts's pattern, labelled FAKE, idempotent, off under `FAKE_REVIEWERS=off` and in production — which 35.7 adds as a counted unit. In production every grant is a named second admin's `staff.role.set`, and a second account for the same person stays refused (34.1 rule 2).**
3. On the "Not yet an account" tab, should a lead show masked contact or ids only? **Decided 2026-09-15: full contact, not ids only. A lead row shows its name if given, its e-mail and phone in full, its channel, created date and assurance level, to the three ops roles, every look logged (`LOG_EVERY_LOOK`), never exported (T4's wording); interview and boarded-loan rows keep ids only; un-named video visits stay a count; the accounts list itself stays masked as 34.2 rule 2 says.**
4. While the owner is the only officer, should a money act taken as officer execute directly (35.8 as authored) or should every money act be a two-person proposal even for an officer? **Decided 2026-09-15: keep the default — 35.8's officer path with the compensating controls named there — `sole_officer_money_acts` counted daily by `work.log.recon`, on `ops.report`, in the evidence pack and in the access review's rationale — and the sunset that makes every money act a proposal for everyone the day a second officer enrols. T16 exercises the officer path as 35.8 authors it.**
5. Should Home merge every held role's work into one list, or keep one role at a time? **Decided 2026-09-15: one merged list with the role on each row and "Act as" as a filter (T1's wording); a multi-role person sees all their work at once, and each row still says which authority it needs; 35.8 keeps the queue's role filter on the server either way.**
