# Section 8 — Credit Reporting

## Overview

Section 8 is where the platform's loan state leaves the building and lands on a consumer's credit file. Every month the platform must furnish the status of every subserviced loan to the four "major credit repositories" Fannie Mae names — Equifax, Experian, Innovis and TransUnion (Servicing Guide C-4.1-01, 07/15/2020; F-1-23, 06/09/2021) — in the CDIA Metro 2® format, and it must do so under the Fair Credit Reporting Act's furnisher duties (15 U.S.C. 1681s-2), Regulation V Subpart E (12 CFR 1022.40–.43 and Appendix E), the Reg X notice-of-error credit-reporting bar (§1024.35(i)) and RESPA §6(e)(3), the SCRA's bar on adverse reports for exercising SCRA rights (50 U.S.C. 3919), the Bankruptcy Code's automatic stay and discharge, the FCRA identity-theft block (15 U.S.C. 1681c-2), Reg F's "contact before you furnish" rule for FDCPA-covered loans (12 CFR 1006.30(a)), and state furnisher statutes (e.g., Cal. Civ. Code §1785.25). Three processes make up the section: **8.1 Furnish tradeline (Metro 2)** — the monthly snapshot, the Metro 2 generator with its full field mapping and status/special-comment decision matrix, per-bureau delivery adapters, acknowledgment/reject processing and out-of-cycle corrections; **8.2 Dispute handling (e-OSCAR / ACDV)** — bureau-forwarded ACDVs through the "Services by e-OSCAR" API, Reg V direct disputes, the AI investigation engine, response codes, AUD corrections and reinsertion notices; and **8.3 Suspend credit reporting**, which the blueprint tied to the retired Fannie Mae topic C-4.1-02 and which is rebuilt here as the **credit-reporting overlay and suppression engine** (bankruptcy Consumer Information Indicators, SCRA, disaster comment codes, dispute flags, the 60-day NoE bar, deceased borrowers, identity-theft blocks, FDCPA pre-furnishing contact, transfer holds) with the Reg V Appendix E accuracy-and-integrity program implemented as code and monitored continuously.

**Architecture fit.** A new agent package, `agents/credit-reporting` (defined in 8.1's data model; tool allowlist modeled on the `case` agent's read tools plus the Metro 2/e-OSCAR commands), owns all three processes. It reads the ledger (`ledger_entries`, `payments`, `payment_allocations`), `loan_terms`, `cases` (lossmit, forbearance, deferral, modification, shortsale, dil, foreclosure, bankruptcy, scra, noe, sii, fraud), `parties`, `borrowers`, `contacts`, `notices`, and `credit_reporting_suppressions` (created in 4.1, extended here), and writes `metro2_snapshots`, `metro2_files`, `metro2_ack_items`, `credit_disputes`, `credit_reporting_corrections` and `agent_decisions`. Core services used: the **Timer Engine** (month-end snapshot, transmission targets, ACDV/direct-dispute deadlines, the 5-business-day frivolous-dispute notice, the 30-day negative-information notice, 90-day DOFD furnishing, FDCPA 14-day wait, the 60-day NoE gate), the **Notice Registry** (negative-information notice B-1/B-2, direct-dispute results and frivolous notices, address-for-disputes designation), the **integration framework** (`integrations/metro2` with one delivery adapter per bureau — Experian STS, TransUnion EDT, Equifax SFTP, Innovis [UNVERIFIED transport] — and `integrations/eoscar` REST), `agent_runs`/`agent_decisions` for LL-2026-04 governance, and `documents` with retention class `fcra_furnishing_5y` (new; the longer of 5 years after the last furnishing/dispute and Fannie Mae's life-of-loan + 4 years applies).

**Partner / Supermortgage boundary.** Fannie Mae's furnishing duty sits with the servicer; the partner (master servicer of record) remains liable for it (A2-1-07). Supermortgage is the **furnisher** in the FCRA sense — it holds the records, the direct-dispute address, the bureau subscriber codes and the e-OSCAR registration — and furnishes under its own name and codes (open decision 8.1-Q1; the alternative "private-label" furnishing under the partner's subscriber codes requires the partner's e-OSCAR affiliation and the e-OSCAR third-party-service-provider addendum). The partner receives the monthly furnishing certification, dispute statistics and the Appendix E program evidence; regulator/exam responses about credit reporting route to `officer`. No Fannie Mae system is involved in this section, so there are no `human_portal_task` steps for Fannie Mae; bureau and e-OSCAR portals are vendor portals used by employees only for credentialing and exception review, never by scripted automation.

**Dependencies.** 1.1/1.6 (boarding must capture the transferor's last Metro 2 record — Date Opened, Date of First Delinquency, 24-month Payment History Profile, Consumer Information Indicators — so the first cycle neither re-ages nor duplicates; Appendix E III(g)); 1.3/7.1 (the RESPA hello notice and the first periodic statement carry the FCRA §1681s-2(a)(7) negative-information notice B-1 and the direct-dispute address); 2.x cashiering (payment events, effective dates, protected-payment posting); 4.1/4.2 (NoE 60-day bar; RFI does not bar reporting), 4.4 (successors in interest are not furnished unless they assumed the note), 4.5 (complaints alleging inaccurate reporting open 8.2 cases); 11.4 (FDCPA status drives the Reg F §1006.30(a) gate); 12.4–12.9 (forbearance, repayment plan, deferral, Flex Mod trial and permanent modification, short sale, Mortgage Release statuses); 13.3/13.8/13.9 (foreclosure milestones; SCRA); 14.1–14.4 (bankruptcy petition/plan/discharge feed the CII overlay; 14.4 is redefined as a consumer of 8.3); 16.1–16.3 (payoff → status 13); 17.2/17.3 (final cycle: status 05 and hand-off of DOFD/PHP); 18.1/18.2 (QC sampling, MORA); 19.1 (retention); 19.4 (fair-lending monitoring of dispute outcomes).

**Changes in flight (verified 2026-09-09).** Servicing Guide edition Aug. 12, 2026: **C-4.1-02 "Suspending Credit Bureau Reporting" no longer exists** — SVC-2020-03 (July 15, 2020) "removed the requirement to suspend credit reporting for borrowers impacted by a disaster event" and left C-4.1-01 plus procedure F-1-23 (then numbered F-1-24) as the only credit-reporting topics; the blueprint's rows 8.3 and 14.4 are re-based accordingly. The CARES Act §4021 accommodation-reporting rule (15 U.S.C. 1681s-2(a)(1)(F)) is tied to a "covered period" that ended 120 days after the COVID-19 national emergency was terminated by Pub. L. 118-3 (April 10, 2023) — i.e., **August 8, 2023**; it no longer governs new accommodations, so forbearance/deferral reporting falls back to the CDIA Credit Reporting Resource Guide (CRRG) and Reg V's accuracy definition. The CFPB withdrew Circular 2022-07 (reasonable investigation of disputes) and Bulletins 2013-09 and 2014-01 on May 12, 2025; the statutory and Reg V duties they described are unchanged. The 2026 CRRG (CDIA) changed K3 segment field descriptions, Account Type codes within Portfolio Type, bankruptcy FAQs, an ECOA "X" clarification and a new "Maturity Date" term — the CRRG is access-controlled, so every Metro 2 field rule below that could not be checked against it is marked **[UNVERIFIED — CRRG 2026]** and must be validated by the licensed CRRG holder during build. e-OSCAR's current release is eo2026.07 (Sept. 3, 2026); its 2026 releases changed API authentication (auth/v2), ACDV response endpoints, payload encryption and notification endpoints. LL-2026-04 (AI/ML governance, effective Aug. 6, 2026) applies to the dispute-investigation agent.
