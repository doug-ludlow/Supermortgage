### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-11)

**A. Fannie Mae seller/servicer approval and ongoing eligibility (partner side).**

*Selling Guide A1-1-01, Application and Approval of Seller/Servicer (fetched 2026-09-11; Guide edition Sept 2, 2026)* — VERIFIED (text is short): "Sellers/servicers must be approved to do business with Fannie Mae. Fannie Mae determines a seller/servicer's qualifications by reviewing the seller/servicer's financial condition, organization, staffing, selling and servicing experience, and other relevant factors." The topic defers "required documentation, minimum business requirements, application review fee, and any special approvals needed" to Fannie Mae's website. The Path-to-Approval package (Form 582, Form 1002, audited financials, Risk and Servicer Self-Assessments, business plan, 24 months' demonstrated experience, fidelity/E&O, licensing and good standing in all jurisdictions, decision target "90 days or less" from a complete package) is recorded in the subservicing research (project doc 02-research §4.3, verified 2026-09-09) — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — website content not re-read today]**.

- Net worth: "$2.5 million, plus an amount equal to or greater than the sum of" "0.25% of the portion … serviced for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, plus 0.35% of the portion … serviced for Ginnie Mae, plus 0.25% of Other Servicing UPB".

**C. Third-party originations, outsourcing, and flow-downs.** *Selling Guide A3-3-01, Outsourcing of Mortgage Processing and Third-Party Originations (last updated 02/07/2024)* — VERIFIED: "A third-party origination is any loan that is completely or partially originated, processed, underwritten, packaged, funded, or closed by an entity other than the seller (or its parent company, controlled affiliate or subsidiary) that sells the loan to Fannie Mae. This includes mortgage brokers or correspondents, which are known as third-party originators." Exception: "If a seller enters into a contract with a third party known for the quality of its underwriting (such as a mortgage insurer) to help the seller in underwriting its mortgage originations, the loans will not be considered third-party originations." "Sellers remain fully responsible to Fannie Mae for functions that are outsourced to third parties." Required written policies for TPO management include "A method for verifying, and periodically reverifying, a third-party originator's compliance with applicable laws, licensing, and qualifications for originating loans", written QC plans, senior-management reporting, performance standards, suspension/termination, annual financial-statement reviews and quarterly performance reviews; "Certain Special Feature Codes are required at delivery for third-party originations" (SFC 211 correspondent / 212 broker — 00a-fnma §5).

*Analysis — is SM a TPO?* Read literally, a loan "partially … processed, underwritten, packaged … or closed by an entity other than the seller" is a third-party origination, and SM is neither the partner's parent, controlled affiliate nor subsidiary; the only textual exception is the "third party known for the quality of its underwriting (such as a mortgage insurer)" contract-underwriting carve-out. But the definitional sentence equates TPOs with "mortgage brokers or correspondents" — entities that *deal with the borrower and originate in their own channel* — and the SFC list has codes only for those two. SM does not solicit in its own name, is not the creditor, does not table-fund, and does not take applications as a broker; it is a fulfillment/technology provider acting in the partner's name under the partner's licenses and credentials. **Recommended position:** (i) treat SM as an *outsourced-processing/technology provider* to which the "Sellers remain fully responsible" sentence and the A2-1-01 (Servicing Guide) technology-provider regime apply, and (ii) nevertheless run the full A3-3-01 TPO-management program over SM (approval file, licensing re-verification, QC plan, quarterly performance, annual financials) because the definition is broad enough to reach it; (iii) deliver **no** SFC 211/212 (SM is neither broker nor correspondent) but confirm in writing with the partner's Fannie Mae account team before the first delivery — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — Fannie Mae has not been asked]** (open question 2). The contract-underwriting carve-out is not relied on (SM is not "known for the quality of its underwriting" in the MI sense).

**E. SAFE Act / Reg G–H — who must be a licensed MLO.** *12 CFR 1008.103 (eCFR up to date as of 9/08/2026)* — VERIFIED: (a) a state must prohibit "an individual from engaging in the business of a loan originator … unless the individual first: (1) Registers … through … NMLSR, and (2) Obtains and maintains a valid loan originator license"; (b) "engages in the business" = in a commercial context, habitually or repeatedly, takes an application **and** offers or negotiates terms for compensation or gain; (c)(1) "An individual 'takes a residential mortgage loan application' if the individual receives a residential mortgage loan application for the purpose of facilitating a decision whether to extend an offer of residential mortgage loan terms to a borrower or prospective borrower"; (c)(2) offers or negotiates = "Presents for consideration by a borrower or prospective borrower particular residential mortgage loan terms", "Communicates directly or indirectly with a borrower, or prospective borrower for the purpose of reaching a mutual understanding about prospective residential mortgage loan terms", or "Recommends, refers, or steers a borrower or prospective borrower to a particular lender or set of residential mortgage loan terms, in accordance with a duty to or incentive from any person other than the borrower", plus "Receives or expects to receive payment of money or anything of value in connection with the activities described". **(d): "a state must prohibit an individual who is an independent contractor from engaging in residential mortgage loan origination activities as a loan processor or underwriter with respect to any dwelling or residential real estate in the state, unless the individual first:" "Registers as a loan originator through and obtains a unique identifier from the NMLSR, and" "Obtains and maintains a valid loan originator license from the state."** (e) exemptions: (e)(3) "An individual who performs only clerical or support duties and: Who does so at the direction of and subject to the supervision and instruction of an individual who:" "Is licensed and registered in accordance with paragraph (a) of this section, or" "Is not required to be licensed in accordance with paragraph (e)(5)"; (e)(4) "An individual who performs only purely administrative or clerical tasks on behalf of a loan originator"; also real-estate brokerage (unless compensated by a lender/broker/originator), timeshare, registered depository employees, government/HFA employees, bona fide nonprofit employees.

*12 CFR 1008.23 definitions (eCFR 9/08/2026)* — VERIFIED: "Clerical or support duties" "Include: (i) The receipt, collection, distribution, and analysis of information common for the processing or underwriting" of a residential mortgage loan; and (ii) communicating with a consumer to obtain the information necessary for processing or underwriting "to the extent that such communication does not include" "offering or negotiating loan rates or terms, or counseling consumers about residential mortgage loan rates or terms"; clerical or support duties do **not** include "Taking a residential mortgage loan application; or Offering or negotiating terms of a residential mortgage loan". **"Independent contractor" means "an individual who performs his or her duties other than at the direction of and subject to the supervision and instruction of" "an individual who is licensed and registered in accordance with § 1008.103(a), or is not required to be licensed".** "Loan processor or underwriter" means "an individual who, with respect to the origination of a residential mortgage loan, performs clerical or support duties" "at the direction of and subject to the supervision and instruction of: (1) A state-licensed loan originator; or (2) A registered loan originator". "Origination of a residential mortgage loan" = "all residential mortgage loan-related activities from the taking of a residential mortgage loan application through completion of all required loan closing documents and funding". Appendix A (00a-fed §12): underwriting decisions ("Making an underwriting decision about whether the borrower or prospective borrower qualifies") and "Arranging the loan closing" are *not* offering/negotiating.

*12 CFR 1008.107 (eCFR 8/27/2026)* — VERIFIED: renewal requires meeting the §1008.105 issuance standards and "annual continuing education requirements, which must include at least 8 hours of education approved by the NMLSR" — "at least 3 hours of Federal law and regulations", "2 hours of ethics (including instruction on fraud, consumer protection, and fair lending issues)", "2 hours of training related to lending standards for the nontraditional mortgage product marketplace".

*Reg Z §1026.36(f)–(g) (00a-fed §12)* — VERIFIED: the loan originator organization must ensure individuals are licensed/registered "to the extent the individual is required to be licensed or registered under the SAFE Act" and must place the organization's and the individual's name and NMLSR ID on "the credit application; the disclosures required by §1026.19(e) and (f); the note or loan contract; and the security instrument".

*NMLS renewal (NMLS Resource Center, "Annual Renewal Overview for Companies", © 2026)* — VERIFIED: "The annual renewal period in NMLS begins on November 1 and ends on December 31 each year."; "If a renewal request is not submitted by December 31, companies may have the opportunity to complete the process during the reinstatement period from January 1 through the end of February."; companies should ensure "sponsored mortgage loan originators (MLOs) have met continuing education requirements" before renewal.

*Design consequences (with 00a-fed §12).* (1) The tests are written for "an individual"; an AI is neither licensable nor an "independent contractor"; no CFPB/CSBS/NMLS statement on AI "taking an application" exists (**UNVERIFIED-negative, 00a-fed §12**). The platform therefore keeps `origination.ai_mlo_intake = assisted`: the AI is a tool operating "at the direction of and subject to the supervision and instruction of" the partner-employed `mlo_of_record`, who presents particular terms and approves LE terms and locks (20.3, 21.1, 21.4), and whose NMLSR ID appears on the application, LE/CD, note and security instrument. (2) SM staff cannot be MLOs *of record* for the partner: an MLO license is sponsored by the employing/contracting company's license in NMLS; an SM employee can be an MLO of record only if SM holds a lender/broker license in that state and sponsors the individual — which would make SM, not the partner, the originating entity for that state's licensing purposes and would contradict the operating model. Default: the partner employs and sponsors every `mlo_of_record`. (3) SM's *human* processors/underwriters (`underwriting_reviewer`, `licensed_specialist`, needs-list waivers) are not partner employees; where their duties are "clerical or support duties" performed "at the direction of and subject to the supervision and instruction of" the partner's licensed MLO they fit the federal (e)(3) exemption, but the state statutes below mostly require *employee* status for the exemption, so SM's humans are "independent contractors" in the state sense and the state matrix governs. (4) The AI agents' processing/underwriting work is not licensed activity because no individual performs it; every state's exemption text is nonetheless recorded in `license_requirements` with the AI position, and states that could read an *entity* processing/underwriting requirement onto SM (processor registration, exemption letter, broker license) are treated as requiring SM to hold that credential regardless of whether the work is done by humans or agents.

**G. RESPA table funding.** *12 CFR 1024.2(b) (eCFR 9/08/2026; 00a-fed §6.7)* — VERIFIED: "Lender means, generally, the secured creditor or creditors named in the debt obligation and document creating the lien. For loans originated by a mortgage broker that closes a federally related mortgage loan in its own name in a table funding transaction, the lender is the person to whom the obligation is initially assigned at or after settlement." "Table funding means a settlement at which a loan is funded by a contemporaneous advance of loan funds and an assignment of the loan to the person advancing the funds. A table-funded transaction is not a secondary market transaction (see § 1024.5(b)(7))." "Mortgage broker means a person (other than an employee of a lender) that renders origination services and serves as an intermediary between a borrower and a lender in a transaction involving a federally related mortgage loan, including such a person that closes the loan in its own name in a table-funded transaction." *§1024.5(b)(7)* (eCFR 9/09/2026, re-read 2026-09-11 — VERIFIED): "A bona fide transfer of a loan obligation in the secondary market is not covered by RESPA and this part, except with respect to RESPA (12 U.S.C. 2605) and subpart C of this part (§§ 1024.30-1024.41). In determining what constitutes a bona fide transfer, the Bureau will consider the real source of funding and the real interest of the funding lender. Mortgage broker transactions that are table-funded are not secondary market transactions."

*Resolution.* Two legal forms are available for SM's warehouse money: **Form A — secured loan to the partner:** the partner is the creditor named in the note and security instrument; SM advances funds under a warehouse credit agreement secured by a security interest in the note (for eNotes, "control" under E-SIGN §7021/UETA §16 through the eRegistry Secured Party entry and Fannie Mae's Funding Agreement — 27.1 corrects the UCC 9-105 citation, which covers electronic chattel paper, not transferable records; possession via bailee for paper); SM is Interim Funder (MERS) / Secured Party (eRegistry); the partner sells the loan to Fannie Mae in a bona fide secondary-market transfer and repays the advance from purchase proceeds. No assignment of the loan to SM occurs at settlement → not "table funding"; the partner is the RESPA "lender" and the TILA "creditor" (the person to whom the obligation is initially payable, §1026.2(a)(17)); LE/CD/Reg B/HMDA all name the partner. **Form B — purchase at settlement:** SM advances funds against a contemporaneous assignment of the loan to SM → "table funding"; SM becomes the RESPA "lender", the partner a "mortgage broker" that "closes the loan in its own name", the transaction is not a §1024.5(b)(7) secondary-market transfer, §8/AfBA analysis changes, and the Fannie Mae seller would be delivering a loan it no longer owns at settlement (repurchase from SM would be needed) — incompatible with the operating model. **Recommended structure: Form A**, recorded as `origination.warehouse_legal_form = secured_loan_to_partner` (config owned here; 27.1's `warehouse_facilities` carries the executed facility documents), with the open decision being only whether the partner's economics (SM bears third-party costs; gain-on-sale recovers costs; residue to the borrower as rate) are documented as a *services-and-warehouse fee arrangement* rather than a purchase price — 27.2 owns the accounting; counsel must confirm the "real source of funding / real interest" test is satisfied by a bona fide credit facility with recourse to the partner (open question 3).

**Discrepancies vs blueprint.**
1. "Form 582 (partner)… annual" — Form 582 is due "no later than 90 days after the end of the seller/servicer's fiscal year" (A4-1-02), the AFS the same, Form 1002 quarterly 30/60 days; the clocks are owned by 18.4/18.3 and only *referenced* here; SM files its own Form 582 as a servicer/TSP.
2. "A2-1 technology/AI clauses" — the ≥ 20,000-loan technology-provider regime is *Servicing* Guide A2-1-01, not the Selling Guide topic of the same number (which lists the Lender Contract documents and special approvals); LL-2026-04 is a Lender Letter, not an A2-1 clause.
3. "NMLS annual by Dec 31" is right but incomplete: the renewal *window* opens Nov 1, a reinstatement period runs Jan 1 to the end of February, and MLO renewal is gated by 8 hours of NMLSR-approved CE (§1008.107).
4. "SM holds any third-party processor/underwriter/servicer licenses a state requires" hides three regimes — exemption letter (OH), registration (NC; IL entity license), individual-plus-entity licensing (TX, WA, CT, CA, AZ) — and Florida's no-entity-license contract-processor model; the matrix is nine states verified, the rest UNVERIFIED.
5. The row omits the RESPA "lender"/"table funding" consequence of the warehouse legal form (§1024.2(b)) — resolved above.
6. The row omits the TSP-side approvals with unpublished lead times (TSP certification 2–6 months; eMortgage approval; Technology Manager TSP-product assignment; Form 101) that sit on the critical path of Stage O-2.
7. "Net worth/liquidity per FHFA" — the FHFA figures are unchanged since the Aug 17, 2022 final requirements; the "2023–2025 updates" are phased effective dates (9/30/2023, 12/31/2023, 3/31/2024), and the origination-liquidity component (50 bps of HFS + fallout-adjusted IRLC pipeline, > $1B originated) is the only origination-specific input.

#### State machine

*License:* `planned` → `applied` (NMLS filing evidence) → `pending` → `approved` | `approved_conditions` | (denied → `planned` with reason). `approved` → `renewal_requested` (Nov 1–Dec 31) → `approved` (renewed, new `expires_at` = Dec 31 next year). `approved` → `terminated_expired` on Jan 1 if not renewed (NMLS sets the status; the platform mirrors) → `approved` via `reinstated` (Jan 1–Feb 28/29) or → `planned` (re-application). Any → `suspended`/`revoked` (regulator action; also an 18.4 org-change/regulatory-action event) — terminal until a new application. Guards: `renewed` requires, for individuals, `ce_completed_at` within the renewal year and `ce_hours ≥ 8`; for companies, all sponsored MLOs' renewals attested (NMLS mechanics). Transitions are made only from NMLS sync or documented evidence — the agent never marks a license `approved` without a Consumer Access/B2B record or an issued license document.
*Fannie Mae approval:* `planned` → `applied` → `testing` (TSP certification) → `granted` → `active` (all conditions met: e.g., for eMortgage: eRegistry participation + eVault + warehouse eNote agreement + eNote Indicator process) ⇄ `conditions`/`suspended` → `revoked`/`expired`.
*State readiness (derived per state, drives `SM_LICENSE_STATE_GATE`):* `closed` → `open` when: partner company license(s) of every type in `lender_license_types` are `approved`; branch license if required; ≥ 1 `mlo_roster` member assignable in the state; SM's `processing_underwriting_entity` credential `approved`/`exempt` where `processor_license_required ∉ {none}`; `verification_status ≠ unverified` (or an `officer` risk acceptance with `attorney` memo recorded — never for `unverified` processor rules); `ai_intake_position ≠ unresolved`.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **State readiness** (`SM_LICENSE_STATE_GATE`): `open(S) = partner_company_ok(S) ∧ branch_ok(S) ∧ mlo_available(S) ∧ sm_processing_ok(S) ∧ matrix_ok(S) ∧ ai_position_ok(S)` where `sm_processing_ok(S) = (processor_license_required(S) = none) ∨ (∃ licenses{holder_kind=sm_company, jurisdiction=S, activity_scope ∋ processing_underwriting_entity ∨ exempt_letter, status ∈ approved, exempt})`; `matrix_ok(S) = verification_status(S) ≠ unverified ∨ officer_risk_acceptance(S) with attorney memo` (never for `processor_license_required = unverified`); `ai_position_ok(S) = ai_intake_position(S) ≠ unresolved`. Human-touchpoint routing: if `independent_processor_individual_license_required(S)` then every `underwriting_reviewer`/`licensed_specialist` action on an S-property file must be performed by a person whose `licenses` row for S (`processor_underwriter_individual` or `mlo_individual`) is `approved`, else by a partner employee; enforced by the escalation router (`escalations.assignee` must pass `isLicensedFor(person, S, activity)`).

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
- **Agent:** `compliance-sentinel` (licensing persona) with `security-records` for evidence custody. Tools: `nmls.sync`, `licenses.upsert` (status only from evidence), `matrix.propose` (drafts `license_requirements` rows from statute text with citations; never sets `verified` — counsel does), `readiness.compute(state)`, `roster.recompute`, `roster.reassign(mlo_id)`, `approvals.upsert` (from evidence), `eligibility.computeOrigInputs(period)`, `packages.form582SellerSide`, `packages.tpoQuarterly`, `portal_task.create`, `timers.read`, `escalations.open`.
- **End-to-end behavior:** keeps every license and approval current from NMLS/evidence; computes state readiness nightly and on every event; blocks commands through `SM_LICENSE_STATE_GATE`; drives the renewal season (checklists to `officer` and each MLO; CE tracking; reinstatement); reassigns files when an MLO becomes unassignable; watches state licensing pages and CSBS/NMLS announcements and drafts matrix updates for counsel; computes the quarterly origination eligibility inputs and the annual $1B test; assembles the seller-side Form 582 and A3-3-01 packages; maintains the TSP certification and eMortgage approval pipeline with `fnma_portal_operator` tasks.
- **Decision record** (`agent_decisions`): `{subject (state | license_id | mlo_id | approval_id | period), rule_set_version (nmls.licensing vN; license_requirements version), inputs (nmls records, evidence document ids), computation (readiness predicates | liquidity arithmetic), outcome, rationale, confidence, escalation?, model_version, prompt_version}`.

- **Human path (AI off):** the licensing workspace exposes the registries, readiness report and renewal checklists; gates are enforced by the same predicates.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **MLO license lapses mid-file** (expired Jan 1; file consummating Jan 8): reassign before any new disclosure; the CD issued Dec 30 stands; the note/security instrument (§1026.36(g)) must show an MLO who is licensed on the date the documents are *signed* — 26.1 regenerates the note/security instrument NMLSR fields from the current assignment before signing.
- **Partner expands into a new state**: readiness `closed` until partner license, MLO and SM credential exist; marketing suppression (20.2) automatic.
- **State reclassifies contract processing as licensed activity** (e.g., a new rule like OAC 1301:8-7-32): `compliance-sentinel` flags; counsel updates the row; if SM lacks the credential the state closes for *new* applications while in-flight files complete under a documented transition plan (or are routed to partner employees).
- **Sponsorship conflict**: an SM individual licensed in CT may be sponsored by only one person at a time — the platform refuses an assignment that would require dual sponsorship.
- **Fannie Mae revokes/suspends the partner's seller approval**: all delivery gates close (29.4), pipeline handled under the partner's contingency (loans sold to another investor are out of scope → `officer`/`attorney`); SM's warehouse exposure escalates (27.1).
- **Form 101 terminated by Fannie Mae**: `FNMA_TSP_PRODUCTION_CERT_GATE` closes for partner scope (19.3); portal fallbacks by the partner's own users.
- **TSP certification delayed beyond launch**: UI fallbacks per product (DU UI, EarlyCheck UI, UCDP UI, UCD import) by `fnma_portal_operator`; Loan Delivery is portal-only regardless.
- **eMortgage approval conditions** (e.g., limited to certain eClosing providers): `fnma_approvals.conditions` gate 26.2's vendor choice.
- **Reg H "independent contractor" ambiguity**: where SM staff work *under* the partner's licensed MLO but are not partner employees, the federal exemption may apply while the state's "as an employee" language does not — the state rule governs (fail-closed).
- **AI-generated communications with employers/borrowers during processing** are "communicating with a consumer to obtain the information necessary for the processing" — clerical/support by definition when performed under the MLO's supervision; the agent never offers or negotiates rates or terms in those channels (prompt guard + classifier; 21.1).
- **License data mismatch** (NMLS shows `approved` but the state portal shows a pending action): the stricter status wins; `officer` reconciles within 1 business day.

Supports Fannie Mae eligibility/MORA questions ("licensing and good standing in all jurisdictions"), state multistate exams (CSBS/NMLS), the partner's TPO-program evidence, and litigation over unlicensed activity (proof that no individual acted without a license and that the AI operated under a licensed MLO's supervision).

### Open questions / decisions
1. **SM entity credential strategy per state.** Default: obtain the specific processing/underwriting credential wherever one exists (OH exemption letter, NC registration, IL entity license, TX company license) and a mortgage broker/lender license where that is the only vehicle to sponsor individual licensees (WA, AZ, CT, CA); route human touchpoints to partner employees in states where SM chooses not to license; keep unverified states closed.
2. **Is SM a "third-party originator" under A3-3-01 and does any SFC apply?** Default: run the full TPO program over SM; deliver no SFC 211/212; obtain the partner's account team's written view before the first delivery.
3. **Warehouse legal form.** Default: Form A (secured loan to the partner); counsel opinion on §1024.2(b)/§1024.5(b)(7) "real source of funding" before go-live; Form B prohibited by config.
4. **AI intake position per state.** Default: `assisted_required` everywhere (MLO of record presents terms and approves LE/locks); `autonomous` only on a written state-specific memo, reviewed annually.
5. **May SM employees ever be MLOs of record?** Default: no — every `mlo_of_record` is partner-employed and partner-sponsored; revisit only if SM obtains lender/broker licenses and the operating model changes.
6. **NMLS B2B Access vs manual Consumer Access.** Default: subscribe to B2B if available for the partner's and SM's records; otherwise the 25.1 manual procedure with a 30-day freshness rule.
7. **Remittance type assumption for the eligibility arithmetic** (A/A for whole-loan cash). Default: A/A; confirm C2-1.1 before the first quarterly input.
