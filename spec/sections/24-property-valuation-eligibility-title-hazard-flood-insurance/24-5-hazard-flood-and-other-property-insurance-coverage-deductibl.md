### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Carrier, policy and lender responsibility — all property types.** Selling Guide B7-3-01, *General Property Insurance Requirements for All Property Types* (12/14/2022): the insurer must meet one of: "AM Best Company 'B' or better Financial Strength Rating," "Demotech, Inc. 'A' or better Insurance Financial Stability Rating," "Kroll Bond Rating Agency 'BBB' or better Insurance Financial Strength Rating (IFSR)," "S&P Global 'BBB' or better Insurer Financial Strength Rating"; "An insurer is only required to meet the rating category requirement for one of the rating agencies, even if they are rated by multiple rating agencies." "Fannie Mae also accepts the following types of property insurance policies if they are the only coverage that can be obtained at the time of the loan closing or policy renewal: policies obtained through state or territory insurance plans, including a state's Fair Access to Insurance Requirements (FAIR) plan" (and the other listed plan types — not fully extracted, **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**). "If the lender or servicer, as applicable, is covered by a mortgage impairment (or mortgagee interest) insurance policy, and the issuer meets either the AM Best Financial Strength Rating or S&P Global Insurer Financial Strength Rating ... Fannie Mae does not require confirmation that the borrower's property insurance coverage is with an insurer that meets Fannie Mae's rating requirements." "The lender or servicer must ensure that the insurer, policy, and coverage meet Fannie Mae's requirements."

**One- to four-unit coverage, form, perils and deductibles.** B7-3-02 (08/05/2026): "Property insurance policies for one- to four-unit properties securing loans purchased by Fannie Mae should be written on a 'Special' coverage form or equivalent." Minimum required perils: fire or lightning, explosion, windstorm (including named storms), hail, smoke, aircraft, vehicles, riot or civil commotion. "The property insurance policy must provide coverage on a replacement cost basis, with the exception of roofs; property insurance policies that provide such terms of coverage will be deemed to provide sufficient coverage. Roofs must be insured, but do not have to be insured on a replacement cost basis." "The maximum allowable deductible for all required property insurance perils for one-to four-unit properties is 5% of the property insurance coverage amount." "If the property insurance policy includes separate deductibles for different required perils, such as windstorm or wildfire, each individual deductible must not exceed 5% of the property insurance coverage amount." Flood is cross-referenced to B7-3-06. Earthquake, sinkhole and mine subsidence are **not** among the required perils; B7-3-05 (12/14/2022) says only that if a lender "becomes aware of a subject property's exposure to perils that are not covered as required in *Required Perils*" it must "contact Fannie Mae to determine whether additional coverage is necessary" — there is no sentence saying earthquake coverage is or is not required (**inference from the peril list; [PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**). LL-2026-03 (03/18/2026): for 1–4 unit properties "These changes are effective immediately"; "We are retiring the requirements related to documenting the replacement cost value to verify the property insurance policy coverage amount."

**Project master policies.** B7-3-03 (08/05/2026): "The master property insurance coverage amount must equal at least 100% of the estimated replacement cost value," documented by one of "Guaranteed replacement cost coverage, or its equivalent," "Extended replacement cost coverage, or its equivalent," "A replacement cost value estimate provided by the insurer," "The project's insurance risk appraisal," or "A statement from the insurer or other applicable professional"; "Master property insurance coverage policies covering project developments should be written on a 'Special' coverage form or equivalent. At a minimum, the coverage must include the perils covered by a commercial 'Broad' coverage form" (fire, lightning, explosion, windstorm, hail, smoke, aircraft/vehicles, riot, vandalism, sprinkler leakage, sinkhole collapse, volcanic action, falling objects, weight of snow/ice/sleet, water damage); "The maximum allowable deductible for all required property insurance perils is 5% of the master property insurance coverage amount"; "The maximum allowable per unit deductible for all required property insurance perils covered by a master property insurance policy is $50,000 per unit"; boiler and machinery "required if the project development has central heating or cooling. The coverage amount must equal the lesser of $2 million or the replacement cost value"; building-ordinance coverage (demolition, undamaged portions, increased construction cost); condo master policies "must be endorsed with a Condominium Association Coverage Form or its equivalent." LL-2026-03: "Last, we are retiring the requirement that project developments have inflation guard coverage"; master/unit-owner changes: "Lenders are encouraged to implement these changes immediately but must do so for all loans with application dates on or after July 1, 2026."

**Unit owner (HO-6) policies.** B7-3-04 (08/05/2026): required when "any portion of the interior of the unit or improvements to the unit are not covered by the master property insurance policy, or the master property insurance policy includes a per unit deductible"; amount "at least equal to the greater of: an amount sufficient to cover any portion of the interior of the unit or improvements to the unit not covered by the master property insurance policy in order to restore the unit to its condition prior to a loss event; or the amount of the per unit deductible"; maximum deductible "the greater of: 5% of the property insurance coverage amount, or $2,500."

**Evidence of insurance.** B7-3-07 (08/05/2026): "A lender or servicer may accept a certificate of property insurance in lieu of a complete property insurance policy if the certificate includes all of the information needed"; "Electronic data files are acceptable in lieu of hard copy insurance policies if all the requirements in the following table are met," including that the lender/servicer retain "sufficient information about the insurance policy, the subject property, and the borrower to ensure compliance with Fannie Mae's requirements," and "The lender's or servicer's errors and omissions insurance policy must acknowledge electronic data transfers and fully protect the lender or servicer and Fannie Mae against losses incurred as the result of erroneous data files or transfers." The topic states **no minimum policy term and no rule tying the effective date to the note date** (the binder/declarations must simply evidence coverage in force — SM policy: effective on or before the disbursement date, 12-month term on purchases with the first year's premium paid at closing; **[policy — the Guide is silent]**).

**Mortgagee clause, named insured, cancellation.** B7-3-08 (12/14/2022): "The applicable insurance policy must include (or have attached) a 'standard' or 'union' mortgagee clause (without contribution) ..."; "When Fannie Mae is not named in the mortgagee clause, the lender's name, followed by the phrase 'its successors and/or assigns,' and mailing address must be shown as the mortgagee"; "If the lender is not the servicer, the servicer's name, followed by 'its successors and/or assigns,' and mailing address must be specified"; "If Fannie Mae is named, the clause must read: 'Fannie Mae, in care of (insert servicer's name and address here).'" "In all cases, the insurer must be instructed to send all correspondence, policies, and bills to the servicer (or to both the first and second mortgage servicers)." "MERS must not be named as mortgagee or loss payee on any property insurance policy." Named insured: 1–4 unit "All persons holding title to the subject property as named insured"; master policies "The HOA as the named insured"; co-ops "The co-op corporation as the named insured." "The property insurance policy must provide for written notice to the named insured and mortgagee(s) before the insurer can cancel the policy" — no minimum number of days is stated. **The Guide's operative words are "its successors and/or assigns"; the industry's "ISAOA/ATIMA" ("as their interests may appear") tail is customary and not required — [PARTIALLY VERIFIED as to industry usage].**

**Flood — Fannie Mae.** B7-3-06 (02/07/2024): "The lender and servicer must determine whether the property is located in an SFHA, a CBRS, or an OPA by using the FEMA Standard Flood Hazard Determination form"; SFHA zones begin with "A" or "V"; "The minimum amount of flood insurance required for first mortgages must be equal to the lesser of 100% of the replacement cost value of the improvements, the maximum coverage amount available from NFIP, or the unpaid principal balance (UPB) of the loan"; "The deductible must not exceed the maximum deductible amount currently offered by NFIP for one- to four-unit properties insured under an NFIP Dwelling Form"; "The flood insurance policy must be one of the following: a standard policy issued under the NFIP; or a policy issued by a private insurer, provided the terms and amount of coverage are at least equal to that provided under an NFIP policy ... and the insurer meets Fannie Mae's rating requirements"; RCBAP: "The coverage amount for the building must be at least equal to the lesser of 80% of the replacement cost value, or the maximum coverage amount available from NFIP per unit. If the master flood insurance policy meets the minimum coverage requirement of 80% of the replacement cost value, but the per unit coverage amount does not meet the requirement ... the unit owner must maintain a supplemental policy"; "If the lender or servicer determines that a principal or residential detached structure is located in an SFHA, but the community does not participate in the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), the loan is not eligible for purchase by Fannie Mae"; a non-residential detached structure alone in the SFHA → coverage "not required on either structure"; "A Policy Declaration page is acceptable evidence of flood insurance"; SFC 180 "No Flood Insurance — Not a Special Flood Hazard Area"; and (in the NFIP-lapse context) lenders "Continue to make flood determinations, provide timely, complete and accurate notices to borrowers in accordance with applicable laws, and comply with all other flood insurance requirements." **No sentence in B7-3-06 requires escrow of flood premiums.**

**Flood — statute** (00a-fed §10, VERIFIED; 42 U.S.C. 4003 read today): 4012a(b)(1)(A) binds "regulated lending institutions"; 4012a(b)(3): "The Federal National Mortgage Association and the Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation shall implement procedures reasonably designed to ensure that ... any loan purchased ... is covered for the term of the loan by flood insurance"; 4012a(b)(1)(B)/(b)(7): private flood insurance "at least as broad as the coverage provided under a standard flood insurance policy" must be accepted; 4012a(d)(1) escrow of premiums applies to regulated lending institutions; 4012a(e) force placement "within 45 days after notification" (servicing 9.6). 4003(a)(10) (current through Aug 20, 2026): "regulated lending institution" means "any bank, savings and loan association, credit union, farm credit bank, Federal land bank association, production credit association, or similar institution subject to the supervision of a Federal entity for lending regulation"; 4003(a)(9): "lender" means "a regulated lending institution or Federal agency lender"; 4003(a)(11) defines "servicer" as the person receiving periodic payments "including amounts for taxes, insurance premiums, and other charges." 4104a(a)(1): written notice "a reasonable period in advance of the signing of the purchase agreement, lease, or other documents"; (a)(3) content: the SFHA warning "in a form to be established by the Administrator," "a description of the flood insurance purchase requirements under section 102(b) of the Flood Disaster Protection Act of 1973," that coverage "may be purchased under the national flood insurance program and is also available from private insurers," and other Administrator-required information. 2022 Interagency Q&A (87 FR 32826), Notice 3: "The Agencies generally regard 10 days as a 'reasonable time' interval." SFHDF: FEMA Form FF-206-FY-21-116, OMB 1660-0040, "expires 2026-09-30" with no renewal notice located (00a-fed §10) — the form version is a `jurisdiction_rules`/vendor configuration item.

**NFIP limits, deductibles, effective dates.** 44 CFR 61.6 (eCFR current as of 9/04/2026): single-family dwelling building "$250,000," contents "$100,000"; other residential building "$500,000"; residential condominium building "$250,000 times the number of units in the building." 44 CFR 61.5 (8/26/2026): "FEMA must provide policyholders with deductible options in various amounts, up to and including $10,000." 44 CFR 61.11 (9/03/2026): the 30-day waiting period ("12:01 a.m. (local time) on the 30th calendar day after") does not apply to loan closings — coverage is effective "as of the time of the loan closing, provided the written request for the coverage is received by the NFIP and the flood insurance policy is applied for and the presentment of payment of premium is made at or prior to the loan closing"; map-revision 13-month one-day rule; post-wildfire exception.

**Escrow of flood premiums — finding.** Selling Guide B2-1.5-04, *Escrow Accounts* (04/01/2020): "Fannie Mae advocates the establishment of an escrow account for the payment of taxes and insurance"; "Unless required by law, lenders may waive escrow account requirements for an individual first mortgage, provided the standard escrow provision remains in the mortgage loan legal documents"; the lender's written policies must base waivers not "solely on the LTV ratio" but on "whether the borrower has the financial ability to handle the lump sum payments"; "escrow deposits for the payment of premiums for borrower-purchased mortgage insurance (if applicable) are mandatory"; "Fannie Mae does not require an escrow deposit for property or flood insurance premiums for an individual unit in a condo, co-op, or PUD when the project in which the unit is located is covered by a blanket insurance policy." Servicing Guide B-1-01 (09/11/2024): flood premiums are listed among escrow charges the servicer administers; "The servicer may not waive the individual escrow requirement for MIPs when the premiums are paid monthly"; no flood-escrow mandate. **Conclusion (confidence: VERIFIED for the three texts read; PARTIALLY VERIFIED as a legal conclusion pending the partner's charter):** for a non-bank partner selling to Fannie Mae, neither the Selling Guide nor the Servicing Guide nor Reg X requires escrow of flood premiums; the statutory mandate in 42 U.S.C. 4012a(d) and 12 CFR 22.5 applies only if the partner is a regulated lending institution (or the loan is an HPML, where §1026.35(b) requires an escrow for taxes and creditor-required insurance — 23.4/30.3). SM's design: flood is an escrow line whenever the loan is escrowed (fixture: escrowed), and `jurisdiction_rules.partner.regulated_lending_institution` flips the line to non-waivable (servicing 3.8's `FLOOD_12CFR22_5_ESCROW_GATE` then applies).

**Project liability and fidelity.** B7-4-01 (08/05/2026): condo and co-op projects (except those qualifying for a waiver of project review) must carry general liability of "at least $1 million for bodily injury and property damage for any single occurrence" with a "separation of insureds or severability of interests provision." B7-4-02 (08/05/2026): "Fidelity/crime insurance is required for all condo and co-op projects" except waiver-of-review projects, projects with 20 units or fewer, or coverage needs of $5,000 or less; amount "the sum of three months of assessments on all units in the project" (with financial controls) or "the maximum funds that are in the custody of the HOA or co-op corporation, or its management agent, at any time"; a management agent's own policy "is not an acceptable alternative"; "In states that have statutory fidelity/crime insurance requirements, Fannie Mae accepts those requirements in place of its own." (Project-level evidence is collected with 24.3's project review; 24.5 stores and tests it.)

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. "12 CFR 22/339/760 analogues — applicability to non-bank lenders": the agency rules and 42 U.S.C. 4012a(b)(1)/(d) bind "regulated lending institutions" (4003(a)(10)); the partner is bound through Fannie Mae (4012a(b)(3) → B7-3-06) and the servicer definition, not directly — the row treats the agency rules as if they applied.
2. The row's "coverage" nuance is stale: since LL-2026-03 the sufficiency test is the replacement-cost basis (roof exception), not an amount test; RCV documentation is retired; the inflation-guard requirement is retired.
3. The row omits the July 1, 2026 mandatory date (master/HO-6), the $50,000 per-unit cap, the $2,500 HO-6 deductible floor, the 5% per-peril rule, the one-agency rating rule and the mortgage-impairment alternative.
4. "Escrow implications" — neither Guide mandates flood-premium escrow for a non-bank seller; only monthly BPMI escrow is mandatory (B2-1.5-04) — the servicing spec 3.8's "flood escrow mandate" applies only when the partner is a regulated lending institution.
5. The flood notice statute does not literally bind a non-bank "lender"; the notice is delivered as a Fannie Mae "applicable laws"/best-practice obligation and because state laws may require it — the row's "≥ 10 days" is the Interagency Q&A's "generally ... reasonable" figure, not a statutory deadline.
6. B7-3-07 sets no minimum term or effective-date rule; "policy effective on/before disbursement" is SM policy.
7. The SFHDF form number and OMB expiration must be configuration (expires Sept 30, 2026), not code.
8. B7-4 project liability/fidelity rules are absent from the row's artifacts; B7-3-05's "contact Fannie Mae" peril rule is likewise absent.

- Flood vendor messages: `determination_result` (SFHDF data + PDF), `community_status`, `multiple_structures`; borrower/FEMA documents (LOMA/LOMR, elevation certificate).
- Evidence from the borrower's agent/carrier: declarations page, binder, certificate, ACORD/EDI, electronic verification via the tracking vendor; master-policy certificates from the HOA/management company (24.3 project package); RCBAP declarations.
- `closing.scheduled` (26.1) → notice-timing and evidence gates; `funding.authorized` request (26.3) → gates; `disclosure.cd.delivered` (25.2) → premium and escrow figures reconciled.
- `loan.funded` → hand-off to 9.1/9.6 (policies verified at origination become `verified` rows; LOL contract linked).

#### State machine

Flood: `ordered` → `received` → (`in_sfha=false` ∧ no residential structure in SFHA) `not_required` [SFC 180] | (`in_sfha=true`) `notice_due` → `notice_delivered` → `coverage_pending` → `coverage_verified` (policy passes amount/deductible/type/rating and is effective at closing) | `ineligible` (non-participating community; CBRS/OPA per B7-3-06 — terminal unless private coverage rules permit, open question 24.5-Q2). Hazard/master/HO-6: `requirement_computed` → `evidence_requested` → `evidence_received` → `verified` | `deficient` (deficiency row; agent drives cure) → `verified`. Terminal for origination: `verified` at funding (hand-off to 9.1) or `withdrawn`. Transitions are performed by the `title-closing` agent; `officer` only for B7-3-05 "contact Fannie Mae" outcomes and rating exceptions.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Requirement derivation** (same engine as 9.1): hazard always; wind/hail separately when excluded from the base policy; master policy for condo/co-op/attached-PUD projects (B7-3-03) unless the project's legal documents place the duty on unit owners; HO-6 when the master has a per-unit deductible or excludes interior/improvements (B7-3-04); flood when any part of the principal structure or a residential detached structure serving as security is in an SFHA (zones A*/V*); liability and fidelity evidence for condo/co-op projects not qualifying for waiver of review (fidelity also not required for ≤ 20 units or ≤ $5,000 coverage need).
2. **Hazard adequacy** (rule set `fnma.insurance.2026-08`): PASS iff `coverage_basis ∈ {replacement_cost, extended_rc, guaranteed_rc}` (roof may be ACV) ∧ `coverage_form = special` (or all eight required perils present) ∧ `deductible_pct ≤ 5.0000%` of `coverage_dwelling_cents` for the policy deductible and for every per-peril deductible ∧ carrier rating meets one of AM Best ≥ B / Demotech ≥ A / S&P ≥ BBB / KBRA ≥ BBB (or FAIR plan when the only coverage available, or `mortgage_impairment_relief`) ∧ mortgagee clause valid ∧ named insureds = title holders (24.4 vesting; trustee for trust vesting). `deductible_pct = deductible_cents ÷ coverage_dwelling_cents` in decimal.js compared without rounding. **No amount test** against UPB or RCV is applied (retired). Worked example (refinance fixture): loan $560,000.00, appraised $800,000.00, insurer's replacement-cost estimate $520,000.00 → the policy must be written on a replacement-cost basis (dwelling coverage as written by the carrier, here $520,000.00); the deductible cap is 5% × 52,000,000 cents = **2,600,000 cents ($26,000.00)**; a $5,000 all-peril deductible (0.9615%) passes; a separate 2% wind/hail deductible ($10,400.00) passes; a 5% hurricane deductible ($26,000.00, exactly 5.0000%) passes; a 6% named-storm deductible ($31,200.00) fails → `deductible_excess`. Under the retired pre-March-2026 rule the coverage-amount test would have compared $520,000 with the $560,000 UPB and the 80%-of-insurable-value floor — the engine no longer does this.
3. **Master/HO-6 arithmetic** (applications on/after July 1, 2026 — both fixtures): master `coverage ≥ 100% × RCV` by one of the five documentation options; master deductible ≤ 5% of master coverage and per-unit deductible ≤ $50,000; HO-6 amount ≥ max(restoration amount of uncovered interior/improvements, per-unit deductible); HO-6 deductible ≤ max(5% × HO-6 coverage, $2,500). Worked example (Columbus condo variant of the purchase fixture): master policy RCV statement $12,000,000, coverage $12,000,000 (100%), master deductible $250,000 (2.08%) with a per-unit deductible of $25,000 → HO-6 required; interior/improvement restoration estimate $40,000 → HO-6 coverage ≥ max($40,000, $25,000) = **$40,000**; HO-6 deductible ≤ max(5% × $40,000 = $2,000; $2,500) = **$2,500**; a master per-unit deductible of $60,000 would fail the $50,000 cap (`master_lapse`-class deficiency; the project must amend or the unit is ineligible).
4. **Flood amount and deductible**: `flood_required_amount_cents = min(rcv_improvements_cents, nfip_max_cents (25,000,000 for 1-unit; 50,000,000 "other residential" for 2–4 units per 44 CFR 61.6 — [PARTIALLY VERIFIED that 2–4 unit dwellings fall under "other residential"]), note_amount_cents)`; deductible ≤ NFIP maximum option (1,000,000 cents). Worked example (Zone AE variant of the refinance fixture): RCV $520,000, NFIP max $250,000, UPB $560,000 → required **$250,000.00**; deductible ≤ $10,000; an NFIP Dwelling Form policy with $250,000 building coverage and a $5,000 deductible passes; a private policy must show terms "at least equal to" the SFIP and a carrier meeting the B7-3-01 rating rule (private coverage above $250,000 is acceptable but not required). RCBAP example (40-unit building, RCV $12,000,000): required building coverage ≥ min(80% × $12,000,000 = $9,600,000; $250,000 × 40 = $10,000,000) = **$9,600,000**; per-unit allocation $240,000; the unit's own requirement is min(unit RCV share, $250,000, UPB $412,000) = $250,000 → the allocation is short by $10,000 → a supplemental unit flood policy of ≥ $10,000 is required.
5. **Notice timing**: `notice_reasonable_period_days = consummation_date − effective_receipt_date`; PASS if ≥ 10 `calendar_days`; if the closing date moves earlier, the gate re-evaluates. Worked example: Zone AE determination received Tue Oct 6, 2026 (with the title order on intent to proceed); notice e-delivered and e-sign-acknowledged Wed Oct 7; consummation Fri Nov 6 → 30 days ≥ 10 → PASS. Purchase fixture: determination Mon Oct 19, notice delivered Tue Oct 20, closing Wed Nov 18 → 29 days. A notice first delivered Mon Nov 2 for a Nov 6 consummation (4 days) fails the gate; the agent either moves consummation to ≥ Thu Nov 12 or records the reason and obtains the borrower's acknowledgment before signing (open question 24.5-Q1).
6. **NFIP effective date at closing**: for a new NFIP policy the application and premium presentment must occur "at or prior to the loan closing" — the agent requires the flood declarations or the WYO application with premium paid before the `FNMA_B7_3_06_FLOOD_COVERAGE_GATE` opens; for refinances with an existing policy, an assignment of the policy/mortgagee change is required.
7. **Mortgagee clause test**: PASS iff the clause names the partner "its successors and/or assigns" (ISAOA) with the SM servicing address (c/o Supermortgage) — or the servicer form of the clause where the partner is not the servicer — and MERS is absent; the ATIMA tail is accepted but not required.
8. **Escrow implications** (30.3 consumes): escrowed loan → hazard, flood and (where the master policy does not cover) HO-6 premiums become `escrow_lines` with the next due dates; non-escrowed loan → flood line waivable unless `partner.regulated_lending_institution=true` or HPML; premiums paid at closing appear on the CD as prepaids (25.2) and the initial deposit is computed by 30.3 (aggregate accounting; cushion ≤ 1/6 per §1024.17(c)). Worked figures for 30.3: hazard $1,950.00/yr paid at closing for 12 months (purchase) and a flood NFIP premium $1,150.00/yr → monthly lines $162.50 and $95.83 (rounded to cents; 30.3's rounding rule governs).
9. **Deficiency handling at origination**: any FAIL opens `insurance_deficiencies` (stage `origination`) and a `condition` (ptf); the agent requests corrected evidence from the borrower's agent/carrier; the only outcomes are `cured`, `waived_by_policy` (FAIR plan/state cap with `officer` acknowledgment) or `withdrawn`.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`title-closing` agent (tools: `orderFloodDetermination`, `parseSFHDF`, `renderFloodNotice`, `deliverNotice`, `computeInsuranceRequirements`, `requestEvidence`, `extractEvidence`, `lookupCarrierRating`, `confirmWithCarrier`, `evaluateAdequacy`, `checkMortgageeClause`, `openDeficiency`, `computeFloodAmount`, `evaluateGates`, `seedEscrowLines`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end: orders the determination with the title order; if SFHA, renders and delivers the notice within one business day and tracks the 10-day period against the scheduled consummation; computes requirements at DU findings; requests evidence from the borrower's agent; extracts and verifies evidence (carrier confirmation when extraction confidence < 0.90 on any key field, as in 9.1); tests adequacy; drives deficiencies; evaluates gates; seeds escrow lines for 30.3; hands the verified records to 9.1/9.6 at funding. Decision record: `{application_id, determination{zone, sfha, community, form_version, vendor_ref}, notice{delivered_at, channel, receipt_date, days_before_consummation}, requirements, evidence[{kind, document_id, extraction_confidence}], adequacy_results, deficiencies, gates, rationale, rule_set_version, model_version}`. Guardrails: never mark a policy adequate without the carrier-rating check and mortgagee-clause check; never accept an NFIP policy without evidence of application and premium at or before closing; never skip the notice on an SFHA loan (even where the statute may not literally bind the partner); never set SFC 180 when any residential structure is in an SFHA; never override the $50,000/5%/$2,500 tests. Escalations: `officer` for B7-3-05 "contact Fannie Mae" cases and rating exceptions (SLA 2 `business_days_creditor`); `human_agent` on request; the insurance agent/carrier as external parties with 2-business-day follow-ups. Borrower-facing: the notice and evidence requests disclose automation; language preference (Form 1103) drives translated cover notes (the notice itself in English plus translation where the state requires — **[UNVERIFIED state matrix]**).

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Multiple structures with only a non-residential detached structure in the SFHA → not required (B7-3-06); the SFHDF must show the structures; `sfc_180=true` only if no residential structure is in the SFHA.
- Community not participating in the NFIP → `ineligible`; the agent stops the loan and notifies 23.1/21.6 (adverse action reasons must cite collateral, not the borrower).
- CBRS/OPA property → flood coverage unavailable under NFIP; B7-3-06 lists CBRS/OPA in the determination scope; eligibility outcome open question 24.5-Q2 (default: ineligible).
- Determination changes between order and closing (map revision) → re-run the notice gate; a new SFHA finding fewer than 10 days before consummation follows rule 5's short-period path.
- LOMA/LOMR in hand → the vendor re-issues the determination as out of the SFHA; flood not required; document retained (9.6 handles later remaps).
- Carrier downgraded below the rating rule between evidence and funding → deficiency; FAIR plan or replacement policy; mortgage-impairment relief if SM's policy applies.
- Wildfire/hurricane moratoria: binders unavailable → the agent cannot fund without evidence; 21.4 lock exposure; FAIR plan as last resort.
- Trust vesting: named insured must include the trustee(s) (24.4) — otherwise `named_insured` deficiency.
- Escrow-waived borrower on an HPML → escrow (including flood) is mandatory under §1026.35(b) (23.4/30.3) regardless of the flood-escrow finding.
- Existing NFIP policy on a refinance assigned to the new lender: the mortgagee-clause change must be evidenced before funding; NFIP "assignment" is not needed for a refinance by the same owner but the mortgagee endorsement is.
- Vendor SFHDF on the expired form version after Sept 30, 2026 → accepted only if FEMA/OMB has extended or re-approved the form (configuration flag `sfhdf.form_version_accepted`); otherwise re-order.
- Evidence extracted with low confidence on the deductible → carrier confirmation required; never assumed.

#### Test cases and acceptance criteria
- **24.5-T1** Given the refinance fixture with dwelling coverage $520,000 on a replacement-cost basis (roof ACV), Special form, AM Best A carrier, deductible $5,000, wind deductible 2%, when evaluated, then `verified` with `hazard_deductible_max_cents=2600000` and all tests PASS.
- **24.5-T2** Given the same policy with a named-storm deductible of 6% ($31,200), when evaluated, then `deductible_excess` deficiency; given exactly 5% ($26,000), then PASS.
- **24.5-T3** Given a policy written on an actual-cash-value basis for the dwelling, when evaluated, then `acv_dwelling` deficiency (roof ACV alone would pass).
- **24.5-T4** Given a Zone AE determination received Tue Oct 6, 2026 and the notice e-signed Wed Oct 7, when consummation is Fri Nov 6, then `FDPA_4104A_FLOOD_NOTICE_GATE` passes (30 days); given the notice first delivered Mon Nov 2, then the gate fails and `consummate` is refused unless the short-period path (reason + acknowledgment) is recorded.
- **24.5-T5** Given Zone AE, RCV $520,000, UPB $560,000, when the requirement is computed, then `flood_required_amount_cents=25000000` and `flood_deductible_max_cents=1000000`; an NFIP policy of $250,000 with a $5,000 deductible applied and paid Nov 5 passes; a $200,000 policy fails (`flood_insufficient`).
- **24.5-T6** Given a 40-unit RCBAP with RCV $12,000,000 and coverage $9,600,000 and a unit with UPB $412,000, when evaluated, then the building passes (80% rule) and the unit requires a $10,000 supplemental policy (`flood_insufficient` until provided).
- **24.5-T7** Given a condo master policy with a $60,000 per-unit deductible on an application dated Oct 19, 2026, when evaluated, then FAIL (cap $50,000); given $25,000 and an HO-6 of $40,000 with a $2,500 deductible, then PASS.
- **24.5-T8** Given a mortgagee clause naming "MERS as nominee for [Partner]", when checked, then `mortgagee_clause` deficiency; given "[Partner], its successors and/or assigns, c/o Supermortgage, P.O. Box …", then PASS (with or without "as their interests may appear").
- **24.5-T9** Given a Zone X determination with no residential structure in an SFHA, when finalized, then `not_required`, `sfc_180=true`, no notice, and 29.3 receives SFC 180.
- **24.5-T10** Given a non-participating community, when the determination is received, then `ineligible` and the loan is stopped with a collateral-based reason.
- **24.5-T11** Given the partner flagged `regulated_lending_institution=false` and an escrow-waived non-HPML loan in an SFHA, when escrow lines are seeded, then the flood line is `waivable=true`; given the flag true, then `waivable=false` (12 CFR 22.5).
- **24.5-T12** Given a carrier rated only by Demotech at "A" and unrated elsewhere, when evaluated, then the rating test PASSES (one agency suffices); given Demotech "S" with no other rating, then `rating_fail` unless FAIR plan/mortgage-impairment relief applies.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Short-period notice path** — when an SFHA is discovered < 10 days before a scheduled consummation, allow closing with a documented reason and borrower acknowledgment (Interagency Q&A permits a shorter "reasonable" period case by case)? Recommended default: allow only with acknowledgment signed before the closing documents and a rescheduling offer; never for purchase contracts signed after the determination without the notice.
2. **CBRS/OPA properties** — treat as ineligible (NFIP unavailable) or allow private flood meeting B7-3-06? Recommended default: ineligible pending Fannie Mae confirmation (`officer` escalation on the first case).
3. **Flood-premium escrow for non-escrowed loans** — offer/require? Recommended default: require escrow of flood premiums on all SFHA loans as SM policy (waivable only with the borrower's written election on non-HPML loans where the partner is not a regulated lending institution); revisit if the partner's charter changes.
4. **Minimum policy term and effective date** (the Guide is silent) — Recommended default: effective on/before disbursement; 12-month term with the first year paid at closing for purchases; refinances accept the in-force policy with ≥ 30 days remaining plus renewal handling by 9.1.
5. **Private flood "compliance aid" statement** — accept the 12 CFR 22.3(c) compliance-aid language as sufficient evidence of NFIP equivalence? Recommended default: accept the statement plus the B7-3-01 rating check; otherwise require a term comparison by the tracking vendor.
