### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Depository-asset verification standard — B3-4.2-01, Verification of Deposits and Assets (05/04/2022). VERIFIED (quoted in 22.1).** Statements must identify the institution, the borrower as account holder, at least the last four digits of the account number, the period covered, all deposit and withdrawal transactions and the ending balance; period: purchases "the most recent full two-month period of account activity (60 days, or, if account information is reported on a quarterly basis, the most recent quarter)"; refinances "the most recent full one-month period of account activity (30 days, or ... the most recent quarter)"; Form 1006 must be "requested directly from the depository institution" and "sent directly from the depository institution"; a lender holding the account "may produce a printout ... directly from its system."

**Depository accounts, large deposits, business assets — B3-4.2-02, Depository Accounts (12/14/2022). VERIFIED.** "Funds held in a checking, savings, money market, certificate of deposit, or other depository accounts may be used for the down payment, closing costs, and financial reserves. The funds must be verified as described in B3-4.2-01." "Unverified funds are not acceptable for the down payment, closing costs, or financial reserves." When a Form 1006 without activity detail is used, the lender must verify the source of funds for "accounts opened within the last 90 days of the application date, and account balances that are considerably greater than the average balance reflected on the VOD." **Large deposits:** "A large deposit is defined as a single deposit that exceeds 50% of the total monthly qualifying income for the loan." Refinances: "Documentation or explanation for large deposits is not required; however, the lender remains responsible for ensuring that any borrowed funds ... are considered." Purchases: "If funds from a large deposit are needed to complete the purchase transaction (that is, are used for the down payment, closing costs, or financial reserves), the lender must document that those funds are from an acceptable source." Note: "If the source of a large deposit is readily identifiable on the account statement(s), such as a direct deposit from an employer (payroll), the Social Security Administration, or IRS or state income tax refund, or a transfer of funds between verified accounts, and the source of the deposit is printed on the statement, the lender does not need to obtain further explanation or documentation." Reduction: "Verified funds must be reduced by the amount (or portion) of the undocumented large deposit ... and the lender must confirm that the remaining funds are sufficient for the down payment, closing costs, and financial reserves." Partial sourcing: "When a deposit has both sourced and unsourced portions, only the unsourced portion must be used to calculate whether or not it must be considered a large deposit" (the topic's example: $4,000 income, $3,000 deposit of which $2,500 is a documented tax refund → $500 unsourced = 12.5%, not a large deposit). DU: "The DU validation service automates the assessment of large deposits. When assets are validated, DU issues a message indicating which large deposits require documentation." **Business assets:** "Business assets may be an acceptable source of funds for the down payment, closing costs, and financial reserves. The borrower must be listed as an owner of the account and the account must be verified in accordance with B3-4.2-01"; the self-employment analysis (B3-3.5, 22.3) governs whether the withdrawal impairs the business **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — the cash-flow-impact sentence was not returned by the extraction; 22.3's written business evaluation is the control]**.

**DU documentation of depository assets — B3-4.4-02, Requirements for Certain Assets in DU (06/01/2022). VERIFIED.** "For depository assets (checking and savings accounts, money market funds, and certificates of deposit), DU will require the following: two consecutive monthly bank statements (60 days of account activity) for all purchase transactions, or one monthly statement (30 days of account activity) for all limited cash-out and cash-out refinance transactions." "Monthly bank statements must be dated within 45 days of the initial loan application date. Quarterly bank statements must be dated within 90 days of the initial loan application date, and the lender must confirm that the funds in the account have not been transferred to another asset account that is verified with more current documentation." "A Verification of Deposit (Form 1006) can be obtained in place of bank statements." "When DU validates assets, DU issues a message indicating the acceptable documentation. Compliance with the DU message satisfies the requirement for documenting assets." Other DU entries: earnest money is "entered in Section 2b in the loan application" and "treated as a credit to the transaction"; sale of personal assets — "Enter the value of personal assets that will be converted to a liquid asset (or sold) prior to closing"; proceeds from sold properties "must be included in a depository account"; secured borrowed funds "entered in Section 2b using the asset type Secured Borrowed Funds"; bridge loans "in the asset section 2a." **B3-4.4-01, DU Asset Verification (12/16/2020). VERIFIED.** The findings identify "Funds Required to Close" and "Reserves Required to be Verified"; DU determines reserves "based on the overall risk assessment of the loan casefile and the minimum reserves that may be required for the transaction"; "DU does not consider the amount of non-liquid assets. Non-liquid assets do not have to be verified, and will not be identified in a verification message."; "Excess Available Assets do not generally need to be verified."

**Age — B1-1-03 (04/02/2025). VERIFIED (22.1 owns the gate).** "Credit documents must be no more than four months old on the note date"; for consecutive statements "the date of the most recent statement must be no more than four months old on the note date."

**Reserves — B3-4.1-01, Minimum Reserve Requirements (08/07/2024). VERIFIED.** "Liquid financial reserves are those liquid or near liquid assets that are available to a borrower after the mortgage closes" — "checking or savings accounts; investments in stocks, bonds, mutual funds, certificates of deposit, money market funds, and trust accounts; the amount vested in a retirement savings account; and the cash value of a vested life insurance policy." Excluded: "Funds that have not been vested; funds that cannot be withdrawn under circumstances other than the account owner's retirement, employment termination, or death; stock held in an unlisted corporation; non-vested stock options and non-vested restricted stock; personal unsecured loans; rent-back credit; interested party contributions (IPCs); any amount of a lender contribution; and cash proceeds from a cash-out refinance transaction on the subject property." DU: "Two months' reserves for a second home transaction"; "Six months' reserves for the following: a two- to four-unit principal residence transaction, an investment property transaction, and a cash-out refinance transaction with a DTI ratio greater than 45%"; "There is no minimum reserve requirement for one-unit principal residence transactions." Multiple financed properties: "If the borrower owns other financed properties, additional reserves must be calculated and documented for financed properties other than the subject property and the borrower's principal residence" — "2% of the aggregate UPB if the borrower has one to four financed properties", "4% ... five to six", "6% ... seven to ten financed properties (DU only)." "Eligible gift funds (but not gifts of equity) may be used to satisfy reserve requirements."

**Gifts — B3-4.3-04, Personal Gifts (02/04/2026). VERIFIED.** Acceptable donors: a relative — "spouse, child, or other dependent, or by any other individual who is related to the borrower by blood, marriage, adoption, or legal guardianship" — or a non-relative with a familial relationship: "domestic partner (or relative of the domestic partner), individual engaged to marry the borrower, former relative, or an individual with a long-standing familial-like or mentorship relationship." "The donor may not be, or have any affiliation with, the builder, the developer, the real estate agent, or any other interested party to the transaction" **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — returned by one extraction pass and not the other; the sentence is long-standing Guide text]**. "Gift funds may fund all or part of the down payment, closing costs, or financial reserves subject to the minimum borrower contribution requirements below." Minimum borrower contribution table: LTV/CLTV/HCLTV 80% or less, 1–4 unit principal residence or second home — "A minimum borrower contribution from the borrower's own funds is not required. All funds needed to complete the transaction can come from a gift."; greater than 80%, one-unit principal residence — same sentence; greater than 80%, two- to four-unit principal residence or second home — "The borrower must make a 5% minimum borrower contribution from their own funds." (HomeReady: B5-6-02.) Gift letter: "specify the actual or the maximum dollar amount of the gift"; "include the donor's statement that no repayment is expected"; "indicate the donor's name, address, telephone number, and relationship to the borrower." (The pre-2026 bullet "the date the funds were transferred" is no longer in the list.) Pooled funds with a donor who lives with the borrower: "A certification from the donor stating that they have lived with the borrower for the past 12 months and will continue to do so in the new residence" and "Documents that demonstrate a history of borrower and donor shared residency." Transfer evidence: "a copy of the donor's check and the borrower's deposit slip, a copy of the donor's withdrawal slip and the borrower's deposit slip, evidence of the electronic transfer of funds from the donor's account to the borrower's account or to the closing agent; a copy of the donor's check to the closing agent, or a settlement statement showing receipt of the donor's check." Not transferred before settlement: "the lender must document that the donor gave the closing agent the gift funds in the form of an electronic transfer, certified check, a cashier's check, or other official check." "Gifts are not allowed on an investment property." **Gifts of equity — B3-4.3-05 (10/07/2020). VERIFIED.** Principal residence and second home purchases; may fund down payment and closing costs "but cannot be used towards financial reserves"; donor and minimum-contribution rules follow B3-4.3-04; "the gift of equity is not subject to Fannie Mae's interested party contribution requirements"; file must contain "a signed gift letter" and "the settlement statement listing the gift of equity."

**Grants and lender contributions — B3-4.3-06 (05/07/2025). VERIFIED.** A grant is "funds donated from acceptable entities for all or part of the down payment, closing costs, or financial reserves" (government agencies, housing finance agencies, "a nonprofit organization exempt from taxation under Section 501(c)(3)", Federal Home Loan Banks, employers, Indian tribes, lenders) — never "the property seller or other interested party in the transaction." Lender contributions "provided ... to fund borrower-paid closing costs and prepaid fees" — "may not be used to fund any portion of the down payment or financial reserve requirements"; premium-pricing lender credits are a different thing ("The lender credit is derived from premium pricing"); when the lender is an interested party "any amount of a lender contribution ... must be considered as an IPC when calculating the maximum financing concessions." Documentation: "a copy of the letter awarding the grant to the borrower or a copy of the legal agreement" plus "a copy of the donor's canceled check, a copy of the settlement statement showing receipt of the check, or similar evidence." **Lender incentives — B3-4.1-03, Lender Incentives (07/02/2025). VERIFIED.** An incentive is "cash, a cash-like gift (for example, a gift card), or other item of value that is not a lender credit toward the loan transaction"; when "the lender is, or is affiliated with, an interested party to the transaction, the incentive must be treated as a sales concession."

**EMD — B3-4.3-09, Earnest Money Deposit (05/04/2022). VERIFIED.** Verify with "a copy of the borrower's canceled check or a written statement from the holder of the deposit"; "Bank statements must evidence that the average balance for the past two months was large enough to support the amount of the deposit"; "If the deposit is being used as part of the borrower's minimum contribution requirement, the lender must verify that the funds are from an acceptable source"; "Large earnest money deposits and deposits that exceed the amount customary for the area should be closely evaluated."

**Sale of personal assets — B3-4.3-18 (12/16/2020). VERIFIED.** Document "The borrower's ownership of the asset for all asset types that are titled assets, for example automobile title"; "The value of the asset, as determined by an independent and reputable source, if the proceeds represent more than 50% of the total monthly income used in qualifying"; "The transfer of ownership of the asset, as documented by either a bill of sale or a statement from the purchaser"; receipt via "deposit slips, bank statements, copies of the purchaser's canceled check or an equivalent payment source." "The lender must use the lesser of the estimated value (as determined by the independent source) or actual sales price when determining the amount of funds for the transaction."

**Retirement, securities, secured borrowed funds, anticipated sales proceeds, cash on hand.** B3-4.3-03 (06/30/2015). VERIFIED: "Vested funds from individual retirement accounts (IRA/SEP/Keogh accounts) and tax-favored retirement savings accounts (401(k) accounts)" are acceptable; the lender must "confirm that the account is vested and allows withdrawals regardless of current employment status"; when used for closing the lender documents receipt; for reserves "Fannie Mae does not require the funds to be withdrawn from the account(s)." B3-4.3-01 (06/30/2015). VERIFIED: "either the most recent monthly or quarterly statement from the depository or investment firm; or a copy of the stock certificate, accompanied by a newspaper stock list"; when used for closing and the value exceeds the amount needed by 20% or more, no liquidation evidence is needed, otherwise "evidence of the borrower's actual receipt of funds realized from the sale or liquidation must be documented"; for reserves "100% of the value of the assets (as determined above) may be considered, and liquidation is not required"; "Non-vested stock options are not an acceptable source of funds for the down payment, closing costs, or reserves." B3-4.3-15 (10/30/2009). VERIFIED: loans secured by "automobiles, artwork, collectibles, real estate, or financial assets, such as savings accounts, certificates of deposit, stocks, bonds, and 401(k) accounts"; verify "the terms of the secured loan", "evidence that the party providing the secured loan is not a party to the sale", "evidence that the funds have been transferred to the borrower"; "When loans are secured by the borrower's financial assets, monthly payments for the loan do not have to be considered as long-term debt"; "If the borrower uses the same financial asset as part of their financial reserves, the lender must reduce the value of the asset by the amount of proceeds and related fees for the secured loan." B3-4.3-10 (02/23/2016). VERIFIED: "Sales Price – (Sales Costs + All Liens) = Estimated Proceeds" when a price exists, "90% of Listing Price – All Liens = Estimated Proceeds" otherwise; "If the proceeds from the sale of a currently owned home are needed for the down payment and closing costs on the new house, the lender must verify the source of funds by obtaining a copy of the settlement statement on the existing home before, or simultaneously with, the settlement on the new home"; employer buyouts require the executed buy-out agreement. B3-4.3-20 (04/01/2009). VERIFIED: "Cash-on-hand is not an acceptable source of funds for the down payment or closing costs"; "For HomeReady mortgages, cash-on-hand may be considered an acceptable source of funds for the down payment and closing cost" (conditions in B5-6 — **[UNVERIFIED — not fetched]**). Personal unsecured loans are excluded from reserves (B3-4.1-01) **[B3-4.3-17 not fetched]**.

**Virtual currency — B3-4.1-04 (05/04/2022). VERIFIED (topic) / PARTIALLY VERIFIED (2025–26 status).** Virtual currency "such as cryptocurrencies" may fund the down payment, closing costs and reserves only with "documented evidence that the virtual currency has been exchanged into U.S. dollars", the funds "held in a U.S. or state regulated financial institution" and "verified in U.S. dollars prior to the loan closing"; large deposits from an exchange require documentation that the funds came from the borrower's virtual-currency account; "Virtual currency may not be used for the deposit on the sales contract (earnest money) for the purchase of the subject property"; income paid in virtual currency is not usable. The FHFA directive of June 25, 2025 (ordering the Enterprises to prepare proposals to count cryptocurrency held on U.S.-regulated centralized exchanges as reserves without conversion) is reported only by secondary sources (Consumer Finance Monitor, Aug 14, 2025; CryptoTimes, Mar 26, 2026); **no Selling Guide announcement, lender letter or topic change was found on singlefamily.fanniemae.com or in the Sept 2, 2026 Guide edition** — the conversion rule stands.

**IPCs — B3-4.1-02, Interested Party Contributions (05/07/2025). VERIFIED (extends 00a-fnma §4.8).** Interested parties: "the property seller, the builder or developer, the real estate agent or broker, any affiliate of the above, or any party that can benefit from the sale of the property at the highest price and influence the sales price or real estate transaction." Financing concessions are "financial contributions towards the loan transaction from interested parties" acceptable toward "Borrower closing costs, including prepaids; or Borrower homeowners' association (HOA) assessments covering any period after the settlement date (limited to no more than 12 months)"; "Typical fees and/or closing costs paid by a seller in accordance with local custom, known as common and customary fees or costs, are not subject to Fannie Mae maximum financing concessions." Sales concessions: "IPCs that take the form of non-realty items ... contributions such as cash/cash-like gifts; rebates, such as those from real estate agents or brokers, which are not credited towards the transaction; furniture, automobiles, decorator allowances, moving costs, and other giveaways; lender incentives" — "Sales concessions must be deducted from the property's sales price and the lower of the reduced sales price or appraised value must be used to calculate LTV/CLTV ratios." Limits (principal residence or second home): CLTV greater than 90% → 3%; 75.01–90% → 6%; 75% or less → 9%; investment property, all CLTV → 2%, "calculated using the lower of the sales price or appraised value (not the loan [amount])"; "Financing concessions that exceed the below limits are considered sales concessions and" must be deducted with LTV/CLTV recalculated. "If a temporary or permanent interest rate buydown is being offered to the borrower, and the subsidy is funded by an interested party or a lender affiliated with one, the cost of that subsidy must be included in the IPC calculation." "Mortgages with undisclosed IPCs are not eligible for sale to Fannie Mae. Examples ... moving expenses, payment of various fees on the borrower's behalf, 'silent' second mortgages held by the property seller, and other contributions that are given to the borrower outside of closing and are not disclosed on the settlement statement." "Loans with any type of payment abatement are not eligible for sale to Fannie Mae, even if they are disclosed on the settlement statement."

B2-1.2-03, HCLTV (02/23/2016). B5-5.1-02, Community Seconds Loan Eligibility (05/03/2023). VERIFIED: providers — "a federal agency, state, county, or similar political subdivision of a state", "a nonprofit organization exempt from taxation under Section 501(c)(3)", employers, Federal Home Loan Banks; "A Community Seconds provider must not be the property seller or other interested party"; "The maximum CLTV is 105% with a Community Seconds loan, unless the first mortgage has an independent CLTV cap"; terms may be "fully amortizing, equal monthly payments", deferred, or "deferred over the entire term"; "When repayment is deferred for five years or more, a lender is not required to include" the payment in the DTI (22.5); funds may cover "all or part of the down payment", "closing costs", renovations or "a permanent interest rate buydown"; "A minimum borrower contribution from the borrower's own funds is not required" at LTV ≤ 80% on 1–4 units; deliver "SFC 118."

**LCOR cash back — B2-1.3-02, Limited Cash-Out Refinance Transactions (10/08/2025). VERIFIED.** An LCOR may include "paying off the existing first mortgage", "financing the payment of closing costs, points, and prepaid items", "paying off a subordinate mortgage lien (including prepayment penalties) used to purchase the subject property", and "providing cash back to the borrower (or any other party) in an amount that, in aggregate, does not exceed the greater of 1% of the new refinance loan amount or $2,000"; paying off "a subordinate lien that was not used to purchase the property" makes the loan a cash-out refinance (PACE/energy exceptions); excess proceeds "may also be applied as a principal balance curtailment in accordance with B2-1.5-05."

**DU validation service (assets) — B3-2-02 (02/05/2025). VERIFIED.** Validatable: "Checking, Savings, Certificates of Deposit, Stocks, Money Market, Mutual funds, Retirement." Report content: "30 days of account activity for refinance transactions," "60 days of account activity for purchase transactions," "The most recent quarter, if account information is reported on a quarterly basis"; for income/employment validation from asset data "the lender must order an asset verification report with a minimum of 12 months of data from an authorized vendor" (also the basis of DU's cash-flow assessment and positive rent history — 22.2/22.3). "When a large deposit needs to be documented, DU will issue a message specifying the amount of the large deposit, as well as the institution name and account number ... If no message is issued by DU, then no documentation of any large deposit appearing on the asset report is required when assets have been validated." "When the DU validation service validates assets on transactions with gift funds, the lender must ensure that the gift funds that appear as deposits on the asset verification report (if any) meet the requirements in B3-4.3-04." Borrower authorization is required; relief per A2-2-04. Suppliers (00b-orig F1, page dated 2026-09-10): AccountChek, Blend Labs, Finicity, FinLocker, Plaid, PointServ, Truv (supplemental reports from the starred vendors); "The report provider (not the distributor) sends the report data electronically to the DU validation service."

**Cash to close on the LE/CD — Reg Z §1026.37(h) and §1026.38(i) (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026; owned by 21.2/25.2). PARTIALLY VERIFIED here (text not re-read in this process).** The CD's "Calculating Cash to Close" table (total closing costs, closing costs paid before closing, closing costs financed, down payment/funds from borrower, deposit, funds for borrower, seller credits, adjustments and other credits, cash to close) is the borrower-facing figure; 25.2's `cd_figure_sources`/`cd_consistency_checks` are the reconciliation targets.

**DU tolerances (referenced) — B3-2-10 (12/04/2019), 23.1.** Verified reserves below 90% of the findings' requirement require resubmission (`B3_2_10_RESERVES_90PCT`); purchase loan-amount changes always require resubmission.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. **"2 months statements" is the purchase rule.** Refinances need "the most recent full one-month period" (B3-4.2-01) / "one monthly statement (30 days of account activity)" (B3-4.4-02).
2. **DU imposes a statement-date rule the row omits:** monthly statements "dated within 45 days of the initial loan application date" (quarterly: 90 days) — a not-before gate anchored to the application date that runs alongside B1-1-03's four-months-on-the-note-date rule.
3. **The large-deposit rule is purchase-only, single-deposit, unsourced-portion-only**, waived when the source is printed on the statement, and automated by DU when assets are validated (no DU message → no documentation).
4. **B3-4.1-01 is "Minimum Reserve Requirements"**, not minimum borrower contribution; the minimum-contribution table lives in **B3-4.3-04** (and B5-6-02 for HomeReady). The row also misses the multiple-financed-property reserve tiers (2%/4%/6%) and the exclusions (IPCs, lender contributions, cash-out proceeds, unsecured loans, rent-back credit).
5. **Virtual currency is B3-4.1-04**, still requires conversion to U.S. dollars and is barred as EMD; the June 25, 2025 FHFA directive has produced **no Guide change** as of the Sept 2, 2026 edition.
6. **IPC limits are computed on the lower of sales price or appraised value**, buydown subsidies funded by an interested party count, excess is a sales concession that lowers the price and re-rates LTV/MI, and payment abatements make the loan ineligible outright.
7. **Community Seconds allow CLTV up to 105%** (B5-5.1-02) — the row's "CLTV/HCLTV" needs the exception; HCLTV is computed only when a HELOC exists.
8. **LCOR cash back is now "the greater of 1% of the new refinance loan amount or $2,000"** (B2-1.3-02, 10/08/2025) — resolves 23.1's "[UNVERIFIED]" cap; the cash-to-close reconciliation must enforce it.
9. **Gifts:** the gift letter no longer lists "the date the funds were transferred" (02/04/2026 text); eligible donors now include long-standing familial-like/mentorship relationships; gifts may fund reserves but gifts of equity may not; gifts are barred on investment properties; 5% own-funds contribution applies only to 2–4 unit principal residences and second homes above 80%.
10. **Cash-to-close reconciliation with the CD is a Reg Z accuracy matter** (§1026.38(i)), not a Selling Guide one — the row lists no federal source.

#### State machine

Per asset: `declared` → `documentation_requested` (22.1 needs list or DU asset report ordered) → `documented` → (`verified` | `rejected{unverified_funds | owner_mismatch | ineligible_source}`) → `sourced` (all large deposits resolved or not applicable) → `usable` (usable_cents computed) → `finalized` (snapshot for the final DU submission) → (`reverified` on closing move or new statement | `withdrawn`). Guards: `verified` requires owner match, statement standards (B3-4.2-01), DU 45/90-day rule (purchase/refinance count), and 22.1 freshness ≥ scheduled note date; `usable` requires offsets applied (EMD, secured loan, unsourced deposits) and `usable_for` consistent with the use. Gift: `declared` → `letter_received` → `transfer_verified` → `complete` | `rejected` (interested-party donor, missing no-repayment statement, investment property). Worksheet: `computed{stage}` → `reconciled` (CD match to the cent) | `variance` (→ 25.2 corrected CD or figure correction) → `final` at `closing.consummated`. Terminal for the process: `assets.finalized` and a `final` worksheet whose `sufficient = true` and `cash_back_ok = true`; reopened only by a closing move, a CD version, a fee/payoff change or a new asset fact.

#### Business rules and calculations

**R1 — Statement count and dating.** `required_statements = 2 (purchase) | 1 (refinance)` consecutive monthly statements, or the most recent quarter; `du_45d_ok = period_end_of_most_recent ≥ application_date − 45 days` (quarterly: 90 days); freshness `expires_at = add_months(period_end_of_most_recent, 4) ≥ scheduled_note_date` (22.1 R1/R2). *Refinance fixture:* application Mon Oct 5, 2026 → statement dated on/after **Fri Aug 21, 2026**; note date Fri Nov 6 → also dated on/after Mon Jul 6 (moot). A September statement (Sept 1–30, 2026) passes both; an August statement (Aug 1–31) passes (Aug 31 ≥ Aug 21); a July statement fails the 45-day rule even though it passes the four-month rule. *Purchase fixture:* application Mon Oct 19, 2026 → floor **Fri Sept 4, 2026**; the required pair is August + September (Sept 30 ≥ Sept 4) or September + October. If the closing slips to Fri Dec 18, 2026, the four-month rule requires the most recent statement dated on/after Tue Aug 18, 2026 — the September statement still passes; the 45-day rule never re-bases because it is anchored to the initial application date.

**R2 — Owner and source standards.** `owner_match` requires a borrower among the holders (joint accounts: full balance usable when a borrower is a holder and the co-holder is not an interested party; a non-borrower co-holder's written access statement is a policy add-on — Q2). Internet/faxed statements must show the institution name and source banner (22.1 R5); Form 1006 must come directly from the depository.

**R3 — Large deposits (purchase only).** `threshold_cents = floor(total_monthly_qualifying_income_cents × 50 / 100)`; for each deposit, `unsourced_cents = amount − sourced`; `large_deposit = unsourced_cents > threshold_cents` (strictly greater, per "exceeds"); readily identifiable sources printed on the statement are `sourced` without further evidence; when funds from a large deposit are needed for closing or reserves, `usable_cents` of the account is reduced by `unsourced_cents` until sourced. DU-validated accounts: only deposits named in a DU message are tested. Refinances: `status = waived_refinance`, but a deposit that evidences a new loan opens an `application_liabilities` row (22.5). *Worked example (purchase fixture, Borrower A's checking, Sept 1–30, 2026 statement):* deposit **$9,000.00 on Mon Sept 14, 2026**; total monthly qualifying income $8,200.00 (23.2's HomeReady figure) → threshold $4,100.00 → large. (With the $7,500.00 income variant in the brief the threshold is $3,750.00 — same conclusion.) Sourcing: $6,500.00 is a transfer from the borrower's verified brokerage account and the statement prints "TRANSFER FROM SCHWAB …7781" → readily identifiable, sourced; $2,500.00 is the sale of a motorcycle — title copy, bill of sale dated Sept 12, purchaser's check image and the deposit line → sourced (value per B3-4.3-18: $2,500.00 < 50% of income, so no independent valuation is needed). `unsourced_cents = 0` → not a large deposit after sourcing. Had the $2,500.00 remained unexplained, `unsourced = 250,000 cents ≤ 410,000` → not large → no reduction. Had the whole $9,000.00 been unexplained → `usable_cents` of the account falls by $9,000.00 and the sufficiency test in R8 re-runs.

**R4 — Usable amount per asset.** `usable_cents = max(0, verified_balance − secured_loan_offset − emd_offset − unsourced_deposit_offset) × (10000 − haircut_bps) / 10000` (integer cents, floor). `emd_offset` applies when the EMD check cleared after the statement's period end (the balance still contains the funds the CD will credit as "Deposit"). Securities and vested retirement: haircut 0 (B3-4.3-01 "100% of the value"; B3-4.3-03 vested balance; no withdrawal required for reserves); non-vested options/restricted stock: `usable = 0`; unlisted-corporation stock: reserves-ineligible; lender contribution/gift of equity/IPC: `usable_for = closing`; cash-out proceeds: reserves-ineligible; anticipated sales proceeds: `usable = price − (sales_costs + liens)` or `0.9 × listing − liens` until the settlement statement replaces it; secured borrowed funds: usable in full with the payment excluded from DTI only when secured by a financial asset (B3-4.3-15); unsecured loans: never.

**R5 — Gifts.** Gift usable only when `kind`, donor relationship and `donor_interested_party_check = clear`; `amount_usable = min(amount_stated (or maximum), transfer_amount_verified)`; if not yet transferred, the closing instruction (26.1) requires an official check/wire from the donor and the settlement statement must show it (gate `FNMA_B3_4_3_04_GIFT_TRANSFER_GATE`). Minimum own-funds contribution: `own_funds_required = 5% × price` only for 2–4 unit principal residences and second homes with LTV/CLTV/HCLTV > 80%; else 0. Investment property → gift rejected. Gift of equity: `usable_for = closing`, recorded as a seller credit on the CD, excluded from the IPC test (B3-4.3-05). *Worked example (purchase fixture):* gift **$10,000.00** from Borrower A's mother (relationship `parent`), letter states $10,000.00 maximum and "no repayment is expected", donor name/address/phone present; wire from the donor's account to the borrower's checking on Mon Oct 26, 2026 evidenced by the donor's wire confirmation and the borrower's October statement line → `transfer_verified`; 1-unit principal residence at 90% LTV → no minimum own-funds contribution.

**R6 — Reserves.** `reserves_required = max(du_findings.reserves_required, formula)` where `formula = months × qualifying_PITIA` with months = 0 (1-unit principal), 2 (second home), 6 (2–4 unit principal, investment, cash-out with DTI > 45%), plus `other_financed_reserves = pct × Σ UPB of financed properties other than the subject and the principal residence` (pct = 2% for 1–4 financed properties, 4% for 5–6, 6% for 7–10), plus 22.3's employment-offer or 22.2's nontraditional-credit requirements when applicable. Only assets with `usable_for ∈ {reserves, both}` count; gift funds count, gifts of equity/IPCs/lender contributions/cash-out proceeds do not. *Worked example (purchase fixture):* 1-unit principal residence → formula 0; DU findings "Reserves Required to be Verified" **$8,000.00** (23.1's example) → required $8,000.00. Variant: the borrowers also own two financed rentals with UPBs $250,000.00 and $160,000.00 (aggregate $410,000.00; three financed properties including the subject) → 2% × $410,000.00 = **$8,200.00** additional → required $16,200.00. 23.1's 90% tolerance: verified reserves of $14,580.00 (exactly 90.00%) do not force a resubmission, but `SM_RESERVES_VERIFIED_GATE` still requires the final findings' figure to be met at closing.

**R7 — IPCs.** `ipc_base = min(sales_price, appraised_value)`; band by CLTV (principal residence/second home: > 90% → 3%; 75.01–90% → 6%; ≤ 75% → 9%; investment → 2%); `max_financing_concessions = floor(ipc_base × pct / 100)` in cents; `financing_concessions = Σ ipc_items{kind ∈ financing_concession, buydown_subsidy (interested-party funded), hoa_prepaid ≤ 12 months}` (common-and-customary seller fees excluded); `excess = max(0, financing_concessions − max)` → `sales_concession`; `adjusted_price = sales_price − Σ sales concessions`; LTV/CLTV recomputed on `min(adjusted_price, appraised)`; the band is re-evaluated on the new CLTV until stable (at most two iterations because the band can only tighten). *Worked example (purchase fixture):* price $457,800.00, appraised $460,000.00 → base $457,800.00; CLTV 90.00% → 6% band → max **$27,468.00**. Seller pays $5,000.00 of closing costs and funds a 2-1 temporary buydown costing $8,640.00 (illustrative) → $13,640.00 ≤ $27,468.00 ✓. Variant: seller credit $30,000.00 → excess $2,532.00 → sales concession → adjusted price $455,268.00 → LTV = 412,000 ÷ 455,268 = 90.496% → 24.6 rounds to 91% → MI coverage 30% (B7-1-02) and the CLTV band becomes > 90% → 3% → max $13,658.04 → the $30,000.00 credit is now $16,341.96 over → iterate: the agent presents the borrower/seller with the compliant structure (credit capped at the band maximum, price re-negotiated, or buydown removed) and records the outcome; 21.5 issues a revised LE; 23.1 resubmits.

**R8 — Funds-to-close worksheet and CD reconciliation.** Purchase: `cash_to_close = down_payment + total_closing_costs − costs_paid_before_closing − costs_financed − emd − seller_credits − lender_credit − gifts_paid_at_closing − grants/community_second_at_closing − other_credits` (sign conventions per §1026.38(i)); `funds_to_verify = max(cash_to_close, 0) + reserves_required`; `sufficient = Σ usable_closing ≥ max(cash_to_close,0) AND Σ usable_reserves_after_closing ≥ reserves_required`, where `usable_reserves_after_closing = Σ usable(both) − max(cash_to_close,0) + Σ usable(reserves only)`. Reconciliation: `reconciled_to_cd = (worksheet.cash_to_close_cents == cd.cash_to_close_cents)`; any variance is itemized by CD line and routed to 25.2 (figure correction or corrected CD) — the worksheet never overrides the CD. *Worked example 1 (purchase fixture, CD v1 prepared Thu Nov 12, 2026 for consummation Wed Nov 18):* price $457,800.00; loan $412,000.00; down payment **$45,800.00**; total closing costs (J) $8,712.00 (illustrative: loan costs $1,960.00 + other costs $6,752.00 including 13 days' prepaid interest $935.47 = $412,000 × 0.06375 ÷ 365 × 13, rounded half-up; homeowner's premium $1,380.00; initial escrow deposit; title and recording); paid before closing $0.00; EMD **$5,000.00** (check dated Fri Oct 16, cleared Tue Oct 20 — after the Sept 30 statement); seller credit $5,000.00; lender credit $515.00 (20.4's 12.5 bps cap); gift $10,000.00 already in the borrower's account (not a CD credit). `cash_to_close = 45,800.00 + 8,712.00 − 5,000.00 − 5,000.00 − 515.00 = $43,997.00` → CD line "Cash to Close $43,997.00" must match. Verified assets: checking $31,240.18 (Sept 30 balance; large deposit sourced per R3) − `emd_offset` $5,000.00 = $26,240.18 usable; savings $14,900.00; October statement shows the $10,000.00 gift → +$10,000.00; vested 401(k) $38,000.00 (`both`; no withdrawal required for reserves). Usable for closing = 26,240.18 + 14,900.00 + 10,000.00 = **$51,140.18 ≥ $43,997.00** ✓; reserves after closing = 51,140.18 − 43,997.00 + 38,000.00 = **$45,143.18 ≥ $8,000.00** ✓ → `sufficient = true`. Without the gift: $41,140.18 < $43,997.00 → shortfall **$2,856.82** → needs-list item (additional verified funds or gift) before CD issuance; if the $9,000.00 deposit were wholly unsourced: 51,140.18 − 9,000.00 = $42,140.18 → shortfall $1,856.82.

**R9 — LCOR cash back (refinance).** `cash_to_borrower = loan_amount − payoffs − total_closing_costs + credits` (the CD "Payoffs and Payments" alternative table, §1026.38(t)(5)(vii)(B), 25.2); `cap = max(floor(loan_amount × 1 / 100), 200,000 cents)`; `cash_back_ok = cash_to_borrower ≤ cap`; cures: principal curtailment at closing `≥ cash_to_borrower − cap` (B2-1.5-05; recorded in `principal_curtailment_cents` and on the CD) or a loan-amount reduction (23.1: decrease ≤ 5% is within tolerance; 21.5 revised LE if before CD). *Worked example 2 (refinance fixture, CD v1 Mon Nov 2, 2026, consummation Fri Nov 6, disbursement Thu Nov 12):* loan $560,000.00; payoff of the existing first mortgage per 24.4's demand (good through Fri Nov 13, 2026) **$547,912.40** (illustrative; includes per-diem to the good-through date); total closing costs $6,318.00 (illustrative: loan costs $1,720.00; title/recording $1,978.00; prepaid interest Nov 12–30 = 19 days × $93.97 = $1,785.48 ($560,000 × 0.06125 ÷ 365 = $93.9726 per diem, rounded at the total); initial escrow deposit $834.52 for taxes $500.00/month and insurance $120.00/month per 30.3's aggregate analysis); cap = max($5,600.00, $2,000.00) = **$5,600.00**; `cash_to_borrower = 560,000.00 − 547,912.40 − 6,318.00 = $5,769.60` → **exceeds the cap by $169.60** → `lcor.cash_back.exceeded`; cure A: principal curtailment of $169.60 at closing (cash to borrower $5,600.00, UPB after funding $559,830.40 — 26.3/30.2 board the curtailed balance and 29.3 delivers the post-curtailment UPB); cure B: reduce the loan to $559,830.00 (−$170.00 = 0.03%, within 23.1's 5% decrease tolerance; P&I falls to $3,401.59; requires a corrected CD but no new waiting period unless the APR test fails — 25.2). Refinance assets: DU "Funds Required to Close" $0.00 and no reserves for a 1-unit principal LCOR → no asset verification is required unless DU messages ask for it; large deposits are not tested (`waived_refinance`); the borrower's one-month statement (Sept 1–30, 2026) is still collected because the intake declared checking assets and 23.4's ATR record requires the assets relied on.

**R10 — Subordinate financing in the worksheet.** New DPA/Community Seconds: `community_second_cents` is a credit at closing only when the provider is eligible (B5-5.1-02) and the note is executed at settlement; CLTV = (first-lien amount + drawn HELOC + closed-end UPB) ÷ min(price, appraised); Community Seconds CLTV ≤ 105% unless the first mortgage has an independent cap; HCLTV computed only when a HELOC exists (full line amount). Existing liens on a refinance: 24.4's `subordinations.terms_ok` and resubordination status feed the worksheet (`payoffs` vs "remains"); a subordinate lien discovered after the decision triggers re-underwriting (B2-1.2-02; 23.1 resubmission). *Worked example (purchase fixture variant):* Ohio Housing Finance Agency DPA second $13,734.00 (3% of $457,800.00, deferred, forgivable — illustrative program) → CLTV = (412,000 + 13,734) ÷ 457,800 = 92.998% ≤ 105% ✓; the deferred payment is excluded from DTI when repayment is deferred five years or more (22.5); SFC 118; the IPC band is keyed to CLTV 92.998% → > 90% → 3% → max financing concessions $13,734.00 — the seller credit of $5,000.00 is within it.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`verification` agent, 22.4 tool set: `declareAssets`, `orderAssetReport`, `parseStatement`, `evaluateDeposits`, `requestSource`, `verifyGift`, `checkDonorInterestedParty`, `recordIpc`, `testIpcLimits`, `computeReserves`, `buildFundsToCloseWorksheet`, `reconcileToCd`, `testLcorCashBack`, `proposeCure`, `finalizeAssets`, `writeDecision`. End-to-end behavior: on `application.received` it orders DU asset reports (with authorization) for every declared depository account, opens needs-list items for non-aggregatable assets, builds the first worksheet from the LE figures, and thereafter recomputes on every fee, payoff, contract, valuation or DU change; it sources large deposits by first reading the statement for printed sources, then asking the borrower a single structured question per deposit in the portal; it never treats a borrower's explanation as sufficient without the evidence the Guide names; it reconciles every CD version to the cent and hands variances to 25.2 with the line-by-line diff. Decision record `{application_id, worksheet_version, assets[] with usable_cents and offsets, deposits[] with sourcing evidence ids, gifts[], ipcs[] with band arithmetic, reserves calc, cd_reconciliation, rule_set_version, model_version, rationale, confidence}`. Guardrails: never accept unverified funds, cash-on-hand outside HomeReady, unsecured loans, gifts from interested parties or on investment properties, or virtual currency without dollar conversion; never change a figure to make the worksheet balance — every change traces to a document, vendor report, fee item or borrower-requested change; never contact a donor or purchaser outside the documented evidence path. Escalations: `underwriting_reviewer` when insufficiency or an ineligible source would drive a denial/counteroffer/NOIA (21.6) and for any IPC restructuring that changes terms (counteroffer test, 23.2-Q3); `fraud-risk` (22.6) for suspected undisclosed IPCs/silent seconds, donor–seller affiliation, fabricated statements (22.1 integrity failures), or deposits tied to a party to the sale; `human_agent` on request; `licensed_specialist` per `jurisdiction_rules`. Borrower-facing rules: every request states the Guide reason in plain language, the partner is named as lender, AI use is disclosed, and no request asks for more than the rule requires (§1002.5/UDAAP; 31.2 monitoring of documentation burden). Human path: ops console shows the same worksheet with manual overrides logged as `agent_decisions.override`.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Statement period gaps or a missing page → `documented` blocked; the extractor's running-balance check (22.1) must reconcile before `verified`.
- Account opened within 90 days of application with a Form 1006 only → source-of-funds verification for the opening deposit (B3-4.2-02).
- Quarterly statement accounts → 90-day rule and the transfer-check sentence (funds not moved to a more-currently-verified account) enforced by cross-account transfer matching.
- EMD paid from an account not in the file → the EMD's source account must be verified (average two-month balance supports the deposit); EMD paid by a third party → gift rules apply; EMD in virtual currency → ineligible.
- Gift wired directly to the settlement agent → transfer evidence is the settlement statement; the closing instruction names the donor and requires official funds.
- Donor is the listing agent's spouse → `donor_interested_party_check = match` → gift rejected; 22.6 reviews for an undisclosed IPC.
- Seller-paid "moving allowance" outside closing → undisclosed IPC → loan ineligible until restructured and disclosed; payment abatement of any kind → ineligible.
- Business account used by a self-employed borrower → 22.3's written evaluation must state that the withdrawal does not impair the business; if not, the funds are excluded.
- Retirement account with a hardship-only withdrawal restriction → not usable for closing; usable for reserves only if vested and withdrawable on termination/retirement/death (B3-4.1-01 exclusion otherwise).
- Assets validated by DU but the borrower later moves funds → supplemental report or new statements; DU resubmission (23.1) because validation data changed.
- Cash-out refinance with DTI > 45% → six months' PITIA reserves; cash-out proceeds cannot be counted as reserves.
- Departing residence sale falls through before closing → proceeds asset withdrawn; bridge loan (DTI impact in 22.5) or additional verified funds; otherwise reschedule.
- DPA program with a shared-appreciation feature → must qualify as a Community Seconds; otherwise ineligible (B2-1.2-04).
- CD cash-to-close differs from the worksheet by cents due to rounding of prepaid interest → the CD figure governs; the worksheet adopts the CD's per-line rounding (25.2's convention) and re-reconciles.
- LCOR overage discovered at the closing table (payoff shortfall refund) → principal curtailment on the final CD; if the settlement agent disbursed the excess, 26.3 records the post-closing curtailment and 29.3 delivers the correct UPB.
- Fraudulent asset report (synthetic institution) → vendor supplier list check (only Fannie Mae-listed suppliers) and 22.6 review.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Reserve haircut policy for securities and retirement.** The Guide allows 100% of vested/market value; some lenders apply 70% overlays. Default: 0% haircut (Guide text), with a partner-configurable `haircut_bps` per asset type recorded in the decision.
2. **Joint accounts with a non-borrower co-holder.** The Guide requires only that a borrower be an account holder. Default: full balance usable; obtain a co-holder access statement only when the DU message or the statement suggests restricted access.
3. **DU asset report as the default evidence.** Default: order a DU asset report for every depository account before requesting statements (fewer borrower documents, automated large-deposit assessment, rep-and-warrant relief); statements only when the institution is not aggregatable.
4. **Cash-on-hand for HomeReady.** B5-6 conditions were not fetched. Default: disabled (`homeready.cash_on_hand=false`) until 23.2/22.4 verify B5-6-02's conditions.
5. **Cryptocurrency without conversion.** No Guide change found despite the FHFA directive. Default: conversion required (B3-4.1-04); re-check the Guide at each edition and hold a feature flag `assets.crypto_reserves_unconverted=false`.
6. **Who absorbs an LCOR overage cure.** Default: principal curtailment at closing (borrower keeps the cap amount); loan-amount reduction only when the overage is known before the CD.
7. **Common-and-customary seller fees by state.** The exclusion from IPC limits depends on local custom. Default: a `jurisdiction_rules.customary_seller_fees` list (owner's title policy in OH/AZ where customary, transfer taxes by custom) maintained with 24.4; anything not listed counts.
