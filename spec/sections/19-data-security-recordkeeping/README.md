# Section 19 — Data, Security & Recordkeeping

## Overview

Section 19 is the cross-cutting "Records & Security Program as code" that every other section leans on but none owns: what the platform keeps, for how long, for whom, under what protections, how it proves that, and how it handles the moment something goes wrong. It has four processes: **19.1 Records retention** (retention-class registry, lifecycle/legal-hold/purge engine, records-production service including the Reg X §1024.38(c)(2) five-day servicing file), **19.2 GLBA safeguards** (the information-security program mapped control-by-control to the FTC Safeguards Rule, Fannie Mae's Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement and 23 NYCRR Part 500, with control tests as scheduled jobs and an incident-response runbook whose 36-hour / 72-hour / 30-day / state clocks are Timer Engine definitions), **19.3 Fannie Mae data/technology-provider requirements** (the A2-1-01 ≥20,000-loan regime — 180-day change notice, 5-business-day contract-event notices, mandatory contract clauses — plus the Consolidated Technology Guide, Form 101, Form 582 disclosure inputs, and the vendor/AI-provider oversight registry that LL-2026-04 and the Supplement demand), and **19.4 Fair lending data elements** (the restricted store for race, ethnicity, age, gender and preferred language for loans originated on/after March 1, 2023, its access controls, its transfer on servicing transfers, and the fair-servicing monitoring pipeline including bias testing of AI agent decisions).

In the architecture baseline the section is executed by the `security-records` agent (records requests, retention lifecycle, disposal attestations, incident triage, vendor assessments, fair-lending data custody) with `compliance-sentinel` (timers, rule-version watch, Guide/Lender Letter watch), `qc-audit` (control-test sampling and evidence review), `case` (NoE/RFI productions that consume the records service, Section 4), `boarding` (fair-lending data intake, 1.1), `transfer` (MISMO exports on transfer-out, 17.3) and every decisioning agent whose `agent_decisions` feed the fair-servicing monitor (`lossmit-underwriter`, `default-collections`, `cashiering`, `insurance-property`, `foreclosure-ops`, `borrower-comms`). Core services used: the `documents` store with SHA-256 hashes and WORM replication, append-only `loan_events`/`ledger_entries`/`agent_decisions`/`notices` tables, the Timer Engine, the Notice Registry (breach and regulatory notice templates), the integration framework (secure file transfer, print/mail, IdP/SIEM/scanner adapters) and the KMS-backed vault.

Partner/Supermortgage boundary. Records of Fannie Mae loans are **Fannie Mae's property**; the partner (master servicer of record) and Supermortgage both "hold these documents solely for the benefit of Fannie Mae" (Selling Guide A2-4.1-02, incorporated by Servicing Guide A2-5-01). Supermortgage is simultaneously (i) a Fannie Mae-approved subservicer with its own Supplement, Technology Guide, Form 101 and Form 582 obligations, (ii) the partner's "third-party technology provider for the performance of critical servicing functions" under A2-1-01, so the partner–Supermortgage agreement must carry the A2-1-01 clauses and both parties must be able to give Fannie Mae 180 days' notice of a provider change, (iii) an FTC-jurisdiction "financial institution" under the Safeguards Rule (servicing loans is "financial in nature," 12 CFR 225.28(b)(1); 16 CFR 314.1(b) covers customer information "of other financial institutions that have provided such information to you"), and (iv) once registered as a New York mortgage loan servicer, a Part 500 "covered entity." The partner signs Form 582 (with the Supplement attestation), receives Supermortgage's incident notices so it can meet its own 36-hour clock, and is the "Master Subscriber" on Form 101; Supermortgage prepares everything, runs the program, and signs its own attestations.

Conventions: Section 19 does not branch on remittance type (A/A, S/A, S/S) — its rules apply to every loan identically; the only delinquency counter it references is `regx_days_delinquent` (Reg X §1024.31 definition), used by the 19.4 monitoring metrics `FC_DAYS_120_TO_REFERRAL` (days from the 120th day of delinquency to `foreclosure.referral.sent`) and the delinquency-depth control variable; all rules cite the Servicing Guide edition of Aug. 12, 2026 and eCFR as of the verification dates given.

Dependencies: 1.1 (boarding populates `documents`, retention classes and the 19.4 restricted store from the transferor tape), 4.1/4.2 (NoE/RFI responses call the 19.1 records service and use the `records_inventory` table defined in 4.2), 7.4/7.5 (E-SIGN consent evidence and the Reg P privacy notice — privacy *notice* obligations stay in 7.5; 19.2 is the Safeguards *program*), 8.x (FCRA furnishing records, `fcra_furnishing_5y`), 11.4 (Reg F 3-year retention for loans boarded in default), 12.x (Reg B 25-month retention of loss-mitigation decisions and the decision records the fair-servicing monitor consumes), 13.x/14.x (litigation holds), 17.3 (transfer-out export of files and fair-lending data), 18.1 (QC plan consumes 19.2 control-test results), 18.2 (MORA productions), 18.4 (Form 582 — 19.3 supplies the technology-provider/subservicer disclosure and the Supplement attestation evidence), 18.7 (Selling Guide A4-1-01 material-change notices).

Changes in flight that shape the build (verified 2026-09-09 unless noted): Fannie Mae's Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement (current PDF dated Sept. 2, 2025; mandatory since Aug. 12, 2025 per SVC-2025-01/research/00a §6.2) with its 36-hour incident clock, annual independent assessment and officer attestation; **LL-2026-04** AI/ML governance (effective Aug. 6, 2026) with vendor flow-down and disclosure on request; the **Consolidated Technology Guide (Apr. 21, 2026)** — credentials only to employees, 90-day/365-day credential resets, no scraping, audit rights, return-or-destroy on termination, TLS cipher cutoff Oct. 23, 2026 (research/00b); the FTC Safeguards Rule's FTC-notification element (16 CFR 314.4(j), effective May 13, 2024); NYDFS Part 500's universal MFA and asset-inventory requirements (in force since Nov. 1, 2025); CFPB Circular 2022-04 (data security as UDAAP) **withdrawn May 12, 2025** — the statutory UDAAP theory survives but there is no Bureau guidance; HUD's proposed rescission of the Fair Housing Act discriminatory-effects rule (proposed Jan. 14, 2026; supplemental Aug. 10, 2026; **not final**; the statute still supports disparate-impact claims under *Inclusive Communities*); Colorado's AI Act in force June 30, 2026; N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §899-aa's 30-day consumer-notice deadline; and the CCPA's GLBA carve-out (Cal. Civ. Code §1798.145(e)) that does *not* extend to the §1798.150 breach private right of action.
