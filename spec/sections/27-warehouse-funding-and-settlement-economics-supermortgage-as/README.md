## Overview

**Scope.** Section 27 covers the money and the collateral between the settlement agent's wire and the partner's gain on sale: the facility under which SM funds the partner's closings and controls the notes until Fannie Mae pays (27.1 — advances, wet/dry limits, bailee letters, MERS Interim Funder and eRegistry Secured Party, UCC perfection, daily SOFR accrual, aging, curtailments, kick-outs and repurchases, borrowing base, covenants and the daily partner report), and the settlement that follows Fannie Mae's purchase (27.2 — the Whole Loan Purchase Advice, the three-way match of advice, bank receipt and advance, the same-day warehouse payoff and release of SM's interest, gain-on-sale computation with the expected-vs-actual proof of the borrower rate pass-through, the MSR data hand-off, the 12.5 bps platform-fee accrual, post-purchase adjustments and the GL export). Product scope is the inventory's: Fannie Mae conventional conforming first liens sold whole-loan for cash with servicing retained; MBS, servicing-released and non-Fannie executions are out of scope, so the facility funds only loans with a live PE–Whole Loan commitment. Everything is written to the Selling Guide edition of Sept 2, 2026, the Loan Delivery User Guide of May 2026, the Warehouse Lender User Guide of Sept 2025, MERS eRegistry Procedures Release 16.25 and MERS System Procedures Release 26.1 (May 17, 2026), the uniform UCC text (with the 2022 Article 12 exclusion of transferable records), 15 U.S.C. §7021, 11 U.S.C. §§101(47)/559 and Reg X as current on eCFR (Sept 8, 2026).

**Agents.** One agent, `warehouse`, owns both processes: it evaluates collateral eligibility from platform records (never re-underwriting), computes availability, prepares each advance wire, tracks the collateral chain (trust receipts, Secured Party, Interim Funder, Transfer of Control), accrues interest and fees, ages and curtails, reports daily, and on the purchase side matches proceeds, posts the waterfall, releases the collateral, computes the gain and the pass-through reconciliation, issues the MSR hand-off, accrues the platform fee and exports the GL. Every amount is deterministic code; the LLM drafts partner-facing narratives and classifies unstructured custodian and Fannie Mae correspondence. Humans appear only at the enumerated touchpoints: `funding_approver` (SM) on every outbound wire — advances and partner residuals — under dual control; `officer{party=sm}` for bailee-letter and Form 2004A signatures, covenant waivers, kick-outs, set-offs and write-offs; `officer{party=partner}` for repurchase funding, covenant certification, haircut-reserve funding and funds-transfer correction letters under its Form 360 authority; `signing_officer{party=partner}` for MERS/eRegistry transactions the Controller or Member must sign (Add Secured Party, Interim Funder); `fnma_portal_operator{party=sm}` in SM's warehouse-lender Loan Delivery org (wire approval, Bailee Letter Name) and `fnma_portal_operator{party=partner}` for LSDU post-purchase adjustments and Fannie Mae Connect fallbacks. The `funder` (26.3), `post-closing` (26.4) and `secondary` (29.x) agents feed and consume §27 events; `compliance-sentinel` receives the pass-through exceptions for 31.2.

**Partner/SM boundary.** The partner is the borrower under the facility (full recourse), the owner of every loan until Fannie Mae remits (Selling Guide C1-2-03), the MERS originator/Controller, the Fannie Mae seller whose Form 482 payee code names SM's collection account "to match the bailee letter on file," the seller of record on the purchase advice, the owner of the gain on sale and of the MSR, and Fannie Mae's counterparty for post-purchase adjustments. SM is the secured lender — never the owner — bailor under its own bailee letters, MERS Interim Funder (paper notes) and eRegistry Secured Party (eNotes), party to Fannie Mae's eNote Funding Agreement, bearer of third-party origination costs recovered from proceeds under the term sheet, subservicer accruing the platform fee, and operator of every ledger. The section's structural recommendation is a **loan-and-security agreement rather than a master repurchase agreement**: the partner funds each settlement with money it borrowed in its own name, so it remains the Reg X "lender" and no closing is "table funding"; SM's fulfillment agents act for the partner under the Services Agreement, and the `warehouse` agent's only loan-level act is an objective collateral-eligibility check — the two hats are separated by contract, information barrier and audit log. Every consumer-facing artifact continues to name the partner as creditor; nothing in §27 touches the borrower's terms, payee or servicer, so no consumer notice is generated here.

**Changes in flight that shape the build.** (1) MERS System Procedures Release 26.1 (May 17, 2026) supersedes the 24.2 cited in the servicing research — `mers.proc.26.1`; the eRegistry manual remains Release 16.25 and deletes the Secured Party automatically on Transfer of Control, which is why the Fannie Mae Funding Agreement, not the registry, protects SM between transfer and payment. (2) The 2022 UCC amendments (Article 12) are being adopted state by state and expressly exclude transferable records — eNote control stays with E-SIGN §7021/UETA §16 and the eRegistry; the section corrects the blueprint's UCC 9-105 citation. (3) Loan Delivery has no API (00b-orig F6) and SM's warehouse-lender approvals are a separate portal-only island with a 90-day sign-in rule. (4) Purchase advice data are available by daily JSON API (Sellers and Servicers products) but the field schema is login-gated — the design stores raw JSON and maps in the integration environment. (5) Fannie Mae's partial-month interest convention at purchase is not stated in the Selling Guide; the matcher computes both 30/360 and actual/365 and locks per partner on the first production advice. (6) LLPA Matrix 09.09.2026 (VantageScore 4.0 grids) means the Purchase Ready-date LLPA can differ from the lock-time forecast — a first-class variance category. (7) Fannie Mae's TLS cutover (Oct 23, 2026) and the Technology Guide's UI-automation ban apply to the Purchase Advice API adapter and the portal islands. (8) HUD's 2010 warehouse-lending solicitation was never answered by guidance; the RESPA "real source of the funding and the real interest of the funding lender" test remains the governing analysis and drives the LSA recommendation.

2. **27.1 ledger core (Stage O-1 with the servicing ledger).** `warehouse_facilities`/`warehouse_advances`/`bailee_letters` column sets, interest accrual with the cumulative-rounding method, capitalization, fees, aging buckets, curtailment/repurchase/kick-out state machine, borrowing base and concentration engine, covenant tests, daily report renderer, ledger accounts and balanced postings. 4. **27.2 settlement core (Stage O-2).** Purchase Advice Sellers/Servicers API adapter (raw JSON retained; field mapping in the integration environment), Committing and Delivery Fee Draft Notifications adapter, bank receipt ingestion, forecast at delivery, three-way matcher with the dual interest-convention logic and tolerance bands, waterfall posting, releases (bailee letter, Interim Funder, Secured Party confirmation), partner residual wires, shortfall drafts, PPA preparation (LSDU/e-mail packages), GL export batches for `sm_gl` and `partner_gl`.

### Section open questions

1. (27.1-Q1) Facility legal form — loan-and-security agreement by default; MRA only on a written counsel opinion that the RESPA table-funding and state-licensing exposure is acceptable and §559 protection is required.
2. (27.1-Q2) Interest treatment — capitalize monthly into the advance (compounding monthly); invoicing available per partner.
3. (27.1-Q3) Aging/curtailment/repurchase parameters — 45 days/10%/60/90 with +50 bps dwell, revisited after 90 days of production.
4. (27.1-Q4) Bailee-letter signature and eNote applicability — e-signed on SM letterhead, wet-ink where the custodian requires; per-loan letter generated for eNotes but sent only when a custodian is party.
5. (27.1-Q5) Custodian of record for paper notes (with 26.4-Q1) — partner's Form 2017 FCC holding for SM under a 9-313(c) acknowledgment; SM-designated custodian if refused.
6. (27.1-Q6) Interim Funder removal timing — 2 business days after payoff (`SM_WH_INTERIM_FUNDER_RELEASE_2BD`), since MERS publishes no deadline.
7. (27.1-Q7) Haircut funding — partner haircut reserve at SM's bank replenished daily; per-advance pre-funding as the alternative.
8. (27.1-Q8) Commercial-lender licensing for SM as warehouse lender (e.g., California Financing Law) — 31.1 analysis before funding a California-domiciled partner [UNVERIFIED].
9. (27.2-Q1) Fannie Mae's partial-month interest convention at purchase — compute both, lock per partner on the first production advice, confirm with Fannie Mae before go-live.
10. (27.2-Q2) Disposition of post-closing expected-vs-actual variances — price/LLPA to the partner, cost variances to SM within a 10% cap, no post-closing borrower adjustment (flag off); tied to 20.4-Q1 on the residual above the 12.5 bps credit cap.
11. (27.2-Q3) Platform-fee start and basis — from the Fannie Mae purchase date on UPB at purchase, then UPB as of the first of each month, prorated first/last periods.
12. (27.2-Q4) Premium recapture allocation — partner bears Fannie Mae's recapture as seller; SM's cost recovery is not clawed back.
13. (27.2-Q5) Partner residual settlement — daily wires net of haircut-reserve top-ups; weekly netting as the alternative.
14. (27.2-Q6) Purchase Advice API field mapping — implement against the Connect report layout; map JSON in the integration environment; retain raw advices.
15. (Section) A plain "Form 2004" and any Fannie Mae-published bailee-letter form were not located — treat as non-existent unless the account team supplies them [UNVERIFIED].
16. (Section) Whether HUD/CFPB ever answered the 2010 warehouse-lending solicitation — none located; the 1992/1994 "real source of the funding" test governs [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — absence].
