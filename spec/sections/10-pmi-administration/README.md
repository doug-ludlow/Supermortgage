# Section 10 — PMI Administration

## Overview

Section 10 runs the life cycle of borrower-paid conventional mortgage insurance (BPMI) on every subserviced loan: keeping the MI record and premium schedule accurate, tracking two loan-to-value paths (scheduled amortization and actual unpaid principal balance) against the property's *original value*, deciding cancellation and termination, issuing the Homeowners Protection Act (HPA) disclosures and notices, stopping premium collection, refunding unearned premium, telling the insurer and Fannie Mae, and re-running the escrow analysis so the payment drops. It also carries the lender-paid MI (LPMI) "financing options" notice and the insurer-initiated rescission/cancellation reporting Fannie Mae requires.

Governing law is the HPA, 12 U.S.C. 4901–4910 (as amended by Pub. L. 106-569): borrower cancellation at 80% of original value (4902(a)), automatic termination on the "termination date" if the mortgagor is current, and otherwise on the first day of the first month after the mortgagor becomes current (4902(b)) — where the **78%** threshold itself is not in 4902(b) but in the statutory definition of *termination date* at **4901(18)** (the date the principal balance, based solely on the initial amortization schedule and irrespective of the outstanding balance, is first scheduled to reach 78 percent of the original value; ARMs use the schedule then in effect), on which 4902(b) then operates, final termination at the amortization midpoint (4902(c)), recalculation after modifications (4902(d)), the 30-day stop on premium collection (4902(e)), the 45-day refund (4902(f)), annual and post-termination notices (4903, 4904), the LPMI notice (4905(c)(2)) and civil liability (4907). Fannie Mae Servicing Guide **B-8.1-04 (05/15/2019; edition Aug. 12, 2026)** adds the holder's requirements — SMDU AVM verification that value has not declined, a current-value path (75%/80%/70% by seasoning and property type) with SMDU-ordered BPO/appraisal at the borrower's expense (F-1-02), the disaster payment-record exception, the 30/45-day finalization rules and LAR 89 reporting (Investor Reporting Manual §3-04) — and Fannie Mae's April 17, 2025 SMDU FAQ makes SMDU (UI or the MI Termination Evaluation and Property Valuation Order Management APIs) mandatory for borrower-initiated evaluations. CFPB Compliance Bulletin 2015-03 (still in force) is the enforcement lens: investor guidelines cannot restrict HPA rights, no valuation may be required for automatic termination, refunds may not sit in escrow.

Architecture fit (architecture-baseline §§3–8): the `pmi` agent owns the section end-to-end, using the **PMI Lifecycle Engine** in `core/pmi` (MI record, schedule projections, eligibility decision service), the Timer Engine for every HPA/Fannie Mae clock, the Notice Registry for the thirteen notice templates defined here, the ledger for refund and premium postings, `integrations/mi/*` adapters, `fnma-smdu` for evaluations and valuation orders, `fnma-lsdu`/`fnma-servicing-events` for LAR 89 and its future event replacement, and `escrow` (Section 3) for the payment change. Cases use `cases` with `case_type='pmi_cancel'`. Eligibility decisions are deterministic rule evaluations recorded in `agent_decisions`; the agent orchestrates, drafts borrower-facing explanations and escalates.

Partner/Supermortgage boundary: the partner is the servicer of record on the MI master policies, SMDU cases and LAR 89 records (submitted under its 9-digit servicer number and Form 101 authorization); Supermortgage performs every step. MI certificates are transferred to the partner's servicer account with each insurer at boarding (Section 1.1/1.6; MGIC requires notice within 60 days). Portal-only steps — SMDU UI before API credentialing, the Fannie Mae invoicing role `INV_CSRS_MI_TERMINATION_RECOVERY_USER`, CRS special remittance code 360 — are `human_portal_task` escalations.

Dependencies: Section 1 (MI boarding data, insurer transfer notices), Section 2 (payment posting, `regx_days_delinquent`/`fnma_delinquency_status`), Section 3 (MI escrow line, interim analysis, surplus refunds; 3.2/3.3/3.5), Section 4 (notice-of-error handling, 4.1), Section 5 (LAR 89, 5.1; escrow and future MI-cancellation events), Section 6 (custodial handling of refunds), Sections 11/12 (delinquency, forbearance/deferral/modification effects, insurer default reporting), Section 15.3 (MI claims), Section 16 (payoff refunds).

Changes in flight: LL-2026-05 names "mortgage insurance cancellations" as a future near-real-time servicing event, but no schema or go-live is published (Servicing Changes Reference Guide v1.0 covers only payment, escrow and delinquency events), so LAR 89 stays the production rail behind a `rule_sets` switch; escrow events (mandatory Dec. 1, 2026) carry MI premium and refund flows; LL-2026-04 (AI governance) applies to the `pmi` agent; the Colorado AI Act (June 30, 2026) is met by keeping the decision deterministic and human-reviewable.
