### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-09)

**Servicing Guide A2-1-01, "Maintaining Fair Lending Data" (12/17/2025; Guide edition Aug. 12, 2026) — verbatim:** "For mortgage loans originated on or after March 1, 2023, the servicer must maintain the following fair lending data elements in a queryable format for each mortgage loan if obtained during the origination process: race of borrower(s), ethnicity of borrower(s), age of borrower(s), gender of borrower(s), and preferred language of borrower(s). Note: In the event of a future transfer of ownership or assumption of the mortgage loan, the servicer is authorized, but not required, to update these data elements." The related-announcement list includes SVC-2023-02 (Apr. 12, 2023), which is consistent with the March 1, 2023 effective date **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — announcement text not retrieved]**.

**Servicing Guide F-1-11 (05/13/2026) — verbatim:** among the items "the transferor servicer must deliver to the transferee servicer": "For mortgage loans originated on or after March 1, 2023, the following fair lending data elements in a format such that they are queryable for each mortgage loan if obtained during the origination process: race of borrower(s), ethnicity of borrower(s), age of borrower(s), gender of borrower(s), and preferred language of borrower(s)." Form 1103 is not mentioned in F-1-11.

**Sources of the data.** FHFA (May 3, 2022): "Lenders will be required to adopt these changes and reporting requirements for loans with application dates on or after March 1, 2023"; the SCIF collects "the borrower's language preference, if any, and on any homebuyer education or housing counseling the borrower received"; "Response by borrowers to the preferred language question in the SCIF will remain voluntary"; lenders "report any data collected from the SCIF to the Enterprise purchasing the loan." **Form 1103 (Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac, 5/2022):** language options English, Chinese, Korean, Spanish, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Other, "I do not wish to respond"; "Your answer will NOT negatively affect your mortgage application. Your answer does not mean the Lender or Other Loan Participants agree to communicate or provide documents in your preferred language." Selling Guide B1-1-01 (05/01/2024) lists "A copy of the Supplemental Consumer Information Form (Form 1103)" in the application package. Race, ethnicity and sex come from the URLA (Form 1003) demographic-information section using the HMDA/Reg C categories, collected under Reg B §1002.13 for dwelling-secured purchase/refinance applications **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — URLA section not fetched; §1002.13 confirmed via the CFPB §1002.15 page's cross-reference]**; age derives from date of birth at application.

**Use restrictions (ECOA/Reg B, FHA).** Reg B §1002.4(a) prohibits discrimination on a prohibited basis in any aspect of a credit transaction; §1002.5(b) bars requesting race, color, religion, national origin or sex "except for monitoring purposes under § 1002.13 or self-testing under § 1002.15" (verified summary) — so the servicer may **not** solicit missing elements after origination; §1002.12(a)(3) permits retaining otherwise-prohibited information "as required to monitor compliance with the Act and this part or other Federal or state statutes or regulations" (verified); §1002.12(b) retention (19.1); §1002.15 self-test privilege covers only tests that create "data or factual information that is not available and cannot be derived from loan or application files" (verified) — **file-derived outcome monitoring is not a privileged self-test**, though appropriate corrective action remains the expected response; loan modifications and other workouts are extensions of credit (§1002.2(q): "refinancing or other renewal of credit … or the continuance of existing credit without any special effort to collect at or after maturity") with adverse-action notices under §1002.9 **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — Section 12 owns the adverse-action mechanics]**; "elderly" means 62 or older (§1002.2(o)) **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**. Fair Housing Act **24 CFR 100.130(b)(3)** (eCFR Sept. 4, 2026) makes it unlawful to engage in "servicing of loans or other financial assistance with respect to dwellings in a manner that discriminates … because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin." Language preference is a national-origin proxy: using it to provide language access is lawful and encouraged; using it to steer outcomes is not.

**Disparate-impact status.** HUD proposed on Jan. 14, 2026 to "remove and reserve 24 CFR part 100, subpart G, which contains § 100.500" (comments closed Feb. 13, 2026; rationale EO 14281 of Apr. 23, 2025 and *Loper Bright*), with a supplemental proposal on Aug. 10, 2026 for HUD's Title VI regulations; **no final rule has been published** (Federal Register API, verified). *Texas Dept. of Housing v. Inclusive Communities* (2015) keeps disparate-impact claims cognizable under the statute; ECOA disparate-impact theory and state fair-lending/fair-housing laws (e.g., N.Y. Exec. Law §296-a **[UNVERIFIED cite]**) are unaffected; federal enforcement appetite is low (research/00a §6.6) but private and state exposure remains. Design conclusion: build outcome monitoring as if disparate-impact liability exists.

**AI layers.** LL-2026-04 (effective Aug. 6, 2026): policies embodying "trustworthy and ethical AI/ML" that "reflect an understanding of legal and regulatory requirements" with risk measurement, and disclosure to Fannie Mae of "the safeguards … implemented to mitigate risks" — fair-lending monitoring of AI decisions is the safeguard Fannie Mae will ask about. Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205, effective June 30, 2026; research/00a §5.6): high-risk AI in lending/housing decisions requires a deployer risk-management program, impact assessments (annually and within 90 days of an intentional and substantial modification; records kept three years **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**), pre-decision notice, adverse-decision explanation and human appeal. CFPB Circulars 2022-03/2023-03 on algorithmic adverse-action reasons were withdrawn May 12, 2025 (verified); Reg B §1002.9's specific-reasons requirement itself is unchanged. The CFPB 2024 Reg X NPRM's language-access provisions are not final (research/00a §1.2).

**Discrepancies vs. blueprint row.** (1) "Maintain queryable" omits the qualifier "if obtained during the origination process" — there is no duty (and under §1002.5(b) no permission) to collect missing elements post-origination. (2) The F-1-11 transfer duty and the "authorized but not required" update note are missing. (3) Sources (URLA demographics; Form 1103 SCIF for language) are not identified. (4) The row is silent on the use restrictions, access controls and monitoring that make the store defensible; the AI-first platform must also show LL-2026-04/Colorado-grade bias controls.

#### State machine
Per in-scope loan: `expected` (in scope, tape pending) → `received` → `validated` | `incomplete` (missing elements → transferor follow-up; after 30 days recorded as `not_obtained` with source evidence) → `maintained` → `transferred_out` (exported in the 17.3 file and retained read-only) | `archived` (loan liquidated; retention runs). Out-of-scope loans (note date < 2023-03-01) are `out_of_scope` with no row. Sub-transition `updated` (new version) only on `correction` (borrower-volunteered written correction or transferor correction) or `assumption`/`ownership_transfer` where the new borrower's monitoring information was lawfully collected. Monitoring run: `scheduled → extracted → computed → flagged → reviewed → actions_assigned → closed`; AI bias test: `scheduled → ran → pass | fail (deploy blocked / production alert) → reviewed`.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Scope and elements.** In scope if `note_date ≥ 2023-03-01`. Elements per borrower: ethnicity (multi-select), race (multi-select), sex ("gender" in the Guide), age at application (DOB → completed years on the application date), preferred language (SCIF value). Only values obtained during origination are stored; missing values are `not_obtained` (source `not_obtained`, with the transferor's confirmation or the absence of a 1003 demographic section/1103 in the file as evidence). The servicer never solicits race/ethnicity/sex/age from a borrower after origination (§1002.5(b)); it may ask about *language* only for language-access purposes and stores that answer in the operational field, not the origination record.
2. **Queryability.** `fl_queries` provide, within one business day, per-loan and aggregate extracts by servicer number, note-date range, state and element for Fannie Mae or a regulator (delivered through the 19.1 production flow with hashes); the enclave supports ad-hoc SQL by certified analysts only, all logged.
3. **Use restriction.** No command handler, agent tool, rule set, scoring model, contact strategy, fee/waiver rule, workout eligibility, prioritization or notice-selection logic may read `restricted_fl.*` — enforced by DB grants (only `fl_analytics`), RLS, the tool allowlists, a CI static check that fails any build referencing the schema outside `packages/fl-enclave`, and a SIEM rule on unexpected principals. The operational `borrowers.preferred_language` may be read by `borrower-comms`, the Notice Registry and the `case` agent for language access only.
4. **Transfer-out/in.** Outbound: one row per in-scope loan/borrower with the five elements (or `not_obtained`), in the Section 17.3 layout (MISMO v3.6-aligned field names **[UNVERIFIED — exact MISMO container names to be confirmed]**), encrypted, hashed, delivered with the transfer file; a transferee acknowledgement is required. Inbound (1.1): validate enumerations, borrower counts and note dates; unknown enumerations map to `not_obtained` with a follow-up.
5. **Ownership transfer/assumption.** "Authorized, but not required" → default: record a new version for the assuming borrower only when Reg B §1002.13 monitoring information was lawfully collected in the assumption application; otherwise leave the origination record unchanged and annotate the assumption.
6. **Loan-level classification for monitoring.** Ethnicity/race: the loan is in a protected group if any borrower reports it ("any-borrower" rule, open decision 2); sex/age/language: primary borrower. Comparison groups: Not Hispanic or Latino; White; Male; under 62; English/not provided. Loans with `not_obtained`/`not provided` are excluded from that dimension but reported as a coverage statistic.
7. **Statistical method.** For rate metrics: adverse impact ratio `AIR = rate_group / rate_comparison` (four-fifths screen: flag `screen` if AIR < 0.80 or > 1.25 with n ≥ 30 in each group); two-proportion z-test (Fisher's exact when any cell < 5); logistic regression with the metric's legitimate controls producing an adjusted odds ratio with 95% CI; for durations: difference in means (t-test) and medians (Mann–Whitney). Flags: `significant` if p < 0.05 after controls; `material` if significant **and** |raw difference| ≥ 5 percentage points (rates) or ≥ 7 days (durations) or adjusted OR outside 0.80–1.25 **[policy thresholds]**. Small cells (n < 10) are suppressed; groups with n < 30 are pooled over rolling 6- or 12-month windows before testing.
8. **Worked example — modification approval rate (quarter).** Hispanic-or-Latino borrowers: n = 250, approvals 155 → 62.0%; comparison: n = 900, approvals 639 → 71.0%. AIR = 0.620/0.710 = **0.873** (passes the four-fifths screen). Pooled p = (155 + 639)/1,150 = 0.6904; SE = √(0.6904 × 0.3096 × (1/250 + 1/900)) = √(0.21374 × 0.0051111) = √0.0010924 = 0.03305; z = (0.620 − 0.710)/0.03305 = **−2.72**; two-sided p ≈ **0.0065** → `significant`. Regression controlling for delinquency depth, MTMLTV, state and AI-vs-human path yields adjusted OR 0.71 (95% CI 0.53–0.95) and the raw gap is 9 percentage points ≥ 5 → **`material`** → `fair_servicing_review` opened with a 30-day clock. Illustrative root cause: lower document-completion rates for Spanish-preferred borrowers; corrective action: in-language document checklists and interpreter follow-up (Section 4), plus re-measurement next quarter.
9. **AI decision bias testing** (every `ai_systems` row with `risk_tier = high_consequential`: `lossmit-underwriter`, `default-collections` prioritization/fee-waiver, `cashiering` fee decisions, `insurance-property` LPI decisions, `foreclosure-ops` referral timing, `borrower-comms` escalation/tone): (a) **attribute leakage** — scan `agent_runs.inputs` and prompts for restricted fields and for proxies not needed by the task; decision agents receive de-identified case packets (names, addresses beyond state, and ZIP replaced by tokens unless the task requires them — open decision 5); (b) **counterfactual perturbation** — a fixed synthetic evaluation set (≥ 500 cases per agent) run with name/language/geography variants; outcome flip rate must be < 1% and any systematic directional shift fails; (c) **outcome parity** — the production monitor's metrics restricted to AI-decided cases versus human-reviewed cases; (d) **explanation consistency** — rationales must cite only rule identifiers and legitimate factors; a classifier flags references to protected characteristics or proxies for `lossmit_reviewer` review. Tests run pre-deploy (gate) and quarterly; failures block deploys or open a sev-1 review.
10. **Privilege and corrective action.** Because §1002.15 does not privilege file-derived analyses, the program is run under counsel direction (attorney-client/work-product where available — open decision 3) and every `material` finding gets documented corrective action or a documented legitimate business justification (e.g., a Fannie Mae eligibility rule) within 30 days.
11. **Colorado AI Act alignment.** Each high-risk system carries an impact assessment (purpose, inputs, outputs, known/foreseeable algorithmic-discrimination risks, mitigation, post-deployment monitoring, transparency measures), the pre-decision AI notice and adverse-decision explanation/human-appeal path (baseline §8 item 5), reviewed annually and within 90 days of a substantial modification.

#### Integrations
- **Boarding tape / transfer file** (1.1, 17.3): encrypted files with the five elements; enumerations validated; manifests and acknowledgements stored.
- **Fannie Mae and regulator queries**: no API; responses produced through 19.1 (`records_requests` → File Transfer Portal/Box for Fannie Mae → `human_portal_task`; regulator SFTP/portal → `officer`).
- **Analytics enclave**: read replica of the restricted schema plus the outcome projections joined inside the enclave; only aggregates leave; no third-party analytics vendor receives row-level data (Technology Guide disclosure limits, GLBA); a statistical-methods vendor, if ever used, is a Tier 1 vendor under 19.3 clauses.
- **Agent runtime**: exposes `agent_runs.inputs` manifests and `agent_decisions` to the bias tests; deploy pipeline enforces the pre-deploy gate.
- Failure handling: a failed monthly run re-runs automatically once; unresolved failures by the 20th escalate to the `officer`.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
- **Agents:** `security-records` (custody — intake validation, data quality, query fulfilment, transfer export, access reviews) and `qc-audit` (monitoring — runs the pipeline, statistical tests, review packages, AI bias tests, impact-assessment drafts); `compliance-sentinel` owns the timers. Neither agent's model prompt ever receives row-level restricted data: the enclave jobs compute deterministically and the agents reason over aggregates, flags and methodology.
- **Tools:** `fl.validateIntake`, `fl.runQuery(code, params)` (approved queries only; logged), `fl.export(transfer_id)`, `monitor.run(period)`, `monitor.test(metric, dimension)`, `bias.runSuite(ai_system, version)`, `reviews.open`, `reports.compile`, `timers.read`, `portal_task.create`.
- **Decision record:** `{run_id, method_version, dataset_hash, metric_code, dimension, groups, n, rates, air, z, p, adjusted_effect, flag, suppression_applied, legitimate_controls_used[], model_version, prompt_version, escalation?}`; for bias tests `{ai_system_id, versions, test_kind, dataset_id, flip_rate, directional_shift, leakage_findings[], pass}`.
- **Escalations:** `material` results and corrective-action plans → `officer` (Chief Compliance Officer) within the 30-day clock; program direction and any regulator production → `attorney`; flagged rationales → `lossmit_reviewer`; Fannie Mae portal deliveries → `fnma_portal_operator`; a failed pre-deploy bias test → engineering owner + `officer` (deploy remains blocked).
- **Guardrails:** decision-path agents have no read path to the restricted schema (grants + allowlists + CI + SIEM); monitoring outputs apply small-cell suppression; no agent may change a decision rule set in response to a finding — corrective actions are versioned `rule_sets` changes approved by the `officer`; queries producing row-level exports require a `records_requests` id.
- **Human path (AI off):** compliance analysts run the same enclave jobs and notebooks; timers unchanged.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Pre-March-2023 loans:** no origination record; monitoring on age (DOB is operational data for all loans) and operational language only; no proxy imputation of race/ethnicity by default (open decision 1).
- **Multiple borrowers / co-borrower removed by assumption:** per-borrower rows; loan-level classification per rule 6; removed borrower's row retained (loan record).
- **Successors in interest:** no origination data; no solicitation; `not_obtained` for the successor.
- **Borrower volunteers a correction in writing:** stored as a `correction` version; never solicited.
- **Transferor delivers non-standard enumerations or free text:** mapped where unambiguous, else `not_obtained` with follow-up; transferor's F-1-11 gap logged for the partner.
- **Transfer-out with `not_obtained` values:** exported as such with the evidence basis; transferee acknowledgement required.
- **AI voice detects a non-English speaker:** operational preference updated only with the borrower's confirmation; the origination record is untouched.
- **State privacy requests (CCPA deletion/access):** GLBA-covered data are exempt (§1798.145(e)); respond under Reg P/GLBA and Fannie Mae ownership — no deletion; §1798.150 breach exposure handled in 19.2.
- **Disparity driven by an investor rule** (e.g., Flex Mod eligibility): documented legitimate justification; notify the partner; retain analysis.
- **Small portfolios:** pooled windows; report coverage and power limitations rather than false comfort.
- **HUD rescission finalized / new CFPB guidance:** methodology unchanged (statutory and ECOA bases); `compliance-sentinel` records the rule-version watch.
- **Breach of the restricted store:** 19.2 incident with `fl_restricted` data class → heightened classification and counsel involvement.
- **Colorado-only obligations:** applied portfolio-wide; Colorado consumers' notices/appeals per baseline §8 item 5.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Proxy imputation (e.g., BISG) for loans without origination data** — default: no; monitor age/language only for pre-2023 loans.
2. **Loan-level classification** for multi-borrower loans — default: "any borrower" for ethnicity/race; primary borrower for sex/age/language.
3. **Counsel-directed monitoring** to preserve privilege where available — default: yes; results still acted on within 30 days.
4. **Materiality thresholds** (5 pp / 7 days / OR outside 0.80–1.25) — default as stated; revisit after two quarters of data.
5. **De-identified inputs to decision agents** (`lossmit-underwriter`, `default-collections`) — default: yes (names/ZIP tokenized; state retained).
6. **Seed the operational `preferred_language` from the SCIF** — default: yes, borrower-controlled thereafter.
7. **HMDA enumerations as the canonical code set** — default: yes; confirm against the current HMDA Filing Instructions Guide.
