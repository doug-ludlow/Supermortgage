## 1. TRID — Reg Z §1026.19(e)/(f), §1026.2(a)(3), §1026.2(a)(6), §1026.37/.38, §1026.22

Source pages: eCFR §1026.19 (current as of 9/08/2026), §1026.2 (9/04/2026), §1026.22 (9/08/2026), §1026.37 (9/08/2026). Verified 2026-09-10.

### 1.1 Application definition (six items) — VERIFIED

§1026.2(a)(3): "The submission of a consumer's financial information for the purposes of obtaining an extension of credit." For §1026.19(e)/(f) transactions the application consists of the consumer's name, income, Social Security number to obtain a credit report, property address, estimate of property value, and mortgage loan amount sought. (eCFR §1026.2(a)(3)(ii).) The Reg X definition (§1024.2(b) "Application") lists the same six items "and any other information deemed necessary by the loan originator" — see §6.7.

### 1.2 Business-day definitions — VERIFIED (load-bearing for every timer)

§1026.2(a)(6): general — "A day on which the creditor's offices are open to the public for carrying on substantially all of its business functions." Specific — "For purposes of rescission under §§1026.15 and 1026.23, and for purposes of §§1026.19(a)(1)(ii), 1026.19(a)(2), 1026.19(e)(1)(iii)(B), 1026.19(e)(1)(iv), 1026.19(e)(2)(i)(A), 1026.19(e)(4)(ii), 1026.19(f)(1)(ii), 1026.19(f)(1)(iii), 1026.20(e)(5), 1026.31, and 1026.46(d)(4), the term means all calendar days except Sundays and the legal public holidays."
Mapping for the Timer Engine: LE 3-day delivery (§1026.19(e)(1)(iii)(A)) and revised-LE 3-day delivery (§1026.19(e)(4)(i)) use the **general** definition; LE 7-day (e)(1)(iii)(B), mailbox rule (e)(1)(iv), intent-to-proceed fee rule (e)(2)(i)(A), revised-LE 4-day (e)(4)(ii), CD 3-day (f)(1)(ii), CD mailbox (f)(1)(iii), and rescission use the **specific** definition. Note that the CD 3-day rule at (f)(1)(ii) is in the list, so the CD "received" clock and the CD mailbox presumption both run on the specific definition.

### 1.3 Loan Estimate timing — VERIFIED

§1026.19(e)(1)(iii): "The creditor shall deliver or place in the mail the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section not later than the third business day after the creditor receives the consumer's application" and "not later than the seventh business day before consummation of the transaction."
§1026.19(e)(1)(iv): "If any disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section are not provided to the consumer in person, the consumer is considered to have received the disclosures three business days after they are delivered or placed in the mail."

### 1.4 Intent to proceed / fee restriction — VERIFIED

§1026.19(e)(2)(i)(A): "Neither a creditor nor any other person may impose a fee on a consumer in connection with the consumer's application for a mortgage transaction subject to paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section before the consumer has received the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section and indicated to the creditor an intent to proceed with the transaction described by those disclosures." Exception: "A creditor or other person may impose a bona fide and reasonable fee for obtaining the consumer's credit report before the consumer has received the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section."

### 1.5 Tolerance categories — VERIFIED

§1026.19(e)(3)(i) (zero tolerance, general rule): "An estimated closing cost disclosed pursuant to paragraph (e) of this section is in good faith if the charge paid by or imposed on the consumer does not exceed the amount originally disclosed under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section, except as otherwise provided in paragraphs (e)(3)(ii) through (iv) of this section."
§1026.19(e)(3)(ii) (10 percent aggregate): "An estimate of a charge for a third-party service or a recording fee is in good faith if: (A) The aggregate amount of charges for third-party services and recording fees paid by or imposed on the consumer does not exceed the aggregate amount of such charges disclosed under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section by more than 10 percent" (plus (B) consumer permitted to shop and (C) provider not an affiliate — read on page, not quoted).
§1026.19(e)(3)(iii) (unlimited, if "consistent with the best information reasonably available to the creditor at the time it is disclosed, regardless of whether the amount paid by the consumer exceeds the amount disclosed"): "(A) Prepaid interest; (B) Property insurance premiums; (C) Amounts placed into an escrow, impound, reserve, or similar account; (D) Charges paid to third-party service providers selected by the consumer consistent with paragraph (e)(1)(vi)(A) of this section that are not on the list provided under paragraph (e)(1)(vi)(C) of this section; and (E) Property taxes and other charges paid for third-party services not required by the creditor."

### 1.6 Revised LE timing — VERIFIED

§1026.19(e)(4)(i): revised disclosures "within three business days of receiving information sufficient to establish that one of the reasons for revision provided under paragraphs (e)(3)(iv)(A) through (F) of this section applies."
§1026.19(e)(4)(ii): "The consumer must receive any revised version of the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section not later than four business days prior to consummation." (If fewer than four specific business days remain, the revision is made on the CD — the (e)(4)(ii) second sentence was on the page; writers should quote it directly.)

### 1.7 Closing Disclosure timing and receipt — VERIFIED

§1026.19(f)(1)(ii)(A): "The creditor shall ensure that the consumer receives the disclosures required under paragraph (f)(1)(i) of this section no later than three business days before consummation."
§1026.19(f)(1)(iii): "If any disclosures required under paragraph (f)(1)(i) of this section are not provided to the consumer in person, the consumer is considered to have received the disclosures three business days after they are delivered or placed in the mail."

### 1.8 Redisclosure — VERIFIED

§1026.19(f)(2)(i): corrected disclosures "so that the consumer receives the corrected disclosures at or before consummation" when no new waiting period is required.
§1026.19(f)(2)(ii): new waiting period (per (f)(1)(ii)(A)) when "(A) The annual percentage rate disclosed under §1026.38(o)(4) becomes inaccurate, as defined in §1026.22. (B) The loan product is changed... (C) A prepayment penalty is added..."
§1026.22(a)(2): APR "shall be considered accurate if it is not more than 1/8 of 1 percentage point above or below" the actual; §1026.22(a)(3) irregular transactions ("multiple advances, irregular payment periods, or irregular payment amounts (other than an irregular first period or an irregular first or final payment)"): "not more than 1/4 of 1 percentage point above or below". §1026.22(a)(4)–(5): mortgage-loan tolerance tied to finance-charge accuracy under §1026.18(d)(1)/§1026.38(o)(2). (TRID FAQ, still published: an overstated APR that is accurate under Reg Z requires a corrected CD "at or before consummation without a new three business-day waiting period".)
§1026.19(f)(2)(iii): events within 30 days after consummation → corrected disclosures "not later than 30 days after receiving information sufficient to establish that such event has occurred."
§1026.19(f)(2)(iv): "non-numeric clerical errors, provided the creditor delivers or places in the mail corrected disclosures no later than 60 days after consummation."
§1026.19(f)(2)(v): refund of amounts exceeding (e)(3)(i)/(ii) "no later than 60 days after consummation" and corrected disclosures reflecting the refund "no later than 60 days after consummation."

§1026.37(m)(6): "A statement of whether the creditor intends to service the loan or transfer..." (Servicing). §1026.37(a)(13): rate-lock statement with date and time the lock expires; closing-cost expiration statement (10 business days — text on page, the fetch truncated the quote; writers should quote directly). §1026.37(n): "By signing, you are only confirming that you have received this form. You do not have to accept this loan because you have..."

### 1.10 2025–2026 status — VERIFIED

No TRID final rule, proposed rule, or interpretive rule for 2025–2026 appears on the CFPB "Rules under development" page (fetched 2026-09-10; mortgage-related entries are Reg B (proposed Nov 13, 2025), Reg X servicing (proposed Jul 10, 2024), PACE (2023)). ## 2. Right of rescission — §1026.23

Source: eCFR §1026.23 (current as of 9/08/2026); §1026.2(a)(6) for the day definition. Verified 2026-09-10. **VERIFIED.**
- Who: §1026.23(a)(1): "each consumer whose ownership interest is or will be subject to the security interest shall have the right to rescind the transaction, except for transactions described in paragraph (f)."
- Period: §1026.23(a)(3)(i): "until midnight of the third business day following consummation, delivery of the notice required by paragraph (b) of this section, or delivery of all material disclosures, whichever occurs last." Business day = specific definition (§1026.2(a)(6): "all calendar days except Sundays and the legal public holidays").
- Extended right: "If the required notice or material disclosures are not delivered, the right to rescind shall expire 3 years after consummation, upon transfer of all of the consumer's interest in the property, or upon sale of the property, whichever occurs first." (§1026.23(a)(3)(i) second sentence, as displayed.)
- Material disclosures: "the required disclosures of the annual percentage rate, the finance charge, the amount financed, the total of payments, the payment schedule, and the disclosures and limitations referred to in §§1026.32(c) and (d) and 1026.43(g)." (§1026.23(a)(3)(ii) as displayed; historically footnote 48.)
- Notice/copies: §1026.23(b)(1): "A creditor shall deliver two copies of the notice of the right to rescind to each consumer entitled to rescind (one copy to each if the notice is delivered in electronic form in accordance with the consumer consent and other applicable provisions of the E-Sign Act)." Content: retention/acquisition of security interest; the right; "How to exercise the right to rescind, with a form for that purpose, designating the address of the creditor's place of business"; effects; expiration date. Model forms H-8 (general) and H-9 (refinancing with original creditor) are in Appendix H (not fetched; UNVERIFIED as to current form text).
- Delay of performance: §1026.23(c): "Unless a consumer waives the right of rescission under paragraph (e) of this section, no money shall be disbursed other than in escrow, no services shall be performed and no materials delivered until the rescission period has expired and the creditor is reasonably satisfied that the consumer has not rescinded."
- Waiver: §1026.23(e): "The consumer may modify or waive the right to rescind if the consumer determines that the extension of credit is needed to meet a bona fide personal financial emergency. To modify or waive the right, the consumer shall give the creditor a dated written statement that describes the emergency, specifically modifies or waives the right to rescind, and bears the signature of all the consumers entitled to rescind. Printed forms for this purpose are prohibited."
- Exempt: §1026.23(f): residential mortgage transactions (purchase-money); (f)(2) "A refinancing or consolidation by the same creditor of an extension of credit already secured by the consumer's principal dwelling," except to the extent of new money; state-agency creditor; subsequent advances; renewal of optional insurance.
- Design note: SM's same-servicer refinance is a refinance by a *different* creditor only if the partner was not the original creditor; when the partner is the original creditor, (f)(2) makes only the new-money portion rescindable (H-9).

## 3. Reg B — 12 CFR 1002

Sources: CFPB interactive regulation pages for §1002.9, §1002.14, §1002.12, §1002.13, §1002.5, §1002.7 (eCFR Part 1002 returned HTTP 503 repeatedly on 2026-09-10; CFPB eRegs shows no "current as of" stamp); Federal Register 2025-08286 (May 12, 2025); Federal Register 2026-07804 (Apr 22, 2026); CFPB final-rule page. Verified 2026-09-10.

### 3.1 §1002.9 timing and content — VERIFIED

§1002.9(a)(1): notify within "(i) 30 days after receiving a completed application concerning the creditor's approval of, counteroffer to, or adverse action"; "(ii) 30 days after taking adverse action on an incomplete application"; "(iii) 30 days after taking adverse action on an existing account"; "(iv) 90 days after notifying the applicant of a counteroffer if the applicant does not expressly accept or use" the credit offered.
§1002.9(a)(2): action taken; name/address of creditor; ECOA notice; name/address of the federal agency; and either the specific reasons or the right to request them within 60 days (with a 30-day response).
§1002.9(b)(2): "The statement of reasons for adverse action required by paragraph (a)(2)(i) of this section must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action. Statements that the adverse action was based on the creditor's internal standards or policies or that the applicant... failed to achieve a qualifying score on the creditor's credit scoring system are insufficient."
§1002.9(c)(1)–(2): within 30 days, notify of action taken or of incompleteness; the NOIA must specify the information needed, designate "a reasonable period of time", and state that failure to respond results in no further consideration; the creditor may first inform the applicant orally.

### 3.2 Official Interpretation for complex/AI models — PARTIALLY VERIFIED

Comment 9(b)(2)-x text was not fetched (page size). What is VERIFIED: the two CFPB circulars that addressed algorithmic adverse-action reasons were withdrawn — Federal Register 2025-08286 (May 12, 2025) lists "Consumer Financial Protection Circular 2022-03: Adverse action notification requirements in connection with credit decisions based on complex algorithms, (87 FR 35864) (June 14, 2022)" and "Consumer Financial Protection Circular 2023-03: Adverse action notification requirements and the proper use of the CFPB's sample forms provided in Regulation B, 89 FR 27361 (Apr. 17, 2024)". "The withdrawals are applicable as of May 12, 2025." "Such withdrawal is not necessarily final. The Bureau intends to continue reviewing all guidance documents to determine whether they should ultimately be retained." The April 2026 Reg B final rule did not amend §1002.9(b)(2) (CFPB rule page: amends "provisions related to disparate impact, discouragement of applicants or prospective applicants, and special purpose credit programs"). Design consequence: the §1002.9(b)(2) rule text and comments 9(b)(2)-1 to -4 (reasons must be specific; sample form checklist not exhaustive) remain the binding standard; the circulars' additional gloss is withdrawn but the underlying regulation is unchanged.

### 3.3 §1002.14 appraisal copies — VERIFIED

(a)(1): copies "promptly upon completion, or three business days prior to consummation" (whichever is earlier); waiver only if obtained "at least three business days prior to consummation or account opening"; copies then "at or prior to consummation". (a)(2): notice "not later than the third business day after the creditor receives an application". (a)(3): "shall not charge an applicant for providing a copy" but "may require applicants to pay a reasonable fee to reimburse the creditor for the cost of the appraisal or other written valuation unless otherwise provided by law." (a)(4): applies "whether credit is extended or denied or if the application is incomplete or withdrawn"; copies within 30 days after the creditor determines consummation will not occur. (a)(5): electronic copies subject to E-SIGN. (b): "consummation", "dwelling" (1–4 units incl. condos and manufactured homes), "valuation" ("any estimate of the value of a dwelling developed in connection with an application for credit"). Business day here = Reg B's own definition (not fetched; UNVERIFIED — writers should read §1002.2(g) before setting the unit).

### 3.4 §1002.5 information rules — VERIFIED

(a)(1) may request any information except as limited; (a)(2) required collection incl. §1002.13; (a)(4) permissible voluntary collection of ethnicity/race/sex for otherwise-exempt loans; (b) no inquiry into "race, color, religion, national origin, or sex" except self-test or optional title; (c) spouse information limits; (d)(1) marital status only "married," "unmarried," "separated"; (d)(2) alimony/child-support disclosure; (d)(3) no inquiry into childbearing.

### 3.5 §1002.7(d) joint intent — VERIFIED

§1002.7(d)(1): "Except as provided in this paragraph, a creditor shall not require the signature of an applicant's spouse or other person, other than a joint applicant, on any credit instrument if the applicant qualifies under the creditor's standards of creditworthiness for the amount and terms of the credit requested. A creditor shall not deem the submission of a joint financial statement or other evidence of jointly held assets as an application for joint credit." Commentary (as displayed): intent to be a joint applicant must be evidenced at the time of application. §1002.7(d)(4): spouse's signature may be required on instruments necessary under state law to reach the security property.

## 17. Surprises — changes since 2024 material that the specs must absorb
1. **Colorado's AI Act no longer exists in its 2024 form.** SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026) replaced it with an ADMT transparency statute: no impact assessments, no AG 90-day notice, no explicit algorithmic-discrimination duty of care; pre-use notice, 30-day adverse-outcome explanation, human review, correction, 3-year records, AG enforcement with cure; "financial or lending services" still covered; ECOA/FCRA-notice deemed compliance. 31.2 and 21.6 should be rewritten around SB 26-189 (operative Jan 1, 2027 per secondary sources).
2. **CFPB Reg B final rule (effective July 21, 2026)** removes the effects test from Reg B and narrows discouragement; combined with EO 14281 and HUD's pending rescission of 24 CFR 100.500, federal disparate-impact exposure is being dismantled by regulation — but the statutes, state laws, and Fannie Mae contractual expectations are unchanged.
3. **Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 (AI adverse-action reasons) were withdrawn May 12, 2025** ("not necessarily final"); §1002.9(b)(2) and its commentary still govern.
4. **The standalone RESPA Servicing Disclosure Statement is reverse-mortgage-only** (§1024.33(a)); for TRID loans the servicing intent is on the LE (§1026.37(m)(6)). Retire `NTC_REGX_1024_33A_SDS` for forward loans.
5. **§1024.17 has no escrow record-retention period**; the "5 years" in the process inventory is stale — §1024.38(c)(1) (1 year after discharge/transfer) is the Reg X rule; longer retention comes from Fannie Mae and state law.
6. **2026 dollar thresholds**: QM loan-amount tiers $137,958/$82,775; QM points-and-fees $4,139/$1,380 tiers; HOEPA $27,592/$1,380; HPML appraisal exemption $34,200; HPML small-creditor $2.785B / $12.485B.
7. **FinCEN's June 2024 AML program NPRM is dead**; the April 10, 2026 NPRM superseded it (comments closed June 9, 2026; no final rule). §1029.220 CIP remains reserved for RMLOs. OFAC recordkeeping is now 10 years.
8. **The SFHDF's OMB approval expires September 30, 2026**; a renewed/revised form is likely within weeks — do not hard-code the form version.
9. **SECURE Notarization Act is not law** (both 2025 bills in committee); MISMO RON V2 still pending re-release in 2026; RON remains a state-by-state matrix (49 states + DC per a secondary survey, California phased through 2030).
10. **HMDA thresholds are 25/200 and no 2025–2026 rulemaking was found**; the 2026 FIG made no significant changes; no 2027 FIG located yet.
11. **TCPA**: AI-generated voices are "artificial" (FCC 24-17); revocation by "any reasonable method" honored within 10 business days (in effect since April 11, 2025); the one-to-one consent rule was vacated (11th Cir., Jan 24, 2025) — 20.2's consent design should target prior express written consent for marketing plus the revocation mechanics, not one-to-one consent.
12. **§1026.36(g) NMLSR ID** goes on the application, the LE and CD (§1026.19(e)/(f) disclosures), the note, and the security instrument — the inventory's "1003/LE/CD" list omits the note and security instrument.
13. **NYDFS Part 500 Second Amendment is fully phased in** (MFA for all users and asset inventory by Nov 1, 2025); a NY-licensed partner (and SM if NY-licensed as a servicer) must certify by April 15 each year.
