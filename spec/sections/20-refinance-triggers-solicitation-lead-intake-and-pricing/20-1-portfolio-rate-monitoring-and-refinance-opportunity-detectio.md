### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Fannie Mae Selling Guide B2-1.3-04, Prohibited Refinancing Practices (last updated 08/04/2021; Related Announcements SEL-2021-07 08/04/2021, SEL-2020-05 09/02/2020, SEL-2019-07 08/07/2019).** Four headings: *Lender Solicitation for Refinancing*, *Prearranged Refinancing Agreements*, *Agreements to Advance Borrower Payments*, *Conditional Tenders of Payment*. Solicitation: "Sellers/servicers may not: specifically target Fannie Mae borrowers for offers to refinance" and may not "treat loans they hold in their own portfolios and those sold to another investor or Fannie Mae as separate classes of loans for purposes of promoting refinancing." Third bullet: sellers/servicers may not, as a means of making a loan eligible for repurchase, "encourage a borrower to refrain from making loan payments." Prearranged agreements: "A seller/servicer may not deliver a loan to Fannie Mae if the seller/servicer (or any affiliate or third-party originator) and the borrower have entered into an arrangement for special terms (such as reduced fees) for a future refinance." Conditional tenders: "Conditional tenders of payment are not an acceptable alternative to refinancing for Fannie Mae loans." Remedy: "Fannie Mae will require the seller/servicer to repurchase the loan." **The topic contains no sentence describing permitted "general advertising"** — three independent extractions of the page (2026-09-10) found no occurrence of "advertis", "does not prohibit" or "mass"; the only permission is implicit in the prohibition's scope (targeting *Fannie Mae* borrowers and treating investor classes differently). The Servicing Guide topic A2-2-01, Refinance and Lending Practices (02/14/2018) contains no substantive text of its own — it says "See the *Selling Guide* B2-1.3-04, Prohibited Refinancing Practices for policies on questionable and prohibited refinancing" and "See the *Selling Guide* A3-2-02, Responsible Lending Practices." B5-7-01 (High LTV Refinance, 08/06/2025) restates: "Lenders may not solicit Fannie Mae loans for refinancing except in accordance with standard requirements in *Lender Solicitation for Refinancing* found in B2-1.3-04." **Who it binds:** the seller/servicer (the partner) and, by the Lender Contract flow-down, SM as its servicer/service provider. **Design consequence:** the selection rule may never read investor identity, Fannie Mae loan number, MBS pool, remittance type, or any field derived from Fannie Mae ownership; the run covers the entire subserviced book (every investor, including the partner's own portfolio loans) with identical criteria; the only Fannie Mae-ownership lookup in this section (B2-1.3-02 LTV > 95% eligibility) happens *after* the borrower engages, as an eligibility fact, never as a selection input.

**Selling Guide A3-2-02, Responsible Lending Practices (09/01/2021).** "Each seller/servicer must use prudent, sound, and responsible business practices in its marketing and origination efforts." Sellers must "seek to offer the lowest-cost product with the lowest-risk loan terms for which they qualify" and must "not steer borrowers toward a particular loan program to qualify the borrower for a loan in an effort to misrepresent the borrower's true credit or income related qualifications." HOEPA loans and "Loans subject to prepayment penalties are ineligible for sale to Fannie Mae." No "tangible net benefit" test exists in the Selling Guide — the benefit test below is a platform/state-law rule, not a Fannie Mae rule.

**Selling Guide B2-1.3-02, Limited Cash-Out Refinance Transactions (10/08/2025; Related Announcements SEL-2025-08 10/08/2025, SEL-2025-04, SEL-2025-02, SEL-2024-08, SEL-2023-06, SEL-2023-05, SEL-2022-05, SEL-2020-03, SEL-2019-07).** A limited cash-out refinance obtains "a new first mortgage secured by the same property to pay off an existing first mortgage" and may include "financing the payment of closing costs, points, and prepaid items," "paying off a subordinate mortgage lien (including prepayment penalties) used to purchase the subject property," and "providing cash back to the borrower (or any other party) in an amount that, in aggregate, does not exceed the greater of 1% of the new refinance loan amount or $2,000." "Only subordinate liens used to purchase the property may be paid off and included in the new mortgage." Ownership: "At least one borrower on the new loan must be an owner (on title) of the subject property at the time of the initial application"; co-owner buyouts require that "the secured property was jointly owned for at least 12 months preceding the disbursement date." LTV/CLTV/HCLTV > 95%: "The lender must inform DU that the existing loan is owned (or securitized) by Fannie Mae using the Owner of Existing Mortgage field," documented from "the lender's servicing system, the current servicer (if the lender is not the servicer), Fannie Mae's Loan Lookup tool, or any other source as confirmed by the lender." SEL-2025-08 (Oct 8, 2025): "The amount of cash back that may be provided directly to the borrower (or other recipient) may not exceed the greater of 1% of the UPB of the new loan or $2,000" — "Lenders may take advantage of these changes immediately." **VERIFIED.** There is no note-date seasoning for a limited cash-out refinance.

**Selling Guide B2-1.3-03, Cash-Out Refinance Transactions (12/10/2025) — reused from 00a-fnma §4.3 (VERIFIED).** "At least one borrower must have been on title for at least … six months prior to the disbursement date of the new loan"; "If an existing first mortgage is being paid off through the transaction, it must be at least 12 months old at the time of refinance, as measured by the note date of the existing loan to the note date of the new loan"; delayed-financing and inheritance/legal-award exceptions; temporary buydowns ineligible; student-loan cash-out feature (cash back "does not exceed the greater of 1% of the new refinance loan amount or $2,000" — wording corrected 2026-09-11 against the topic page). Eligibility Matrix (DU 12.1, eff. Aug 5, 2026; 00a-fnma §4.1): cash-out 1-unit principal residence 80% LTV; LCOR 1-unit principal 97% FRM / 95% ARM; second home cash-out 75%; investment LCOR 75%, cash-out 75%/70% [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — 3–4-unit row not extracted].

**Selling Guide C1-1-01, Execution Options (02/05/2025).** Premium pricing recapture: "With respect to any loan that pays off within 120 days from the whole loan purchase date or the MBS issue date," Fannie Mae recovers "the premium, or the amount by which the purchase price exceeded the par price (100% of the face value)" multiplied "by the UPB of the loan at the time of purchase"; "At Fannie Mae's sole discretion, the reimbursement may be reduced by the amount equal to the total of all LLPAs paid in connection with delivery of a loan less a processing fee of 50 basis points based on the UPB at acquisition." Age of loan: "a loan must be no more than six months old measured from the first payment date to the 'Purchase Ready' date." **VERIFIED.** Consequence: a trigger-driven refinance that pays off a loan inside 120 days of the partner's whole-loan purchase costs the partner the premium — the trigger suppresses proactive solicitation inside that window.

**GLBA / Reg P, 12 CFR 1016 Subpart C (eCFR up to date as of 9/02/2026) and §1016.11 (CFPB eRegs).** §1016.13(a)(1): the opt-out requirements do not apply when you disclose NPI to a nonaffiliated third party to perform services for you or functions on your behalf, if you "(i) Provide the initial notice in accordance with § 1016.4" and "(ii) Enter into a contractual agreement with the third party that prohibits the third party from disclosing or using the information other than to carry out the purposes for which you disclosed the information"; (a)(2) extends this to joint marketing under a "joint agreement" ("a written contract pursuant to which you and one or more financial institutions jointly offer, endorse, or sponsor a financial product or service"). §1016.14(a): no opt-out for disclosures "as necessary to effect, administer, or enforce a transaction that a consumer requests or authorizes," including servicing. §1016.11(c): a third party receiving NPI under §1016.14/.15 "may disclose and use that information only" to the disclosing institution's affiliates, its own affiliates, or "pursuant to an exception in § 1016.14 or § 1016.15 in the ordinary course of business to carry out the activity"; §1016.11(d): information received *outside* those exceptions (i.e., under §1016.13 or after opt-out) may be disclosed only to the disclosing institution's affiliates, the recipient's affiliates, or "any other person, if the disclosure would be lawful if you made it directly to that person." §1016.15(a)(1): exception "with the consent or at the direction of the consumer, provided that the consumer has not revoked the consent or direction." **VERIFIED.** Design (analysis in Business rules §6): the refinance program is the *partner's* product marketed to the *partner's* customers using the partner's servicing data; SM operates it as the partner's service provider under a §1016.13 contract — no opt-out is required and none is offered for this use. Marketing "additional Supermortgage products" would be a disclosure to a nonaffiliated third party (SM) for SM's own purposes — outside §1016.13/.14/.15 — requiring the partner's privacy notice to describe the sharing and a §1016.10 opt-out honored before use; the default design forbids it.

**FCRA (15 U.S.C. 1681a, 1681b; uscode.house.gov current through Sept 8–9, 2026).** §1681a(d)(2)(A)(i): a "consumer report" excludes a "report containing information solely as to transactions or experiences between the consumer and the person making the report" — the servicing ledger (payments, UPB, rate, escrow, MI, occupancy declarations) is transaction/experience data and may be used for the trigger without FCRA permissible purpose. §1681b(a)(3)(A) permits a consumer report for "a credit transaction involving the consumer … and involving the extension of credit to, or review or collection of an account of, the consumer" (00a-fed §7). A soft pull to *select* borrowers for a refinance offer is neither a consumer-initiated transaction nor a review of the existing account; it is a prescreen under §1681b(c) and would require a "firm offer of credit" (§1681a(l): an offer "that will be honored if the consumer is determined, based on information in a consumer report on the consumer, to meet the specific criteria used to select the consumer for the offer") with the §1681m(d) notice (16 CFR 642 short/long notices — 20.2). **Design:** the trigger uses no consumer reports; the representative score on file from origination is used only as an *estimate* for benefit modelling (flagged `score_source=origination_file`), and the real pricing pull occurs after the consumer initiates (20.3).

**Reg V Subpart C, 12 CFR 1022.21 (CFPB eRegs).** "You may not use eligibility information about a consumer that you receive from an affiliate to make a solicitation for marketing purposes to the consumer, unless" notice and opt-out are given; "You receive or use an affiliate's eligibility information if a service provider acting on your behalf receives or uses that information"; exceptions include "a consumer with whom you have a pre-existing business relationship" and "in response to a communication about your products or services initiated by the consumer." **VERIFIED (structure).** SM and the partner are not affiliates (no common ownership or control), so §1022.21 does not govern the partner→SM flow; it would apply only if SM or the partner shared eligibility information with an affiliate for that affiliate's marketing.

**Reg B, 12 CFR 1002 (CFPB eRegs; 00a-fed §3).** §1002.4(a) prohibits discrimination on a prohibited basis "regarding any aspect of a credit transaction"; §1002.4(b) discouragement was narrowed by the April 22, 2026 final rule (effective July 21, 2026) to statements showing intent to discriminate, and the effects test was removed from §1002.6(a) — but the statute, state fair-lending laws, Colorado SB 26-189 and the Fannie Mae contractual expectations (A3-2-01; LL-2026-04) are unchanged (00a-fed §3.8, §17.2). §1002.5(b): no inquiry into "race, color, religion, national origin, or sex" — the trigger's selection inputs are financial and loan-term facts only.

**UDAAP, 12 U.S.C. 5531 (current through Sept 9, 2026).** Unfair: "causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to consumers which is not reasonably avoidable by consumers" and "not outweighed by countervailing benefits to consumers or to competition"; abusive: "materially interferes with the ability of a consumer to understand a term or condition" or "takes unreasonable advantage of … a lack of understanding on the part of the consumer of the material risks, costs, or conditions." A "no-cost refinance" claim where the third-party costs are recovered through the note rate, or an offer that resets a 28-year remaining term to 30 years without saying so, is the UDAAP exposure the benefit disclosure below is built to avoid.

**Massachusetts G.L. c.183 §28C (malegislature.gov, fetched 2026-09-10; currency not shown on page — [PARTIALLY VERIFIED as to amendment history]).** "A lender shall not knowingly make a home loan if the home loan pays off all or part of an existing home loan that was consummated within the prior 60 months or other debt of the borrower, unless the refinancing is in the borrower's interest." The borrower's-interest factors include a lower monthly payment after accounting for costs and fees, a modified amortization period, cash proceeds exceeding refinancing expenses, a reduced interest rate, conversion from adjustable to fixed, and response to a bona fide personal need or court order (as extracted). Other state anti-flipping/net-tangible-benefit statutes (e.g., New York, New Mexico, North Carolina, Illinois) exist and must be loaded into `jurisdiction_rules.refi_borrower_interest_rule` before go-live in those states **[UNVERIFIED — not fetched]**.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. The assignment's expectation of a "general advertising" carve-out in B2-1.3-04 is wrong: the topic (08/04/2021) contains no such sentence; permitted conduct is only what the prohibition does not reach (portfolio-wide, investor-blind promotion).
2. The row cites "FCRA §615(d)/Reg V Subpart C (affiliate marketing)": §615(d) is the *prescreen* notice (no prescreen is used by default), and Reg V §1022.21 governs *affiliate* sharing — SM and the partner are nonaffiliated; GLBA §1016.13/.11 is the governing rule for the partner→SM data flow.
3. The inventory's "cash-out seasoning" is a cash-out-only rule; LCOR has no note-date seasoning, only the title-at-application rule (B2-1.3-02) and the 12-month joint-ownership rule for buyouts.
4. The LCOR cash-back cap is "the greater of 1% of the new refinance loan amount or $2,000" (SEL-2025-08, 10/08/2025), not the pre-2025 "lesser of 2% or $2,000."
5. The fixture P&I of $3,402.63 does not reproduce: $560,000 × (0.06125/12) ÷ (1 − (1 + 0.06125/12)^−360) = $3,402.6190…, which rounds half-up to **$3,402.62**. This spec uses $3,402.62 and the discrepancy is carried to 21.2/25.2 for the LE/CD.
6. The row omits Fannie Mae premium recapture (C1-1-01, 120 days from purchase) — a real cost of an aggressive trigger.
7. The row omits state borrower's-interest statutes (MA c.183 §28C) which are hard gates for MA properties.
8. The "self-improving mortgage" cannot be a *contractual* promise of future refinance terms: B2-1.3-04 forbids delivering a loan where the seller/servicer and borrower "have entered into an arrangement for special terms (such as reduced fees) for a future refinance." The program must be a standing, portfolio-wide offer on the same terms as everyone else, never a covenant, side letter or marketing "guarantee."
9. Servicing Guide A2-2-01 (02/14/2018) adds nothing; the row's "Servicing Guide" duties are the Selling Guide's.

### Build spec

#### Inputs and triggers
- Schedule: `SM_REFI_TRIGGER_DAILY` at 06:30 ET after the rate-sheet snapshot `rate_sheet.published` (20.4) — one run per partner program per day.
- Events: `rate_sheet.published` (intra-day republish ≥ 12.5 bps move triggers an ad-hoc run), `loan.boarded` (new loans enter the universe), `payment.received`/`escrow.analysis.completed`/`mi.cancelled`/`mi.terminated` (servicing 2.x/3.x/10.x names; refresh cached terms), `lead.created{channel=organic}` where the party matches a subserviced borrower (borrower-request path), `consent.revoked{kind}` and `marketing.suppression.recorded` (20.2) (suppression refresh), `loan.paid_in_full` (servicing 16.2's canonical payoff event — `loan.paid_off` is a retired alias that no process emits) and `loan.transfer_out.completed` (servicing 17.3) remove the loan from the universe.

#### State machine

`detected` → (`suppressed` terminal-for-run | `offer_ready`) ; `offer_ready` → `offered` (20.2 `marketing.touch.sent`) → `engaged` (20.3 `lead.created` linked) → `converted` (`application.received`) | `declined` (borrower says no; sets cooldown) | `expired` (`SM_REFI_OPPORTUNITY_EXPIRY_30`). `requested` (borrower-initiated) → `offer_ready` directly (skips solicitation gates; pricing still passes eligibility). Transitions are performed by the `intake` agent; `suppressed` may only be overridden to `offer_ready` by the partner `officer` with a logged reason and never for a Fannie Mae-ownership reason. Terminal: `converted`, `declined`, `expired`, `suppressed`.

#### Business rules and calculations

Calendars: `calendar_days` unless stated; money in cents (`bigint`); rates as decimals to 5 places (note rates on the 0.125% grid); rounding half-up to cents at the step stated.
1. **Universe.** Every active loan on the subserviced book (`loans.status=active`, any investor, any remittance type), excluding: bankruptcy active, foreclosure referred, loss-mitigation plan active, `regx_days_delinquent > 0` at run time (policy: current loans only), `refi_do_not_solicit`, deceased-borrower/SII-pending cases, transfer-out pending, loans inside `FNMA_C1_1_01_PREMIUM_RECAPTURE_120` (proactive path only). The exclusion list is investor-blind by construction (no investor field is readable by the rule engine; a unit test asserts the rule set's referenced columns).
2. **Candidate construction (per loan).** Product = fixed-rate 30-year LCOR on the same occupancy (also evaluate 20/15-year and a term matching the remaining term, and ARM→fixed where the existing loan is an ARM within 12 months of its first adjustment). `loan_amount_cents = payoff_estimate + prepaid_interest_estimate + net_escrow_deposit_estimate` rounded up to the next $1,000 only if the resulting cash-back stays ≤ `max(1% × loan_amount, $200,000 cents)`; otherwise round to the cent. Cash-out candidates only on borrower request (never proactively) and only when both cash-out gates are open and LTV ≤ Eligibility Matrix cash-out limit. Value = indexed origination value or AVM; `ltv = loan_amount / value` (4 dp). Prescreen: LTV ≤ 97% (1-unit principal FRM), ≤ 95% ARM, second home ≤ 90%, investment ≤ 75% (Eligibility Matrix as extracted — [PARTIALLY VERIFIED for 3–4 units]); if LTV > 95% the B2-1.3-02 Fannie Mae-ownership condition becomes an *eligibility flag to be verified after engagement* (Loan Lookup API) and is stored as `requires_fnma_ownership_check=true` — it never changes selection.
3. **Rate and price.** `note_rate_candidate` = the pass-through rate (`rate_sheets` price by product and 45-day lock) solved by the 20.4 pass-through algorithm: the lowest note rate on the 0.125% grid such that `net_premium(rate) = (price(rate) − 100)/100 × loan_amount − ΣLLPA(rate-independent) ≥ third_party_costs` (`sm_cost_schedule`, 20.4). `pi_cents = round_half_up(L × r/12 ÷ (1 − (1 + r/12)^−n))`.
4. **Benefit metrics** (`benefit_metrics` schema): `rate_delta_bps = (r0 − r1) × 10,000`; `pi_delta_cents = pi0 − pi1`; `payment_delta_cents = pi_delta + mi_delta` (MI dropping because new LTV ≤ 80% counts); `borrower_paid_costs_cents` (= 0 by program design except state/recording items the term sheet excludes **[UNVERIFIED]**; prepaids and escrow deposits are not costs); `breakeven_months = borrower_paid_costs / payment_delta` (0 when no borrower-paid costs; ∞ when payment_delta ≤ 0); `lifetime_interest_delta_cents = (pi1 × n1 − L1) − (pi0 × n0_remaining − upb0)`; `same_term_pi_cents` and `same_term_lifetime_interest_delta_cents` for a new term equal to the remaining term; `npv_cents = Σ_{m=1..H} payment_delta / (1 + r1/12)^m` over `holding_period_months` H = 84 (default), plus `balance_delta_at_H` (existing balance at H vs new balance at H) reported separately; `seven_year_total_cost_delta = (pi0 × H + bal0_H) − (pi1 × H + bal1_H)`.
5. **Fire rule.** `offer_ready` iff `rate_delta_bps ≥ min_rate_reduction_bps` (25) **and** `npv_cents > min_npv_cents` **and** `seven_year_total_cost_delta > 0` **and** prescreen passes **and** state rule passes **and** no suppression. When `lifetime_interest_delta_cents > 0` (the 30-year reset costs more over the full term) the offer is still allowed only if the same-remaining-term candidate also has `npv > 0`, and the offer must present the same-term option first (UDAAP: the term reset is disclosed with both numbers).
6. **GLBA use test (executed as code, not as policy prose).** Every trigger read of servicing data is tagged `purpose=partner_program:<program_id>`; the rule engine refuses any `partner_programs.kind` not owned by the partner; a static check fails the build if a marketing job references `product_owner != partner`. Consequence table: partner's own refinance program → §1016.13 service-provider use, no opt-out; SM's own products → prohibited by default (`open question 3`); joint product with a third-party lender → requires a §1016.13(b) "joint agreement" and the partner's notice to describe it.
7. **Investor blindness.** The rule engine's read model `v_refi_universe` excludes `investor_id`, `fnma_loan_number`, `pool_number`, `remittance_type`, `mbs_flag`, `sfc_codes`; `fnma_purchase_date` is exposed only to the recapture gate (which suppresses, never selects). Any change to the view requires `officer` + `compliance-sentinel` sign-off.
8. **Fair-lending controls.** Selection inputs are limited to: note rate, UPB, remaining term, product, occupancy, LTV estimate, state, delinquency status, MI status, consent/suppression flags. Prohibited: ZIP/census tract, language preference, age/DOB, name, any `applicant_demographics` field, credit score (until consumer-initiated). Each run writes a fair-lending extract (offers vs universe by HMDA demographic categories where available from `hmda_records`, under the `applicant_demographics` access log) to 31.2; disparities beyond the 31.2 threshold escalate to `officer` before the next run.
9. **Frequency, cooldown, expiry** per the timers table; a `declined` borrower may still request at any time (request path bypasses cooldown).
10. **Borrower-benefit disclosure content** (feeds 20.2 creative and 20.3 conversation): current rate/payment, new rate/payment, that third-party costs are paid by SM and recovered from the sale of the loan through the rate (plain language: "the rate you are offered already includes those costs; you could get a lower rate by paying them yourself"), the remaining term vs new term with both lifetime-interest figures, the cash-back figure and cap, that the offer is not a commitment, and the APR/payment example in Reg Z §1026.24(f) form (20.2).

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`intake` agent (tools: `loadUniverse`, `buildCandidate`, `priceCandidate` (20.4), `computeBenefit`, `checkStateRule`, `checkGates` (`assertGateOpen`), `writeDecision`, `emitOfferReady`, `writeFairLendingExtract`). Behavior: for each loan the agent runs the deterministic pipeline (rules 1–9) — the LLM is used only to draft the plain-language benefit explanation from the computed metrics and to classify borrower free-text requests into `trigger_kind`; it never chooses the rate, the amount or the fire decision (those are code). Decision record: `{opportunity_id, loan_id, run_id, rule_set_version, model_version, prompt_version, existing_terms, candidate_terms, benefit_metrics, gates:[{code,status}], fire, suppression_reasons, explanation_text, confidence}`. Guardrails: cannot read investor fields (enforced by the view), cannot pull credit, cannot contact the borrower (20.2/20.3 do, after consent gates), cannot override a suppression, cannot construct a cash-out candidate without a borrower request. Escalations: partner `officer` (rule-set changes; recapture-window requests; suppression overrides), `compliance-sentinel` (fair-lending disparity), `licensed_specialist` (state rule failure with a borrower insisting), `human_agent` on borrower request. Human path: ops console shows the same pipeline with per-loan "explain" and a manual `requested` entry for phone-in requests.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Rate sheet republished intra-day with a larger drop** → ad-hoc run; opportunities already `offered` are re-priced only at 20.3/20.4 (the offer itself says "rates change daily").
- **ARM near reset** → candidate is fixed-rate; benefit uses the fully indexed rate for the payment comparison and the current payment for `pi_delta`, both disclosed.
- **MI still in force, new LTV ≤ 80%** → `mi_delta` counted; if new LTV > 80%, BPMI re-required at a possibly higher premium — compute and disclose.
- **Escrow shortage or delinquent taxes** → LCOR may not finance delinquent taxes without escrow (B2-1.3-03 lists it for cash-out; LCOR prepaids allowed) — flag for 30.3.
- **Second lien not used for purchase** → cannot be paid off in an LCOR; candidate becomes a subordinate-financing LCOR (LLPA row) or a cash-out (if eligible) with disclosure.
- **Borrower deceased / SII pending** → excluded until 4.4 confirms.
- **Loan purchased by Fannie Mae < 120 days ago** → suppressed (proactive).
- **Property in a state with a borrower's-interest statute** → gate; failure blocks the offer, never silently converts.
- **AVM failure** → indexed value with widened LTV band; > 90% candidates suppressed; never an appraisal substitute.
- **Duplicate detection** (two runs, same day) → idempotency key `(loan_id, as_of_date, program_id)`.
- **Borrower opted out of marketing / TCPA revoked / DNC** → `suppressed{reason=marketing_suppression}`; the request path still works when the borrower initiates.
- **Fannie Mae-ownership needed for > 95% LTV** → checked after engagement; if not owned, the candidate is re-priced at ≤ 95% or withdrawn with an explanation.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Is the "self-improving mortgage" marketed as a promise?** Default: **no** — it is a standing program described in general terms ("we monitor rates for every borrower we service and offer a refinance when it benefits you"), with no contractual right, no special terms and no fee waivers unique to prior customers (B2-1.3-04 prearranged-agreement risk). Counsel and the partner's Fannie Mae account team to confirm the marketing language before launch.
2. **Minimum benefit floor.** Default: 25 bps rate reduction and positive 84-month NPV and positive 7-year total cost delta; same-term option always presented. Alternative: 50 bps floor (fewer offers, lower recapture exposure).
3. **Marketing SM's own products from servicing data.** Default: **prohibited**; revisit only with a partner privacy-notice change and a §1016.10 opt-out program.
4. **Residual premium above third-party costs.** Default: lender credit up to 12.5 bps of the loan amount, remainder retained by SM as rounding (term sheet **[UNVERIFIED]**); alternative: always step the rate down and never credit.
5. **Value source for benefit modelling.** Default: indexed origination value; AVM optional per vendor contract; never for underwriting.
6. **Loans delinquent ≤ 30 days.** Default: excluded from proactive solicitation (UDAAP/Fannie Mae "refrain from making payments" optics); request path allowed with a current-status condition at O4.
