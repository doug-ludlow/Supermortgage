### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-09)

**Source verified:** 15 U.S.C. 1692a (uscode.house.gov, verified 2026-09-09); 12 CFR Part 1006 (Regulation F) §§1006.2, 1006.6, 1006.14, 1006.18, 1006.30, 1006.34, 1006.38, 1006.42 (eCFR, current Sept. 4, 2026); 12 CFR 1024.39(c)–(d) and comments (11.2); CFPB interpretive rule 81 FR 71977 (Oct. 19, 2016) — not withdrawn (May 12, 2025 notice checked; the withdrawal covered Reg F advisory opinions on pay-to-pay fees, time-barred debt and medical debt, and Bulletins 2013-07 and 2015-07 on UDAAP in debt collection); 47 CFR 64.1200 (11.1). **Not re-fetched:** 15 U.S.C. 1692c, 1692d, 1692e, 1692f, 1692g (statutory text summarized from Reg F, which implements them); §1006.100 (record retention) **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**; case law on servicers as debt collectors **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — cites from memory]**; state overlays **[UNVERIFIED]**.

**Who is a "debt collector" (15 U.S.C. 1692a(6); §1006.2(i)).** "Any person who uses any instrumentality of interstate commerce or the mails in any business the principal purpose of which is the collection of any debts, or who regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another." Exclusions include a creditor's officers/employees collecting in the creditor's name, affiliates whose principal business is not debt collection, and — the operative one — any person collecting a debt "which was originated by such person" (F)(ii) or "which was not in default at the time it was obtained by such person" (F)(iii). A mortgage servicer (or subservicer) that obtains servicing of a loan **already in default** is a debt collector for that loan; one that obtains it before default is not, even if the loan later defaults (Schlosser v. Fairbanks Capital Corp., 323 F.3d 534 (7th Cir. 2003); Bridge v. Ocwen Federal Bank, 681 F.3d 355 (6th Cir. 2012) **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**). Neither the statute nor Reg F defines "default"; the CFPB expressly declined to define it in the 2020 Reg F final rule **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — preamble not re-fetched]**; courts look to the contract and the parties' servicing documents. The uniform Fannie Mae instrument treats failure to pay when due as a default (late charges after the grace period are a separate remedy) — so the conservative determination is "any periodic payment past due as of the date Supermortgage obtained servicing," which is the rule Section 1.1 already encodes (`fdcpa_debt_collector_flag = regx_days_delinquent_at_boarding > 0 OR bk_active OR fc_active`). Once determined, the status is fixed for the life of Supermortgage's servicing of that loan.

**Reg F operative rules for a debt-collector loan.**
- *Definitions (§1006.2):* "communication" = conveying information regarding a debt through any medium; "attempt to communicate" includes leaving a limited-content message; **limited-content message** (voicemail only): business name that does not indicate debt collection, a request to reply, the name(s) of natural persons to contact, a telephone number — optionally salutation, date/time, suggested reply times, and "may speak with any representative"; nothing else. "Consumer" for §1006.6 includes a deceased consumer's spouse, parent (minor), guardian, executor/administrator, and "a confirmed successor in interest, as defined in Regulation X."
- *Time/place (§1006.6(b)(1)):* no communication or attempt at a time/place known or which should be known to be inconvenient; absent contrary knowledge, "before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. local time at the consumer's location" is inconvenient (for email/text the send time counts); *attorney (§1006.6(b)(2)):* if the consumer is represented by an attorney regarding the debt and the collector knows it and can readily ascertain the attorney's contact, no direct communication unless the attorney fails to respond within a reasonable period or consents; *workplace (§1006.6(b)(3)):* no contact at work if the employer is known to prohibit it; exceptions (§1006.6(b)(4)) for the consumer's prior consent given directly to the collector or a court's permission.
- *Cease communication (§1006.6(c)):* on written notice that the consumer refuses to pay or wants communications to stop, the collector "must not communicate or attempt to communicate further" except (1) to advise that efforts are terminated, (2) to notify of specified remedies the collector may invoke, (3) to notify that a specified remedy will be invoked. Reg X §1024.39(d)(3) modified notice and responses to borrower-initiated loss-mitigation communications are within these exceptions/safe harbors (11.2).
- *Third parties (§1006.6(d)):* no communication in connection with collection with anyone other than the consumer, the consumer's attorney, a CRA (if permitted), the creditor and its attorneys, or the collector's attorney; email/text safe-harbor procedures (§1006.6(d)(3)–(5)): email only to an address the consumer used to communicate (and did not opt out), or with prior consent, or a creditor-provided address after a notice/opt-out process; **text only if the consumer texted from that number within the last 60 days, or gave consent and the collector confirmed within 60 days (via a complete and accurate database) that the number was not reassigned**; every email/text must carry "a clear and conspicuous statement describing a reasonable and simple method by which the consumer can opt out" and no fee may be charged (§1006.6(e)).
- *Frequency (§1006.14(b)):* presumption of violation for more than seven calls in seven consecutive days per person per debt, or any call within seven consecutive days after a telephone conversation (conversation day = day 1); exclusions for consent-based calls within seven days, calls not connected to the dialed number, and calls to counsel/CRA/creditor.
- *False/misleading (§1006.18):* no misrepresentation of "the character, amount, or legal status of any debt," no threats of action not intended or unlawful, no false credit information; **disclosures (§1006.18(e)):** initial communication — the collector "is attempting to collect a debt and that any information obtained will be used for that purpose"; each subsequent communication — "this communication is from a debt collector"; same language as the communication; formal pleadings exempt; **assumed names (§1006.18(f))** permitted if "used consistently and the debt collector can readily identify any employee using an assumed name."
- *Unfair practices (§1006.22; 15 U.S.C. 1692f):* no collection of "any amount … unless such amount is expressly authorized by the agreement creating the debt or permitted by law," no postcards, no envelope language indicating collection.
- *Furnishing (§1006.30(a)):* no furnishing to a CRA before speaking with the consumer (in person/by telephone) or mailing/emailing about the debt and waiting "a reasonable period of time" for a notice of undeliverability (comment 30(a)(1)(ii)-1: 14 consecutive days **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**); *transfers of discharged debt (§1006.30(b)):* a secured debt discharged in bankruptcy may be transferred only with notice that personal liability was discharged.
- *Validation information (§1006.34):* provide in the initial communication (§1006.34(a)(1)(i)(A)) or **within five days of that initial communication** (§1006.34(a)(1)(i)(B)) — the rule text has **no** exclusion for weekends or legal public holidays here, so this is **five calendar days**; the weekend/holiday parenthetical belongs only to §1006.34(b)(5)'s assumed-receipt computation, below — unless the consumer has paid; contents (c)(1)–(4): the §1006.18(e) statement; collector name/dispute address; consumer name/address; creditor at the itemization date; account number (may be truncated); current creditor; **itemization date** (one of: last periodic statement/invoice date, charge-off date, last payment date, transaction date, judgment date); amount on that date; itemization of interest, fees, payments and credits since; current amount; the validation-period end date with the dispute/original-creditor/assumption statements; the CFPB website statement (www.cfpb.gov/debt-collection); electronic dispute instructions if sent electronically; the consumer-response tear-off with the dispute prompts; **(c)(5) residential mortgage debt:** for a mortgage subject to Reg Z §1026.41 periodic statements, the collector may attach the most recent periodic statement in lieu of the itemization amounts, referencing it on the notice; **Model Form B-1** = safe harbor; Spanish/other translations permitted with the English notice; the **validation period** ends 30 days after the consumer receives or is assumed to receive the information (assumed receipt: any date at least five days — excluding legal public holidays, Saturdays and Sundays — after the collector provides it).
- *Disputes (§1006.38):* no overshadowing during the validation period; on a timely written original-creditor request or dispute, "cease collection" until the information/verification is sent (or a duplicative dispute is notified); failure to dispute is not an admission.
- *Sending disclosures (§1006.42):* in a manner "reasonably expected to provide actual notice, and in a form that the consumer may keep and access later"; electronic validation notices require E-SIGN §101(c) consent.
- *Records (§1006.100):* retain evidence of compliance for three years after the last collection activity (call recordings three years after the call) **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**.

**Reg X/Reg Z interplay (verified in 11.2 and the 2016 interpretive rule).** A debt-collector servicer must still send the §1024.39(b) notice (modified per (d)(3) after a cease); may respond to borrower-initiated loss-mitigation communications after a cease; may communicate with a confirmed successor in interest; must keep sending Reg Z §1026.41 periodic statements and other legally required servicing notices (escrow, force-placed, transfer) — the CFPB has treated statements as outside §805(c) in practice, but counsel must confirm the permitted-notice matrix **[UNVERIFIED]**. The §1024.39 EI notice and Reg F validation notice may be sent together (comment 39(b)(2)-3) provided no overshadowing.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Determination (at boarding, immutable).** `debt_collector = (regx_days_delinquent_at_boarding > threshold_days) ∨ bk_active ∨ fc_active ∨ accelerated`, evaluated as of the servicing-transfer effective date (the date Supermortgage "obtained" the debt for servicing); `threshold_days` default **0** (any installment past due on the transfer date, grace period notwithstanding — conservative; 1.1 aligned); a partner/counsel override may set 30 with a documented rationale. Loans boarded current stay non-DC forever; loans boarded in default stay DC forever (a later cure does not change the status — §1692a(6)(F)(iii) looks at the time of obtaining). Newly originated loans boarded from the partner's origination pipeline are never DC.
2. **Initial communication design.** For DC loans the RESPA transfer/hello letter (1.3) is the initial communication: it carries the §1006.18(e) initial disclosure and **encloses** `NTC_REGF_1006_34_VALIDATION_B1` (so (a)(1)(i) is met and no five-day clock runs); if a call happens first (e.g., an inbound borrower call on boarding day), the disclosure is given orally after verification and the written notice is sent within five `calendar_days` (oral validation is permitted but the platform always sends the written notice). Worked example: hello call Wed 2026-09-30 → validation notice must be mailed by **Mon 2026-10-05** (Sept 30 + 5 calendar days; §1006.34(a)(1)(i)(B) has no weekend/holiday exclusion, so the intervening Sat/Sun count); mailed Fri 2026-10-02 → `assumed_receipt_on` = **Fri 2026-10-09** (Oct 5, 6, 7, 8, 9) → `validation_period_end_on` = **Sun 2026-11-08** (printed on the notice as "November 8, 2026"). Legal public holidays use the `federal` calendar (Columbus Day Oct 12 falls after receipt; irrelevant here).
3. **Validation notice content (Model Form B-1, mortgage variant).** Itemization date = the date of the transferor's last periodic statement (`last_statement`) when available in the boarding data, else `last_payment` date; amounts in `bigint` cents from the boarded ledger: `amount_on_itemization_cents` (total amount then owed: UPB + accrued interest + escrow advances + late charges + fees as of that date); since-then columns from `ledger_entries`; `current_amount_cents` as of the notice date; alternatively (c)(5) the most recent periodic statement is attached and the notice states "See the enclosed statement for the itemization." Example: itemization date 2026-08-17 (last statement); amount then $312,540.22; interest since $1,238.11; fees since $165.00 (two late charges); payments since $0.00; credits $0.00; current amount **$313,943.33** as of 2026-10-02 (round-half-up at cents on each line; the current amount is the ledger total, not a re-sum, and any rounding difference is shown on the credits line). Consumer-response section with the three dispute prompts and the original-creditor prompt; CFPB website line; Spanish tagline (optional (d)(3)(vi)) → full Spanish notice on request.
4. **Overshadowing.** During the validation period no communication may demand payment by a date earlier than `validation_period_end_on`, threaten action inconsistent with the dispute right, or bury the dispute statement; the EI notice (11.2) and statements pass a template check; collection calls may continue (with the disclosure) but scripts avoid deadline language.
5. **Disputes/OC requests.** Written (mail/email/portal/fax; text accepted by policy) dispute within the period → `collection_ceased_at`; the Contact Engine suspends outbound collection communications (statements and legally required notices continue), 8.x marks the tradeline disputed if already furnished; the `case` agent (4.1) produces verification (copy of note/deed of trust, payment history from the transferor and Supermortgage, itemization) → `NTC_REGF_1006_38_VERIFICATION` → `collection_resumed_at`. A dispute after the period is an NoE/RFI (4.1) and an FCRA direct dispute (8.2) as applicable — no Reg F cease. Duplicative disputes: notify and reference the prior response (§1006.38(d)(3)).
6. **Cease requests.** Written → `cease_scope=written_full`: all outbound collection communications stop; permitted: `NTC_REGF_1006_6C_CEASE_ACK` (once), notices that a specified remedy will be invoked (e.g., breach/acceleration letter — 13.x; counsel matrix), the §1024.39(d)(3) EI notice, responses to borrower-initiated loss-mitigation communications, Reg Z statements and other legally required notices per the counsel matrix. Oral "stop contacting me" → `cease_scope=oral_calls_only`: calls/SMS/email stop (TCPA revocation + policy), mail continues, standard EI variant; the agent tells the borrower a written request stops all collection contact (never discourages it). A cease by one joint obligor stops communications with that consumer; the co-obligor may still be contacted (FDCPA is per consumer), but the Reg X (d) notice variant is loan-wide (comment 39(c)(1)(ii)-2 logic).
7. **Attorney representation.** Known representation with ascertainable contact → all communications to counsel; a 30-day non-response (policy "reasonable period") re-opens direct contact after a documented attempt; bankruptcy counsel per 14.x; foreclosure litigation counsel per 13.x.
8. **Deceased consumers.** Communications only with the executor/administrator, spouse, or confirmed successor (§1006.6(a)(4)); location information may be sought from others under §1006.10 without disclosing the debt; 4.4 successor process governs document requests.
9. **Furnishing gate.** For DC loans, 8.x may not furnish until a verified telephone/in-person conversation has occurred **or** 14 days have passed since the validation notice was mailed/emailed with no undeliverability notice; the gate opens automatically and is logged.
10. **Amounts and fees.** Every amount stated is read from the ledger; fees on DC loans are limited to those "expressly authorized by the agreement … or permitted by law" (2.x fee engine flags `fdcpa_authorized`); no convenience/pay-to-pay fees on DC loans (policy: none platform-wide).
11. **Disclosures on calls.** After verification: initial call → full statement; later calls → "this communication is from a debt collector"; meaningful identity (15 U.S.C. 1692d(6)): "Supermortgage" plus the agent name — AI personas use a registered assumed name from `assumed_name_registry` (§1006.18(f)), consistently, and the transcript identifies the model/agent run.
12. **Electronic channels.** Email only to addresses meeting §1006.6(d)(4) (borrower-used/consented; never workplace addresses known to be prohibited); SMS per (d)(5) with the RND check ≤60 days; subject lines/previews never reference the debt; opt-out in every message; opt-outs honored at commit.
13. **State overlays.** `rosenthal_ca`: apply the Reg F conduct rules (time/place, harassment, disclosures) to all CA loans; `ma_940_cmr_7`: cap 2 calls/7 days to the residence for MA consumers (all loans); NYC/NY rules per counsel — all applied by the Contact Engine's `strictest_cap()`.

#### Outputs and artifacts

- No ledger postings; no investor events.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`default-collections` (FDCPA sub-engine): computes the determination at boarding (`fdcpa.determine`), assembles and sends the validation notice (`validation.build` from the ledger, `notice.render`, `checklist.verify`, `print.request`/`edeliver.send`), maintains the validation period and gates (`gates.evaluate` is called by every outbound command on the loan), processes disputes/OC requests (`dispute.open`, `verification.assemble` via the `case` agent's tools, `dispute.close`), applies cease/attorney/deceased overlays, and opens the furnishing gate. `borrower-comms`: intent detection for cease/dispute/attorney/deceased/bankruptcy/"stop calling" with structured outputs (`intent.detect` → events), disclosure delivery (`disclosure.play` variants), LCM voicemail, verification of identity before any debt reference. Decision record: `{loan_id, determination{basis, days_at_boarding, threshold, rule_version}, initial_communication{at, channel}, validation{itemization_kind, amounts, sent_at, assumed_receipt, period_end, checklist}, gates_evaluated[{code, result, evidence}], intents_detected[{kind, span, confidence}], overlays_applied[], model_version, prompt_version}`. Guardrails: the AI never states that a debt is "not disputed," never threatens litigation/foreclosure timing not confirmed by 13.x, never contacts a represented consumer directly, never mentions the debt to an unverified party, never sends an email/SMS without the opt-out line, never adds a fee. Escalations: `officer` (determination overrides, timer breaches, cease-matrix ambiguities), `attorney` (litigation threats from the borrower, contested verification, court orders), `human_agent` (borrower request; disputes raised orally with distress), `lossmit_reviewer` (n/a), `fnma_portal_operator` (n/a). Human path when AI is off: gates remain enforced in the command layer (they are not prompt-dependent); humans use console scripts with the disclosure text hard-coded.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Transfer-in from another debt collector**: Supermortgage sends its own validation notice (each collector's obligation) — counsel to confirm; prior disputes/ceases boarded from the transferor's file (1.7) are honored from day 1.
- **Loan boarded current, defaults later**: not a DC loan; Reg F not applicable; Rosenthal (CA) and other state creditor rules still apply via the Contact Engine.
- **Cure after boarding**: status unchanged; disclosures continue on collection communications; once current, routine servicing communications (statements, escrow notices) are not "in connection with collection" — policy still carries the subsequent-communication disclosure on delinquency-related communications only.
- **Bankruptcy**: automatic stay governs; validation notice/collection communications are suspended (comment 39(c)-2 logic; 14.x); after discharge, no collection communications against the debtor personally — informational notices only with the discharge disclaimer.
- **Successor in interest**: confirmed successor = consumer; communications permitted (2016 safe harbor; §1006.6(a)); potential successors: no debt details until confirmed (4.4).
- **Oral dispute**: not a Reg F cease trigger; policy opens a 4.5 complaint/4.1 inquiry and the agent explains how to dispute in writing; no adverse credit furnishing on the disputed amount for 60 days if it is also an NoE (§1024.35(i)).
- **Dispute + foreclosure referral pending**: collection communications cease, but 13.x legal notices proceed per counsel (they are "specified remedy" notices); verification prioritized.
- **Email bounce / SMS failure**: address/number flagged; the furnishing gate does not open on the mail path if an undeliverability notice arrives within 14 days.
- **Vendor undeliverability feed outage**: gate stays closed until the feed is confirmed current.
- **Litigator/"serial plaintiff" flag** (optional scrub vendor): human-only handling.
- **Transfer-out**: `fdcpa_status`, disputes, ceases and attorney designations in the transfer file; the transferee makes its own determination.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **11.4-Q1 "Default" threshold.** Default 0 days as of the transfer date (aligned with 1.1); counsel may set 30 with rationale — partner sign-off required.
2. **11.4-Q2 Permitted communications after a written cease.** Default matrix: cease acknowledgement, EI (d)(3) notice, remedy notices (breach/acceleration, foreclosure legal notices), Reg Z statements, escrow/FPI/transfer notices, responses to borrower-initiated loss-mitigation communications — counsel to confirm.
3. **11.4-Q3 Validation notice with the hello letter.** Default: enclosed with the RESPA transfer notice for DC loans (one mailing; the EI notice may also be enclosed if already due) with an overshadowing review.
4. **11.4-Q4 Itemization date.** Default: transferor's last periodic statement date; fallback last payment date; (c)(5) substitute used when the statement is available electronically.
5. **11.4-Q5 Oral cease handling.** Default: honor for calls/SMS/email; mail continues; explain the written option.
6. **11.4-Q6 Transfer-in from a debt-collector transferor.** Default: send Supermortgage's own validation notice.
