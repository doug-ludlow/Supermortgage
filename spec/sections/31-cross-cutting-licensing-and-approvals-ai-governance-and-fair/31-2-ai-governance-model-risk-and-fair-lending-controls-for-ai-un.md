### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-11)

**A. LL-2026-04 — Fannie Mae AI/ML governance (April 8, 2026; "Effective: 120 days from publication" = Aug 6, 2026; applies to "All Fannie Mae Single-Family Sellers and Servicers").** VERIFIED (PDF re-read 2026-09-11). The seller/servicer must maintain "policies and procedures regarding the development, implementation, use and maintenance of any AI/ML system" that, at a minimum: "be transparent and communicated to appropriate personnel who have job responsibilities in areas" that use AI/ML; "incorporate the characteristics of trustworthy and ethical AI/ML"; "reflect an understanding of legal and regulatory requirements regarding AI/ML"; "reflect risk management activities based on the lender's risk tolerance"; and "have an owner(s) that implements, maintains and reviews the policies and procedures at least annually". It must "Manage risks and appropriate governance of subcontractor and vendor use of AI/ML that is no less protective" of these requirements and "Comply with the requirements in the Fannie Mae Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement". "Upon request by Fannie Mae, the seller/servicer must promptly disclose the types of AI/ML used, the purpose" and manner of use, "the safeguards … implemented to mitigate risks related to the use of AI/ML, and such other information as Fannie Mae may require" (00a-fnma §1). **The Lender Letter does not literally require an inventory, a risk assessment, monitoring, validation or bias testing** — those are how a seller/servicer evidences "risk management activities based on the lender's risk tolerance" and answers the disclosure request; this spec treats inventory, pre-deployment assessment, monitoring and annual review as the program's implementation of the letter, not as quoted mandates. No definition of AI/ML appears in the letter (VERIFIED-negative); the program applies the NIST AI RMF sense of an AI system plus SR 11-7's "model" definition (below) so that deterministic rule sets and LLM agents are both inventoried.

**B. Reg B after the April 22, 2026 final rule (91 FR 21620; effective July 21, 2026).** *§1002.6(a) as displayed on CFPB eRegs (fetched 2026-09-11)* — VERIFIED: "Except as otherwise provided in the Act and this part, a creditor may consider any information obtained" "so long as the information is not used to discriminate against an applicant on a prohibited basis"; "The Act does not provide that the 'effects test' applies for determining whether there is discrimination". *Comment 6(a)-2* — VERIFIED: "The Act prohibits practices that discriminate on a prohibited basis regarding any aspect of a credit transaction"; "The Act does not provide for the prohibition of practices that are facially neutral as to prohibited bases" "except to the extent that facially neutral criteria function as proxies for protected characteristics" or are "designed or applied with the intention of advantaging or disadvantaging individuals based on protected characteristics". *§1002.4(b) discouragement (as amended; verified in 21.6)*: statements the creditor "knows or should know would cause a reasonable person to believe that the creditor would deny, or would grant on less favorable terms" because of a prohibited basis. *§1002.9(b)(2)* — VERIFIED (00a-fed §3.1): the statement of reasons "must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s) for the adverse action"; internal-standards or "failed to achieve a qualifying score" statements "are insufficient". *Comments 9(b)(2)-1 to -4 (CFPB eRegs, fetched 2026-09-11)* — VERIFIED (fragments): "A creditor must disclose the principal reasons for denying an application or taking other adverse" action; "disclosure of more than four reasons is not likely to be helpful to the applicant"; "The specific reasons disclosed under §§ 1002.9(a)(2) and (b)(2) must relate to and accurately" describe the factors actually considered; "A creditor need not describe how or why a factor adversely affected an applicant. For example," "the notice may say 'length of residence' rather than 'too short a period of residence.'"; credit-scoring systems: "If a creditor bases the denial or other adverse action on a credit scoring system, the reasons" must be the factors for which "the applicant's score fell furthest below the average score for each of those factors achieved" by approved applicants (or another approved method). *Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03* withdrawn May 12, 2025 (00a-fed §3.2) — "not necessarily final". *What remains binding:* the statute (15 U.S.C. 1691(a)) and §1002.4(a) prohibit discrimination on a prohibited basis; disparate *treatment* (including the proxy and intent clauses of comment 6(a)-2) is squarely covered; §1002.9(b)(2) reason accuracy is unchanged; §1002.5(b)/§1002.13 collection and use limits are unchanged; §1002.12(b)(1) retention 25 months; SPCP restrictions for for-profit creditors (§1002.8(b)(3)–(4) — not applicable to this product set).

**C. Federal disparate-impact posture.** E.O. 14281 (90 FR 17537, Apr 28, 2025): "It is the policy of the United States to eliminate the use of disparate-impact liability in all contexts to the maximum degree possible" (00a-fed §13.6). HUD proposed (Jan 14, 2026) to "remove and reserve subpart G, consisting of §100.500" and supplemented the proposal on Aug 10, 2026 (91 FR 51416); **no final rule as of 2026-09-11** — VERIFIED (00a-fed §13.6); *Inclusive Communities* (2015) keeps statutory FHA disparate-impact claims cognizable (19.4). **Design conclusion (unchanged from 19.4): build outcome monitoring as if disparate-impact liability exists** — because state law imposes it, Fannie Mae expects it contractually, and the next administration or a court may restore it federally.

**D. Fannie Mae contractual fair-lending expectations.** *Selling Guide A3-2-01, Compliance with Laws (12/10/2025)* — VERIFIED: "The seller/servicer (any subservicer or third-party originator it uses) and any licensee of Fannie Mae technology must: comply with, all federal, state, and local laws" including "the Fair Housing Act or related regulations" and "the anti-discrimination provisions of the Equal Credit Opportunity Act or related regulations", and must "establish policies and procedures to monitor appraisals for prohibited language to eliminate discriminatory practices". LL-2026-04's "trustworthy and ethical AI/ML" and "legal and regulatory requirements" elements make fair-lending controls over AI decisions a Lender Contract matter irrespective of the federal effects-test posture. Servicing Guide A2-1-01 "Maintaining Fair Lending Data" (race, ethnicity, age, gender, preferred language "in a queryable format" for loans originated on/after March 1, 2023) — verified in 19.4; the origination platform is the *source* of those elements (`applicant_demographics`, Form 1103) and 30.2 delivers them to the 19.4 restricted store.

**E. State fair-lending law still using disparate impact (two verified).** *California — 2 CCR §12060 (Fair Employment and Housing Council; "New article 7 (sections 12060-12063) filed 9-16-2019; operative 1-1-2020")* — VERIFIED (LII copy): liability attaches to "practices that are not motivated by discriminatory intent when the practices have a discriminatory effect"; a "practice has a discriminatory effect where it actually or predictably results in a disparate impact on a group of individuals" or "creates, increases, reinforces, or perpetuates segregated housing patterns, based on membership in a protected class"; "A single person may pursue a claim based upon a practice that has disparate impact on a group"; "Practices that have a discriminatory effect may still be lawful if supported by a legally sufficient justification" (burden-shifting in §§12061–12063 — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — not fetched]**; FEHA's housing provisions reach "financial assistance" for dwellings, Gov. Code §12955(e) — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**). *New Jersey — N.J.A.C. 13:16-1.1 et seq. (Division on Civil Rights; published 57 N.J.R. 12(2), December 15, 2025; effective on adoption)* — **PARTIALLY VERIFIED (Holland & Knight, Jan 28, 2026; njoag.gov PDF returned 403)**: practices with a "disproportionately negative effect on members of a protected class" violate the Law Against Discrimination regardless of intent; the respondent must prove the practice is "necessary to achieve a substantial, legitimate, nondiscriminatory interest" and that "no less discriminatory alternative" exists; for housing/mortgage lending the burden shifts to the respondent once disparate impact is shown; DCR's January 2025 guidance treats algorithmic tools the same way. Other states (New York Exec. Law §296-a, Massachusetts c.151B §4(3B), Illinois, Washington, Minnesota) are carried as `unverified` in `jurisdiction_rules.fair_lending` until counsel verifies.

**F. Colorado SB 26-189, "Automated Decision-Making Technology" (signed May 14, 2026; C.R.S. 6-1-1701 to 6-1-1709; "this act takes effect January 1, 2027" and "applies to consequential decisions made on or after January 1, 2027" — Section 5, as read from the enrolled act in 21.6).** VERIFIED (bill page re-read 2026-09-11; enrolled act via 21.6). ADMT = "a technology that processes personal data and uses computation to generate output, including predictions, recommendations, classifications, rankings, scores" or other information used to make, guide or assist a decision; consequential decision = "a decision that relates to an individual's access to, eligibility for, or compensation related to education, employment, housing, financial or lending services, insurance, health-care services, or essential government services and public benefits"; adverse outcome (6-1-1701(1)) = a consequential decision that "denies, terminates, revokes, or materially reduces" access/eligibility or imposes "materially less favorable differentiated price, cost, compensation, or other material terms" (21.6). **Developer duties (6-1-1702):** provide deployers with "technical documentation describing the covered ADMT's intended uses, categories of training data, known limitations, and instructions for appropriate use and human review" and "Notify deployers of material updates or modifications to the covered ADMT". **Deployer duties (6-1-1704/1705):** "clear and conspicuous notice to consumers at the point of interaction with a covered ADMT" before use to materially influence a consequential decision; "a plain language description of a covered ADMT's role within 30 days after the covered ADMT makes a consequential decision that results in an adverse outcome"; "the right to request meaningful human review and reconsideration following a covered ADMT making a consequential decision resulting in an adverse outcome" ("to the extent commercially reasonable", 6-1-1705(1)(a)(ii)); requests for "personal data and correction of factually incorrect personal data used by a covered ADMT" (excluding "opinions, predictions, scores, or protected evaluations"); records "necessary to demonstrate compliance with the act for at least 3 years" (6-1-1703 — both developers and deployers). **Deemed compliance (6-1-1704(6)(a)):** a creditor providing notice under the "Equal Credit Opportunity Act, 15 U.S.C. sec. 1691 et seq." and "Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. sec. 1681 et seq." complies to the extent that notice "also satisfies the notice or disclosure requirements of this section". **Enforcement:** AG under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act; before January 1, 2030 the AG must "provide the developer or deployer with a 60-day notice and opportunity to cure the alleged violation, if a cure is deemed possible"; AG "adopt rules to clarify these post-adverse outcome disclosure requirements by January 1, 2027". **Gone:** SB24-205's impact assessments, the 90-day AG self-report, and the express algorithmic-discrimination duty of care (00a-fed §13.1, §17). Roles: the partner is the deployer, SM the developer; 21.6 owns the consumer-facing artifacts (`NTC_CO_SB26_189_ADMT_NOTICE` pre-use and explanation variants; timers `CO_SB26_189_1704_ADVERSE_EXPLANATION_30`, `CO_SB26_189_1705_HUMAN_REVIEW_30`; retention `co_admt_3y`; the `adverse_actions.co_admt` block); 31.2 owns the developer duties, the material-influence determination and the records program.

(§13-75-101(4)); chapter 72 sunset extended to "July 1, 2027" (§63I-2-213(5)). **H. California CCPA/CPPA ADMT regulations (OAL approval and filing Sept 22–23, 2025; effective Jan 1, 2026).** VERIFIED (definitions, approved text) / PARTIALLY VERIFIED (dates from White & Case, Sept 25, 2025): "'Automated decisionmaking technology' or 'ADMT' means any technology that processes personal information and uses computation to replace human decisionmaking or substantially replace human decisionmaking"; "substantially replace human decisionmaking" = "a business uses the technology's output to make a decision without human involvement. Human involvement requires the human reviewer to: (A) Know how to interpret and use the technology's output to make the decision; (B) Review and analyze the output of the technology, and any other information that is relevant to make or change the decision; and (C) Have the authority to make or change the decision based on their analysis"; "Significant decision" = "a decision that results in the provision or denial of financial or lending services, housing, education enrollment or opportunities, employment or independent contracting opportunities or compensation, or healthcare services"; "Financial or lending services" = "the extension of credit or a loan, transmitting or exchanging funds, the provision of deposit or checking accounts, check cashing, or installment payment plans". §7010(c)–(d): a business using ADMT "as set forth in section 7200, subsection (a), must provide consumers with a Pre-use Notice in accordance with section 7220" and an opt-out link "in accordance with section 7221, subsection (c)(1)". Compliance date for ADMT uses **January 1, 2027**; pre-use notice content (purpose, opt-out and access rights, how the technology works, information affecting outputs, no retaliation); opt-out exception where the business provides "alternative decision-making with human appeal authority"; access requests answered with "plain-language explanations" of logic and outcome; risk assessments before initiating processing, reviewed every three years or within 45 days of material change, first submission/attestation to the CPPA **April 1, 2028** — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — §§7150, 7200, 7220–7222 not read verbatim (PDF truncated by the fetcher)]**. *Applicability:* the CCPA exempts GLBA-covered personal information (Civ. Code §1798.145(e), verified in 19.2) but not the business; whether a mortgage application decision is "processing of personal information" outside the GLBA carve-out is a legal question — **default: treat the partner as subject to the ADMT article for California applicants from Jan 1, 2027 and satisfy it with the Colorado artifacts plus a California pre-use notice variant and the human-appeal exception** (open question 3).

**K. Model risk management for a non-bank.** SR 11-7 (Federal Reserve/OCC, April 4, 2011) does not bind SM or the partner; it is the reference standard examiners, Fannie Mae and the partner's auditors will use. VERIFIED (federalreserve.gov, fetched 2026-09-11): a model is "A quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, financial, or mathematical theories, techniques, and assumptions to process input data into quantitative estimates."; model risk is "The potential for adverse consequences from decisions based on incorrect or misused model outputs and reports."; validation comprises "Evaluation of Conceptual Soundness", "Ongoing Monitoring" and "Outcomes Analysis"; "Validation is done by staff who are not responsible for model development or use and do not have a stake in whether a model is determined to be valid."; "Organizations should maintain an inventory of models implemented for use, under development for implementation, or recently retired."; "All model components—inputs, processing, outputs, and reports—should be subject to validation; this applies equally to models developed in-house and to those purchased from or developed by vendors or consultants." Applied here: every agent (rule set + prompt + model provider version) is a "model"; DU is Fannie Mae's model (not validated by SM — its outputs are consumed and its usage rules are tested); the LLM provider is a vendor model whose components are validated through evaluation suites and monitoring, not through access to weights.

**L. Fair-lending testing practice.** FFIEC Interagency Fair Lending Examination Procedures (August 2009) — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — document not fetched today]**: the three evidence types (overt disparate treatment; comparative-file disparate treatment; disparate impact), underwriting and pricing risk factors (approval/denial-rate disparities, pricing disparities, discretion, exceptions, steering) and comparative file review of marginal approvals/denials are the design basis for the monitoring metrics below. **Discrepancies vs blueprint.**
1. "Colorado SB24-205 (as amended; effective June 30, 2026)" is superseded: SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026) repealed and re-enacted the regime; it applies to consequential decisions on or after Jan 1, 2027; there are **no impact assessments and no AG 90-day self-report**; the duties are notice, 30-day explanation, human review, correction, 3-year records, with an ECOA/FCRA deemed-compliance clause and AG cure. The row's "impact assessments before deployment and annually (CO)" is therefore policy (LL-2026-04 "risk management activities") rather than a Colorado mandate. **Cross-section note:** servicing 19.4's `CO_AI_ACT_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT_365` / `_MOD_90D` and `impact_assessments` were written to SB24-205 and should be re-based (not redefined here).
2. "CFPB Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 (status)" — withdrawn May 12, 2025; §1002.9(b)(2) and comments 9(b)(2)-1…-4 are the binding standard.
3. "Disparate-impact policy after 2025 federal changes" — the Reg B effects test was removed effective July 21, 2026; HUD's rescission is not final; the statute, *Inclusive Communities*, California 2 CCR §12060 and New Jersey N.J.A.C. 13:16 keep disparate-impact exposure alive; Fannie Mae's A3-2-01/LL-2026-04 expectations are contractual.
4. "Utah AI Policy Act" — the operative disclosure duties are now Utah Code §13-75-101 to -106 (S.B. 226, 2025), not §13-72-302; disclosure on request applies to every generative-AI consumer interaction; "prominently at the start" applies to high-risk interactions in regulated occupations (applicability to mortgage lending UNVERIFIED).
5. "California CCPA ADMT regulations" — effective Jan 1, 2026 with an ADMT compliance date of Jan 1, 2027 and a risk-assessment attestation due Apr 1, 2028 (PARTIALLY VERIFIED); applicability to GLBA-covered processing is an open legal question.
6. "NY/NJ/IL developments" — the material New Jersey development is a fair-lending rule (N.J.A.C. 13:16, Dec 15, 2025) with explicit disparate-impact liability and an AI gloss, not an AI statute; Illinois and NYC measures are employment-only.
7. LL-2026-04 requires an annual *policy* review by an owner; it does not literally require an inventory, monitoring or validation — the row's "annual AI policy review" is right, but the other program elements are implementation choices made here.
8. The row omits Texas TRAIGA (effective Jan 1, 2026; intent-based; no private-lender disclosure duty), SR 11-7 as the model-risk reference, and the Reg B §1002.5(b)/§1002.13 collection-and-use limits that shape what the monitoring pipeline may see.

#### State machine

*AI system (origination):* `registered` → `assessed` (pre-deployment assessment approved) → `evaluated` (eval suite pass) → `bias_tested` (19.4 four tests pass, where `bias_test_cadence ≠ n_a`) → `partner_notified` (version-change notice to the partner; Colorado developer docs where `materially_influences_consequential_decision`) → `deployed` ⇄ `monitored` (monthly/quarterly cycles; drift alerts) → `restricted` (kill-switch: human path only) → `retired`. Guards: `deployed` requires all of the above and, for `risk_tier = high_consequential`, the partner `officer`'s approval record; any `material` finding attributable to a system moves it to `monitored{remediation}` with a 30-day review clock; a failed quarterly bias test moves it to `restricted` for the affected decision kind until re-tested.
*Fair-lending run:* `scheduled → extracted → computed → flagged → reviewed → closed` (19.4 pattern); findings `none | screen | significant | material | suppressed`; `material` opens a `fair_lending_reviews` row and blocks closure until a corrective action or documented legitimate justification exists.
*Consumer AI-rights request:* `received → routed (21.6 for Colorado; 20.3 for Utah; CA path) → in_review (second reviewer) → decided → communicated → closed`.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Inventory and classification.** Every origination agent version is an `ai_systems` row. `materially_influences_consequential_decision = true` for `underwriter` (credit decision, counteroffer, NOIA, conditions), `pricing` (quotes, locks, exceptions), `valuation` (review outcomes, ROV), `fraud-risk` (holds that delay or end an application), `compliance-tester` (blocking results that change terms), `intake` (pre-qualification outcomes). `false` for `disclosure`, `funder`, `warehouse`, `post-closing`, `secondary`, `hmda`, `boarding`, `title-closing` (document assembly; MI/insurance ordering) — they act after a decision or on non-consumer counterparties; a change that gives any of them decision influence re-classifies it. `ca_substantially_replaces_human = false` for denials/counteroffers/NOIAs because `underwriting_reviewer` must "Know how to interpret", "Review and analyze" and "Have the authority to make or change the decision" (documented in the reviewer procedure and evidenced per decision by `reviewer_action`); `true` for approvals decided without reviewer action — hence approvals are covered ADMT uses under CA §7200 if the CCPA applies (they are not "adverse outcomes" under Colorado but they are "significant decisions" under CA).
2. **Pre-deployment assessment** (per `ai_impact_assessments{pre_deployment}`): (a) purpose and decision kinds; (b) inputs attestation — a static scan of the agent's tool allowlist, prompt templates and rule sets proves no read path to `applicant_demographics` (CI check as in 19.4-T4) and lists every input variable; (c) proxy review — each input is classified {legitimate_credit_factor, proxy_risk, prohibited} with disposition (e.g., ZIP/census tract: prohibited as a decision input; permitted only for AMI/loan-limit lookups; first names and surnames: never passed to the model in decision prompts — tokenized; language preference: never a decision input); (d) evaluation results; (e) bias tests (19.4 four kinds); (f) fair-lending back-test on the golden set and, for changes to an in-production system, a shadow run over the prior quarter's applications comparing outcomes by dimension; (g) LDA search when the change introduces or alters a threshold/policy; (h) human-review design; (i) monitoring plan and kill-switch; (j) residual risk and approvals. SR 11-7 mapping: (a)–(c) = conceptual soundness; (d)–(g) = outcomes analysis; (i) = ongoing monitoring.
3. **Decision-record minimum** (enforced by the runtime): no `decision.issued` event is accepted without an `agent_decisions` row carrying inputs manifest, rule-set/prompt/model versions, ranked `principal_factors`, rationale limited to rule identifiers and legitimate factors (classifier `rationale_guard` rejects any reference to a prohibited basis or proxy), and — for denials/counteroffers/NOIAs — a `reviewer_action`. 21.6 derives the §1002.9(b)(2) reasons from `principal_factors` (≤ 4 reasons; comment 9(b)(2)-1) with reason text from the approved taxonomy (comment 9(b)(2)-3: "length of residence"-style statements suffice); for credit-score-driven factors the FCRA key factors come from the bureau (comment 9(b)(2)-4 is satisfied by the bureau's reason codes because the partner does not operate its own credit-scoring system — DU is Fannie Mae's; DU's risk factors are cited as factors "actually considered").
4. **Reason accuracy test.** Monthly, `qc-audit` recomputes the principal factors for a sample of adverse actions from the frozen inputs (rule replay + a second independent model pass) and compares to the issued notice: `accuracy = matches / sample`. Worked example: October 2026 — 31 adverse actions issued; sample = 25 (random, stratified by reason); 24 match; 1 mismatch (notice cited "Excessive obligations in relation to income" and "Delinquent past or present credit obligations"; replay ranks "Insufficient income for amount of credit requested" above the delinquency factor after a corrected income figure) → accuracy 0.96 < 0.98 → corrective action: the income-recalculation ordering bug is fixed (rule-set `uw.reasons` v1.4 → v1.5, itself a `substantial=false` change needing eval only), the affected borrower receives a corrected statement of reasons within 5 business days (21.6 corrected-notice path), and the November sample is raised to 100 %. Includes the 21.6 fixture denial (Oct 26, 2026: DTI 51.3 % → reason 1; June 2026 30-day late → reason 2) which replays exactly.
5. **Outcome monitoring — underwriting.** Population: `hmda_records` with `action_taken_date` in the period and `action_taken ∈ {1 originated, 2 approved not accepted, 3 denied, 4 withdrawn, 5 incomplete}`; metric `UW_APPROVAL_RATE` = (1+2)/(1+2+3) by dimension from `applicant_demographics` (enclave join; small cells < 10 suppressed; groups < 30 pooled over rolling 3/6/12-month windows). Statistics as in 19.4 rule 7: AIR four-fifths screen, two-proportion z (Fisher when any cell < 5), logistic regression with legitimate controls (representative score band, DTI band, LTV/CLTV band, reserves, product, occupancy, loan purpose, DU recommendation, AI-vs-human path, month), `material` = significant after controls **and** raw gap ≥ 5 percentage points or adjusted OR outside 0.80–1.25 **[policy thresholds as in 19.4]**. **Worked example (October 2026 actions):** Hispanic or Latino applicants n = 180, approvals 153 (85.0 %); comparison (Not Hispanic or Latino) n = 620, approvals 558 (90.0 %). AIR = 0.850/0.900 = **0.944** (passes the four-fifths screen). Pooled p = 711/800 = 0.88875; SE = √(0.88875 × 0.11125 × (1/180 + 1/620)) = √(0.098873 × 0.0071685) = √0.00070878 = 0.026623; z = (0.850 − 0.900)/0.026623 = **−1.878**; two-sided p ≈ **0.060** → `screen` (not significant at 0.05); pooled Aug–Oct 2026: 470/540 (87.04 %) vs 1,680/1,860 (90.32 %): AIR 0.964; pooled p = 0.89583; SE = √(0.89583 × 0.10417 × (1/540 + 1/1860)) = 0.014932; z = **−2.200**; p ≈ **0.028** → `significant`; regression with controls yields adjusted OR 0.91 (95 % CI 0.72–1.15) and the raw gap is 3.3 pp < 5 → **not `material`** → no review clock, but the finding is reported with the "denial reason mix" cut: the excess denials concentrate in reason 1 (DTI) → an LDA search is opened voluntarily on the DTI-related condition policy (e.g., whether the agent's documentation requests for variable income are stricter than B3-3 requires).
6. **Outcome monitoring — pricing.** Metric `PR_RATE_SPREAD_BPS`: `rate_spread` (28.3; APR − APOR) and `PR_PRICE_TO_BORROWER_BPS` (final price after the SM cost-recovery pass-through, 20.4/27.2) regressed on LLPA drivers (score band, LTV, product, occupancy, units, lock period, lock date, state) — because the platform prices deterministically from the LLPA matrix and a published rate sheet with no MLO discretion (§1026.36(d) flat/fixed compensation; 25.1), the expected adjusted difference is zero; threshold: adjusted mean difference > 10 bps with p < 0.05 → `material`. `PR_EXCEPTION_GRANT_RATE`: pricing exceptions requested/granted by dimension. **Worked example (Q3 2026 exceptions):** group A requests 140, granted 22 (15.71 %); comparison requests 190, granted 41 (21.58 %): AIR = **0.728** (< 0.80 → `screen`); pooled p = 63/330 = 0.19091; SE = √(0.19091 × 0.80909 × (1/140 + 1/190)) = 0.043775; z = **−1.340**; p ≈ **0.180** → not significant; disposition: `screen` only, pooled to the 12-month window; every exception must already carry a documented, non-discretionary reason code (`pricing_exception_reviews.reason ∈ {competitor_match_documented, error_correction, cure, relationship_policy}`) so the review examines reason-code mix rather than free-text.
7. **Steering and product mix.** `ST_PRODUCT_MIX_HOMEREADY`: share of HomeReady-eligible applicants (AMI API) offered HomeReady, by dimension; `UW_COUNTEROFFER_RATE`: counteroffers (adverse outcomes under Colorado) as a share of decisions. **Worked example (October 2026):** counteroffer rate group A 31/220 (14.09 %) vs comparison 52/610 (8.52 %): AIR (adverse direction) = 1.653; pooled p = 0.100; SE = 0.023593; z = **2.359**; p ≈ **0.018** → `significant`; raw gap 5.6 pp ≥ 5 → **`material`** if it survives controls (adjusted OR 1.48, CI 1.05–2.09 in the example) → `fair_lending_reviews` row opened Nov 16, 2026, due **Wed Dec 16, 2026**; root cause found: the agent's counteroffer logic proposed a lower loan amount whenever DU returned Approve/Ineligible for LTV, without first offering the MI/coverage alternatives available under B7-1 — corrective action: rule-set `uw.counteroffer` v2.1 (offer the least-restrictive eligible alternative first), shadow-run over Aug–Oct, eval + bias tests, partner notice, deploy Dec 9; re-measure in the December run.
8. **Less-discriminatory-alternative (LDA) search.** Triggered by any `material` finding, by pre-deployment assessments that introduce a threshold/policy, and annually for the core underwriting policy set. Method: enumerate alternatives (e.g., thresholds, documentation tiers, ordering of alternatives, use of compensating factors already permitted by the Selling Guide), estimate predictive performance (early-payment-default/QC-defect proxies; approval accuracy vs DU) and disparity by dimension on the enclave dataset; select the alternative that preserves the legitimate interest with the smallest disparity; record the search whether or not a change is made (California/New Jersey burden-shifting evidence).
9. **Attribute leakage and proxies.** Decision agents receive de-identified packets: names, street address (beyond state and the property-eligibility fields), e-mail, and language preference are tokenized in prompts; census tract/ZIP are used only by deterministic lookups (AMI, loan limits, flood) outside the model; the leakage test (19.4 kind (a)) runs on every `agent_runs.inputs_manifest` sample; the counterfactual test (kind (b)) perturbs names/language/geography on ≥ 500 synthetic cases per agent version — flip rate must be < 1 % with no directional shift.
10. **Human-review touchpoints and reversibility.** `underwriting_reviewer` (21.6/23.3) approves every denial, counteroffer and NOIA before notice; Colorado "meaningful human review and reconsideration" and appeals go to a *different* individual with authority to change the decision (`consumer_ai_rights_requests` → 21.6 human-review path, 30-day policy clock `CO_SB26_189_1705_HUMAN_REVIEW_30`); the California opt-out is satisfied by the human-appeal exception (the consumer may demand a human decision; the platform routes the file to the human path: reviewer decides from the evidence without the agent's recommendation, using the same Selling Guide rules); `mlo_of_record` presents and approves terms (20.3/21.1/21.4) — the pricing agent's outputs are recommendations to a licensed individual, which is also the Utah "high-risk interaction" mitigation (a human is in the terms loop).
11. **Colorado developer duties (SM).** Before Jan 1, 2027 SM delivers to the partner, per covered system, `DOC_AI_SYSTEM_CARD` containing intended uses, categories of training data (for LLM agents: the provider's published training-data description plus SM's fine-tuning/evaluation datasets, if any; for rule sets: "none — deterministic"), known limitations (document-extraction error rates, income-type coverage, language coverage), and instructions for appropriate use and human review (reviewer procedure, reversal authority, escalation). Every `substantial=true` change ships an updated card before deployment (`CO_SB26_189_1702_MATERIAL_UPDATE_NOTICE`). Worked example: cards for `underwriter` v3.0, `pricing` v2.4, `valuation` v1.8, `fraud-risk` v2.0, `intake` v1.6 and `compliance-tester` v1.3 delivered and acknowledged **Tue Dec 15, 2026**; a Colorado denial decided **Tue Jan 12, 2027** (21.6-T10) is therefore within the regime with docs in place; the explanation is due Thu Feb 11, 2027 (21.6) and a human-review request received Wed Jan 20 is answered by a second reviewer by Fri Feb 19, 2027.
12. **Governance evidence pack** (`PACK_AI_GOVERNANCE`, quarterly to the partner; on request to Fannie Mae through 19.3's disclosure timer): inventory export (systems, versions, decision kinds, risk tier, human touchpoints), policy and annual review minutes, assessments (pre-deployment/annual/material change), evaluation and bias-test summaries, monitoring runs and findings with dispositions, reason-accuracy results, LDA searches, decision-record samples (redacted), Colorado cards/notices/human-review statistics, California artifacts, vendor (model provider) attestations. Fannie Mae's request is answered "promptly" (19.3 default 5 `business_days_fannie_et`) from the latest pack.
13. **Annual policy review** (`FNMA_LL2026_04_POLICY_REVIEW_365`, 19.3): the owner reviews `POL-AI-01` against the year's findings, incidents, rule changes (CFPB/HUD/Colorado AG rules/CPPA), and vendor changes; the partner adopts the reviewed policy for its Lender Contract attestation; minutes stored.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
- **Agents:** `qc-audit` (independent evaluation and monitoring persona — runs eval suites, bias tests, monthly/quarterly runs, reason-accuracy replays, drafts LDA searches and review packages), `compliance-sentinel` (timers; rule-version watch on CFPB/HUD/Colorado AG/CPPA/state AG/Fannie Mae; deploy-gate evaluation), `security-records` (evidence custody; enclave access). Tools: `inventory.upsert`, `assess.draft`, `eval.run(suite, version)`, `bias.runSuite` (19.4), `monitor.run(scope, period)`, `monitor.test(metric, dimension)`, `reasons.replay(sample)`, `lda.search(subject)`, `drift.watch`, `pack.compile`, `cards.render`, `gate.evaluate`, `escalations.open`, `timers.read`.
- **End-to-end behavior:** registers every agent version from deploy metadata; drafts the pre-deployment assessment from static analysis of tools/prompts/rules and the evaluation results; runs the tests; blocks deployment until approvals exist; runs the monthly and quarterly monitoring deterministically inside the enclave and reasons over aggregates and flags; opens reviews and drafts root-cause hypotheses (e.g., reason-mix cuts, condition-count cuts, AI-vs-human splits); drafts LDA searches; compiles packs, cards and disclosure responses; watches for law changes and re-opens assessments.
- **Decision record:** `{subject (system/version | run | finding | request), inputs (versions, dataset_hash, population), computation (statistics with parameters), outcome, rationale, rule_set_version (POL-AI-01 vN; METH-FL-02 vN), model_version, prompt_version, confidence, escalation?}`.
- **Guardrails:** the agents never approve a deployment, never change a decision rule set or prompt in response to a finding (corrective actions are versioned changes approved by `officer` and re-gated), never mark a finding `closed` without an `officer` disposition, never send row-level demographic data to a model, never contact a borrower about a fair-lending review, and never draft consumer explanations that cite a prohibited basis (rationale guard).
- **Escalations:** deployment approval (SM `officer`; partner `officer` for high-risk systems); `material` findings, corrective actions and legitimate-justification memos (`officer`, both entities; counsel direction — `attorney`); Colorado AG notices, regulator inquiries and privilege calls (`attorney`); Colorado human review and appeals (second `underwriting_reviewer`, 21.6); Fannie Mae disclosure (`officer` via 19.3); drift auto-restriction (`officer` + engineering owner).
- **Human path (AI off):** compliance analysts run the enclave jobs and notebooks with identical outputs; the deploy gate remains code-enforced.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Demographic data withheld** ("information not provided"): excluded from that dimension and reported as coverage; BISG proxy used only for the "not provided" population when the methodology permits (open question 6); never for decisions.
- **Small partner volume** (< 30 per group per month): pooled 6/12-month windows; power limitations reported rather than false comfort.
- **DU-driven disparities** (e.g., a Refer with Caution pattern): documented legitimate justification (Fannie Mae's model); reported to the partner; SM cannot alter DU; the LDA search examines only SM's overlays (documentation requests, condition policy, counteroffer ordering).
- **Model-provider silent update**: detected by eval-suite drift (golden-set deltas) → `restricted` for high-risk decision kinds until re-evaluated; contractual notice breach logged (19.3).
- **Reviewer rubber-stamping** (reviewer approval rate 100 % with median review time < 60 s): flagged by `UW_REVIEWER_OVERRIDE_RATE`/time metrics → reviewer procedure retraining; California "human involvement" evidence at risk → treated as `substantially_replaces_human = true` until fixed.
- **Colorado AG rules (by Jan 1, 2027) change notice content**: `compliance-sentinel` opens a template change (21.6) with a 30-day implementation clock; existing explanations remain valid for decisions already made.
- **HUD finalizes the rescission / CFPB issues new guidance**: methodology unchanged (statutory and state bases); documented in the annual review.
- **Consumer requests correction of data used by the ADMT** (Colorado): if the corrected data changes the decision inputs, 21.6 re-runs the decision through the human path and issues a new decision/notice; the original record is preserved (append-only).
- **Complaint alleging discrimination**: 5-business-day triage; the file's decision record is re-reviewed by a different reviewer; linked to the monthly finding if pattern-level.
- **Incident exposing prompts/logs at the model provider**: 19.2 incident; `agent_runs.inputs_manifest` scopes affected applications; demographic data are never in prompts, so no restricted-class exposure.
- **AI off**: decisions default to the human path with the same rules; monitoring continues on human decisions (AI-vs-human split becomes human-only).

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Materiality thresholds** (5 pp / 10 bps pricing / OR outside 0.80–1.25; small-cell 10; pooling 30) — default as stated (aligned with 19.4); revisit after two quarters.
2. **Counsel-directed monitoring for privilege** — default: yes (Reg B §1002.15 self-test privilege does not cover file-derived analyses; work-product where available); findings still acted on within 30 days.
3. **California CCPA/ADMT applicability** to mortgage application decisions (GLBA carve-out) — default: treat as applicable from Jan 1, 2027 for California applicants (pre-use notice variant, human-appeal route, access explanations, risk assessment) pending counsel's memo; cost is low because the Colorado artifacts are reused.
4. **Partner approval of every high-risk deployment** vs approval of the policy and annual attestation only — default: partner `officer` approves each high-risk system's pre-deployment and material-modification assessments (the partner is the deployer and creditor).
5. **Version-change notice period to the partner** — default 10 creditor business days before deployment; emergency fixes (security, correctness) may deploy on 1 business day's notice with post-hoc assessment within 10 days.
6. **BISG proxying for "not provided" applicants** — default: yes for monitoring only, with the CFPB 2014 methodology, excluded in any state whose law bars proxy inference (none identified), never for decisions; report proxied and self-reported populations separately.
7. **Treat approvals decided without reviewer action as California "substantially replaces human decisionmaking"** — default: yes (conservative), which makes the pre-use notice/opt-out apply to all California applicants if the CCPA applies.
