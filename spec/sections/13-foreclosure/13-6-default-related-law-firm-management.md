### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-09)

**A4-2.2-01, Selecting and Retaining Law Firms (05/08/2024 — verified today).** "The servicer must collect data from the law firm" and "conduct appropriate due diligence to evaluate whether the law firm meets Fannie Mae's minimum requirements" (F-2-04); "Maintain documentation evidencing its law firm selection process, including how law firms are solicited, evaluated, and selected"; inform rejected firms; retain rejected-firm information "for the longer of any retention period applicable to the servicer, or seven years." "The servicer must submit a Servicer Selection Form (Form 200) to Fannie Mae for each law firm it wishes to retain," one "for each jurisdiction"; "By submitting Form 200, the servicer certifies the law firm's satisfaction of Fannie Mae's minimum requirements." "Within 15 business days following the submission of Form 200, Fannie Mae expects that it will be in a position to issue a 'No Objection' determination, an 'Objection' determination, or request additional information." On "No Objection" the firm "must attend Fannie Mae new law firm training and sign a limited retention agreement with Fannie Mae" that "will recognize and reflect a joint attorney-client relationship between the law firm, Fannie Mae and the servicer" and "controls in the event that there are any inconsistent provisions in any agreement between the law firm and the servicer" (form is confidential, on the password-protected site). "The servicer cannot rely upon the fact that another servicer previously submitted Form 200." Special Counsel retained at the servicer's expense under indemnification obligations is exempt from selection rules but must be notified to Fannie Mae, keep Fannie Mae and the original firm updated, and transition the matter back. **No "Fannie Mae Retained Attorney Network" exists in the current Guide** — the blueprint's/brief's "retained attorney network" language is legacy (pre-2013 RAN).

**F-2-04, Firm Minimum Requirements (10/11/2023 — verified today; 27 items).** End-to-end default practice; office in a retained jurisdiction; two jurisdiction-specific references; statewide coverage; sufficient matters in the past 24 months; "at least two Qualifying Attorneys in each jurisdiction ... one with at least eight years and a second with at least five years of jurisdiction-specific experience"; disclosure of sanctions (5 years) and bar complaints (10 years); E&O by tier — Tier I (0–4,499 foreclosures) "$1 million per occurrence ... aggregate ... $3 million", Tier II (4,500–19,999) $5M/$5M, Tier III (20,000+) $8M/$8M; financial ability to advance costs; attorney-to-file and staff-to-file ratios; information-security controls with PII "encrypted at rest at all times"; certification that documents "have been and continue to be prepared, executed, and notarized in compliance with applicable law"; no substantial adverse practice (HOA foreclosures, debtor/mortgagor representation); vendor/affiliate disclosure (title, posting, publication, service); written QC/supervision policies; "capability to provide daily reporting to Fannie Mae, including via a web-based attorney reporting system"; business continuity and succession plans; no offshore legal work or PII.

**A4-2.2-02, Law Firm Management and Oversight (11/12/2014 — verified today).** "The servicer is fully responsible for managing and overseeing all aspects of the performance and compliance of any law firm to which it makes a referral," "accountable and responsible to Fannie Mae for any delays or losses resulting from deficiencies in the law firm's performance," and "must reimburse Fannie Mae for any losses suffered because a law firm did not meet its responsibilities." Written oversight policies "reasonably designed to ensure that the law firms handling of Fannie Mae default-related matters are in compliance with the Mortgage Default Counsel Retention Agreement"; review frequency by risk ("legal, reputational, and financial"), volume, performance, staffing-ratio changes, systemic-issue litigation, media, prior reviews; fifteen minimum monitoring elements (eligibility; retention-agreement incl. "fee and cost guidelines"; Guide; law; capacity; reputational risk; information security/data management/fraud prevention; document custody; business continuity; E&O; financial viability; staffing; ratios; training; quality of work); make methodology, schedules, vendors, findings and remediation plans available to Fannie Mae; Fannie Mae may audit firms directly; the servicer ensures firm training on Announcements; provide requested data/reports and system access. Escalations "Within two business days of discovery, or sooner if circumstances warrant" for eleven matters (suspension/transfer/termination triggers; bar complaints/sanctions/systemic litigation; data breaches; fraud; governmental/Congressional inquiries; media inquiries; capacity; retention-agreement breach; systemic issues; significant servicer process issues; material ownership/organizational change incl. a named partner leaving) — "via email" to Legal (F-4-02: loanservicing@fanniemae.com "To report escalated matters to Fannie Mae"), naming points of contact and supplying requested information; Fannie Mae may direct.

**A4-2.2-03 (04/12/2017 — verified today).** "The servicer cannot directly or indirectly require or encourage law firms to use specified vendors"; firms choose vendors; no conflict-influenced arrangements; firms need Fannie Mae's prior written approval to use "a vendor that is the servicer itself, an outsourcing company, or other third-party vendor utilized by the servicer"; the servicer must not receive "a direct or indirect benefit" from outsourcing/vendor arrangements; "may not charge ... any outsourcing fee, referral fee, packaging fee, or similar fee"; nobody in the servicer's chain may charge law firms; "direct sourcing" data access is permitted. **E-5-06 (11/12/2014):** no technology/e-invoice fees charged to attorneys; reimbursable technology fee max "$25.00 per loan for the life of a default"; e-invoicing "$5.00 ... relating to a foreclosure," "an additional $5.00 ... if a bankruptcy is filed," "$10.00 for the life of the loan"; "No portion ... may be charged as a cost to the borrower or be charged to the attorney"; attorneys may integrate their systems with the servicer's vendor at no cost.

**A4-2.2-04 (08/15/2018 — verified today).** Notify Fannie Mae "within two business days or sooner if circumstances warrant" of information warranting suspension/transfer/termination; "Provide Fannie Mae with prior notice at least five business days before implementing the decision" with an implementation plan; inform the firm; keep Fannie Mae updated; retain records "seven years after the decision"; Fannie Mae may direct investigations, suspensions, transfers or terminations and may manage them itself; consider transferee capacity, fee proration, "Avoiding duplicative fees and costs," continuing E&O; report transferee identity and matters; "The servicer must not charge Fannie Mae or the borrower for any fees or costs associated with transferring matters." E-1.1-01: 5 BD notice for ≥30 transfers in 6 months from one firm to another in the same state; prior approval if a sale already occurred.

**Fees — E-5-04 (02/12/2020) and E-5-05 (02/12/2020 — verified today).** Maximum fees in the **Allowable Foreclosure Attorney Fees Exhibit** (amounts not retrieved today **[UNVERIFIED figures]**); "The allowable fee applies for the life of the default and covers services necessary to complete a routine foreclosure action"; a cured-then-re-defaulted loan earns "a new allowable foreclosure fee ... without the need for excess fee approval"; excess fees via SF CPM (sfcpm_excess_fees@fanniemae.com per F-4-02); judicial services A–L and non-judicial A–K listed; "Full attorney fees cannot be considered to be earned until all of the steps necessary to complete the foreclosure and vest title in Fannie Mae, including any post-sale confirmation or ratification proceedings, have been completed"; costs excluded (title, filing, recording, process, publication); non-reimbursable: title-curative work caused by origination/servicing, defenses based on origination/servicing/payment disputes, overhead (travel, notary, copies, calls to the servicer). **Milestone invoicing (E-5-05):** judicial — title requested 30%, title reviewed 40%, complaint filed 50%, service started 60%, service complete 70%, affidavit/judgment prepared 80%, judgment to court 90%, bid reviewed/confirmed 95%, sale held/documents recorded 100%; non-judicial — title requested 30%, title reviewed 65%, notices started 75%, first legal 85%, sale package 95%, sale held/recorded 100%; "The servicer must not prorate foreclosure attorney fees between milestones"; interrupted proceedings: resume ⇒ balance to the allowable; restart ⇒ fees for the last milestone completed plus the new action's allowable; never resumed ⇒ last milestone completed; the servicer must "Pay the law firm for the fees and costs incurred by the law firm even if sufficient funds were not collected from the borrower" and include all legal fees/costs in reinstatement/payoff quotes; Fannie Mae reimburses uncollected amounts where "services were performed to protect Fannie Mae's interests; services were actually rendered; and the fees, costs ... were actual, reasonable, and necessary." **E-5-02 (02/12/2020):** review/approve fees and costs ("actual, reasonable, and necessary"), lawful services, permitted borrower charges, sufficiently detailed invoices, milestone alignment, "Pay the fees and costs and retain paid invoices." Reimbursement claims are filed in Property 360 within 60 days of the milestone (research/00b F6; 15.2).

**E-3.2-05 SLAs** (13.3): ack 2 BD; documents 3 BD; bids ≥5 BD; advances 10 BD. **DRA (research/00b F7; DRA User Guide 07/25/2023):** "Only firms can enter data in DRA"; servicers view only; firms report foreclosure/bankruptcy milestones (first event "referral received"); Event List/Definitions and State Milestones Cross Reference Table; firms need the DRA Attorney role.

(2) The blueprint's system is the "law-firm network"; the Guide's system of record for milestones is **DRA**, which the servicer cannot write to — the platform must reconcile. (3) "Automation class c (court actions)" is right for filings and appearances (attorney) but everything else (selection due diligence, oversight reviews, invoice review, milestone tracking, escalations) is AI-performed here. #### State machine
Firm: `candidate` → `due_diligence` → `form200_pending` → {`no_objection` → `training/lra` → `retained`} | `rejected`; `retained` → `suspended` (referrals stopped; matters may continue or transfer) → `retained` | `terminated` (all matters transferred). Matter: `referred` → `acknowledged` → `active` → (`transferred` | `completed` | `closed_reinstated/workout/payoff`). Invoice: `received` → `reviewing` → `approved`/`partially_approved`/`rejected`/`excess_pending` → `paid` → `claimed` (15.2). Only the partner `officer` can move a firm to `retained` (Form 200 is its certification) or `terminated`; Supermortgage's agent proposes with the package.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Selection**: a firm may receive referrals only when `attorney_retentions` for the state is `retained` (Form 200 "No Objection" + training + LRA) and F-2-04 evidence is current (E&O tier by the firm's annual foreclosure volume; Tier I ≥ $1M/$3M). Special Counsel (indemnification matters) bypasses selection but is notified to Fannie Mae and tracked with a transition-back obligation.
2. **Risk-based review frequency**: score = f(volume share, timeline performance (13.5 root causes), complaint rate, invoice exception rate, staffing-ratio changes, media/bar signals, prior findings); score bands ⇒ annual desk review (low), semi-annual (medium), on-site + semi-annual (high). All 15 elements evidenced.
3. **Invoice review** (deterministic): each fee line must match the matter's state/method schedule and the *last completed milestone* (cumulative percentage; no proration); a resumed action bills only the remainder; a restart bills the prior last milestone plus the new action per E-5-05; costs must be itemized with receipts (title, filing, recording, process, publication); excluded/overhead items rejected; technology fee ≤ $25 per loan per life of default; e-invoice fees ≤ $5 (+$5 with bankruptcy; $10 life of loan); excess fees route to SF CPM before approval; borrower-chargeable portion = permitted by note/security instrument/state law (2.7 caps; bankruptcy Rule 3002.1 for post-petition fees, 14.x). Worked example (illustrative allowable fee **A = $2,250.00** — placeholder until the exhibit is loaded): non-judicial; firm invoices at "first legal" 85% ⇒ cumulative fee earned $1,912.50; previously paid 65% ($1,462.50) ⇒ approve $450.00; costs: recording $38.00 (receipt), publication $412.75 (receipt), courier $22.00 (overhead ⇒ reject). Sale postponed for the servicer's late bid ⇒ continuance cost lines rejected as non-reimbursable (E-3.2-05) and charged to Supermortgage per allocation. Judicial example: interrupted at "service complete" (70%) by a completed modification ⇒ firm earns 70% × A; new default 9 months later ⇒ new allowable A applies without excess approval (E-5-04).
4. **Escalations**: categories map to the eleven A4-2.2-02 items plus A4-2.2-04 triggers; clock 2 BD from discovery (same day for breaches/fraud); email to loanservicing@fanniemae.com with POCs; Form 20 instead when the matter is litigation (13.7).
5. **Transfers**: capacity check of transferees; fee proration to avoid duplicates (transferor paid to last milestone; transferee's fee = allowable − amount paid, unless Fannie Mae directs otherwise); no transfer fees to Fannie Mae/borrower; 30-in-6-months counter per (from-firm, to-firm, state); post-sale transfers need prior approval.
6. **DRA reconciliation**: expected DRA events derived from internal milestones/instructions (referral received ≤2 BD after referral; first legal; sale scheduled; sale postponed; sale held; bankruptcy filed/relief); exceptions when missing, late (> 2 BD) or inconsistent (dates differ > 1 day); exceptions block 5.4 status-code submission only if the internal record is doubtful (agent judgment with evidence).
7. **Scorecard** (monthly, per firm/state): ack timeliness, first-legal timeliness vs state norm, milestone cadence, sale postponements attributable to the firm, DRA completeness/timeliness, invoice exception rate, contested-matter outcomes, complaints, 13.5 exposure share.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`foreclosure-ops` (with `qc-audit` for reviews) performs due diligence (evidence extraction from firm submissions), prepares Form 200 packages, runs invoice review, reconciles DRA, monitors firm risk signals, drafts escalations and transfer plans, and produces scorecards. Tools: `firm.get`, `documents.extract`, `fee_schedule.get`, `invoice.review` (rules engine), `dra.snapshot.import`, `attorney.message.send`, `email.send{fnma_legal}`, `ap.payment.create`, `escalation.create`. Decision record: `{firm_id, matter_id?, action ∈ {select, review, invoice, escalate, transfer, scorecard}, inputs, rule_results[], model_findings[], outcome, rationale, model_version}`. Guardrails: the model cannot approve an invoice line the rules reject; Form 200 certification is the partner `officer`'s (legally the servicer's certification to Fannie Mae); suspension/termination decisions are `officer` decisions with the AI package; litigation instructions are the `attorney`'s domain — the agent never instructs a firm on legal strategy beyond Guide-mandated instructions (hold/postpone/bid/certify). Escalations: `officer` (Form 200, suspensions, transfers, Fannie Mae escalations that assert the partner's position), `attorney` (contested matters, fee disputes over legal work), `fnma_portal_operator` (DRA, HomeTracker, P360), `human_agent` (firm relationship calls when the AI's outreach fails twice). AI-off path: queues in the ops console with the same rule engine.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Firm's E&O lapses mid-matter: pause referrals; existing matters continue; escalate within 2 BD if coverage cannot be evidenced.
- Named partner leaves (organizational change): 2-BD escalation; review triggered; possible re-Form 200 at Fannie Mae's direction.
- Firm data breach: 2-BD Fannie Mae escalation *and* the 36-hour Fannie Mae incident notice if Supermortgage/partner data is involved (Section 19; research/00a §6.2).
- Fannie Mae directs a transfer: implement per direction; no 5-BD notice needed (Fannie Mae-initiated), but reporting of transferee/matters required.
- Invoice for a milestone the DRA does not show: hold payment pending reconciliation (policy), but never beyond the 30-day payment SLA once the firm evidences the milestone.
- Restarted foreclosure after a failed trial: fee logic per E-5-05 (last milestone + new allowable).
- Bankruptcy filed: same firm (E-1.2-01) unless another jurisdiction; bankruptcy fee exhibit (14.x).
- Transfer-in of matters from another servicer's firms: firms must be retained by the *partner* (Form 200 non-transferable) — plan a 15-BD Form 200 cycle before cutover or a Fannie Mae-approved interim arrangement (1.3).
- Vendor invoice platform charging firms: prohibited (A4-2.2-03) — contract review gate before go-live.

### Open questions / decisions
1. Which entity's name appears on Form 200 and the LRA — default: the partner (servicer of record), with Supermortgage named as subservicer/administrator in the firm engagement letter.
2. Invoice platform: build in-house (`attorney_invoices`) vs. a default-industry vendor — default: in-house rules engine with direct-sourcing API for firms; any vendor must not charge firms.
3. Payment SLA — default: 30 days from approval; 10 BD review.
4. DRA access approach — default: daily portal export by `fnma_portal_operator`; request a servicer-side report/API from Fannie Mae at onboarding.
5. Review-frequency model thresholds — default: annual/semi-annual/on-site bands as above, partner-approved.
