### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Who is covered — 31 CFR 1010.100(lll) (eCFR 9/08/2026) — VERIFIED.** "Loan or finance company. A person engaged in activities that take place wholly or in substantial part within the United States in one or more of the capacities listed below, whether or not on a regular basis or as an organized business concern"; excludes "an individual employed by a loan or finance company or financial institution under this part"; "A loan or finance company is not a financial institution as defined in the regulations in this part at 1010.100(t)." (lll)(1)(i) "Residential mortgage lender. The person to whom the debt arising from a residential mortgage loan is initially payable on the face of the evidence of indebtedness or, if there is no such evidence of indebtedness, by agreement, or to whom the obligation is initially assigned at or immediately after settlement." (ii) "Residential mortgage originator. A person who accepts a residential mortgage loan application or offers or negotiates terms of a residential mortgage loan." (iii) residential mortgage loan: a loan secured by a lien on "A residential structure that contains one to four units."

**Program — 31 CFR 1029.210 (9/09/2026) — VERIFIED.** "(a) Each loan or finance company shall develop and implement a written anti-money laundering program that is reasonably designed to prevent the loan or finance company from being used to facilitate money laundering or the financing of terrorist activities. The program must be approved by senior management"; the company must "make a copy of its anti-money laundering program available to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network or its designee upon request." (b)(1): "incorporate policies, procedures, and internal controls based upon the loan or finance company's assessment of the money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with its products and services"; the policies "shall include provisions for complying with the applicable requirements of subchapter II of chapter 53 of title 31, United States Code and this part," "integrating the company's agents and brokers into its anti-money laundering program," and "obtaining all relevant customer-related information necessary for an effective anti-money laundering program"; (b)(2) designate "a compliance officer who will be responsible for ensuring that" the program is implemented, updated and that personnel are trained; (b)(3) "provide for on-going training of appropriate persons concerning their responsibilities under the program"; (b)(4) "provide for independent testing to monitor and maintain an adequate program," which "may be conducted by a third party or by any officer or employee of the loan or finance company, other than the person designated" as compliance officer; (c) "Compliance with this section shall be examined by FinCEN or its delegates, under the terms of the Bank Secrecy Act." **§1029.220 is [Reserved]** — no CIP rule for loan or finance companies (00a-fed §9). §§1029.310–.315 reserved (no CTR).

**SAR — 31 CFR 1029.320 (9/01/2026) — VERIFIED.** (a)(1): a loan or finance company "shall file with FinCEN, to the extent and in the manner required by this section, a report of any suspicious transaction" conducted or attempted by, at, or through the company involving or aggregating funds or other assets "of at least $5,000"; (a)(2): the transaction (i) "involves funds derived from illegal activity or is intended or conducted in order to hide or disguise funds or assets derived from illegal activity"; (ii) is designed "to evade any requirements of this part or any other regulations promulgated under the Bank Secrecy Act"; (iii) "Has no business or apparent lawful purpose or is not the sort in which the particular customer would normally be expected to engage"; (iv) "Involves use of the loan or finance company to facilitate criminal activity"; (b)(1) "by completing a Suspicious Activity Report ('SAR')"; (b)(2) filed "in accordance with the instructions to the SAR"; (b)(3) "A SAR shall be filed no later than 30 calendar days after the date of the initial detection … If no suspect is identified … may delay filing a SAR for an additional 30 calendar days … but in no case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 calendar days after the date of such initial detection"; (b)(4): for violations "requiring immediate attention, such as suspected terrorist financing," "immediately notify by telephone an appropriate law enforcement authority" in addition to filing; (c): retain a copy "for a period of five years from the date of filing the SAR"; supporting documentation "deemed to have been filed with the SAR" and made available on request to "FinCEN, or any Federal, State, or local law enforcement agency, or any Federal regulatory authority"; (d): "No loan or finance company … shall disclose a SAR or any information that would reveal the existence of a SAR," with exceptions for FinCEN/law-enforcement/regulatory disclosures and sharing within the corporate organizational structure, and a duty when subpoenaed to "decline to produce" and "notify FinCEN"; (e) safe harbor ("protected from liability for any such disclosure"); (f) examination by FinCEN or its delegates. **FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual (bank guidance, applied by analogy) — PARTIALLY VERIFIED:** "The 30-day (or 60-day) period does not begin until an appropriate review is conducted and a determination is made that the transaction under review is 'suspicious' within the meaning of the SAR regulation"; "the review should be completed in a reasonable period of time"; decisions not to file must be documented ("including the specific reason for filing or not filing a SAR"); continuing activity: "file SARs for continuing activity after a 90 day review with the filing deadline being 120 calendar days after the date of the previously related SAR filing" (also FinCEN SAR FAQ #16) — **FinCEN's SAR FAQs of October 9, 2025 make this advisory, not regulatory**: "A financial institution is not required to conduct a separate review—manual or otherwise—of a customer or account following the filing of a SAR" and institutions "may instead file SARs as appropriate in line with applicable timelines"; SM keeps the 90/120-day cycle as policy (re-verified 2026-09-11). **FinCEN BSA E-Filing (00b-orig N13) — VERIFIED:** discrete (online) filing or batch XML (manual attach or SDTM server-to-server); batch testing with TCC "TBSATEST" and "at least 25 forms/activities total"; every accepted batch receives an acknowledgement "that contains a BSA Identifier and all corresponding validation errors"; Supervisory User enrollment per filing organization (EIN).

**FinCEN rulemaking — VERIFIED (00a-fed §9).** Federal Register 2026-07033 (April 10, 2026; comments closed June 9, 2026; RIN 1506-AB72): "FinCEN does not intend to finalize the 2024 Program NPRM, and it should be considered withdrawn and superseded by this proposed rule"; the proposal applies to loan or finance companies and adds a documented risk assessment process ("identify, assess, and document"), a program "reasonably designed to ensure compliance with the BSA," independent testing, ongoing training, a compliance officer and board approval; no compliance date proposed; **no final rule as of 2026-09-10** — the 2012 four-pillar §1029.210 is operative; the program is designed to the proposed structure.

**OFAC — 31 CFR 501 Subpart C (9/03/2026) — VERIFIED.** §501.601: records "for at least 10 years after the date of such transaction"; for blocked property, "for the period of time that such property is blocked and for at least 10 years after the date such property is unblocked" (re-read 2026-09-11). §501.603(a): "Any U.S. person (or person subject to U.S. jurisdiction …)" "holding, unblocking, or transferring property blocked"; (b)(1): initial blocked-property reports "within 10 business days from the date that property becomes blocked," submitted through the "OFAC Reporting System (ORS), available on OFAC's website"; (b)(2) annual reports "annually by September 30" as of June 30; (b)(3) unblocking reports "within 10 business days from the date blocked property is unblocked"; required content includes the property description, location, account numbers, value in U.S. dollars, blocked date, the sanctions target and legal authority. §501.604: rejected-transaction reports by "Any U.S. person" "within 10 business days of the rejected transaction," through ORS, with the transaction description, parties, property, value, target and authority. OFAC Sanctions List Service: SDN and Non-SDN Consolidated lists as downloads (Advanced Sanctions Data Model and legacy flat files), public, no auth (00b-orig N4). Business days for OFAC are federal business days — unit `business_days_federal`.

**Fannie Mae A3-4-03, Preventing, Detecting, and Reporting Mortgage Fraud (12/10/2025) — VERIFIED.** "A seller/servicer must notify Fannie Mae if a reasonable basis exists to conclude any misrepresentation or fraud occurred in connection with the origination, sale, or servicing of the loan"; "Before notifying Fannie Mae about any misrepresentation or fraud, a seller/servicer should conduct appropriate due diligence"; the notice is due **"within 30 days" "using the self-report functionality in Loan Quality Connect."** Program: written policies to "have proper hiring practices in place," to check the "Federal Housing Finance Agency's Suspended Counterparty Program list before engaging the services of any contractor or vendor" (plus the GSA Excluded Parties List and HUD LDP list), to "aggressively sample loans that have a high risk for fraud as part of the quality control process," and procedures "to report suspected fraud to the proper authorities and to Fannie Mae"; "A record of activity under the internal audit and management control systems must be maintained and made available to Fannie Mae upon request." Fannie Mae's Mortgage Fraud Prevention page (fraud trends dated August 12, 2026): report suspected fraud through the "Suspected Mortgage Fraud Report" form (https://fims.my.salesforce-sites.com/MortgageFraudReport) or "1-800-2FANNIE (1-800-232-6643)"; resources include fraud alerts, red flags by stage, scheme characteristics and DU red-flag messages. A3-4-02 (08/07/2018) is "Data Quality and Integrity" ("All data entered into DU must be verifiable").

**Red Flags — 16 CFR 681 (9/03/2026) — VERIFIED.** §681.1(a): "This section applies to financial institutions and creditors that are subject to administrative enforcement of the FCRA by the Federal Trade Commission pursuant to 15 U.S.C. 1681s(a)(1)"; "creditor" per 15 U.S.C. 1681m(e)(4); "Red Flag means a pattern, practice, or specific activity that indicates the possible existence of identity theft"; "Service provider means a person that provides a service directly to the financial institution or creditor"; covered accounts include mortgage loans; (d): a written Identity Theft Prevention Program that identifies, detects and responds to Red Flags and is updated periodically; (e): initial board approval, board/senior-management oversight, staff training, "appropriate oversight of service provider arrangements"; Appendix A §VI: staff "should report to the board … at least annually, on compliance," addressing "The effectiveness of the policies and procedures … service provider arrangements; significant incidents involving identity theft and management's response; and recommendations for material changes." **12 CFR Part 1022 Subpart J is "[Reserved]"** (eCFR 9/08/2026) — there is no CFPB §1022.90. **FCRA §609(e), 15 U.S.C. 1681g(e) — VERIFIED:** a business entity that has provided credit to, or entered into a transaction with, a person who allegedly made unauthorized use of the victim's means of identification must provide "a copy of application and business transaction records in the control of the business entity" to the victim (or authorized law enforcement) "not later than 30 days after the date of receipt of a request," after identity verification and proof of the claim ("a copy of a police report" or the standardized identity-theft affidavit), "without charge," subject to the decline grounds in §609(e)(5) ("Authority to decline to provide information"; (e)(6) is the limitation on liability — re-read 2026-09-11).

**Fannie Mae A3-2-01 (12/10/2025) — VERIFIED (28.2):** compliance with "all federal, state, and local laws" expressly including OFAC and "Bank Secrecy Act/anti-money laundering requirements." **LL-2026-04** governs the `fraud-risk` agent as AI/ML use (28.1 quotes).

**Discrepancies vs blueprint.**
1. **"Fannie Mae fraud report 'promptly'" is wrong: A3-4-03 sets 30 days via Loan Quality Connect**, after due diligence, from the "reasonable basis" determination.
2. **A3-4-02 is Data Quality and Integrity**, not a fraud program topic; the program duties are all in A3-4-03.
3. **The Red Flags citation must be 16 CFR 681** for a non-bank creditor; 12 CFR 1022 Subpart J is reserved.
4. **No CIP rule exists for RMLOs** (§1029.220 reserved); identity verification is a program control, not a CIP obligation — the blueprint's "identity" expectations come from the program, Fannie Mae and Red Flags.
5. The SAR clock's anchor ("initial detection") is the determination that activity is suspicious after a reasonable review, not the alert — the blueprint states only the 30/60 days.
6. **OFAC records are retained 10 years**, not 5; annual blocked-property reports (Sept 30) and rejected-transaction reports (10 business days) are additional OFAC duties the row omits.
7. **FinCEN's 2024 program NPRM is withdrawn; the April 10, 2026 NPRM is pending** — the row's "independent testing; training" pillars remain, and a documented risk assessment and board approval are designed in ahead of any final rule.
8. Two entities may be RMLOs (partner and SM); SAR confidentiality (§1029.320(d)) constrains their information sharing — the blueprint treats reporting as a single-entity function.
9. Victim record requests (FCRA §609(e), 30 days) and identity-theft handling are obligations the row folds into "state."

### Operational prerequisites
- **Partner:** written AML program approved by senior management (with the proposed-rule elements: documented risk assessment, board approval), designated `bsa_officer`, training curriculum and records, independent-test schedule; BSA E-Filing Supervisory User enrollment for the partner (EIN); Red Flags ITPP approved by the board with SM listed as a service provider; Fannie Mae fraud policies per A3-4-03 (hiring, exclusion-list screening of contractors/vendors, QC sampling); LQC access for self-reports; a law-enforcement contact protocol (lead time: 4–8 weeks; before the first application).
- **SM:** its own AML program and `bsa_officer` (default under open question 1), BSA E-Filing enrollment, OFAC SLS ingestion (daily list refresh; change archive; fuzzy matching thresholds documented), ORS account for OFAC reports, `fraud` adapter (DataVerify DRIVE / FraudGuard-class) and identity vendors (22.6), exclusion-list screening of every appraiser, settlement agent, PDC vendor and employee (FHFA SCP, GSA EPL/SAM, HUD LDP) with refresh cadence, the `sar_confidentiality_acl` compartment, and the `fraud-risk` agent's evaluation suite (LL-2026-04).
- **Both:** a written information-sharing protocol that respects §1029.320(d) (case facts flow; SAR existence does not, unless joint filing); a joint-SAR procedure; training for anyone with SAR access.

### Build spec

#### Inputs and triggers
- Detection signals: `document_integrity_checks.result = fail` (22.1), `credit_alerts` (fraud alerts, SSN discrepancies — 22.2), identity-verification failures and synthetic-identity scores (22.6 `verifications{kind=identity|ssn}`), `verifications{kind=ofac}` potential matches (22.6), DU red-flag/observation messages (23.2), occupancy contradictions (22.6/28.1/28.2), appraisal-integrity findings and appraiser-identity issues (24.2), wire-fraud indicators (24.4/26.3 `wire-verification`), undisclosed-debt patterns (22.2), employer/asset fabrication, straw-buyer/non-arm's-length indicators, QC findings tagged `fraud_misrepresentation` (28.1/28.2), servicing-side signals after boarding (early payment default with occupancy red flags, insurance/mailing changes — 28.2, 6.x/9.x), borrower-reported identity theft, third-party tips (Fannie Mae alerts, law enforcement), employee tips.
- Transaction signals for AML: funds to close from unverifiable sources, structuring patterns in asset statements, third-party payoffs, cash-out proceeds directed to third parties, rapid refinance churn, foreign-sourced funds without documentation, sanctioned-country nexus.
- Schedules: daily OFAC list refresh and re-screen of every open application, every party (borrowers, sellers, settlement agents, appraisers, employers named as income sources, gift donors) and every payee before a wire; monthly exclusion-list refresh; annual program review, training, independent test, Red Flags board report; Sept 30 OFAC annual report if any property is blocked.
- Requests: FCRA §609(e) victim record requests; subpoenas and law-enforcement requests (including any FinCEN 314(a)/(b) matters — applicability to loan or finance companies **[UNVERIFIED]**, open question 6); Fannie Mae investigation requests (A3-4-03 "made available to Fannie Mae upon request").

#### State machine

`fraud_cases.triage_status`: `open` (signal aggregation crossed the case threshold or a tip arrived; `production_hold` applied when the application is open) → `under_review` (agent investigation: document re-examination, independent verifications, vendor fraud reports, party screening, interviews through existing channels) → `suspicious_determined` (`initial_detection_at` set; `subject_identified` set; SAR drafting starts; `fnma_reasonable_basis_at` set when the Fannie Mae standard is met — the two anchors can differ) | `not_suspicious` (documented rationale; hold released; conditions may still be opened by production for the underlying deficiency) ; `suspicious_determined` → `closed` after the SAR decision, the Fannie Mae report (if the loan was delivered or is deliverable) and the production outcome (denial for automatic-denial factor via 21.6 with reasons that never reveal a SAR; withdrawal; or, for a closed loan, servicing/QC follow-up). Guards: `suspicious_determined` requires an agent decision record reviewed by the `bsa_officer` within the triage SLA (the officer may also set it directly); `initial_detection_at` is immutable once set; `closed` requires `sar_decisions` present (file or no_file with rationale).
`sars.status`: `draft` → `officer_review` → `approved` → `filed` (discrete: officer submits; batch: officer approves and the batch is transmitted; either way the officer's act is the filing decision) → `acknowledged` (BSA ID stored) | `rejected` (validation errors → corrected → re-filed within the original deadline); `officer_review` → `no_file` (rationale in `sar_decisions`). Continuing activity: `acknowledged` → `continuing_review.scheduled` (90-day review) → new `sars` row with `continuing_activity_of` and `due_at = prior filed_at + 120 calendar days`.
`ofac_hits.disposition`: `potential` → `false_positive` (agent with documented match analysis; `bsa_officer` for scores above the auto-clear threshold) | `confirmed_match` → `blocked_property` (funds/loan proceeds/payments in SM's or the partner's possession that must be blocked — interest-bearing blocked account) or `rejected_transaction` (a transaction refused, e.g., a wire from a sanctioned party) → report → annual report while blocked → `unblocking` report if licensed/unblocked.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Case threshold.** A `fraud_cases` row opens when the weighted signal score ≥ the case threshold (policy; calibrated by 31.2), on any confirmed identity failure, on any OFAC `potential` hit not auto-cleared, on any QC `fraud_misrepresentation` finding, or on any tip. A case on an open application applies `SM_FRAUD_PRODUCTION_HOLD`; production continues to gather ordinary conditions but cannot close.
2. **Triage and "initial detection."** The agent's investigation must end in a triage decision within 10 calendar days (`SM_FRAUD_TRIAGE_SLA_10`), reviewed by the `bsa_officer`; `initial_detection_at` = the timestamp of `suspicious_determined` (the FFIEC reading: "does not begin until an appropriate review is conducted and a determination is made"). The 10-day SLA is a deliberate "reasonable period"; a case that cannot be resolved in 10 days is escalated, and the officer may set `suspicious_determined` on the facts then known.
3. **$5,000 test.** `amount_cents` = the funds or assets involved or aggregated (loan amount for origination fraud; the suspicious transaction amount for AML) — mortgage cases exceed $5,000 in practice; below the threshold a SAR is voluntary (policy: file when a subject is identified and the activity is a crime).
4. **SAR deadlines.** `due_at = initial_detection_at::date + 30 calendar days`; if `subject_identified = false`, `due_at = + 60`, but the SAR is filed as soon as a subject is identified. Worked: detection **Mon Oct 19, 2026 → SAR due Wed Nov 18, 2026**; no subject → Fri Dec 18, 2026. Detection Wed Nov 25, 2026 → due Fri Dec 25, 2026 (Christmas): the rule has no holiday adjustment — file by Thu Dec 24 (policy: never the next business day). Continuing activity: SAR filed Wed Nov 18, 2026 → 90-day review ends Tue Feb 16, 2027 → continuing SAR due Thu Mar 18, 2027 (+120).
5. **Fannie Mae report.** `fnma_reasonable_basis_at` is set when the due-diligence checklist (the agent's investigation record plus officer review) supports a "reasonable basis to conclude" misrepresentation or fraud "in connection with the origination, sale, or servicing of the loan"; `due_at = + 30 calendar days`; channel: LQC self-report ("Self Report of Lender QC Findings" with deficiency category Fraud/Misrepresentation) for delivered loans; the Suspected Mortgage Fraud Report form for undelivered loans and third-party schemes (appraisers, agents) where the partner elects to report; the partner `officer` approves. Worked: reasonable basis **Wed Nov 25, 2026 → due Fri Dec 25, 2026 → submitted Thu Dec 24** (policy); detection Mon Oct 19 with reasonable basis the same day → due Wed Nov 18 (coincides with the SAR date but is a separate act).
6. **OFAC screening and reports.** Screening (22.6) runs at application, on every new party, daily on open files, and within 1 day before funding/every outbound wire; matching uses name/alias/DOB/place/ID with a documented threshold; `false_positive` requires a written analysis; a `confirmed_match` on a borrower blocks the transaction (no loan); funds received from a blocked person are placed in a blocked interest-bearing account and reported within 10 business days; a refused wire to/from a sanctioned party is a rejected transaction reported within 10 business days. Worked: blocked Thu Nov 12, 2026 → `business_days_federal` count Nov 13 (1), 16 (2), 17 (3), 18 (4), 19 (5), 20 (6), 23 (7), 24 (8), 25 (9), 27 (10) (Thanksgiving Thu Nov 26 excluded) → **report due Fri Nov 27, 2026**; rejected transaction Fri Oct 9, 2026 → Oct 13 (1; Columbus Day Oct 12 excluded), 14, 15, 16, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 (9), 26 (10) → due Mon Oct 26, 2026. Annual report by Wed Sept 30, 2026 for property blocked as of June 30, 2026.
7. **Red Flags responses.** Each ITPP red flag maps to a response: CRA fraud alert/active-duty alert → contact the consumer as required by FCRA §605A before extending credit (22.2); address discrepancy → reconcile (22.6); document anomalies → integrity re-check and independent source; identity failure → decline path with an "unable to verify identity" reason (21.6 taxonomy) and, if identity theft, a `fraud_cases` row; notices from victims → `identity_theft_requests`. Annual board report: effectiveness, service-provider oversight (SM's controls), significant incidents, recommendations.
8. **Adverse action interplay.** A denial driven by fraud/identity findings uses the 21.6 reason taxonomy (e.g., "Unable to verify identity," "Unable to verify income") — never text that reveals a SAR or an investigation; comment 9(b)(2)-8 automatic-denial factors apply; Colorado explanation duties are satisfied with the same reasons.
9. **Confidentiality boundary.** SAR existence, drafts, filings and BSA IDs live only in `sar_confidentiality_acl`; the partner and SM see each other's SAR information only when (a) they file jointly, or (b) one is within the other's corporate organizational structure; case facts (documents, verification results, screening results) are shared freely for underwriting, QC and Fannie Mae purposes; a subpoena for SAR material triggers the "decline to produce and notify FinCEN" procedure by the `bsa_officer` and counsel.
10. **Independent testing and training.** Independent test annually (policy) by a tester other than the compliance officer (internal audit or a third party) covering the risk assessment, the `fraud-risk` agent's alerting quality (false-negative sampling against QC and servicing outcomes), SAR timeliness/quality, OFAC screening effectiveness (seeded test names), training coverage, record retention; findings tracked to remediation. Training at onboarding and annually for "appropriate persons," including engineers who change the agent's rules/prompts (LL-2026-04 overlap).

`fraud-risk` agent (tools: `aggregateSignals`, `openCase`, `applyHold`, `reexamineDocuments`, `orderIndependentVerification`, `screenParty`, `analyzeOfacMatch`, `interviewBorrower` (through app/voice with disclosure), `buildTimeline`, `assessScheme`, `computeAmount`, `recommendTriage`, `draftSarPackage`, `draftNarrative`, `draftOfacReport`, `draftFnmaReport`, `draftVictimRecordsPackage`, `scheduleContinuingReview`, `openOfficerEscalation`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end: it turns signals into a case, investigates with independent sources, screens every party, produces the evidence timeline and a triage recommendation, drafts the SAR (structured fields and a who/what/when/where/why/how narrative that "completely describe[s] the extent and nature of the suspicious activity"), the OFAC and Fannie Mae reports, and the victim-records package, and keeps every deadline visible. Decision record: `{case_id, signals, investigation_steps:[{tool, source, result, evidence_refs}], scheme_assessment, amount_cents, triage_recommendation, rationale, confidence, officer_review:{escalation_id, decision, at}, sar:{drafted_at, narrative_hash}, timers, rule_set_versions (bsa.1029; ofac.501; fnma.selling.2026-09-02; fcra.681), model_version, prompt_version, inputs_hash}`.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Signals below the case threshold on an application that is later denied for ordinary reasons: no case; signals retained for pattern analysis; QC discretionary sampling may still pick the loan.
- Subject identified after a 60-day-track SAR is filed with unknown subject: file a corrected/amended SAR (policy: within 30 days of identification) referencing the prior BSA ID.
- The partner and SM disagree on filing: each RMLO decides for itself; a joint SAR requires both; a unilateral filing is never disclosed to the other except as the rule permits.
- SAR rejected by E-Filing validation: correct and re-file the same day; the original deadline governs.
- Borrower is a victim of identity theft (application submitted by an impostor): case opened; the real person is contacted through verified channels; §609(e) records provided on request within 30 days; credit-bureau notifications per FCRA; the impostor's application is denied with "Unable to verify identity"; SAR evaluated (subject unknown → 60-day track).
- OFAC potential hit on a settlement agent or appraiser: transaction paused; the party replaced if the match cannot be cleared within the day; rejected-transaction report if a wire was refused.
- Blocked funds and the borrower's loan: the loan is not the blocked property if the borrower is not the target; counsel evaluates whether the transaction can proceed without the blocked payee; OFAC licence process.
- Fannie Mae requests the fraud record of activity (A3-4-03): produced from `fraud_cases` without SAR material (the SAR compartment is excluded; the underlying facts are not).
- Servicing-side detection after boarding (e.g., payment from a sanctioned account; occupancy fraud surfacing in loss mitigation): the same case model; servicing agents open the case; timers identical.
- Elder financial exploitation or trafficking indicators: SAR with the applicable activity type; adult-protective-services referral where state law permits; borrower-protection steps through `human_agent`.
- Continuing activity on a servicing relationship (repeated third-party payments): 90-day review cycle and 120-day filings until the activity stops.
- The 2026 NPRM is finalized: `bsa_program` already carries the risk assessment and board approval; the compliance date drives a version bump, not a redesign.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Is SM a "loan or finance company" in its own right?** Default: yes (its AI intake "accepts a residential mortgage loan application" in the partner's name) — SM maintains its own §1029.210 program and `bsa_officer`, enrolls in BSA E-Filing, and files jointly with the partner where both are involved; legal to confirm whether SM can instead be integrated solely as the partner's "agent" under §1029.210(b)(1) **[UNVERIFIED]**.
2. **SAR anchor policy.** Default: `initial_detection_at` = the `suspicious_determined` timestamp after a review capped at 10 calendar days; the officer may back-date to the first assembly of the facts when the facts were already conclusive.
3. **Discrete vs batch filing.** Default: discrete filing by the `bsa_officer` from the AI-prepared package; batch XML/SDTM when volume exceeds ~10 SARs a month.
4. **Fannie Mae reporting channel for undelivered loans.** Default: LQC self-report only for delivered/committed loans; the Suspected Mortgage Fraud Report form for third-party schemes at the partner's election; no A3-4-03 duty for a never-delivered loan **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — A3-4-03 speaks of "the loan" in connection with origination, sale, or servicing; the LQC form requires a Fannie Mae loan number]**.
5. **Independent-test frequency.** Default: annual (the rule sets none).
6. **314(a)/314(b) applicability.** Default: treat FinCEN 314(a) requests as not applicable to loan or finance companies (not "financial institutions" under §1010.100(t)) unless FinCEN says otherwise; register for 314(b) voluntary sharing only after legal review **[UNVERIFIED]**.
7. **Blocked-funds handling in a settlement flow.** Default: block and report; counsel applies for an OFAC licence; the loan proceeds not destined for the blocked payee continue; the borrower is told only that a payee could not be paid (no SAR disclosure).
8. **State mortgage-fraud reporting.** Default: `jurisdiction_rules.fraud_reporting` populated by 31.1 research before launch in each state **[UNVERIFIED]**.
