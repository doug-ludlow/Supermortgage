### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Good faith — §1026.19(e)(3)(i) (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026) — VERIFIED.** "An estimated closing cost disclosed pursuant to paragraph (e) of this section is in good faith if the charge paid by or imposed on the consumer does not exceed the amount originally disclosed under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section, except as otherwise provided in paragraphs (e)(3)(ii) through (iv) of this section." This is the zero-tolerance default for every charge not carved out below (creditor and affiliate charges, third-party services the consumer cannot shop for, transfer taxes, and — per comment 19(e)(3)(i)-5 as quoted on the CFPB TRID FAQ page (VERIFIED there): "The actual total amount of lender credits, whether specific or general (i.e., non-specific), provided by the creditor that is less than the estimated lender credits disclosed on the Loan Estimate is an increased charge to the consumer" — lender credits in the downward direction).

**10 percent aggregate — §1026.19(e)(3)(ii) — VERIFIED.** "An estimate of a charge for a third-party service or a recording fee is in good faith if: (A) The aggregate amount of charges for third-party services and recording fees paid by or imposed on the consumer does not exceed the aggregate amount of such charges disclosed under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section by more than 10 percent; (B) The charge for the third-party service is not paid to the creditor or an affiliate of the creditor; and (C) The creditor permits the consumer to shop for the third-party service, consistent with paragraph (e)(1)(vi) of this section." §1026.19(e)(1)(vi): "(A) ... A creditor permits a consumer to shop for a settlement service if the creditor permits the consumer to select the provider of that service, subject to reasonable requirements. (B) ... The creditor shall identify the settlement services for which the consumer is permitted to shop in the disclosures ... (C) ... If the consumer is permitted to shop for a settlement service, the creditor shall provide the consumer with a written list identifying available providers of that settlement service and stating that the consumer may choose a different provider for that service." (21.2 owns the list, `NTC_REGZ_1026_19E1VI_PROVIDER_LIST`.)

**Best information reasonably available — §1026.19(e)(3)(iii) — VERIFIED.** "An estimate of any of the charges specified in this paragraph (e)(3)(iii) is in good faith if it is consistent with the best information reasonably available to the creditor at the time it is disclosed, regardless of whether the amount paid by the consumer exceeds the amount disclosed under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section ... good faith is determined under this paragraph (e)(3)(iii) even if such charges are paid to the creditor or affiliates of the creditor, so long as the charges are bona fide: (A) Prepaid interest; (B) Property insurance premiums; (C) Amounts placed into an escrow, impound, reserve, or similar account; (D) Charges paid to third-party service providers selected by the consumer consistent with paragraph (e)(1)(vi)(A) of this section that are not on the list provided under paragraph (e)(1)(vi)(C) of this section; and (E) Property taxes and other charges paid for third-party services not required by the creditor."

**Revision reasons — §1026.19(e)(3)(iv) (A)–(F) — VERIFIED (each quoted).** Introductory text: "For the purpose of determining good faith under paragraph (e)(3)(i) and (ii) of this section, a creditor may use a revised estimate of a charge instead of the estimate of the charge originally disclosed under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section if the revision is due to any of the following reasons:"
- (A) "Changed circumstances cause the estimated charges to increase or, in the case of estimated charges identified in paragraph (e)(3)(ii) of this section, cause the aggregate amount of such charges to increase by more than 10 percent. For purposes of this paragraph, 'changed circumstance' means: (1) An extraordinary event beyond the control of any interested party or other unexpected event specific to the consumer or transaction; (2) Information specific to the consumer or transaction that the creditor relied upon when providing the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section and that was inaccurate or changed after the disclosures were provided; or (3) New information specific to the consumer or transaction that the creditor did not rely on when providing the original disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section." Examples used by this build (the illustrations in comment 19(e)(3)(iv)(A)-2 are **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — Supplement I not readable in full today; examples restated from the CFPB guide]**): (1) a hurricane closes the county recorder or the appraiser's territory; a title company goes out of business; (2) the borrower's stated income or the estimated property value used for the LE proves inaccurate, or the borrower becomes ineligible for the program after a job change; (3) the appraisal reveals a detached ADU requiring a complex-assignment fee or a rent schedule, a second lien surfaces on title requiring a subordination, the property turns out to be in a flood zone.
- (B) "The consumer is ineligible for an estimated charge previously disclosed because a changed circumstance, as defined under paragraph (e)(3)(iv)(A) of this section, affected the consumer's creditworthiness or the value of the security for the loan." Example: a lower appraised value moves LTV above 80 % so MI is now required (purchase fixture at 90 % already has it; a refinance at 70 % that appraises at $690,000 → LTV 81.2 % adds MI and its LLPA).
- (C) "The consumer requests revisions to the credit terms or the settlement that cause an estimated charge to increase." Examples: loan amount increase, product change, a borrower-paid lock extension (21.4), adding a borrower.
- (D) rate lock — 21.4 owns (`REGZ_1026_19E3IVD_LOCK_REVISED_LE_3BD`).
- (E) "The consumer indicates an intent to proceed with the transaction more than 10 business days, or more than any additional number of days specified by the creditor before the offer expires, after the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section are provided pursuant to paragraph (e)(1)(iii) of this section." §1026.37(a)(13)(ii) requires the LE to state "the date and time (including the applicable time zone) at which estimated closing costs expire" (21.2 owns `REGZ_1026_37A13_COSTS_EXPIRE_10BD`); comment 37(a)(13)-4 (CFPB eRegs, VERIFIED): once intent is indicated within the time specified, the expiration "are left blank on any subsequent revised disclosures".
- (F) new construction — "the creditor may provide revised disclosures to the consumer if the original disclosures ... state clearly and conspicuously that at any time prior to 60 days before consummation, the creditor may issue revised disclosures. If no such statement is provided, the creditor may not issue revised disclosures, except as otherwise provided in paragraph (e)(3)(iv) of this section." Out of scope (construction-to-permanent excluded by the operating model) but the engine supports the statement flag for new-construction purchases where the builder's completion is more than 60 days out.

**Timing — §1026.19(e)(4)(i)–(ii) — VERIFIED.** (e)(4)(i): "... the creditor shall provide a revised version of the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section or the disclosures required under paragraph (f)(1)(i) of this section (including any corrected disclosures provided under paragraph (f)(2)(i) or (ii) of this section) reflecting the revised estimate within three business days of receiving information sufficient to establish that one of the reasons for revision provided under paragraphs (e)(3)(iv)(A) through (F) of this section applies." General business days (§1026.2(a)(6) first sentence — (e)(4)(i) is not in the specific list; 00a-fed §1.2). (e)(4)(ii): "The creditor shall not provide a revised version of the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section on or after the date on which the creditor provides the disclosures required under paragraph (f)(1)(i) of this section. The consumer must receive any revised version of the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section not later than four business days prior to consummation. If the revised version of the disclosures required under paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section is not provided to the consumer in person, the consumer is considered to have received such version three business days after the creditor delivers or places such version in the mail." Specific business days for both the four-day limit and the presumption ((e)(4)(ii) is in the §1026.2(a)(6) list). Comment 19(e)(4)(ii)-1 (the CD may carry the revised estimate when fewer than four business days remain) — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — text not readable; the rule text of (e)(4)(i) already permits the (f)(1)(i) disclosures to be the revised disclosure]**.

**Cure and refund — §1026.19(f)(2)(v) — VERIFIED.** "If amounts paid by the consumer exceed the amounts specified under paragraph (e)(3)(i) or (ii) of this section, the creditor complies with paragraph (e)(1)(i) of this section if the creditor refunds the excess to the consumer no later than 60 days after consummation, and the creditor complies with paragraph (f)(1)(i) of this section if the creditor delivers or places in the mail corrected disclosures that reflect such refund no later than 60 days after consummation." Calendar days from consummation. A cure applied on the CD at closing (a lender credit equal to the excess) prevents the excess from being "paid by the consumer" at all; this build uses the closing-table cure first and the 60-day refund only for excesses discovered after consummation (25.1 post-closing test, 28.1/28.2 QC).

**Record retention — §1026.25(c)(1)(i) (00a-fed §15) — VERIFIED.** Evidence of compliance with §1026.19(e) "for three years after the later of the date of consummation, the date disclosures are required to be made, or the date the action is required to be taken"; the CD and related records five years ((c)(1)(ii)).

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. The row lists "expiration of LE" as a trigger; the rule's reason (E) is keyed to *when the consumer indicates intent* (more than 10 business days, or the longer period the creditor stated, after the LE is provided), not to the LE's age. 21.2's timer supplies the expiry instant; this process acts only when intent arrives after it.
2. The row omits (e)(3)(iv)(B) (ineligibility from a changed circumstance affecting creditworthiness or value) and the precondition for the 10 % bucket in (A) ("increase by more than 10 percent") — a changed circumstance that raises a shoppable third-party fee within the 10 % cushion does not permit a reset.
3. The row's "not later than 4 specific business days before consummation (else use CD)" hides the first sentence of (e)(4)(ii): no revised LE at all on or after the day the CD is provided, regardless of days remaining.
4. The 60-day duty is two duties: refund the excess, and deliver/mail a corrected CD reflecting the refund — both within 60 calendar days after consummation. 25.2 owns the corrected-CD notice; this process owns the refund timer and the corrected-CD trigger.
5. Baseline addendum §3 enumerates `changed_circumstances.kind` without a value for reason (B); this process adds `basis` (the regulation paragraph) and `kind='eligibility_change'`.
6. Fixture: the orchestrator's illustration uses a $150 → $180 recording fee; the 21.2 LE baseline for the Phoenix refinance carries $70 (Maricopa County table). Worked example 2 uses $150 → $180 for the Columbus, OH purchase fixture (Franklin County) instead; worked example 1 uses the 21.2 baseline. P&I is $3,402.62 (21.2 discrepancy 5).
7. The row cites "Official Interpretations 19(e)(3)/(4)" as if readable; Supplement I to Part 1026 could not be read in full today — every comment-based rule below is flagged, and 25.1 must confirm each before using it as a test oracle.

#### State machine

`changed_circumstances`: `detected` (from an event or a borrower request) → `evaluated_valid` | `evaluated_invalid` (agent decision with basis and evidence; invalid = the change is not one of (A)–(F), e.g., the creditor's own fee increase, a vendor's price increase without a transaction-specific cause, a decrease) → for valid: `revised_le_scheduled` → `revised_le_delivered` (`reflected_on='le'`) | `reflected_on_cd` (when the CD is already provided or fewer than four specific business days remain) → `closed`. Baseline reset happens at `evaluated_valid` for zero-tolerance items and, for bucket items, only when `ten_pct_threshold_test.exceeds`. Invalid changes never reset; the increase becomes a cure candidate.

`tolerance_tests`: `pass` | `cure_required` → `cured_at_closing` (lender credit on the CD) | `refund_required` (post-consummation) → `refunded`; `escalated` when `total_excess_cents` exceeds the policy threshold (`rule_sets.cure_review_threshold_cents`, default 50,000 = $500) pending `compliance-sentinel` review (the cure still posts; the review is root-cause).

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Tolerance class derivation (never entered by hand).** `zero` if `paid_to_kind ∈ {creditor, affiliate}` or `shop_status='cannot_shop'` or the item is a transfer tax or a lender credit; `ten_percent` if (`mismo_fee_type` is a recording fee) or (`paid_to_kind='third_party'` and `shop_status='can_shop_on_list'` and not affiliate); `unlimited` if the item is in (e)(3)(iii)(A)–(E): prepaid interest, property insurance premiums, escrow/impound deposits, third-party services from a provider the consumer selected off the list (`can_shop_off_list`), property taxes and third-party services not required by the creditor. A recording fee is `ten_percent` regardless of shopping (the rule names "a recording fee" separately from third-party services).
2. **Zero test.** For each zero item: `excess_i = max(0, actual_cents_i − baseline_cents_i)`. Lender credits: `shortfall = max(0, baseline_credit_cents − actual_credit_cents)`; both SM-borne-cost credits and rate-dependent credits count (21.2 rule 6 / 21.4 rule 5). Cents are integers; no rounding arises.
3. **10 % test.** `bucket = {fee_items | tolerance_class='ten_percent' at the comparison time}`; `baseline_sum = Σ baseline_cents` over the items that were in the bucket on the baseline LE **plus** the baseline of any item added later with a valid changed circumstance; `actual_sum = Σ actual_cents` over bucket items on the comparison disclosure (new items without a baseline count at $0 baseline); `limit_cents = floor(baseline_sum × 110 / 100)` — integer floor is the conservative (borrower-favorable) rounding when the product is not a whole cent (open question 1); `excess = max(0, actual_sum − limit_cents)`. An item the consumer moved off the list mid-stream leaves the bucket (it becomes `unlimited`) and its baseline leaves `baseline_sum` (comment-based — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**; open question 2).
4. **Unlimited test.** For each (e)(3)(iii) item: pass if `estimate_source` was the best information reasonably available at `estimated_at` (freshness ≤ `fee_table_max_age_days`; the input facts — rate, expected disbursement date, tax bill, insurance quote — were those known at the time); the engine records `reasonableness` ∈ {consistent, stale_source, unsupported}. Prepaid interest example: LE v1 estimated 19 days × $93.97 (365-day basis, 21.2) = $1,785.43 for a Nov 12 disbursement; at the CD the disbursement is Nov 12 and 6.125 % is unchanged → $1,785.43; a Nov 13 disbursement would give 18 days × $93.97 = $1,691.46 — a decrease, no issue; a later disbursement (Nov 9? no — earlier) is impossible after rescission; any increase is judged on whether the Oct 5 estimate was consistent with the information then known.
5. **Changed-circumstance validity.** `valid` requires: (i) basis ∈ (A)–(F) with the narrative mapping to the operative words; (ii) `information_received_at` evidence; (iii) for (A)/(B) a transaction- or consumer-specific fact (a vendor's general price increase is not specific to the transaction; the creditor's own estimation error is not "information ... that was inaccurate" unless the inaccuracy was in information relied upon, e.g., the borrower's stated value); (iv) for bucket items, `increase > threshold` where `threshold_cents = ceil(bucket_baseline_sum × 10 / 100)` and `increase = revised_bucket_sum − bucket_baseline_sum` (the rule says "increase by more than 10 percent"; ceil makes "more than" strict at whole cents).
6. **Baseline reset scope.** Zero items: only `affected_fee_item_ids` reset to the revised estimate. Bucket items: on a valid (A)/(B)/(C) with `exceeds=true`, the bucket baseline becomes the revised bucket sum (all bucket items' baselines are refreshed to their revised estimates) — the conservative alternative (reset only affected items) is open question 3. Basis (E) resets every item to current estimates (a new offer). Basis (C) resets the charges the request causes to increase. Basis (D) resets points/credits and other rate-dependent charges (21.4).
7. **Timing.** `revised_le_due = addBusinessDays(information_received_at::date, 3, 'creditor')` end of day. `latest_receipt = subtractBusinessDays(consummation_date, 4, 'regz_specific')`. If `today > latest_receipt − 3 specific days` and no confirmed e-delivery is possible, or if `cd_delivered_at` exists, the revised estimate is reflected on the CD (initial or corrected) instead; the (e)(4)(i) clock is satisfied by that CD.
8. **Decreases.** No revised LE is required for good-faith purposes; the engine may issue an informational revised LE when other terms change; the CD shows the lower actual.
9. **Cure.** At the CD stage, `cure_cents = Σ zero excess + lender-credit shortfall + ten_pct excess`; the CD carries a lender credit "Includes $X.XX credit for increase in closing costs above legal limit" (25.2's §1026.38(h)(3) statement) and the borrower's cash to close falls by `cure_cents`. Post-consummation: refund `cure_cents` by ACH/check within 60 calendar days and trigger 25.2's corrected CD.
10. **Who pays.** SM funds cures caused by SM's estimation or vendor selection (term sheet: SM bears third-party origination costs); the partner funds cures caused by partner-controlled charges (its own origination fees) — `tolerance_cures.funded_by`.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`disclosure` agent (tools: `classifyFeeChange`, `evaluateChangedCircumstance`, `resetBaseline`, `computeRevisedLEDueDate`, `checkFourDayRule`, `renderRevisedLE`, `deliverDisclosure`, `reflectOnCD`, `runToleranceTest`, `applyCure`, `issueRefund`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end: on every fee-affecting event the agent (1) updates `fee_items.current_amount_cents` with source and time; (2) decides whether the change is an increase in a class that matters; (3) maps the cause to a basis with a narrative quoting the operative words and attaches evidence; (4) for bucket items runs the threshold test; (5) computes due dates and the four-day/CD bar; (6) renders and delivers the revised LE or hands the estimate to 25.2; (7) at each stage runs the tolerance test, applies the cure on the CD, and after consummation issues refunds within 60 days. Decision record (`agent_decisions`): `{cc_id?, application_id, source_event_id, fee_item_ids[], class_before, class_after, basis, narrative, evidence_document_ids[], threshold_test, valid, revised_le_due_at, four_day_check{latest_receipt, cd_delivered_at, route}, test_id?, cure_cents?, rationale, model_version, prompt_version, confidence}`. Guardrails (never alone): never reset a baseline without a `changed_circumstances` row that has `valid=true`, evidence and a basis; never classify the creditor's or SM's own estimation error, a vendor's general price increase, or a fee the agent forgot to disclose as a changed circumstance; never reduce lender credits; never issue a revised LE on or after the CD date; never reset a bucket baseline when the increase is at or below 10 %; never mark a decrease as requiring a revised LE for good faith; never choose the refund route when the excess is known before consummation (cure at closing is mandatory in this build); never let a cure be funded by the borrower in any form (e.g., by raising another fee). Escalations: `compliance-sentinel` (cure above threshold; any late revised LE; any post-consummation refund), partner `officer` (refund release), `mlo_of_record` (only when rate-dependent terms change — 21.4). Borrower-facing: the revised LE is delivered with a plain-language change summary ("What changed and why") generated from the narrative — the summary is not a disclosure and must not contradict the form; the AI never tells the borrower a fee "cannot change" or "will not increase" beyond what the class allows. Human path: the disclosures console shows the fee timeline, lets a human declare/deny a changed circumstance with the same evidence requirements, and lets `officer` release refunds.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Multiple changed circumstances the same day: one revised LE may carry several `cc_ids`; the due date is the earliest of their clocks.
- A changed circumstance discovered after a revised LE is already in flight: a further revised LE is allowed until the CD date; the engine suppresses duplicate deliveries within the same business day by batching before render (but never past the earliest due date).
- Changed circumstance learned within four specific business days of consummation and after the CD: corrected CD (25.2) at or before consummation; if the change also moves the APR beyond 1/8 point, 25.2 imposes a new three-day wait — the agent warns the closer.
- Borrower selects an off-list title company after the LE: the title charges move to `unlimited`; the bucket baseline is recomputed without them; the CPL/endorsements required by the creditor from that provider are still `unlimited` (provider chosen by the consumer) — flagged comment-based rule.
- Affiliate discovered late (the settlement agent is a partner affiliate): the item is re-classified to `zero` from the baseline LE forward; any excess is cured.
- A fee disclosed in the wrong section (e.g., appraisal listed as shoppable): the class derivation from facts, not from the section, governs; the disclosure error is a QC finding (28.1) and the tolerance is applied as the facts require (zero).
- Seller-paid items on a purchase: `paid_by='seller'` items are excluded from the consumer's tolerance sums; a shift of a fee from seller to borrower at the CD is an increase to the borrower (basis (C) only if the borrower requested it).
- Escrow deposit changes at closing (aggregate analysis, 30.3): unlimited class; the LE estimate is judged on the information then known.
- Post-consummation discovery by the servicing platform (e.g., recording fee refund from the county): a refund of an overcharge is not a tolerance cure but a `fee_refund` (25.2 corrected CD within 30 days if the CD becomes inaccurate under (f)(2)(iii)); the engine distinguishes the two.
- Rescinded refinance (25.3): no consummation cure survives; fees refunded under §1026.23(d) — outside this process.
- Timer breach on the 3-day clock: the reset is withdrawn and the original baseline governs (rule 7 in Timers) — the borrower is cured, and the incident is root-caused.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Rounding of the 10 % limit and threshold.** Default: `limit = floor(baseline × 1.10)`, `threshold = ceil(baseline × 0.10)` in cents (borrower-favorable both ways). Alternative: round half up. Partner compliance to confirm.
2. **Off-list provider chosen mid-stream.** Default: the item moves to `unlimited` and leaves the bucket baseline; the shopping evidence (borrower's selection with date) is retained. Alternative (more conservative): keep the original baseline in the bucket and test the off-list charge under best information — flag for counsel.
3. **Bucket reset scope on a valid changed circumstance.** Default: refresh the whole bucket to revised estimates when the aggregate increase exceeds 10 %. Alternative: reset only the affected items. Counsel to confirm against comment 19(e)(3)(iv)(A)-1/-3 once readable.
4. **Basis (E) re-issue.** Default: a new LE with a new 10-business-day expiration and full re-pricing (treated as a new offer). Alternative: hold the borrower to the original estimates if pricing did not move (borrower-favorable but forfeits the reset).
5. **Cure funding split.** Default per term sheet: SM funds cures from SM estimation/vendor causes; partner funds cures on partner-controlled charges; disputes settled monthly.
6. **Informational revised LEs on decreases.** Default: issue only when another term changes; otherwise the CD shows the lower actual. Alternative: always re-issue so the borrower's "loan costs" picture stays current.
