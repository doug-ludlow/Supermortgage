### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Borrower identity and tax identification — B2-2-01, General Borrower Eligibility Requirements (09/03/2025). VERIFIED.** "Fannie Mae purchases or securitizes mortgages made to borrowers who are natural persons" (exceptions: inter vivos revocable trusts, HomeStyle Renovation, certain land trusts); borrowers must have "reached the age at which the mortgage note can be enforced in the jurisdiction where the property is located. There is no maximum age limit." "Fannie Mae requires that each borrower have a valid Social Security number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN), in addition to meeting existing legal residency and documentation requirements." "DU and Loan Delivery may identify data integrity issues pertaining to the borrower's Social Security number. Lenders must take steps to resolve any issues, including invalid format, numbers not issued, borrower age/issue date discrepancies, or Social Security numbers associated with deceased individuals. If a lender cannot resolve any Social Security number inconsistencies, the lender must validate the Social Security number directly with the Social Security Administration (SSA)." Methods: "Form SSA–89 (Authorization for the Social Security Administration to Release Social Security Number Verification), or Electronic Consent Based Social Security Number Verification (eCBSV) Service"; "If using a third-party vendor, the lender must ensure that the vendor goes directly to the SSA to validate the Social Security number using one of the two methods listed above."; "The lender must retain documentation in the loan file that evidences the validation of the Social Security number."; "The lender must deliver the loan with SFC 162 if the Social Security number was validated through the SSA but there is a discrepancy identified with the Social Security number in the credit report, DU, or Loan Delivery edits."; "If the Social Security number cannot be validated with the SSA, the loan is not eligible for sale to Fannie Mae." Related announcements listed on the topic: SEL-2025-07 (Sept 3, 2025), SEL-2022-10 (Dec 14, 2022), SEL-2020-05. ITIN documentation/underwriting specifics are not in the topic **[UNVERIFIED — no ITIN-specific documentation rule found]**.

**Non-U.S. citizens — B2-2-02, Non–U.S. Citizen Borrower Eligibility Requirements (07/28/2015). VERIFIED.** "Fannie Mae purchases and securitizes mortgages made to non–U.S. citizens who are lawful permanent or non-permanent residents of the United States under the same terms that are available to U.S. citizens." The lender determines status "based on the circumstances of the individual case, using documentation it deems appropriate" (as extracted); "By delivering the mortgage to Fannie Mae, the lender represents and warrants that the non–U.S. citizen borrower is legally present in this country." The topic page states "There are no recently issued Announcements related to this topic." The 2025 change in this area is HUD's Mortgagee Letter 2025-09 (effective May 25, 2025) removing non-permanent residents from FHA eligibility (Scotsman Guide, Sept 1, 2025 — secondary; FHA is out of scope); no Fannie Mae announcement on DACA/deferred action or on documentation of non-permanent residents was located **[UNVERIFIED — DACA FAQ status]**. **Reg B §1002.6(b)(7) (CFPB eRegs, current). VERIFIED.** "A creditor may consider the applicant's immigration status or status as a permanent resident of the United States, and any additional information that may be necessary to ascertain the creditor's rights and remedies regarding repayment." Comment 6(b)(7)-1: the creditor "may consider immigration status and differentiate, for example, between a noncitizen who is a long-time resident with permanent resident status and a noncitizen who is temporarily in this country on a student visa."

**Non-arm's-length transactions — B2-1.3-01, Purchase Transactions (11/05/2025). VERIFIED (as extracted).** "Non-arm's length transactions are purchase transactions in which there is a relationship or business affiliation between the seller and the buyer." Permitted for existing properties "unless specifically forbidden for the particular scenario, such as delayed financing"; for newly constructed properties, if the borrower has a relationship or business affiliation (ownership interest or employment) with the builder, developer or seller, "Fannie Mae will only purchase mortgage loans secured by a principal residence" — second homes and investment properties are ineligible. No special appraisal requirement is stated in the topic; gifts of equity from a related seller follow B3-4.3-05 (22.4) and make the loan ineligible for value acceptance (B4-1.4-10 ineligible list, 00a-fnma §4.12 — 24.1). No Selling Guide topic titled "identity of interest" exists; the concept is covered by "interested parties" (B3-4.1-02, 22.4) and by the fraud-prevention duties below.

**Occupancy — B2-1.1-01, Occupancy Types (10/05/2022). VERIFIED (as extracted).** A principal residence is "a property that the borrower occupies as their primary residence" (exceptions: active-duty military with orders, parents/guardians housing a disabled adult child, children housing a parent unable to work); second homes "must be occupied by the borrower for some portion of the year", are "restricted to one-unit dwellings", "must be suitable for year-round occupancy", "the borrower must have exclusive control over the property" and "cannot be subject to any agreements that give a management firm control"; "An investment property is owned but not occupied by the borrower." Fannie Mae's fraud resources: *Reverse Occupancy Scheme* (Jan 2016, rev. Dec 2018): "The subject properties are sold as investment properties"; "Purchasers are first-time home buyers with minimal or no established credit"; "Purchasers have low income but significant liquid assets"; "Purchasers make large down payments"; "Purchasers present 'rent free' letters stating they are not paying rent"; "If the loan doesn't make sense, don't do it!" *Getting It Right – Reverification of Occupancy* (October 2025): red flags include "Borrower has other rental properties", "downgrading to a notably smaller or less expensive home", "Significant or unrealistic commuting distance", insurance policy inconsistent with the disclosed occupancy, and a documented address different from the subject; reverification sources include the insurance policy, internet searches, third-party tools for undisclosed mortgages and residence validation (driver's license, voter and vehicle registration), homestead-exemption applications, door-knock services, servicing mailing-address changes and returned mail.

**Undisclosed real estate — B2-2-03, Multiple Financed Properties (11/05/2025) (23.2 owns the count rule); B3-6-01 (22.5); A3-4-02.** The lender must include every financed property in the count and every property's PITIA/rental in DTI (22.5); "All data entered into DU must be verifiable" (A3-4-02, 22.1). MERS, credit-report mortgage tradelines, public records and fraud-tool property searches are the detection sources (practice; no Guide-prescribed source).

**Fraud prevention and reporting — A3-4-03, Preventing, Detecting, and Reporting Mortgage Fraud (12/10/2025). VERIFIED (extends 22.1).** Written policies must include "proper hiring practices" and screening of employees, contractors and vendors against GSA's System for Award Management (SAM), HUD's Limited Denial of Participation list and "the Federal Housing Finance Agency's Suspended Counterparty Program list before engaging the services of any contractor or vendor"; lenders should "aggressively sample loans that have a high risk for fraud as part of the quality control process." Reporting: when "a reasonable basis exists to conclude any misrepresentation or fraud occurred in connection with the origination, sale, or servicing of the loan", the seller/servicer must notify Fannie Mae "within 30 days using the self-report functionality in Loan Quality Connect" (portal-only — 00b-orig F1); due diligence precedes the report. **A3-4-02, Data Quality and Integrity (08/07/2018). VERIFIED (22.1).** "The lender must have adequate procedures in place to validate the integrity of specific data for each underwriting recommendation"; representations and warranties for "misstatements, misrepresentations, omissions, and data inaccuracies" survive for the life of the loan.

**FCRA fraud and active-duty alerts — 15 U.S.C. 1681c-1(h) (uscode.house.gov, current through Sept. 9, 2026). VERIFIED.** With an initial or active-duty alert on the file, a user "may not establish a new credit plan or extension of credit ... unless the user utilizes reasonable policies and procedures to form a reasonable belief that the user knows the identity of the person making the request"; if the consumer specified a telephone number, the user must contact that number or "take reasonable steps to verify the consumer's identity and confirm that the application for a new credit plan is not the result of identity theft." Extended alerts: no new credit "unless the user contacts the consumer in person or using the contact method" the consumer designated. Initial alert one year; extended alert seven years. **Address discrepancies — 12 CFR 1022.82 (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026). VERIFIED.** Applies to "users of consumer reports that receive a notice of address discrepancy from a consumer reporting agency"; the user must have policies to "form a reasonable belief that a consumer report relates to the consumer about whom it has requested the report" (comparing to CIP-type records, internal records, third-party sources, or verifying with the consumer) and, when it establishes a continuing relationship, furnish "an address for the consumer that the user has reasonably confirmed is accurate" to the CRA "as part of the information it regularly furnishes for the reporting period in which it establishes a relationship." **Red Flags — 16 CFR 681 (eCFR 9/03/2026; 00a-fed §7). VERIFIED.** Applies to "financial institutions and creditors that are subject to administrative enforcement of the FCRA by the Federal Trade Commission"; a "covered account" includes a mortgage loan; each creditor "must develop and implement a written Identity Theft Prevention Program" with four elements (identify relevant red flags, detect them, respond appropriately, update the program), board/senior-management approval, staff training and service-provider oversight. **12 CFR Part 1022 Subpart J is "[Reserved]" (eCFR 9/08/2026)** — there is no "12 CFR 1022.90" Red Flags rule for creditors; §1022.82 is the CFPB-side companion duty.

**BSA/AML — 31 CFR 1010.100(lll) (eCFR 9/09/2026); 31 CFR 1029.210 (9/09/2026); 31 CFR 1029.220 [Reserved]; 31 CFR 1029.320 (9/01/2026). VERIFIED.** A "loan or finance company" is "A person engaged in activities that take place wholly or in substantial part within the United States in one or more of the capacities listed below, whether or not on a regular basis or as an organized business concern" — including a "Residential mortgage lender" ("The person to whom the debt arising from a residential mortgage loan is initially payable on the face of the evidence of indebtedness ... or to whom the obligation is initially assigned at or immediately after settlement") and a "Residential mortgage originator" ("A person who accepts a residential mortgage loan application or offers or negotiates terms of a residential mortgage loan"). §1029.210(a): "develop and implement a written anti-money laundering program that is reasonably designed to prevent the loan or finance company from being used to facilitate money laundering or the financing of terrorist activities"; (b)(1) policies, procedures and internal controls "based upon the loan or finance company's assessment of the money laundering and terrorist financing risks associated with its products and services", integrating agents and brokers; (b)(2) a designated compliance officer; (b)(3) ongoing training of employees, agents and brokers; (b)(4) "independent testing to monitor and maintain an adequate program, including testing to determine compliance of the company's agents and brokers with their obligations" — testing "may be conducted by a third party or by any officer or employee of the loan or finance company, other than the person designated in paragraph (b)(2)"; senior-management approval. §1029.220 is [Reserved] — no CIP rule for RMLOs (31 CFR 1020.220 binds banks). SAR: report "any suspicious transaction relevant to a possible violation of law or regulation" involving "funds or other assets of at least $5,000" "conducted or attempted by, at, or through" the company where the company knows, suspects or has reason to suspect the transaction (i) involves funds from illegal activity or is intended to disguise them, (ii) is designed to evade BSA requirements, (iii) "has no business or apparent lawful purpose" and no reasonable explanation, or (iv) involves the use of the company to facilitate criminal activity (as extracted); "A SAR shall be filed no later than 30 calendar days after the date of the initial detection ... If no suspect is identified ... may delay filing a SAR for an additional 30 calendar days ... but in no case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 calendar days after the date of such initial detection" (00a-fed §9); immediate telephone notification to law enforcement for ongoing violations; retain the SAR and supporting documentation "five years from the date of filing"; confidentiality — the SAR and any information revealing its existence may not be disclosed except to FinCEN/law enforcement/regulators; safe harbor under 31 U.S.C. 5318(g)(3). **FinCEN NPRM (Federal Register 2026-07033, April 10, 2026; FINCEN-2026-0034) — 00a-fed §9. VERIFIED.** "FinCEN does not intend to finalize the 2024 Program NPRM, and it should be considered withdrawn and superseded by this proposed rule"; proposed elements add a documented risk assessment process and board approval; comments closed June 9, 2026; no final rule as of 2026-09-10 — the four-pillar rule remains operative. **FinCEN BSA E-Filing — 00b-orig N13. VERIFIED.** Discrete (web) or batch XML (web upload or SDTM), acknowledgement with a BSA Identifier; Supervisory User enrollment by EIN.

**OFAC — 31 CFR 501.601, .603, .604 (eCFR current as of 9/03/2026). VERIFIED.** Reports are required of "Any U.S. person (or person subject to U.S. jurisdiction ...), including a financial institution, holding, unblocking, or transferring property blocked"; initial blocked-property reports "shall be filed within 10 business days from the date that property becomes blocked" with the filer's identity, a description of the transaction and parties, the blocked person, the property's description/location/value, the date blocked, actions taken and the legal authority; annual reports "by September 30" as of June 30; rejected-transaction reports "within 10 business days of the rejected transaction prohibited by the provisions of this chapter" with equivalent content; reports "must be filed electronically through the OFAC Reporting System (ORS)"; records "for at least 10 years after the date of such transaction." Sanctions List Service (00b-orig N4): SDN and Non-SDN Consolidated lists as public downloads in the Advanced Sanctions Data Model and legacy flat-file formats; change notifications by e-mail. 28.4 owns `OFAC_501_603_BLOCKED_REPORT_10BD` and the §501.604 rejected-transaction timer; this process detects, blocks/rejects and hands off.

**SSA CBSV — 00b-orig N3. VERIFIED.** CBSV confirms whether "name, date of birth, and SSN match SSA's records" (yes/no plus death indicator) and "does not verify identity, citizenship, employment eligibility"; "$5,000" enrollment, "$2.25 per verification request"; online (≤ 10 per submission, immediate) or the CBSV Web Service (machine-to-machine); the company "must be in physical possession of the signed consent forms (Form SSA-89) prior to requesting SSN verifications," retained "five (5) years"; Form SSA-89 versions: 05-2025 for eCBSV, 04-2023 for CBSV; eCBSV eligibility for a non-bank lender **[UNVERIFIED]**. **DU SSN messages.** B2-2-01 confirms DU and Loan Delivery raise SSN data-integrity messages (format, not issued, age/issue-date, deceased); the message catalog is login-gated **[UNVERIFIED — message IDs]**; the DU Potential Red Flag Messages matrix (7.22.24) lists "Excessive submissions can indicate improper manipulation of loan application data" and "the occupancy was modified from investor to primary."

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. **"12 CFR 1022.90" does not exist** — Reg V Subpart J is reserved; the Red Flags rule for the partner is 16 CFR 681 (FTC jurisdiction over non-bank creditors), with §1022.82 (address discrepancies) as the CFPB-side duty.
2. **CIP does not apply** (31 CFR 1029.220 [Reserved]; PATRIOT Act §326 binds banks via 1020.220) — but SM is itself a "residential mortgage originator" and hence a "loan or finance company" needing its own §1029.210 program, which the row does not contemplate.
3. **B2-2-01 accepts ITINs** and requires SSA validation only when a DU/Loan Delivery SSN inconsistency cannot be resolved (SFC 162 on a residual discrepancy; ineligible if the SSA cannot validate) — not a universal CBSV mandate. The row's "B2-2-02 (identity, SSN/ITIN)" cite is wrong: identity/SSN is B2-2-01; B2-2-02 is non-U.S. citizens.
4. **B2-2-02 is unchanged since 07/28/2015**; the 2025 non-permanent-resident change was FHA's (ML 2025-09, eff. May 25, 2025), out of scope.
5. **Non-arm's-length is B2-1.3-01** (purchase transactions), not "B2-1.4-?"; the only categorical restriction is new construction with a builder/developer/seller relationship → principal residence only.
6. **OFAC reporting covers both blocked property (§501.603) and rejected transactions (§501.604)**, each within 10 business days via ORS; recordkeeping is 10 years (§501.601), not the 5 years a 2024 spec would assume.
7. **SAR "detection" is the conclusion of the review**, not the first alert; the row's "from detection" needs the policy anchor defined below; SAR records are kept 5 years and the SAR's existence is confidential — which constrains every borrower-facing artifact and the ECOA reasons.
8. **FinCEN's June 2024 program NPRM is withdrawn**; the April 10, 2026 NPRM is pending — design to the five-element structure but the operative rule is the 2012 four-pillar rule.
9. **A3-4-02 is data quality/integrity**; the fraud program and 30-day Loan Quality Connect self-report are A3-4-03 (owned by 28.4 as `FNMA_A3_4_03_FRAUD_SELF_REPORT_30`).
10. **The row omits the FCRA §605A(h) contact duties** for fraud/active-duty alerts and the A3-4-03 exclusion-list screening (SAM, HUD LDP, FHFA SCP) of contractors and vendors.

### Operational prerequisites
- **Partner (LoR):** written AML program under §1029.210 (risk assessment, designated compliance officer = `bsa_officer`, training, independent testing covering SM as agent; senior-management approval) with a written agency agreement designating SM to prepare SARs and OFAC reports; FinCEN BSA E-Filing Supervisory User enrollment; OFAC compliance policy; written Identity Theft Prevention Program under 16 CFR 681 approved by the board, naming SM as service provider with oversight; §1022.82 address-discrepancy procedures; A3-4-03 fraud policy incl. hiring/exclusion-list screening; Fannie Mae Loan Quality Connect self-report access (`fnma_portal_operator`/`officer`); Form SSA-89 authorizations in the partner's name (the CBSV user is SM or the partner — Q3). Lead time: program documents 4–8 weeks; FinCEN enrollment days; independent test annually.
- **SM:** its own §1029.210 program (Q1) with a designated compliance officer, training and independent testing; identity vendor (document authentication + biometric liveness/face match, IAL2-class) with evaluation suites (LL-2026-04; 31.2 monitors error rates by demographic proxy); SSA CBSV enrollment ($5,000; EIN; Web Service certification) or vendor going directly to SSA; OFAC SLS ingestion job (daily; version pinned per screen) and fuzzy-match screener with tuned thresholds and documented false-positive resolution; fraud-tool contract (DRIVE/FraudGuard/LoanSafe-class) with API access; MERS System lookup access (shared adapter, 26.4); exclusion-list feeds (SAM, HUD LDP, FHFA SCP); `cases` (shared) fraud case type; retention classes `bsa_sar_5y`, new `ofac_records_10y` (defined here), `ssa_89_5y` (defined here). Lead time: CBSV weeks–months; vendors 4–8 weeks.
- **Both:** E-SIGN consent scope `identity` for SSA-89 e-signature (SSA rules for the 05-2025 eCBSV form vs paper 04-2023 CBSV form); TCPA consent for AI-voice callbacks used in fraud-alert contact; `jurisdiction_rules` for state identity/AI-disclosure statutes (CO SB 26-189, CA ADMT — 31.2).

### Build spec

#### Inputs and triggers
- `application.received` / `application_borrowers` rows with identity data captured at 20.3/21.1 (government ID images, selfie/liveness session id, SSN/ITIN, DOB, citizenship/residency declaration, addresses, phone), declarations (occupancy, other property owned, relationship to seller), `purchase_contracts` parties (seller, listing/selling agents, builder), gift donors (22.4), settlement agent/title company (24.4), appraiser/PDC vendor (24.1), employer (22.3).
- `credit.report.received` (22.2): fraud/active-duty alerts, address discrepancy notices, name/SSN/DOB mismatches, mortgage tradelines, inquiries; `credit.udm.alert.received`.
- `document.integrity.failed` / `.flagged` (22.1); `du.findings.received` red-flag messages (SSN, occupancy modified, excessive submissions, casefile reuse); `asset.deposit.unsourced`, `gift.donor.interested_party_suspected`, `ipc.undisclosed.suspected` (22.4); `liability.discovered{mortgage_no_reo}` (22.5); `valuation.received` (24.2: CU flags, flips); `title.commitment.received` (24.4: vesting/seller mismatch, recent transfers).
- Schedules: OFAC list refresh (daily), party re-screen at every party change and 1 `business_days_creditor` before consummation; fraud-tool refresh before CTC; occupancy re-check at CD.

#### State machine

Per borrower identity: `captured` (20.3/21.1) → `verifying` → `verified{ial2}` | `inconclusive` (second method: supervised remote session or in-person at closing with notary credential analysis — 26.2) | `failed` → `investigation.opened`. SSN: `unchecked` → `consistent` (no DU/LD/credit inconsistency) | `discrepancy_open` → `resolved_internally` | `ssa_validation_ordered` → `validated` (with `sfc_162_required` if a discrepancy persists) | `not_validated` → loan ineligible → 21.6 via `underwriting_reviewer`. Party screening: `pending` → `clear` | `potential_match` → `false_positive_resolved` | `match_true` → `blocked/rejected` (→ 28.4; application `fraud_hold`). Investigation: `opened` → `evidence_gathering` → `concluded{...}` → `handed_off` (28.4) | `closed`. Application-level: `screening_open` → `screening_clear` (all borrowers verified, SSN consistent/validated, legal presence established, OFAC clear for all parties, fraud-tool alerts dispositioned, occupancy consistent, REO reconciled, non-arm's-length eligible) → `rescreen_due` (party change or pre-closing) → `screening_final` at consummation; `fraud_hold` overlays any state and blocks `submitDu` (new casefiles), `issueCD`, `consummate`, `funding.authorized` until released by the `fraud-risk` agent's concluded investigation (or `bsa_officer` for OFAC matches). Terminal: `screening_final`; `declined` (21.6); `withdrawn`.

#### Business rules and calculations

**R1 — Identity level.** Each borrower must reach `ial2_remote_doc_biometric`: government ID authenticated (security features, MRZ/barcode consistency, not expired at the screen date), liveness passed, face match ≥ vendor threshold, and data match on name/DOB/address against the application and the credit header; a failed or inconclusive result falls to a supervised remote session, then to in-person verification at closing (notary credential analysis, 26.2) — but `submitDu` for a new casefile waits for at least a supervised remote pass. Expired IDs are not accepted; an ID expiring before the note date is flagged for re-verification at closing.

**R2 — SSN/ITIN.** `ssn_discrepancy_open = true` when any of: DU/Loan Delivery SSN message; credit report header SSN/name/DOB variance; SSA-issued-before-DOB or "not issued" indicators from the fraud tool; deceased indicator. Resolution ladder: (1) documentary (SSN card/W-2/1099/tax transcript name match, ID); (2) SSA validation via CBSV Web Service (SSA-89 signed and in SM's possession; $2.25 per request) or eCBSV; (3) if the SSA cannot validate → ineligible. `sfc_162_required = validated AND discrepancy persists in credit/DU/Loan Delivery` → 29.3 delivers SFC 162. ITIN borrowers: eligible under B2-2-01; the ITIN is validated documentarily (IRS assignment letter, tax transcripts under the ITIN) — no SSA path exists (policy Q4); DU/Loan Delivery data-integrity edits for ITINs **[UNVERIFIED]**.

**R3 — Legal presence.** For `lawful_permanent_resident`: permanent resident card (or I-551 stamp); for `non_permanent_resident`: EAD, or visa with I-94/I-797 evidence; `expires_before_note_date = evidence_expires_on < scheduled_note_date` → require renewal/extension evidence (a pending extension receipt is accepted only under partner policy — Q5). Reg B §1002.6(b)(7) permits considering immigration status only for "rights and remedies regarding repayment"; the assessment records that rationale and never a proxy for national origin (31.2 monitoring). *Purchase fixture:* Borrower B is a non-permanent resident with an EAD expiring 2027-03-31 (21.1); note date Wed Nov 18, 2026 → `expires_before_note_date = false` → `legally_present`; the partner's B2-2-02 representation is supported by the EAD image, the identity verification and the SSN consistency check.

**R4 — OFAC screening.** Screen every party (borrowers, non-borrowing spouse on title, seller, agents, settlement agent/title company, appraiser/PDC, gift donor, employer, DPA provider, POA agent, trustee) against the SDN and Non-SDN Consolidated lists; record `ofac_list_version`; fuzzy match (name transliteration, DOB ±, address/nationality) with thresholds tuned so that potential matches are resolved by comparing at least two non-name identifiers; `match_true` → reject the transaction (no funds accepted) or block property already in SM's/the settlement agent's possession (e.g., an EMD or payoff funds) → 28.4 files within 10 business days via ORS; records retained 10 years. *Worked example (purchase fixture variant):* Wed Nov 4, 2026 the seller's LLC member is a confirmed SDN match (DOB and passport number match) → the purchase is rejected that day (`ofac.match.confirmed{rejected}`, `fraud.hold.placed`) → 28.4's §501.604 report is due 10 `business_days_federal` later: Nov 5, 6, 9, 10, (Nov 11 Veterans Day excluded), 12, 13, 16, 17, 18, 19 → **Thu Nov 19, 2026**. If instead the EMD of $5,000.00 already held by the settlement agent were the blocked property as of Fri Nov 6, the §501.603 initial report would be due **Mon Nov 23, 2026**. The borrower is told only that the transaction cannot proceed (no SAR-related content); ECOA adverse-action reasons come from 21.6's approved list.

**R5 — Fraud-alert contact (FCRA §605A(h)).** Initial/active-duty alert with a phone number → the agent contacts that number (AI voice with automation disclosure and TCPA-compliant purpose; recorded in `fraud_alert.contact.completed` with timestamp, number, outcome) — or takes documented reasonable steps (identity verification + callback to a number on the credit header); extended alert → contact by the consumer's designated method only. *Refinance fixture:* the Oct 5, 2026 tri-merge shows an initial fraud alert on TransUnion (placed Sept 2026) with a phone number → contact completed Tue Oct 6 at 10:14 MST with the borrower confirming the application → gate open before the Oct 7 conditional approval; had the borrower been unreachable through Tue Oct 13 (5 business days; Columbus Day Oct 12 excluded), the file would route to 21.6 as incomplete.

**R6 — Red Flags program logic.** Catalog (ITPP Appendix A-style categories): CRA alerts (fraud/active-duty alerts, credit freezes, address discrepancies, unusual inquiry velocity), suspicious documents (22.1 integrity failures, altered IDs, inconsistent photos), suspicious PII (SSN not issued/deceased/issued before DOB, address = mail drop/prison/commercial, phone = pager/answering service, SSN shared across applications in SM's book), unusual activity (application data changes after DU, multiple applications with the same property/parties), notices (victim, law enforcement, CRA). Each detection creates a `red_flag_events` row; response within 1 business day; the annual program report to the partner's board summarizes events, responses and service-provider oversight.

**R7 — Occupancy risk score.** `signals` scored (illustrative weights; 31.2 validates): distance subject→employer > 100 km (+3), current residence retained with no rental history and proposed rent needed to qualify (+3), purchase price or size materially lower than the current residence (+2), borrower owns ≥ 2 other rentals (+2), rent-free letter (+2), DU "occupancy modified from investor to primary" (+3), insurance quoted as landlord/DP-3 policy (+3), mailing address ≠ subject after closing (+2), first-time buyer with large down payment and low income buying an "investment" property (+3, reverse-occupancy pattern). Score ≥ 5 → `needs_explanation` (borrower's written statement and corroboration: employer confirmation of remote work, school enrollment, lease termination); ≥ 8 or any contradiction → `inconsistent` → investigation. *Refinance fixture:* Phoenix primary residence, employer in Tempe (16 km), homeowner's policy HO-3, mailing address = subject → score 0 → `consistent`. *Purchase fixture variant:* the borrowers keep their current Columbus condo "as a rental" with no lease and need $1,800/month rent to qualify, buy a smaller house 3 km away, and the hazard quote is a DP-3 landlord form → +3 +2 +3 = 8 → `inconsistent` → investigation; 22.5 excludes the rent until B3-3.8 evidence exists; if the explanation fails, the loan is declined via 21.6 (reason: unable to verify occupancy/information provided cannot be verified) — never re-cast as an investment loan at the agent's initiative.

**R8 — Undisclosed REO.** For each borrower: mortgage/HELOC tradelines on the credit report, MERS System lookup by borrower (MIN search — 26.4 adapter), the fraud tool's public-record property search, county assessor data for declared addresses, SM's own subserviced book (internal data; FCRA-permissible as the servicer's own account data), and DU messages; any property not on the REO schedule → `reo.discrepancy.detected` → borrower explanation and documents → either added to `application_reo` (22.5 recomputes DTI; 23.2 recounts financed properties; 22.4 recomputes reserves) or documented as not the borrower's (e.g., co-signed for a relative → contingent-liability rules). *Refinance fixture variant:* the Oct 5 report shows a $210,000 mortgage tradeline (opened 2021) absent from the REO schedule; MERS returns a MIN on a Mesa, AZ property vested in the borrower → borrower explains an inherited rental → 22.5 adds PITIA $1,640.00 and rental income per B3-3.8 (grandfathered structure for this Oct 5 application) → DTI recomputed; 23.1 resubmits; the omission is logged as an unintentional misstatement (no SAR basis) unless the pattern repeats.

**R9 — Non-arm's-length.** Relationship detection: seller name vs. borrower surname/prior addresses/employer; agent = borrower; builder = employer/ownership; title vesting history. Existing property → eligible with standard documentation; gift of equity → 22.4 rules and value-acceptance block (24.1 orders an appraisal); new construction with a relationship → `occupancy = primary` required; `investment/second_home` → ineligible → restructure only if the true occupancy is primary (never coach an occupancy change). *Purchase fixture variant:* the borrowers buy their parents' existing house at $457,800.00 with a $30,000.00 gift of equity → eligible; value acceptance ineligible → traditional appraisal; IPC test excludes the gift of equity (B3-4.3-05).

**R10 — Investigation, SAR and self-report anchors.** `investigation.opened` on any high-severity trigger; the agent gathers evidence, tests hypotheses (identity theft, straw buyer, occupancy, income/asset fabrication, undisclosed IPC/silent second, appraisal collusion, elder/affinity fraud, money laundering through EMD/gift/payoff channels — e.g., third-party wires, structured deposits under $10,000, rapid in-and-out transfers) and concludes within 10 business days; `sar_detection_date = concluded_at` when `sar_candidate = true` (the review's conclusion is the "initial detection" of the suspicious transaction — policy Q2); `amount_cents` = the transaction amount (loan amount, or the funds involved); the SAR is required when ≥ $5,000 and a category applies. *Worked example (refinance fixture variant):* Thu Oct 22, 2026 22.1 confirms an altered paystub (font/kerning and FICA arithmetic failures) → `investigation.opened` Oct 22 (`SM_FRAUD_INVESTIGATION_10BD` due Thu Nov 5); Fri Oct 30 the employer's written VOE shows income 40% lower and the borrower admits editing the document → `investigation.concluded{reasonable_basis_misrepresentation}` Oct 30 → `sar_detection_date` = Oct 30 → 28.4's `BSA_1029_320_SAR_30` due **Sun Nov 29, 2026** (filed by Fri Nov 27 policy; Thanksgiving Nov 26); `FNMA_A3_4_03_FRAUD_SELF_REPORT_30` due Nov 29 as well (self-report even though the loan is not delivered — A3-4-03 covers "origination"; Q6 default: report attempted fraud on withdrawn/declined applications only when the SAR is filed); the application is declined via 21.6 with reasons "income cannot be verified / information provided is inaccurate" (approved by `underwriting_reviewer`), the borrower is never told of the SAR, and the identity is placed on SM's internal watchlist (`red_flag_events{category=notice}`) for future applications. Had no subject been identifiable (e.g., a third party impersonating the borrower), the delay could extend to **Tue Dec 29, 2026** (60 days) but "in no case" beyond.

**R11 — SAR confidentiality guardrail.** Every borrower-facing channel (portal, notices, AI voice, adverse-action reasons, servicing notes after boarding) is scrubbed of the terms "SAR", "suspicious activity report", "FinCEN filing" and of `sar_candidates`/`sars` content; `documents` for SAR packages carry `retention_class = bsa_sar_5y` and an access list limited to `bsa_officer`, `officer`, `qc_officer` and the `fraud-risk` agent; the servicing hand-off (30.4) carries a `fraud_case_ref` flag without narrative.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`fraud-risk` agent (package `agents/fraud-risk`; tools: `verifyIdentity`, `resolveSsn`, `orderCbsv`, `assessLegalPresence`, `screenParty`, `resolveOfacMatch`, `contactForFraudAlert`, `resolveAddressDiscrepancy`, `runFraudTool`, `scoreOccupancy`, `discoverReo`, `assessNonArmsLength`, `logRedFlag`, `openInvestigation`, `gatherEvidence`, `concludeInvestigation`, `prepareSarPackage`, `prepareSelfReportPackage`, `prepareOfacReport`, `placeHold`, `releaseHold`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end behavior: at application it verifies every borrower, screens every party, runs the fraud tool, scores occupancy, reconciles REO and assesses relationships; it consumes every alert from 22.1–22.5, 23.x and 24.x, responds within the Red Flags SLA, and opens/concludes investigations with written hypotheses and evidence; it prepares complete SAR/self-report/OFAC packages and escalates them; it releases holds only on a concluded investigation. Decision record `{application_id, subject, screening results with list versions, identity scores, ssn resolution path, legal presence rationale (§1002.6(b)(7)), occupancy signals and score, reo findings, non-arm's-length assessment, red flags and responses, investigation hypotheses/evidence/conclusion, sar_candidate rationale, rule_set_version, model_version, prompt_version, confidence}`. Guardrails: never files a SAR, OFAC report or Fannie Mae self-report (humans do — 28.4); never discloses SAR existence or content to anyone outside the access list; never uses race/ethnicity/national origin/religion/language preference or demographic-information fields as inputs (immigration status only under §1002.6(b)(7)); never suggests an occupancy, relationship or data change to make a loan eligible; never contacts third parties (employers, sellers, donors) outside documented verification channels; never bypasses a `fraud_hold`; never uses biometric data beyond the verification purpose or retains it past the vendor-contract period without consent (`jurisdiction_rules`). Escalations: `bsa_officer` (SAR decision, OFAC true matches, program questions; SLA `SM_SAR_PACKAGE_5BD` → 28.4's 30-day clock), `officer` (Fannie Mae self-report), `underwriting_reviewer` (any conclusion that drives a denial/NOIA/counteroffer — 21.6; Colorado SB 26-189 human-review expectations), `human_agent` (supervised identity sessions; borrower request), `licensed_specialist` where applicable. Borrower-facing rules: AI disclosure at every contact; identity steps explained in plain language; requests limited to what the rule requires; ECOA reasons only from 21.6's approved list. Human path: investigator console with the same evidence graph; overrides logged.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Borrower with a legal name change (marriage/divorce) → identity documents in the new name plus the linking record; the credit file and SSA validation match on SSN/DOB; 23.1 re-associates credit if the name changes after association.
- Synthetic identity signals (thin file, SSN issued recently for an adult, credit header address mismatch, authorized-user pile-on) → investigation; SSA validation and document authentication decide.
- Twins/junior-senior mixed credit files → identity mismatch to 22.2 for a corrected report; never edit tradelines.
- Borrower is a minor in the property's state (age of enforceability) → ineligible (B2-2-01).
- Borrower with a valid ITIN and lawful presence → eligible; DU/Loan Delivery data-integrity edits for ITIN formats unknown → `fnma_portal_operator`/Integration Team check before first such loan (Q4).
- Non-permanent resident whose EAD expires between CD and note date → renewal evidence or the closing waits; never proceed on an expired document.
- OFAC potential match on a common name → resolved by DOB/nationality/ID; keep the resolution memo; a weak-identifier match that cannot be resolved → treat as a match and reject (conservative), 28.4 decides.
- Seller is a foreign entity in a sanctioned jurisdiction (e.g., Non-SDN Consolidated list program) → program-specific rules (`ofac_program`) — legal review via `bsa_officer`.
- Power-of-attorney closings → the attorney-in-fact is screened (OFAC, identity) and the POA reviewed by 24.4/26.1.
- Inter vivos trust vesting → trustees/settlors screened; the natural-person borrower still verified.
- Fraud alert placed after the report was pulled (alert appears on the refresh) → §605A(h) gate re-evaluated before consummation.
- Address discrepancy on a joint report for only one applicant → resolve per applicant; furnish the confirmed address at boarding.
- Reverse-occupancy pattern across multiple applications with the same agent → cross-application analytics (SM-wide data, same partner) → investigation and A3-4-03 vendor/TPO screening; SAR consideration.
- Undisclosed property found only after funding (post-closing QC) → 28.2/28.4; servicing continues; self-report evaluation.
- Investigation cannot conclude within 10 business days because a third party is unresponsive → `bsa_officer` extends with a written reason; the SAR anchor remains the conclusion date; the loan stays on hold or is declined for inability to verify.
- Borrower withdraws once questioned → investigation still concludes; SAR/self-report evaluated on the attempt; withdrawal recorded in 21.6 (HMDA action taken).
- Law-enforcement inquiry or subpoena → `officer`/counsel; SAR confidentiality preserved; 31 U.S.C. 5318(g) safe harbor noted in the record.
- Vendor outage (identity/fraud tool) → alternative method or hold; no self-attestation substitutes for document + biometric verification.

#### Test cases and acceptance criteria
- **22.6-T1 (IAL2 gate).** Given two borrowers on the purchase fixture (Mon Oct 19, 2026), when Borrower A passes document + liveness + face match and Borrower B's session is inconclusive, then `SM_IDENTITY_IAL2_GATE` blocks `submitDu`, `SM_IDENTITY_RETRY_2BD` is due Wed Oct 21, 2026, and a supervised remote session passing Oct 20 opens the gate.
- **22.6-T2 (SSN discrepancy → SSA validation → SFC 162).** Given DU returns an SSN issue-date/age message for Borrower B (SSN issued 2019, DOB 1988) that documents cannot resolve, when CBSV returns a match with no death indicator, then `ssn.validated{cbsv_web_service}`, the loan is eligible, and because DU's edit persists `sfc_162_required = true` flows to 29.3; given CBSV returns no match, then `not_validated`, the loan is ineligible and 21.6 receives the decline with `underwriting_reviewer` approval.
- **22.6-T3 (legal presence at note date).** Given Borrower B's EAD expires 2027-03-31 and the note date is Wed Nov 18, 2026, then `FNMA_B2_2_02_LEGAL_PRESENCE_GATE` is open; given the EAD expired 2026-11-01 with a pending renewal receipt and partner policy `legal_presence.pending_renewal_accepted = false`, then the gate blocks `consummate` and a condition opens.
- **22.6-T4 (OFAC rejected transaction).** Given a confirmed SDN match on the seller's LLC member on Wed Nov 4, 2026, when `ofac.match.confirmed{rejected}` fires, then `fraud_hold` blocks `issueCD`/`consummate`/`funding.authorized`, 28.4's §501.604 timer computes a due date of Thu Nov 19, 2026 (10 `business_days_federal`, Veterans Day excluded), the record carries `retention_class = ofac_records_10y`, and no borrower notice mentions the screening result.
- **22.6-T5 (OFAC false positive and list version).** Given a potential match on "Maria Lopez" with a different DOB and nationality, when resolved, then `false_positive_resolved` with both identifiers recorded; given a new SLS list version is published Wed Nov 4 after the Nov 3 screen, then `OFAC_SDN_SCREEN_GATE` requires a re-screen before the Nov 5 pre-consummation check and again before funding Thu Nov 12 if another version appears.
- **22.6-T6 (fraud alert contact).** Given an initial fraud alert with a phone number on the Oct 5, 2026 report, when the AI-voice contact is completed Tue Oct 6 with the borrower's confirmation, then `FCRA_605A_H_ALERT_CONTACT_GATE` is open before the Oct 7 conditional approval; given an extended alert with "contact by mail only", then an AI-voice contact does not satisfy the gate and a mailed confirmation with a returned signed statement does.
- **22.6-T7 (address discrepancy).** Given a notice of address discrepancy on Borrower A, when the agent matches the application address to the driver's license and a utility statement, then `address_discrepancy.resolved` and the confirmed address is queued for furnishing at boarding (30.4).
- **22.6-T8 (occupancy score).** Given the purchase-variant signals (rent needed from the retained condo with no lease +3, smaller house +2, DP-3 landlord quote +3), when scored, then 8 → `inconsistent`, `SM_OCCUPANCY_REO_GATE` blocks CTC and an investigation opens; given only the smaller-house signal (+2), then `consistent`.
- **22.6-T9 (undisclosed REO).** Given a $210,000 mortgage tradeline with no REO entry and a MERS MIN vested in the borrower, when the borrower documents an inherited rental, then `resolved_added_to_reo`, 22.5 adds PITIA $1,640.00, 23.2 recounts financed properties (2), 22.4 adds 2% of the other UPB to reserves, and 23.1 resubmits; given the borrower denies ownership and the deed shows a same-name relative, then `resolved_not_borrower` with the deed as evidence.
- **22.6-T10 (non-arm's-length new construction).** Given a borrower employed by the builder buying a newly constructed second home, when assessed, then `eligible = false` (principal residence only) and the loan is declined or restructured only if the true occupancy is primary with evidence; given the parents' existing home with a gift of equity, then eligible, `value_acceptance_blocked = true` (24.1 orders an appraisal) and 22.4 excludes the gift of equity from the IPC test.
- **22.6-T11 (investigation → SAR anchors).** Given an altered paystub confirmed Thu Oct 22, 2026 and the investigation concluded Fri Oct 30 with a reasonable basis for misrepresentation, when `sar.candidate.prepared` is sent to the `bsa_officer` by Fri Nov 6 (`SM_SAR_PACKAGE_5BD`), then 28.4's `BSA_1029_320_SAR_30` is due Sun Nov 29, 2026 and `FNMA_A3_4_03_FRAUD_SELF_REPORT_30` Nov 29; given no subject can be identified, then the outer limit is Tue Dec 29, 2026; the borrower-facing decline names "information provided cannot be verified" and contains no SAR reference.
- **22.6-T12 (Red Flags SLA and program log).** Given a suspicious-document red flag detected Fri Oct 23, 2026 16:30 MST, when no response is recorded by Mon Oct 26 end of day, then `RED_FLAGS_681_RESPONSE_1BD` breaches (sev 3) and the event appears in the annual ITPP report with its response time.
- **22.6-T13 (guardrails).** Given an adverse-action draft generated after a fraud investigation, when scanned, then any reference to "SAR"/"FinCEN" is rejected before 21.6 renders the notice; given a request to use `applicant_demographics` fields in the occupancy model, then the tool call is refused and logged.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Two AML programs.** SM is a "residential mortgage originator" (31 CFR 1010.100(lll)) and so a "loan or finance company" in its own right. Default: SM maintains its own §1029.210 program (compliance officer, training, independent testing, senior-management approval; risk assessment per the 2026 NPRM structure) and acts as the partner's agent for preparation; the partner's `bsa_officer` files SARs for the partner; SM's compliance officer decides whether SM also files (joint SAR where both entities have the obligation). Legal position before launch.
2. **SAR "initial detection" anchor.** Default: the investigation's concluded date (bounded by `SM_FRAUD_INVESTIGATION_10BD`), documented in the AML program; alerts alone do not start the clock.
3. **CBSV enrollee.** Default: SM enrolls (EIN, $5,000, Web Service certification) and holds signed SSA-89s (5-year retention) as the partner's vendor "going directly to the SSA"; eCBSV pursued if SM qualifies as a permitted entity **[UNVERIFIED]**.
4. **ITIN borrowers.** Eligible under B2-2-01 but DU/Loan Delivery handling and documentation are unverified. Default: `borrower.itin_enabled = false` until the Integration Team confirms edits and a documentation standard (IRS assignment letter + transcripts) is adopted.
5. **Expiring non-permanent-resident documentation.** Default: evidence must be unexpired at the note date; a USCIS extension receipt is accepted only with `legal_presence.pending_renewal_accepted = true` set by the partner.
6. **Self-report scope for undelivered loans.** A3-4-03 covers origination fraud; Default: self-report attempted fraud on withdrawn/declined applications when a SAR is filed or when a Fannie Mae-delivered loan of the same parties exists; otherwise log only (28.4 to confirm with Fannie Mae).
7. **Occupancy model thresholds and biometric consent states.** Default: thresholds as in R7 with quarterly 31.2 validation; biometric verification only after explicit consent in states requiring it (`jurisdiction_rules.biometric_consent_required` — list to be built).
