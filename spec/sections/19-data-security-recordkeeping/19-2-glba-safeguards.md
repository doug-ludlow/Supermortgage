### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-09)

**Applicability.** GLBA §501(b)/§505(a)(7) (15 U.S.C. 6801(b), 6805(a)(7)) give the FTC Safeguards authority over financial institutions not supervised by the banking agencies; Dodd-Frank left §501(b) safeguards outside the CFPB's enumerated laws (12 U.S.C. 5481(12)(J)) **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — statute not re-fetched]**, so the CFPB's Reg P governs privacy *notices* (Section 7.5) while the **FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314, eCFR current Sept. 4, 2026)** governs the *program*. Servicing loans is "financial in nature" (12 CFR 225.28(b)(1): "Making, acquiring, brokering, or servicing loans or other extensions of credit … for the account of others"), and 16 CFR 314.1(b) applies "to all customer information in your possession, regardless of whether such information pertains to individuals with whom you have a customer relationship, or pertains to the customers of other financial institutions that have provided such information to you" — i.e., to a subservicer holding the partner's borrowers' information. Section 314.6's small-entity exceptions (fewer than 5,000 consumers) will not apply at any production scale.

**FTC Safeguards Rule elements (16 CFR 314.4, verified):** (a) designate a Qualified Individual (may be employed by a service provider; the institution retains responsibility); (b) a **written** risk assessment with criteria for evaluating risks and for assessing confidentiality/integrity/availability, periodically refreshed; (c) safeguards: (1) access controls and least privilege, (2) inventory of "data, personnel, devices, systems, and facilities," (3) "Encrypt all customer information held or transmitted … both in transit over external networks and at rest" (Qualified-Individual-approved compensating controls only where encryption is infeasible), (4) secure development practices for in-house applications and evaluation of externally developed ones, (5) "Multi-factor authentication for any individual accessing any information system" unless the Qualified Individual approves equivalent controls, (6) secure disposal "no later than two years after the last date the information is used" with the legal/business/infeasibility exceptions (19.1), (7) change management, (8) "Monitor and log the activity of authorized users and detect unauthorized access"; (d) continuous monitoring **or** annual penetration testing plus vulnerability assessments "at least every six months" and after material changes; (e) training and qualified personnel; (f) service-provider selection, contractual safeguards and periodic assessment; (g) evaluate and adjust; (h) a **written incident response plan** covering goals, internal processes, roles/responsibilities/decision authority, internal and external communications, remediation, documentation/reporting, and evaluation/revision; (i) a written report "regularly and at least annually" to the board or equivalent covering program status, risk assessment, control decisions, service providers, test results, security events and recommendations; (j) **FTC notification** — "if the notification event involves the information of at least 500 consumers, you must notify the Federal Trade Commission as soon as possible, and no later than 30 days after discovery," with name/contact, types of information, date range, number of consumers, general description and any law-enforcement delay; "A notification event shall be treated as discovered as of the first day on which such event is known to you." "Notification event" = "acquisition of unencrypted customer information without the authorization of the individual"; key compromise = unencrypted; unauthorized acquisition is presumed absent reliable contrary evidence. §314.5: (j) effective May 13, 2024. Notices are filed on the FTC's electronic form and the FTC publishes them **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**.

**Fannie Mae Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement (PDF dated Sept. 2, 2025; mandatory since Aug. 12, 2025; incorporated by A2-1-01's "formal vendor risk management program" requirement and the Technology Guide).** Verified obligations: align the program with "a current industry standard such as the … NIST Framework or the … ISO 27001 Standard"; designate a senior executive responsible; review annually; "Annually provide a written attestation executed by a duly authorized corporate officer that the Information Security Program … meet[s] the requirements of this Supplement. For SF Lenders, this attestation may be provided through the annual Form 582 process"; access "reviewed and certified by the entitlement owner or user manager at least annually"; "MFA must be implemented for access to privileged accounts"; unique user IDs, no shared accounts; background verifications before access; annual security-awareness training covering "data retention"; written logging/monitoring with defined log-retention periods and protection of logs; "an independent security assessment of the control environment upon the occurrence of any Cybersecurity Incident … and at least annually … by a qualified independent auditor"; a documented vulnerability-management program with "an independent third-party penetration test that is conducted at least annually," regular scanning and risk-rated remediation; a written patch-management program; anti-virus/EDR; encryption of data in transit and at rest and of end-user devices; DLP and removable-media restrictions; network controls with firewall-rule reviews "on a defined frequency" and annual wireless reviews; cloud policies with "secure-by-design principles"; a formal SDLC with secure coding, "code scanning pre- and post-deployment," environment separation, defect management, a "software bill of materials" and third-party/open-source vulnerability assessment; formal change management with back-out procedures; a vendor risk-management program with risk-based re-assessments and contract flow-down — "The Company is responsible for its vendors' compliance with the obligations in this Supplement … to the same extent as if such failure were committed by the Company"; a BCP with threat assessment, BIA, critical functions and workarounds, alternate sites/network, crisis communications naming Fannie Mae contacts, third-party dependencies, annual testing (tabletop or similar), annual governing-body approval, annual threat risk assessment, and third/fourth-party contingencies (explicitly including subservicers); and notification "of any disruptions, including those related to technology, which may impact operations." **Cybersecurity Incident notice:** "Without undue delay and no later than 36 hours after identification of the Cybersecurity Incident, or the reasonable conclusion a Cybersecurity Incident may have occurred," by email to `privacy_office@fanniemae.com` (the Technology Guide appendix renders the address as `privacy-office@fanniemae.com` — **confirm the exact mailbox at onboarding**), with investigations, indicators of compromise, TTPs, whether Fannie Mae systems/Confidential Information were affected, IR-provider and law-enforcement contacts, investigation lead, and extent of exposure; "Cybersecurity Incident" includes loss, unauthorized acquisition/use/modification/disclosure/deletion of or access to Confidential Information, circumvention of security measures, and availability impacts (ransomware, DDoS, business-email compromise — reportable "regardless of impact" per SVC-2025-01, research/00a §6.2 — and vulnerabilities affecting service delivery); SEC filings, if any, within 36 hours of filing; lost/stolen media within 36 hours. Post-incident, Fannie Mae may suspend access and restore it on an attestation by an independent investigative provider or "a signed statement by two officers." **Form 101** (12/9/2025) adds a parallel duty on Supermortgage as Subordinate Subscriber: "notify Fannie Mae and Master Subscriber in writing of any loss or unauthorized access to or use of the Data without undue delay and no later than 36 hours after Subordinate Subscriber becomes aware of the incident." **Consolidated Technology Guide (Apr. 21, 2026):** revoke/reset credentials "no less frequently than … every 90 calendar days for individuals and … every 365 calendar days for systems"; encryption in transit/at rest, EDR/anti-virus, written patch program, DR/back-up, intrusion detection, secure disposal; Fannie Mae may audit "systems, facilities, books, and records"; TLS 1.2 with the listed ECDHE-GCM ciphers, older ciphers disabled **Oct. 23, 2026** (research/00b).

**23 NYCRR Part 500 (Second Amendment, Nov. 1, 2023; verified sections).** Covered entity = "any person operating under or required to operate under a license, registration … under the Banking Law" — Supermortgage's NY Mortgage Loan Servicer registration (research/00a §5.2) makes it one. Key requirements: 500.4 CISO and annual report to the senior governing body; 500.5 penetration testing "at least annually by a qualified internal or external party" and "automated scans … and a manual review of systems not covered … at a frequency determined by the risk assessment, and promptly after any material system changes," with risk-prioritised remediation; 500.6 audit trails able to reconstruct material financial transactions and detect/respond to events, records "not fewer than five years"; 500.7 access privileges (annual review, privileged-account limits); 500.12 "Multi-factor authentication shall be utilized for any individual accessing any information systems of a covered entity" (CISO-approved compensating controls reviewed at least annually) — in force **Nov. 1, 2025**; 500.13(a) asset inventory tracking owner, location, classification/sensitivity, support expiration date and recovery time objectives — in force Nov. 1, 2025; 500.13(b) periodic secure disposal; 500.16 written incident-response plan (adds root-cause analysis and recovery from backups) and BCDR plan, annual testing with critical staff, protected backups tested annually; **500.17(a)**: notify the superintendent "electronically in the form set forth on the department's website as promptly as possible but in no event later than 72 hours after determining that a cybersecurity incident has occurred" (incidents at the entity, affiliates or third-party providers; continuing duty to update); **500.17(c)**: extortion payment notice within 24 hours and a written explanation within 30 days; **500.17(b)**: by **April 15** each year a certification of material compliance or an acknowledgment of noncompliance "signed by the covered entity's highest-ranking executive and its CISO," with supporting records kept five years. "Cybersecurity incident" = an event that (1) requires notice to any government body, self-regulatory agency or supervisory body, (2) has a reasonable likelihood of materially harming a material part of normal operations, or (3) results in ransomware deployment in a material part of the systems. 500.19(a) limited exemptions (fewer than 20 employees/contractors, or < $7.5M NY gross revenue in each of the last three fiscal years, or < $15M year-end total assets; Notice of Exemption within 30 days; 180 days to comply after losing the exemption) may apply pre-launch and lapse quickly; Class A thresholds (≥ $20M NY revenue **and** > 2,000 employees or > $1B total revenue) do not apply.

**State breach-notification laws.** N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §899-aa (verified): notice to affected residents "in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay" and "within thirty days after the breach has been discovered" (law-enforcement delay excepted); notice to the attorney general, department of state and state police, **and to DFS for Part 500 covered entities**; consumer reporting agencies when "more than five thousand New York residents are to be notified at one time"; GLBA-regulated entities that notify under their federal scheme need not duplicate consumer notice but must still notify the AG. Other states are carried in `jurisdiction_rules.breach_notice` — illustrative anchors: Texas 60 days to individuals and AG within 30 days for ≥ 250 residents; Colorado, Florida and Washington 30 days with AG notice at ≥ 500 residents; California "most expedient time possible" with an AG sample notice above 500 residents **[UNVERIFIED — matrix to be populated and re-verified by counsel before go-live]**. CCPA: GLBA-covered information is exempt from the CCPA except the §1798.150 breach private right of action (Cal. Civ. Code §1798.145(e), verified).

**CFPB.** Circular 2022-04 ("Insufficient data protection or security for sensitive consumer information") was **withdrawn May 12, 2025** (verified from the withdrawal notice), together with Circulars 2022-03/2023-03 (algorithmic adverse action) and 2022-07 (dispute investigations — note for Section 8). The statutory UDAAP prohibition (12 U.S.C. 5531/5536) and state UDAP laws remain available theories; no design change.

**SOC 2 / other expectations.** No law requires SOC 2, but the partner (Reg AB/USAP, 18.6), MI companies, warehouse lenders and Freddie Mac's AI bulletin (NIST 800-53/ISO audits, research/00a §3.5) expect an annual **SOC 2 Type II** (Security, Availability, Confidentiality; Processing Integrity for cashiering) — treated as a contractual prerequisite. **LL-2026-04** (effective Aug. 6, 2026) ties the AI governance program to the Supplement and requires vendor governance "no less protective" (19.3).

**Discrepancies vs. blueprint row.** "Continuous" understates the hard clocks: 36 hours (Fannie Mae Supplement and Form 101), 72 hours (NYDFS), 24 hours/30 days (NYDFS extortion), 30 days (FTC ≥ 500 consumers; NY consumers), April 15 (NYDFS certification), annual pen test/attestation/board report/BCP test, six-month vulnerability assessments, 90/365-day credential resets. Automation class (b) is right: the program runs as automated control tests, but regulatory notices and certifications are `officer` acts.

#### State machine
Incident: `alert` → `triage` (agent correlates; severity) → `identified` (S1/S2: Fannie Mae/partner/Form 101 clocks start) → `contained` → `scoped` (counts/states/encryption; FTC/NYDFS/state clocks resolved) → `notified` (all required notices sent) → `eradicated` → `recovered` → `post_incident` (root cause, lessons learned, independent assessment engaged) → `closed`. `determined` (NYDFS) is a flag set on `identified`/`scoped` when any prong is met. Severity: **S1** confirmed exposure/ransomware/material harm; **S2** reasonable conclusion an incident may have occurred, or BEC regardless of impact; **S3** contained security event with no reasonable conclusion of incident (logged; no external notice; reviewed weekly by the CISO); **S4** informational. Transitions to `notified` require every `incident_notices` row with a due date to be `sent` or documented as not required. Human actors: `officer` (sends regulatory notices, signs attestations), `attorney` (privilege, law-enforcement delay, subpoenas), CISO (severity confirmation, determination). Control program: each control cycles `scheduled → ran → pass|fail|exception → remediated`.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Clock anchors.** Fannie Mae: `identified_at` = the earlier of confirmed identification or the "reasonable conclusion" that an incident may have occurred — the SOC's confirmation of an S2 sets it; the agent may not delay identification pending scoping. NYDFS: `determined_at` set when any prong is met — prong (1) is automatically met the moment any FTC or state-regulator notice becomes required, so scoping must complete inside `SM_NYDFS_DETERMINATION_48H`. FTC: `discovered_at` = first day the event was known to anyone at Supermortgage (or a vendor acting for it, once reported).
2. **Counting consumers** (FTC ≥ 500; state AG/CRA thresholds): distinct individuals (borrowers, co-borrowers, confirmed successors, authorized third parties) whose *unencrypted* customer information was acquired; encrypted data counts if the key was, or is presumed, compromised; "presumed" applies unless forensic evidence reliably shows no acquisition. Residents by state from `borrowers.mailing_state` at the incident date.
3. **Worked timeline.** SOC confirms Fri **2026-10-16 14:30 ET** that a print/mail vendor SFTP credential was used by an unknown party to download statement files for 12,400 borrowers (files unencrypted at rest on the vendor side; 3,100 NY residents; 2,000 TX). Partner notice due Sat 2026-10-17 14:30 ET; Fannie Mae Supplement and Form 101 notices due **Sun 2026-10-18 02:30 ET** (sent Sat 09:00 ET by the `officer` from the agent's draft); NYDFS determination made Mon 2026-10-19 10:00 ET (prong 1: FTC and NY notices required) → NYDFS notice due **Thu 2026-10-22 10:00 ET**; FTC notice due no later than **Sun 2026-11-15** (internal target Fri 2026-11-13); NY consumer/AG/DOS/State Police/DFS notices due **2026-11-15** (CRAs not required: 3,100 < 5,000); TX consumers within 60 days (2026-12-15) and TX AG within 30 days (≥ 250 residents; 2026-11-15) **[UNVERIFIED TX terms]**; independent assessment engaged by 2026-10-30 (10 servicer business days) and completed by 2027-01-14; root-cause/lessons-learned within 30 days of recovery.
4. **Encryption standard.** TLS 1.2+ with ECDHE-GCM ciphers only (Fannie Mae profile from Oct. 23, 2026); AES-256-GCM at rest via KMS envelope encryption; PII columns (SSN/TIN, DOB, account numbers, government IDs, biometrics/voiceprints if ever collected) encrypted at the application layer with per-loan data-encryption keys wrapped by an HSM-backed key-encryption key; restricted fair-lending data (19.4) under a separate KEK; annual KEK rotation; DEK rotation on re-encryption jobs; key administrators cannot read data and data administrators cannot export keys (separation of duties enforced by KMS policies).
5. **Access.** Least privilege via role bundles; MFA for every human (FIDO2 for privileged; TOTP minimum); no shared accounts; system access via short-lived credentials/mTLS; Fannie Mae System IDs stored in the vault with automated 365-day rotation and human Fannie Mae credentials 90-day reset (auto-disable on breach); joiner/mover/leaver within 1 business day; quarterly privileged and annual all-account certification; every access to `documents` with `pii_level=high` and to the restricted FL schema is logged with identity, purpose code and request id.
6. **Vulnerability and patch.** Weekly authenticated scans of all assets; container/image scanning at build; SBOM per release; SCA on every PR; remediation SLAs as in the timer table; patches applied per SLA with tested back-out; exceptions approved by the Qualified Individual with expiry ≤ 12 months.
7. **Logging.** SIEM ingests IdP, cloud control plane, database audit (pgAudit), application audit (`loan_events`, `agent_runs`, tool calls), network flow, EDR, DLP; logs are write-once (WORM copy) and retained per `security_logs_5y`; alert rules cover impossible travel, privilege escalation, bulk export, restricted-schema reads, Fannie Mae credential use outside job windows, agent tool-call anomalies (volume, unusual scopes).
8. **Secure SDLC for an AI-built codebase.** Every change (including Claude Code-generated) flows through PR → automated SAST/SCA/secret scanning/tests → human change-approver review for Tier 0/1 systems **[policy; open decision 3]** → signed build with SBOM → staged deploy with automated rollback; production data never in non-production; migrations reviewed for append-only trigger preservation.
9. **Resiliency.** Tier 0 (Postgres SoR, KMS, IdP, timers): RPO ≤ 5 minutes (streaming replication + PITR), RTO ≤ 4 hours (cross-region failover); Tier 1 (cashiering/lockbox/ACH, Fannie Mae reporting outbox, Notice Registry, print/mail): RTO ≤ 8 hours; Tier 2 (portal, telephony, ops console): RTO ≤ 24 hours **[policy — the Supplement sets no RTO/RPO; NYDFS 500.13 requires RTOs in the inventory]**; immutable backups with quarterly automated restore tests and an annual full DR exercise; the Fannie Mae-required contingency for loss of a critical vendor (cloud region, AI provider, print/mail, lockbox) is a documented manual/alternate path per 19.3 with a tested switch-over.
10. **Disposal** per 19.1 (FTC two-year trigger; 500.13(b)); media destruction certificates.
11. **Reporting.** Annual 314.4(i) board report auto-compiled from `control_test_results`, `risk_assessments`, `vulnerabilities`, `security_incidents`, `vendor_assessments`, with recommendations; the NYDFS certification and the Supplement attestation are generated from the same evidence with a "material compliance" checklist; any `fail` without an approved exception at the reporting date forces an acknowledgment of noncompliance (NYDFS) rather than a certification.

#### Integrations
- **IdP** (Okta/Entra-class; API): identity mirror, MFA status, credential ages, SCIM provisioning — auth OAuth client credentials; failure → CTL-SEC-01 "unknown" (fail-closed for reporting).
- **SIEM/EDR/scanner/cloud-config/DLP** (APIs or log shipping): evidence ingestion with hashes; sandbox stubs for tests.
- **KMS/HSM**: key inventory, rotation events, policy exports.
- **Fannie Mae**: incident email (SMTP via the corporate mail system; template `NTC_FNMA_INCIDENT_36H`; officer-sent; read receipt/acknowledgement stored) — not an API; Form 101 notice by email to Technology_Registration and the partner; post-incident attestation letters. Access suspension by Fannie Mae → integration outage playbook (queue outbox; human portal fallback per Section 5).
- **NYDFS**: cybersecurity-incident form and April 15 certification on the DFS portal — human `officer` task (`human_portal_task` package: prefilled form fields, incident narrative, timer due date).
- **FTC**: Safeguards notification electronic form — `officer` task with the prefilled six content elements.
- **State AGs/other regulators**: portals/letters per matrix — `officer` tasks; **consumer notices** via `print-mail` with template variants per state (required-content checklists), substitute notice rules if addresses unavailable; **CRAs** via the Section 8 bureau contacts (notice, not Metro 2).
- **Partner**: contractual notice by email + phone bridge; shared incident channel.
- **Cyber insurer / IR retainer / law enforcement**: contact registry; notices logged.
- Failure handling: every notice has a fallback channel (email → fax/phone → courier) and the timer stays open until evidence is attached.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
- **Agent:** `security-records` (security persona) with `compliance-sentinel` for clocks. End-to-end: runs and interprets control tests, opens exceptions for QI approval, triages alerts into incidents, assigns severity, sets `identified_at` (cannot postpone it), drives containment playbooks through allow-listed automation (disable identity, rotate secret, isolate host, block egress), scopes affected persons/states/encryption status from `documents`/`integration_messages`/access logs, computes every clock, drafts every notice and portal payload with required-content checklists, assembles the board report/attestation/certification packages, and writes the root-cause and lessons-learned drafts.
- **Tools:** `siem.query`, `idp.disableIdentity`, `vault.rotateSecret`, `cloud.isolateHost`, `network.blockEgress` (all logged, reversible, rate-limited), `incident.setSeverity`, `incident.scope`, `notices.draft`, `portal_task.create`, `timers.read`, `controls.runTest`, `exceptions.propose` (no approve).
- **Decision record:** `{incident_id, alerts[], severity, identified_at_basis, prongs_met{nydfs:[…], ftc:bool, states:[…]}, consumer_count_method, encryption_status_evidence, notices_required[], drafts[], containment_actions[], model_version, prompt_version, rule_set_version (breach matrix version), confidence, escalations[]}`.
- **Escalations (mandatory human touchpoints):** every regulatory or partner notice, attestation and certification is sent/signed by an `officer` (baseline §8 item 3 "cyber-incident notices → officer"); NYDFS/FTC/state-AG portal submissions are `officer` tasks (portal submissions carry certifications); forensic engagement, law-enforcement liaison, delay requests and privilege decisions → `attorney`; severity downgrade from S2 to S3 requires CISO approval; the agent may not pay or negotiate extortion; Fannie Mae post-incident officer attestation requires two officers.
- **Guardrails:** containment tools have blast-radius limits (no more than N identities/hosts per action without CISO approval; never disable the `officer` break-glass accounts); notices go only to recipients in the verified registry; prompts to the model provider never include restricted FL data or full SSNs (tokenized); all agent tool calls are in the SIEM.
- **Human path (AI off):** the IR runbook is executable by the CISO team from the ops console with the same timers and templates.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Vendor/fourth-party incident:** the clock starts when Supermortgage identifies (vendor notice received); contracts require ≤ 24-hour vendor notice (19.3) — a late vendor notice is itself logged and reported to Fannie Mae in the details.
- **Partner-side incident affecting Supermortgage-held data:** Supermortgage still notifies Fannie Mae/Form 101 if Fannie Mae Data in its custody is affected; coordination clause in the subservicing agreement designates a single Fannie Mae notice where both are affected (both remain liable).
- **Incident during a servicing transfer:** transferee/transferor and Fannie Mae notified; transfer files re-encrypted/re-sent; 17.3 hold.
- **Ransomware on the SoR:** WORM copies and PITR restore; Fannie Mae reporting deadlines (Section 5) may be missed — file the disruption notice to Fannie Mae under the Supplement immediately and invoke the partner's manual reporting fallback.
- **Fannie Mae suspends API/portal access on suspected risk:** outbox queues; `human_portal_task` fallbacks; re-entry attestation by an independent provider or two officers.
- **Encryption-key compromise:** treat all data under that KEK as unencrypted for FTC/state counting; emergency rotation and re-encryption job.
- **False positives:** S3/S4 never trigger external notices; but an S2 downgraded after the 36-hour Fannie Mae notice was sent gets a follow-up "no impact" update (Supplement "promptly thereafter as requested").
- **Law-enforcement delay:** documented written request pauses consumer/state clocks where the state allows; never pauses the Fannie Mae or NYDFS clocks.
- **Deceased borrowers / returned mail:** substitute notice rules per state; successors notified where confirmed.
- **AI-provider incident (prompt/log exposure):** treated as a vendor incident; scope from `agent_runs` payload manifests (which record which loan ids and data classes were sent to the model per call).
- **NYDFS limited exemption lapses** (headcount/revenue crossing thresholds): 180-day compliance timer; the control program is built to full Part 500 from day one so nothing changes but the filings.
- **Multiple incidents in a period:** each has its own clocks; the board report aggregates.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Security-log retention:** 5 years cold (default, conservative reading of 500.6) vs. 3 years. Default: 5 years, 13 months hot.
2. **Partner incident-notice SLA:** 24 hours (default) vs. 12 hours — set in the subservicing agreement.
3. **Human change approval for production deploys** of Tier 0/1 systems (default: required) vs. fully automated pipelines with post-deploy review.
4. **NYDFS limited exemption** at launch: file the Notice of Exemption if eligible (default: file, but operate to full Part 500).
5. **RTO/RPO tiers** as stated (default) — partner may require tighter cashiering RTO.
6. **Fannie Mae incident mailbox spelling** — verify `privacy_office@` vs `privacy-office@` during onboarding; default: send to the Supplement address and copy the account team.
7. **SOC 2 scope**: Security + Availability + Confidentiality in year 1 (default), add Processing Integrity in year 2.
