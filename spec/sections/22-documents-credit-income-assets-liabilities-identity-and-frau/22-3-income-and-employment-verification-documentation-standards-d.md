### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**General income standards — B3-3.1-01, General Income Information (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** "A stable and predictable flow of income is a foundational element in loan underwriting. The lender must document income is stable, has a documented history of receipt, and is reasonably expected to continue." Continuance: "the lender must document that the income is expected to continue for at least three years from the note date"; "If the income does not have a defined expiration date and the applicable history of receipt of the income is documented ... the lender may conclude that the income is likely to continue." Nontaxable income: "the lender should develop an 'adjusted gross income' for the borrower by adding an amount equivalent to 25% of the nontaxable income to the borrower's income."

**Employment-related income standards — B3-3.2-02, Standards for Employment-Related Income (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** "Lenders must evaluate the borrower's work history to determine whether it reflects a reliable pattern of employment over the most recent two years."; "A shorter employment history may be considered eligible for qualifying purposes if the borrower's employment profile includes positive factors that reasonably offset the shorter employment history."; "A two-year history for each income source is recommended; however, income that has been received for a shorter period of time (but, no less than 12 months) may be considered as acceptable income, as long as there are positive factors to reasonably offset the shorter income history."; "When the borrower's employment history includes different employers, in no instance may the borrower have any gap in employment greater than one month in the most recent 12-month period, unless the employment is considered seasonal income."; "Borrowers with employment gaps during the most recent 12 months may appear to have unstable employment. Lenders must carefully analyze the borrower's current employment to ensure that it is likely to continue." Foreign income: "Copies of signed federal income tax returns for the most recent two years that include foreign employment-related income."; "All documents of a foreign origin must be completed in English, or the lender must provide a translation, attached to each document, and ensure the translation is complete and accurate."; "All income must be translated to U.S. dollars." DU validation: "If a borrower's income is validated by the DU validation service, lenders are not required to determine if the borrower is employed by a family member or interested party to the property sale or purchase." Documentation standards (B3-3.2-01, 03/04/2026) are quoted in 22.1 (paystub "dated no earlier than 30 days prior to the initial loan application date" with "all year-to-date earnings"; W-2s for the "most recent one- or two-year period"; Form 1005 alternative).

**Base income — B3-3.3-01 (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** Fixed base income documentation: "a completed Request for Verification of Employment (Form 1005), or the most recent paystub and most recent W-2"; "a verbal VOE is required." Hourly: "(Hourly gross pay x average # of hours worked per week x 52 weeks) / 12 months". "Variable base income refers to a fixed hourly rate with fluctuating hours, or an hourly rate that varies."; fixed base income involves "guaranteed minimum hours. These hours may have minor variances from pay period to pay period which does not alone preclude the borrower's income from being considered fixed base income."

**Bonus, commission, overtime, tip — B3-3.3-02 (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** "A minimum two-year history is recommended; however, income received for a shorter period, but no less than 12 months, may be considered as acceptable if there are positive factors to reasonably offset the shorter income history." Documentation: "a completed Request for Verification of Employment (Form 1005), or the most recent paystub and two years' W-2s"; "a verbal VOE is required"; "For tip income not reported by the employer, two years' personal tax returns with IRS Form 4137 may be provided in lieu of a W-2." Calculation: "Once the monthly year-to-date income amount is determined, it must be compared to the earnings from previous years."; "Depending on the historical trend of the income, the lender must calculate qualifying income as follows:" — "Stable or Increasing: Calculate an average income amount using year-to-date and previous year's earnings, divided by the number of months included in the year-to-date paystub and W-2s."; "Decreasing: The lender must confirm the current income level has stabilized after the decline; otherwise, the income is not eligible for qualifying. To calculate income, use the year-to-date income divided by months elapsed since the income stabilized."; "When bonus income is used for qualification, the lender must calculate the monthly amount accurately for use in the trending analysis. For example, if a borrower receives an annual bonus on March 31st of each year, the bonus income should be annualized (divided by 12) to determine the appropriate monthly bonus amount."; "The lender must also ensure that all supporting documentation in the file supports the income used in the qualification process."

**Employment offers — B3-3.3-03 (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** Option 1 (paystub before delivery): "The lender must obtain and review the borrower's fully executed offer or contract" identifying employer, borrower, position, pay type/rate and start date; "Prior to delivering the loan, the lender must save the most recent paystub"; verbal VOE required; "The lender must use the monthly income amount in the offer letter as qualifying income"; no SFC. Option 2 (no paystub before delivery): purchase, principal residence, one unit; "The borrower's start date must be no earlier than 30 days prior to the note date OR no later than 90 days after the note date"; fixed base income only; not employed by a family member or interested party; "fully executed and non-contingent offer or contract"; reserves of "Six months' PITIA for the subject property" or the liabilities-plus-PITIA bridge for the months between note date and start date plus one; "The lender must deliver the loan with Special Feature Code 707."

**Temporary leave — B3-3.3-09 (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** Leave "is generally employee-initiated, short in duration and for reasons, including, but not limited to maternity or parental leave, short-term medical disability, or other temporary leave types"; requires "written confirmation from the borrower of their intent to return to work" and employer documentation "that confirms the borrower's expected return to work date"; return before the first payment date: "the lender can consider the borrower's regular employment income in qualifying"; after: "the lender must use the lesser of the borrower's temporary leave income (if any) or regular employment income," supplemented by "available liquid financial reserves"; documentation of "the amount and duration of the borrower's temporary leave income" and "the amount of the regular employment income the borrower received prior to the temporary leave"; "verbal VOE is required."

**Other income — B3-3.4-02 (alimony/child support/equalization; 03/04/2026), B3-3.4-03 (annuity/pension/retirement; 03/04/2026), B3-3.4-12 (public assistance; 03/04/2026), B3-3.4-15 (Social Security; 03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** Alimony/child support: "a copy of a divorce decree or separation agreement (if the divorce is not final)" or other legal agreement/court decree/state-law documentation; "document receipt of income for the most recent six months using sources including, but not limited to: bank statements, cancelled checks, or evidence of other electronic receipt of payments"; "A minimum six-month history is required. The payment history must demonstrate the stability of the income, as reflected in the receipt of full, regular, and timely payments."; separated borrower without an agreement: "the lender should not consider any proposed or voluntary payments as income"; three-year continuance with attention to "the age of the children for whom the support is being paid, or the duration over which alimony is required to be paid"; "The full amount of documented qualifying child support income is nontaxable and may be grossed up."; lump-sum equalization payments excluded. Retirement: verify with "a statement from the organization providing the income," "a copy of retirement award letter or benefit statement," "a copy of financial or bank account statement," "a copy of signed federal income tax return," or "an IRS W-2 form, or an IRS 1099 form"; benefit statements "must specify the income type, amount and frequency of the payment, and include confirmation of the initial start date"; annuity/retirement-account distributions: "The lender must document that income is expected to continue for at least three years from the note date."; "Eligible retirement account balances (from 401(k), IRA, or Keogh) may be combined for the purpose of determining whether the three-year continuance requirement is met. The borrower must have unrestricted access to the accounts without penalty." Public assistance: "The lender must obtain letters or exhibits from the paying agency that state the amount, frequency, and duration of the benefit payments."; three-year continuance. Social Security: own work record (retirement/disability) — "Social Security Administration's (SSA) Award letter, SSA-1099, Most recent signed federal income tax returns (or tax transcripts), or Proof of current receipt"; "Lenders are not required to verify continuance unless they have reason to believe the income may not continue"; benefits on another's record or for a dependent — award letter, proof of current receipt and "Three-year continuance"; "The lender is not required to provide documentation to support that 15% of the Social Security income is nontaxable."

**Self-employment — B3-3.5-01, Underwriting Factors and Documentation for a Self-Employed Borrower (12/13/2023). VERIFIED (as extracted).** "Any individual who has a 25% or greater ownership interest in a business is considered to be self-employed." Two-year history is the standard; "the income of a person who has less than a two-year history of self-employment may be considered, as long as the borrower's most recent signed personal and business federal income tax returns reflect a full year (12 months) of self-employment income" with documentation of "prior income at the same (or greater) level" in a related field. Two years of "signed federal income tax returns (both individual returns and in some cases, business returns) that were filed with the IRS"; one year acceptable when the business has existed five consecutive years with ≥ 25% ownership and "the borrower's individual tax returns show an increase in self-employment income over the past two years from the respective business." "The lender may use Cash Flow Analysis (Form 1084), another type of cash flow analysis, or an automated tool such as Fannie Mae-approved vendor tools."; the analysis must assess "the stability of the borrower's business income and estimate the potential for long-term earnings"; "The lender must prepare a written evaluation of its analysis of a self-employed borrower's personal income, including the business income or loss, reported on the borrower's individual income tax returns." Business existence verification is cross-referenced to B3-3.1-04. Year-to-date P&L/business-statement timing rules and the declining-income wording were not extracted (B3-3.6/B3-3.7) — **[UNVERIFIED — re-fetch before release]**.

**Rental income — B3-3.8-01 (09/02/2026), B3-3.8-02 (09/02/2026); SEL-2026-08 (Sept. 2, 2026). VERIFIED (topics) / PARTIALLY VERIFIED (announcement wording via secondary).** "Generally, rental income from the borrower's principal residence (a one-unit principal residence or the unit the borrower occupies in a two- to four-unit property) or a second home cannot be used to qualify the borrower."; "Lenders may only use positive rental income for qualifying income if the borrower(s) has at least 12 months of property management experience," documented by "the borrower's most recent signed individual federal income tax return (IRS Form 1040), including Schedules 1 and E, reflecting rental income received for any property and supporting 365 Fair Rental Days."; leases for properties not on the 1040 "must contain a minimum term of at least six months"; "When the monthly qualifying rental income minus the full PITIA is positive, the lender must add it to the borrower's total monthly income. When the monthly qualifying rental income minus PITIA is negative, the lender must add the monthly net rental loss to the borrower's total monthly obligations." Subject property (B3-3.8-02): purchases — "a Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule (Form 1007) or Small Residential Income Property Appraisal Report (Form 1025), as applicable" and "a copy of the fully executed lease agreement(s) when a lease is being transferred to the borrower"; "multiply monthly gross rent by 75% for the net rental income amount, then subtract the PITIA of the subject property from the net rental income"; refinances — "Form 1007 or Form 1025, as applicable, to support monthly gross rents; the most recent individual federal income tax returns (IRS Form 1040), including Schedules 1 and E; and copies of the current lease agreement(s)" with Schedule E cash-flow analysis averaged over months in service; less than 12 months of management experience: "if ANRI is positive, the lender may use the rental income to offset the PITIA only."; ADU: "The qualifying rental income amount from the ADU is limited to 30% of the total qualifying income," "Purchase or limited cash-out refinance transactions only." Effective date (SEL-2026-08 as reported by TENA, Sept. 2, 2026): "Revisions related to rental income policy are effective for all loans with application dates on and after November 1, 2026 (lenders, however, are encouraged to implement these changes immediately)." — consistent with 00a-fnma §1 ("required for all loans with application dates on or after November 1, 2026").

**Verbal verification of employment — B3-3.1-04 (03/04/2026). VERIFIED.** "A verbal verification of employment (verbal VOE) must be obtained for each borrower using employment or self-employment income to qualify." Employment income: "The verbal VOE must be obtained within 10 business days prior to the note date for employment income"; must document "name and title of the person who confirmed the employment for the lender, name and title of the person who completed the verification for the employer, date of the call, and the source of the phone number"; "The lender must independently obtain a phone number and, if possible, an address for the borrower's employer. This can be accomplished by using a telephone book, the Internet, directory assistance, or by contacting the applicable licensing bureau."; "If the employer uses a third-party employment verification vendor, the lender must obtain written verification from the vendor of the borrower's current employment status within the same time frame as the verbal VOE requirements." Self-employment: "verify the existence of the borrower's business within 120 calendar days prior to the note date from a third party, such as a CPA, regulatory agency, or the applicable licensing bureau, if possible; or by verifying a phone listing and address for the borrower's business using a telephone book, the internet, or directory assistance." Alternatives (as listed): written VOE; e-mail exchange from the employer's work e-mail address; "the most recent paystub as of that date that, meets the requirements in B3-3.2-01 ...; reflects information for the most recent expected pay period based on the date it is provided and the borrower's pay cadence; and does not include any information indicating the borrower may not be actively employed" (dated no earlier than 15 business days prior to the note date — 00a-fnma §4.7); bank statements dated no earlier than 15 business days prior to the note date (00a-fnma §4.7); third-party vendor; military LES within 120 calendar days; DMDC verification. DU: "When employment is validated by DU, DU includes in its assessment the age of the information in the vendor's database."; "Compliance with the DU message satisfies the requirement for completing the verification of employment." Fannie Mae does not define "business days" for this rule.

**DU validation service — B3-2-02 (02/05/2025); A2-2-04 (04/01/2026). VERIFIED.** "Certain components of the loan file – income, employment, and assets – are eligible for validation by DU using electronic verification reports obtained from vendors."; "The DU Validation Service Verification Report Vendors list provides a listing of authorized report suppliers and report distributors" (00b-orig F1: income/employment — AccountChek by Informative Research, Argyle, Equifax, Experian, Finicity, Truework, Truv; tax transcripts — CoreLogic, DataVerify, Equifax, Halcyon, MeridianLink, Partners Credit & Verification Solutions, PointServ, Xactus; "The report provider (not the distributor) sends the report data electronically to the DU validation service"). Validated: "DU has determined that the information provided on the verification report supports the information entered into DU for the component being validated."; not validated: "The DU message(s) will indicate what documentation, in addition to the verification report, is required."; "The lender must review the verification report relied upon by DU for validation and investigate and resolve any conflicting or contradictory information."; "For loans assessed by the DU validation service, the lender must obtain borrower authorization to receive the information from the vendor."; "Lenders must comply with all DU messages, including ensuring the loan closes by the 'Close by Date' stated in the DU employment validation message."; "To validate income or employment using an asset verification report, the lender must order an asset verification report with a minimum of 12 months of data from an authorized vendor." Relief (A2-2-04): income — "Fannie Mae will not enforce representations and warranties on the accuracy of the lender's income calculations related to the validated income, and the integrity of the data provided on the verification report."; employment — "on the borrower's employment, through the time of closing, with the employer attested to on the loan application, and the integrity of the data provided on the verification report."; conditions — "All of the requirements that pertain to the DU validation service must be met.", "All Verification Messages and Approval Conditions that appear in the DU Underwriting Findings report ... must be satisfactorily resolved and documented accordingly.", "The loan must close by the 'Close by Date' stated in the DU message."; "If there is information that is conflicting with or contradictory to the data that was submitted to DU, the lender must perform due diligence to investigate and ensure that accurate data is entered into DU." DU 12.1 (June 26, 2026; 00a-fnma §3.1): income validation "by income type for each employer individually"; "Equalization Payment" income type; employer-name matching (Mar. 21, 2026).

**Income Calculator — B3-3.1-03 (03/04/2026); Fannie Mae Income Calculator page and DU 12.0 Integration Impact Memo (00b-orig F1). VERIFIED.** "an optional tool to assist the lender in calculating qualifying income"; conditions: "All information submitted to Income Calculator is accurate, complete, and reflects the information on the tax returns being used."; "Tax returns meet the allowable age of documents requirements"; "The amount of qualifying income being used by the lender is not more than the amount of income calculated by Income Calculator."; "A copy of the Findings Report must be maintained in the loan file."; "Enforcement relief from representations and warranties on the accuracy of the income calculation may be provided but the lender remains responsible for the accuracy of the information submitted."; "The lender can choose to use a Fannie Mae-approved technology service provider to extract data from tax returns and auto-populate the required fields." Fannie Mae charges no fee; access paths include "Full TSP Integration" (named providers) and LOS iFrame; DU linkage via DU Spec data points `DU:VerificationReportSupplierType = IncomeCalculator`, `DU:VerificationReportType = IncomeCalculator`, `DU:VerificationReportIdentifier` (messages 3743/3745/3747); "rep and warrant relief only for the calculation, not for data integrity."

**Tax returns, Form 4506-C/8821 and transcripts — B3-3.1-02 (06/03/2026); D1-3-03 (04/01/2026); IRS IVES (page last updated Mar. 10, 2026; 00b-orig N3). VERIFIED.** "The lender must have each borrower whose income is used in qualifying (regardless of income source) complete and sign a separate IRS Form 4506-C at or before closing."; "When all of a borrower's income is validated by the DU validation service, the lender is not required to obtain a signed IRS Form 4506–C for that borrower."; "IRS Form 4506-C can be used to obtain tax transcripts for up to four years or tax periods and is valid for 120 days after completion (including signature) by the borrower."; "An alternative form or process (which may include IRS Form 8821) is also acceptable if it authorizes the release of comparable tax information from the IRS and the borrower has signed an authorization expressly permitting the lender to disclose such IRS-derived information to Fannie Mae." Returns "must be copies of the original returns that were filed with the IRS. All supporting schedules must be included."; each return "must be signed by the borrower unless the lender has obtained one of the following signature alternatives: documentation confirming that the tax returns were filed electronically, a completed IRS Form 4506–C (signed by the borrower) for the year in question, or IRS transcripts that validate the tax return." Transcripts: "When federal income tax information is used to document income for qualifying purposes, the lender may obtain transcripts of the applicable federal income tax documents. For example, the lender may obtain Tax Return Transcripts for Form 1040 or Wage and Income Transcripts for W-2s, 1098s, and 1099s."; Schedule C income validated by DU → tax returns not required; "The lender must retain all tax documents, including either the IRS Form 4506-C or the tax transcript(s) and any subsequent explanation or documentation of discrepancies in the loan file for QC review." QC (D1-3-03): "The lender must reverify the income and employment that was used to underwrite the loan"; "The post-closing QC review must include the lender's submission for tax transcripts to the IRS (or designee) using the IRS Form 4506-C ... when tax returns were required and relied upon in underwriting."; "If the lender obtained the appropriate IRS transcripts during its preclosing process (processing and underwriting), it may use the same documents in its post-closing QC process, without ordering new transcripts." IVES: participant ID "ten digits including leading zeroes"; "$4 fee is required for each transcript requested"; channels WebUI ("Transcript delivery in hours"), A2A "for large volume filing," fax (2–3 business days); e-signature: two-factor or KBA or SSO, prior consent, tamper-proof document, "An audit log of the entire electronic signing ceremony must accompany the document"; audit logs and the 4506-C retained "for a period of 2 years"; monthly audit of "a minimum 125 electronically signed requests"; annual findings by Jan 31. The 120-day signature validity for IVES processing was not on the IRS page read (it is in B3-3.1-02) — treat both as binding.

**Reg B §1002.6(b) (CFPB eRegs, current). VERIFIED.** (b)(2)(i): "Except as permitted in this paragraph, a creditor shall not take into account an applicant's age (provided that the applicant has the capacity to enter into a binding contract) or whether an applicant's income derives from any public assistance program."; (b)(2)(iii): "In a judgmental system of evaluating creditworthiness, a creditor may consider an applicant's age or whether an applicant's income derives from any public assistance program only for the purpose of determining a pertinent element of creditworthiness."; (b)(3): no assumptions or aggregate statistics about childbearing/childrearing or diminished income for that reason; (b)(5): "A creditor shall not discount or exclude from consideration the income of an applicant or the spouse of an applicant because of a prohibited basis or because the income is derived from part-time employment or is an annuity, pension, or other retirement benefit; a creditor may consider the amount and probable continuance of any income in evaluating an applicant's creditworthiness." Alimony/child support/separate maintenance: creditors "shall consider such payments as income to the extent that they are likely to be consistently made." The April 2026 Reg B final rule (effective July 21, 2026) amended §1002.6(a)/comment 6(a)-2 (effects test) and did not change (b) (00a-fed §3.8).

**Reg Z §1026.43(c) (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026). VERIFIED (rule) / PARTIALLY VERIFIED (safe-harbor comment).** (c)(2)(i)–(ii): the creditor must consider "The consumer's current or reasonably expected income or assets, other than the value of the dwelling" and, if relying on employment income, "the consumer's current employment status"; (c)(3): "A creditor must verify the information that the creditor relies on in determining a consumer's repayment ability under §1026.43(c)(2) using reasonably reliable third-party records" (with the oral employment-verification exception when the creditor prepares a record); (c)(4): verify income/assets "using third-party records that provide reasonably reliable evidence," including IRS tax-return transcripts or filed returns, W-2s or similar, payroll statements "including military Leave and Earnings Statements," financial institution records, employer records or third-party information obtained from the employer, government benefit statements, check-cashing and funds-transfer receipts. Comment 43(e)(2)(v)(B)-3 pins the verification safe harbor to "Chapters B3-3 through B3-6 of the Fannie Mae Single Family Selling Guide, published June 3, 2020" (00a-fed §5.4, secondary source — PARTIALLY VERIFIED); 23.4 owns the QM determination and records which standard was used.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. **Renumbering (SEL-2026-02/-08):** VVOE is **B3-3.1-04** (not B3-3.1-07); self-employment is **B3-3.5** (not B3-3.2, which is now employment/income standards); rental income is **B3-3.8** (not B3-3.4, which is now other income); other income is **B3-3.4** (not B3-3.5). The baseline addendum's timer code `FNMA_B3_3_1_07_VVOE_10BD` is stale — the code defined here is `FNMA_B3_3_1_04_VVOE_10BD`.
2. **"10 business days" has no Fannie Mae definition**; the platform must pick a calendar (default `business_days_creditor`, Q1) and must also handle the 15-business-day paystub/bank-statement alternatives and the 120-calendar-day LES/DMDC/self-employment rules the row omits.
3. **"DU validation report expiration" is not a fixed date**: reports follow B1-1-03's four-month rule, DU weighs "the age of the information in the vendor's database," and the binding date is the DU employment-validation **"Close by Date"** (B3-2-02/A2-2-04); a missed Close by Date can be cured with a supplemental report or a VVOE (00a-fnma §3.5).
4. **Form 4506-C is required per borrower at or before closing** unless all of that borrower's income is DU-validated; **Form 8821 is an accepted alternative** (SEL-2026-06); the form is **valid 120 days**; transcripts are **optional in origination** ("may obtain") and **required in post-closing QC** when returns were relied upon, reusable if pulled pre-closing (D1-3-03).
5. **The variable-income calculation changed in 2026**: stable/increasing → (YTD + previous year) ÷ (months in YTD + 12); decreasing → prove stabilization and use YTD ÷ months since stabilization, else ineligible. The pre-2026 "declining but stabilized → use the lower amount" language is gone.
6. **Rental income policy changed (SEL-2026-08)** — 12 months of property-management experience for positive rental income (otherwise PITIA offset only), six-month minimum lease term, ADU capped at 30% of total qualifying income, new departing-residence framework — mandatory for applications on/after **Nov 1, 2026** (the refinance fixture, application Oct 5, 2026, is grandfathered; the platform implements immediately).
7. **Employment offers**: Option 2 requires SFC 707 and the "no earlier than 30 days prior / no later than 90 days after the note date" window; Option 1 requires a paystub before delivery, not before closing.
8. **Social Security**: 15% may be treated as nontaxable without documentation; gross-up is 25% of the nontaxable portion — the row's "non-taxable income gross-up" needs both numbers.
9. **Reg B (b)(2)/(b)(5) also bar age-based and part-time discounting** — not just public assistance; and (b)(3) constrains temporary-leave handling (no assumptions about parental leave).
10. **Foreign income requires two years of U.S. returns** including the foreign income, English translations attached, and conversion to U.S. dollars.
11. **The DU validation service does not carry data through SM**: suppliers transmit to Fannie Mae directly; SM orders, stores the report and references it (00b-orig Part (c)(5)).

#### State machine

Per income source: `declared` → `documentation_requested` (22.1 needs list) → `documented` → `calculated` → (`validated` via DU | `verified_manual`) → `finalized` → (`reverified` on closing move | `withdrawn`). Guards: `finalized` requires (a) a calculation with a versioned formula, (b) evidence meeting B3-3.2-01/B1-1-03 freshness (22.1 gates), (c) continuance documented where required, (d) Reg B flags reviewed (no discount applied), (e) ATR record types recorded. Employment verification: `pending` → `scheduled` (window opens) → `completed` | `failed` (employer unreachable → alternative method) → `expired` (note date moved beyond the window) → re-`scheduled`. Transcript request: `signed` → `ordered` → `received` | `no_record` | `rejected` → `reconciled`. Terminal for the process: `income.finalized` snapshot used by the final DU submission and the credit decision (23.3); reopened only by a closing move or a new fact.

#### Business rules and calculations

All money in cents (bigint); intermediate values keep full precision; rounding to the cent (half up) happens once, at the final monthly figure for each income source; annual-to-monthly division by 12 is exact in cents only after rounding at that step.

**R1 — VVOE window (B3-3.1-04).** `window_start = subtract_business_days(note_date, 10, calendar='creditor')`; a verification is timely iff `window_start ≤ contacted_at::date ≤ note_date`. Refinance fixture, note date Fri Nov 6, 2026, counting back on calendar `creditor` (Mon–Fri less federal holidays): Nov 5 (1), Nov 4 (2), Nov 3 (3), Nov 2 (4), Oct 30 (5), Oct 29 (6), Oct 28 (7), Oct 27 (8), Oct 26 (9), **Fri Oct 23, 2026 (10)** → VVOE no earlier than Oct 23. Purchase fixture, closing Wed Nov 18, 2026: Nov 17 (1), Nov 16 (2), Nov 13 (3), Nov 12 (4), Nov 11 Veterans Day skipped, Nov 10 (5), Nov 9 (6), Nov 6 (7), Nov 5 (8), Nov 4 (9), **Tue Nov 3, 2026 (10)**. Paystub/bank-statement alternative (15 business days): refinance → Oct 22 (11), Oct 21 (12), Oct 20 (13), Oct 19 (14), **Fri Oct 16, 2026 (15)**; purchase → Nov 2 (11), Oct 30 (12), Oct 29 (13), Oct 28 (14), **Tue Oct 27, 2026 (15)**. Self-employment business verification (120 calendar days): refinance → Nov 6 − 120 = **Thu Jul 9, 2026** (Jul 9→Aug 9 = 31, →Sep 9 = 31, →Oct 9 = 30, →Nov 6 = 28; total 120); purchase → Nov 18 − 120 = **Tue Jul 21, 2026**. Every reschedule recomputes the windows; a VVOE done Oct 26 is still valid if the refinance closes on Nov 9 (window Oct 26–Nov 9) but not if it closes Nov 10 (window Oct 27–Nov 10). The employer's phone number must come from `phone_source ∈ {directory_assistance, internet_listing, licensing_bureau, telephone_book, vendor_database}` and never from a borrower-supplied document; the evidence (screenshot/listing) is stored.

**R2 — Base income (B3-3.3-01).** Salary: `monthly = round(annual_salary_cents / 12)`; $75,000.00 → 7,500,000 / 12 = **625,000 cents ($6,250.00)**. Fixed hourly: `monthly = round(rate_cents × avg_hours_per_week × 52 / 12)`; $32.50 × 40 × 52 / 12 = 3,250 × 40 × 52 / 12 = 6,760,000 / 12 = 563,333.33… → **563,333 cents ($5,633.33)**. Hours fluctuating beyond "minor variances" → reclassify as `base_hourly_variable` and apply R3. Documentation: Form 1005, or most recent paystub (22.1 floor: dated ≥ application − 30 days, with YTD) plus most recent W-2; VVOE always.

**R3 — Variable income trending (B3-3.3-02).** Inputs: `ytd_cents`, `ytd_months` (months covered by the YTD paystub, fractional months allowed at 1/12-year precision by pay period), `prior_year_cents` (W-2/1005), `prior_prior_year_cents` (where a two-year history exists). Step 1: `ytd_monthly = ytd_cents / ytd_months`; `prior_monthly = prior_year_cents / 12`. Step 2 (trend): `increasing` if `ytd_monthly > prior_monthly`; `stable` if within ±`stable_band` (Q4, default 5%) of `prior_monthly`; otherwise `decreasing`. Step 3: stable/increasing → `qualifying = round((ytd_cents + prior_year_cents) / (ytd_months + 12))`; decreasing → require `stabilized_since` evidence (paystub-level series showing a flat run) and `qualifying = round(ytd_since_stabilized_cents / months_since_stabilized)`; no stabilization → income not eligible. Worked example A (increasing): overtime 2025 W-2 = 1,080,000 cents ($10,800.00); 2026 YTD through Sept 30 (9 months) = 855,000 cents ($8,550.00). `ytd_monthly = 95,000` ($950.00); `prior_monthly = 90,000` ($900.00) → increasing → `qualifying = (855,000 + 1,080,000) / 21 = 1,935,000 / 21 = 92,142.857…` → **92,143 cents ($921.43)**. Worked example B (decreasing): 2025 overtime 1,440,000 cents ($1,200.00/mo); 2026 YTD 855,000 over 9 months = 95,000/mo → −20.83% → decreasing; the paystub series shows overtime flat at ≈ $950.00/mo since Apr 1, 2026 → `stabilized_since = 2026-04-01`; income Apr–Sep = 570,000 cents over 6 months → **95,000 cents ($950.00)**; with no stabilization evidence → 0 and a written reason. Bonus: annual $12,000.00 paid Mar 31 → 1,200,000 / 12 = **100,000 cents ($1,000.00)** per month for the trend comparison. History: ≥ 24 months recommended; 12–23 months acceptable only with documented offsetting factors (recorded in `inputs.offsetting_factors`).

**R4 — Self-employment (B3-3.5-01; Form 1084; Income Calculator).** `self_employed = ownership_pct ≥ 25`. Returns: two years personal (and business, per structure) unless the one-year conditions hold (business ≥ 5 consecutive years with ≥ 25% ownership and increasing income over the past two years) or DU permits one year (message-driven; 23.2); less than two years of self-employment acceptable only with a full 12-month year on the returns and prior same-level income. Calculation through Form 1084 or the Income Calculator (preferred: rep-and-warrant relief on the calculation when `qualifying ≤ calculator_result` and the Findings Report is filed); the agent drafts the "written evaluation" (B3-3.5-01) as a document (`doc_class = explanation_letter` subclass `se_written_evaluation`). Business existence verified within 120 calendar days (R1). Year-to-date P&L / business-statement rules: **[UNVERIFIED — B3-3.6/3.7 not read; default: require YTD P&L and two months of business statements when more than 120 days have elapsed since the last tax-year end, pending re-fetch].**

**R5 — Rental income (B3-3.8; SEL-2026-08).** Rule-set switch: `application_date ≥ 2026-11-01` → B3-3.8 mandatory; earlier applications may use it (platform default: always use B3-3.8 unless it would remove income the prior rule allowed, in which case the agent records the election). Subject-property purchase: `net = round(gross_rent_cents × 0.75)`; `anri = net − pitia_cents`; if the borrower has ≥ 12 months of property-management experience (Schedule E, 365 Fair Rental Days) → positive `anri` is added to income, negative to liabilities; else positive `anri` offsets PITIA only (`offset_only = true`) and cannot add income. Worked example (investment purchase, Form 1007 gross rent $2,400.00): `net = 240,000 × 0.75 = 180,000` ($1,800.00); PITIA $2,150.00 → `anri = 180,000 − 215,000 = −35,000` → **$350.00 added to monthly obligations** regardless of experience. With PITIA $1,650.00 → `anri = +15,000` ($150.00): experienced → +$150.00 income; inexperienced → offset only (PITIA treated as fully covered, no income). ADU (purchase/LCOR only): `adu_income ≤ 0.30 × total_qualifying_income` — with total qualifying income $9,000.00 the ADU contribution caps at **270,000 cents ($2,700.00)**; DU 12.1 issues the > 30% message. Leases for properties not on the 1040 need a term ≥ 6 months; departing-residence rules (B3-3.8-05) use market rents, reserves and PITIA-offset limits — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — B3-3.8-05 not read; implement from the topic before release]**.

**R6 — Other income and gross-up (B3-3.1-01, B3-3.4-x).** Nontaxable gross-up: `gross_up = round(nontaxable_cents × 0.25)`. Social Security $2,000.00 with 15% treated as nontaxable without documentation: `nontaxable = 30,000`, `gross_up = 7,500` → qualifying **207,500 cents ($2,075.00)**; if the tax return shows a larger nontaxable share, use the documented share. Child support $800.00 (fully nontaxable): `gross_up = 20,000` → **100,000 cents ($1,000.00)**, requiring the decree/order, six months of receipts ("full, regular, and timely"), and `continuance_end_date` derived from the child's age (child turns 18 on Mar 3, 2029 → end Mar 3, 2029 < Nov 6, 2029 → fails 3-year continuance → excluded with a written reason, unless state law extends support). Retirement-account distributions: continuance proved by combined eligible balances ≥ 36 × monthly distribution with unrestricted, penalty-free access. Public assistance: agency letter with amount/frequency/duration; never a reason to discount (Reg B (b)(2)); Section 8 homeownership payments per B3-3.4-14.

**R7 — Employment offers (B3-3.3-03).** Option 1: qualifying income = offer letter monthly amount; the paystub must be saved before delivery (29.4 gate `offer_paystub_saved`); VVOE required. Option 2 (purchase fixture): note date Wed Nov 18, 2026 → start date window **Mon Oct 19, 2026 – Tue Feb 16, 2027** (Nov 18 + 90: →Dec 18 = 30, →Jan 18 = 31, + 29 → Feb 16); reserves: 6 × PITIA or bridge months + 1; SFC 707 at delivery; fixed base income only.

**R8 — Temporary leave (B3-3.3-09; Reg B (b)(3)).** `return_date ≤ first_payment_due_date` (refinance fixture: Jan 1, 2027) → regular income; otherwise `min(leave_income, regular_income)` plus documented liquid reserves to bridge; the agent never infers leave from a pay gap or family status — it asks the borrower only for the return date and employer confirmation; no assumption about childbearing is recorded anywhere (Reg B (b)(3)).

**R9 — DU validation and Income Calculator orchestration.** Order the report(s) from an authorized supplier under the borrower's authorization; store the report and `report_reference_id`; place the supplier code/identifier in the DU submission (23.1); on `validated` → paper requests waived (22.1) and relief recorded (`rep_warrant_relief{component}` in 23.3) subject to Close by Date and message resolution; on `not_validated` → the DU message's documentation list becomes needs-list items; on conflicting information (e.g., vendor employer name ≠ 1003) → investigate and correct DU data (A2-2-04). Income Calculator: submit tax-return data (TSP path if certified, else iFrame/import by the agent), retain the Findings Report, set `qualifying ≤ calculator_result`, pass `DU:VerificationReportIdentifier`. Worked example (refinance fixture): DU employment validation message on Wed Oct 7, 2026 states a Close by Date of Fri Nov 20, 2026 (illustrative) → `FNMA_B3_2_02_DU_CLOSE_BY_GATE` open for the Nov 6 consummation; a slip to Mon Nov 23 → supplemental report or VVOE (R1 window Fri Nov 6 – Mon Nov 23, counting back ten creditor business days and skipping Veterans Day) and DU resubmission.

**R10 — Transcripts and 4506-C/8821.** Each qualifying borrower signs a separate 4506-C (or 8821 with the Fannie Mae-disclosure authorization) at or before closing unless all of that borrower's income is DU-validated; e-signature with two-factor/KBA/SSO and an audit log; validity 120 days (signed Mon Oct 5, 2026 → **Tue Feb 2, 2027**). Origination transcripts are ordered by policy (Q3 default: order Return Transcripts for the years used whenever tax returns are relied upon for qualifying — self-employment, rental, commission with returns, foreign income — and Wage & Income transcripts when W-2/1099 integrity is `flagged` by 22.1); a "no record" response for a required year triggers the B1-1-03 Form 4868 path or exclusion; discrepancies between transcript and return → resolve before `income.finalized` (B3-3.1-02 retention of the discrepancy explanation). QC (28.2) reuses pre-closing transcripts (D1-3-03). IVES cost 400 cents per transcript → `third_party_costs`.

**R11 — Reg B evaluation rules.** For every source flagged `part_time`, `retirement`, `public_assistance`, `alimony_child_support`, `age_related`: the calculation must apply the same formula as for any other source of the same type; the only permitted variable is "amount and probable continuance" (b)(5); alimony/child support are considered "to the extent that they are likely to be consistently made" — the six-month receipt history is the consistency test, not a haircut. The `agent_decisions` record must contain `regb_check = pass` with the formula id used; any exclusion cites a documented continuance or history failure, never the source's nature.

**R12 — ATR record manifest (23.4 hand-off).** For each income source, `atr_record_types` lists the §1026.43(c)(4) categories relied on (e.g., `payroll_statement`, `w2`, `irs_transcript`, `employer_record_third_party`, `government_benefit_statement`); an oral VOE is acceptable only with the written record (R1 fields); at `income.finalized` the manifest is frozen and 23.4 records the verification standard (current Selling Guide vs the June 3, 2020 safe-harbor edition — 23.4-Q).

**R13 — Total qualifying income.** `total_qualifying_cents = Σ monthly_qualifying_cents over sources with used_for_qualifying = true + Σ gross_up_cents`; net rental losses are not netted here — they go to 22.5 as monthly obligations; the ADU cap and the offset-only rentals are applied before summation. Illustration with the sources worked above: base $6,250.00 + overtime $921.43 + Social Security $2,075.00 = **924,643 cents ($9,246.43)**; 22.5 divides the fixture's obligations by this total to produce the DTI and reconciles it to the fixture's 38%.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`verification` agent (income tools: `buildDocumentationMatrix`, `orderVerificationReport`, `submitIncomeCalculator`, `calculateIncome{type}`, `assessTrend`, `computeGrossUp`, `evaluateContinuance`, `runRegBCheck`, `scheduleVvoe`, `sourceEmployerPhone`, `placeVvoeCall`, `recordVvoe`, `verifyBusinessExistence`, `prepareForm4506C`, `orderTranscript`, `reconcileTranscript`, `draftWrittenEvaluation`, `buildAtrManifest`, `finalizeIncome`, `writeDecision`, `escalate`). End-to-end: from the intake declaration it builds the documentation matrix (preferring source-obtained reports over borrower paper), orders reports, calculates each source with the versioned formula, assesses trend and continuance, runs the Reg B check, prepares the 4506-C/8821 for e-signature, orders transcripts by policy, schedules and performs the VVOE inside the window (AI voice with an opening disclosure that the caller is an automated system verifying employment on behalf of the partner; independent phone sourcing; recording only where lawful), verifies business existence, drafts the written evaluation, freezes the ATR manifest and finalizes. Decision record: `{income_id, formula_version, inputs_cents, steps, trend, qualifying_cents, gross_up_cents, continuance, regb_flags, regb_check, evidence_document_ids, du_validation_outcome, calculator_report_id, atr_record_types, rationale, confidence}`. Guardrails — never: use income above the Income Calculator result when the calculator was used; use a borrower-supplied phone number for a VVOE; treat a DU "not validated" as a validation; apply a haircut or exclusion because income is part-time, retirement, public assistance, alimony/child support, or age-related; infer leave, pregnancy or family plans; accept a paystub/W-2 that 22.1 marked `flagged` as the sole evidence; sign or alter Form 4506-C/8821 (the borrower signs; the agent prepares); waive the VVOE, the 4506-C or the 3-year continuance; finalize income after a closing move without re-running the windows. Escalations: `underwriting_reviewer` when finalized income would produce a denial/counteroffer (21.6; SLA 2 `business_days_creditor`) or when transcript discrepancies suggest misrepresentation (with 22.6); `licensed_specialist` where required; `human_agent` on borrower request or when the employer refuses to speak to an automated caller (human operator places the call). Borrower-facing: the needs-list wording explains why each item is needed in plain language; income conclusions are shared with the borrower before DU final submission so errors can be corrected (Reg B (b)(6)(ii)-style correction opportunity). Human path: ops console for manual calculations with mandatory formula id, manual VVOE entry with the same required fields, and manual transcript ordering.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Employer will not verify employment by phone or uses a verification vendor → vendor written verification within the same window (B3-3.1-04); if the vendor's data is stale, DU weighs the age — prefer a fresh vendor pull inside the window.
- Employer verifies "on leave" → switch to B3-3.3-09 rules; if the return date is after the first payment due date and reserves cannot bridge, income is reduced with a written reason (no Reg B inference recorded).
- Borrower paid in cash or by a family employer → DU validation removes the family-employer check only when validated; otherwise obtain tax returns/transcripts and treat the employer relationship as a fraud red flag for 22.6 review.
- Two employers with a gap > 1 month in the last 12 months → not acceptable unless seasonal (B3-3.2-02); the agent documents the analysis.
- Second job < 12 months → excluded (needs ≥ 12 months); the exclusion reason is the history, not the job's nature.
- Self-employed with a business loss on one entity → Form 1084 nets losses; negative business income reduces qualifying income; K-1 < 25% ownership follows B3-3.4-19.
- Tax year turnover during processing (e.g., closing in Jan 2027) → B1-1-03 row (5): 2025 returns required, no extension; the 4506-C years list must include 2025; transcripts for 2025 may not yet be available → "no record" handling per B1-1-03.
- Closing slips past the Close by Date → supplemental report/VVOE and DU resubmission; relief for employment may be lost — recorded in `rep_warrant_relief`.
- VVOE window recomputation on a same-week reschedule (Nov 6 → Nov 9): a VVOE dated Oct 23 falls outside the new window (Oct 26–Nov 9) → re-perform; the agent schedules VVOEs two business days before the note date by default to survive small slips.
- Employment offer with contingencies (background check pending) → option 2 unavailable ("non-contingent"); wait for satisfaction or use option 1.
- Rental income where the borrower has < 12 months experience but positive ANRI → offset only; DU's message and the rule-set switch by application date are both logged.
- Departing residence converted to rental → B3-3.8-05 framework (market rents/reserves/offset limits) — flagged PARTIALLY VERIFIED until the topic is read.
- Foreign income → two years of U.S. returns including the income, English translations, USD conversion (rate source and date recorded).
- Transcript shows an amended return or an amount different from the return → discrepancy explanation retained (B3-3.1-02); if unresolved, income limited to the transcript figure or excluded.
- Borrower refuses to sign a 4506-C/8821 → unless fully DU-validated, the loan cannot close (Fannie Mae requirement); 21.6 handles as incomplete/withdrawn.
- IVES A2A outage → WebUI; if the closing cannot wait for transcripts that policy (not Fannie Mae) requires, the `underwriting_reviewer` may defer the transcript to post-closing QC with a written exception (Fannie Mae permits origination without transcripts).
- Call-recording consent states → the AI voice call is not recorded; the structured `employment_verifications` record is the evidence.

#### Test cases and acceptance criteria
- **22.3-T1 (VVOE window, refinance).** Given note date Fri Nov 6, 2026, when the window is computed on calendar `creditor`, then `window_start = Fri Oct 23, 2026`; a VVOE contacted Thu Oct 22 is `within_window = false` and Fri Oct 23 is `true`.
- **22.3-T2 (VVOE window across Veterans Day, purchase).** Given closing Wed Nov 18, 2026, when computed, then `window_start = Tue Nov 3, 2026` (Nov 11 excluded) and the 15-business-day paystub alternative floor is Tue Oct 27, 2026.
- **22.3-T3 (self-employment verification window).** Given note date Nov 6, 2026, when `FNMA_B3_3_1_04_SE_VERIFY_120` is evaluated, then a Secretary-of-State/licensing verification dated Thu Jul 9, 2026 passes and Wed Jul 8, 2026 fails; for closing Nov 18 the floor is Tue Jul 21, 2026.
- **22.3-T4 (trending, increasing).** Given overtime prior year 1,080,000 cents and YTD 855,000 cents over 9 months, when calculated, then `trend = increasing` and `monthly_qualifying_cents = 92,143`.
- **22.3-T5 (trending, decreasing with stabilization).** Given prior year 1,440,000 cents and YTD 855,000 over 9 months with a documented flat run since Apr 1, 2026 totaling 570,000 over 6 months, when calculated, then `trend = decreasing`, `stabilized_since = 2026-04-01`, and `monthly_qualifying_cents = 95,000`; with no stabilization evidence the result is 0 with reason `not_stabilized`.
- **22.3-T6 (hourly base).** Given $32.50/hour and 40 guaranteed hours, when calculated, then `monthly_qualifying_cents = 563,333`; given hours of 28–44 with no guaranteed minimum, then the source is reclassified `base_hourly_variable` and R3 applies.
- **22.3-T7 (Social Security gross-up).** Given $2,000.00 SS retirement on the borrower's own record with no tax return, when calculated, then `nontaxable_cents = 30,000`, `gross_up_cents = 7,500`, qualifying 207,500 cents, `continuance_basis = retirement_own_record`.
- **22.3-T8 (child support continuance).** Given $800.00 child support with the child turning 18 on Mar 3, 2029 and note date Nov 6, 2026, when evaluated, then `FNMA_B3_3_1_01_CONTINUANCE_3Y` fails (end Mar 3, 2029 < Nov 6, 2029) and the income is excluded with a written reason; with a child born after Nov 6, 2011 it passes and qualifying = 100,000 cents.
- **22.3-T9 (rental 75% and experience).** Given Form 1007 gross rent $2,400.00 and PITIA $1,650.00 on a purchase, when the borrower has 365 Fair Rental Days on the 2025 Schedule E, then +15,000 cents is added to income; with no management history, `offset_only = true` and 0 is added; with PITIA $2,150.00, 35,000 cents is added to liabilities in both cases.
- **22.3-T10 (ADU cap).** Given total qualifying income 900,000 cents and ADU rent yielding 300,000 cents net, when applied, then ADU income is capped at 270,000 cents on a purchase/LCOR and excluded on a cash-out refinance.
- **22.3-T11 (Close by Date).** Given a DU employment validation with Close by Date Nov 20, 2026, when consummation moves to Nov 23, then `FNMA_B3_2_02_DU_CLOSE_BY_GATE` breaches, a supplemental report or VVOE (window Nov 6–23) is required, DU is resubmitted, and the employment relief record is updated.
- **22.3-T12 (4506-C validity and exemption).** Given a 4506-C signed Mon Oct 5, 2026, when validity is computed, then `valid_until = Tue Feb 2, 2027`; given Borrower B's only income is DU-validated, then no 4506-C is required for B and the `consummate` gate passes with A's form alone.
- **22.3-T13 (Reg B no-discount).** Given a part-time base income of $1,800.00/month with 24 months history, when calculated, then the formula id equals the full-time base formula and `regb_check = pass`; any attempt to apply a factor < 1.0 is rejected by the engine.
- **22.3-T14 (employment offer option 2).** Given a purchase closing Nov 18, 2026 with a non-contingent offer starting Mon Feb 15, 2027 and 6 months PITIA reserves, when evaluated, then option 2 is available (start ≤ Feb 16, 2027) and SFC 707 is queued; with a start of Wed Feb 17, 2027 option 2 is refused.
- **22.3-T15 (Income Calculator ceiling).** Given a calculator result of 812,500 cents for a Schedule C borrower, when the agent's Form 1084 analysis yields 830,000 cents, then qualifying is 812,500 cents and the Findings Report id is attached; a manual override above the ceiling is rejected.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Calendar for "10 business days" / "15 business days" (B3-3.1-04).** Default: `business_days_creditor` (Mon–Fri less federal holidays); document the choice in the partner's policy; alternative: calendar days minus weekends only.
2. **VVOE by AI voice.** Default: permitted with an opening automation disclosure and structured record; recording only in one-party-consent states; employers refusing automated calls are handled by a human operator or vendor/written alternatives. Confirm the partner's comfort and any state telemarketing/robocall constraints (not TCPA marketing, but state law varies).
3. **Transcript policy and channel.** Default: order Return Transcripts whenever tax returns are relied upon and Wage & Income transcripts when 22.1 flags W-2/1099 integrity; channel via a DU tax-transcript supplier (also feeds DU validation) with direct IVES A2A as the second channel; the partner (not SM) is the IVES participant of record.
4. **Stable-trend band.** Default ±5% between YTD monthly and prior-year monthly; outside the band and lower → `decreasing`.
5. **Rental rule-set switch for applications before Nov 1, 2026.** Default: apply B3-3.8 immediately (Fannie Mae encourages it) unless it removes income permitted under the prior policy, in which case the agent records the election of the prior rule.
6. **Self-employment YTD P&L/business-statement trigger and departing-residence rules.** Default as stated in R4/R5 pending re-fetch of B3-3.6/B3-3.7/B3-3.8-05.
7. **Verification standard for the ATR safe harbor** (June 3, 2020 Selling Guide vs current) — 23.4 decides; default: record both and treat current-Guide compliance as the operating standard.
