### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-09)

**Servicing Guide A4-1-01, Staffing, Training, Procedures, and Quality Control Requirements (02/12/2025; SVC-2025-01).** Lead-in: "The servicer must monitor its compliance with Fannie Mae's requirements through regular QC procedures it establishes and conducts." The QC procedures must "ensure the mortgage loans are serviced in accordance with sound mortgage banking and accounting principles" and Fannie Mae requirements; "guard against misrepresentation and dishonest, fraudulent, or negligent acts by any parties involved"; protect against errors and omissions by officers and employees; "verify and audit routinely the accuracy of the ARM loan adjustment" and respond to errors timely; and protect Fannie Mae's interest in the security property. Vendors: "The servicer must perform annual QC tests to ensure all outsourcing firms and third-party vendors used by the servicer are fully compliant" with the Guide; "Results of the QC tests must be provided to Fannie Mae upon request." Sanction: "Failure to maintain adequate QC measures will result in the servicer being in breach of its Lender Contract." *QC for delinquency management* must cover: "Monitoring the effectiveness of collection and foreclosure prevention calls to borrowers"; "Conducting periodic reviews of any Behavioral Model Tool" with "details of the model and any analysis available to Fannie Mae upon request" (for this platform the Behavioral Model Tool is the AI collections prioritization in `default-collections` — see AI governance below); "Determining whether documentation of collection and foreclosure prevention activities is accurately maintained"; workout consideration per the hierarchy (D2-3.1-01, F-2-10); documentation of all options considered before a foreclosure decision; internal-control adequacy; "Determining accurate and timely delinquency status information is submitted to Fannie Mae"; borrower communications compliant with the FDCPA, Bankruptcy Code and state law. *QC of systems* (delinquent-loan, bankruptcy and foreclosure monitoring systems): "regularly review and assess the adequacy of internal controls"; "conduct regular testing of compliance with applicable laws in all jurisdictions"; "keep a record of any activity under these internal systems"; "report comprehensive results of all testing to the senior management"; "promptly take appropriate corrective action if these systems identify a problem area"; "make the comprehensive results of all testing and evidence of correction actions taken available for review upon Fannie Mae's request." The topic's *written policies and procedures* list (vendor oversight incl. FHFA Suspended Counterparty Program screening, electronic payment controls, ARM servicing, insurance loss events, unapplied funds, MI termination, fee assessment, successors in interest, servicing transfers, call-center coverage, collections, Behavioral Model Tool, appeals, forbearance, pre-foreclosure sale review, bankruptcy) defines the minimum **QC scope**; Fannie Mae may request the policies and "examples of the application of those policies and procedures to specific instances."

**Servicing Guide A2-1-01 (12/17/2025)** — subservicer/outsource-vendor oversight duties quoted in the Blueprint row above apply to *all* servicers (they sit under "Overview of General Servicer Duties," not under the ≥20,000-loan technology-provider section). Also: servicers must maintain, "in queryable format," fair-lending data elements — race, ethnicity, age, gender and preferred language — for loans originated on or after March 1, 2023 (the population for 18.1's fair-servicing tests). **A2-1-07 (05/13/2026):** the master servicer must "maintain policies and procedures to evaluate the subservicer's compliance and performance with the master servicer's Lender Contract" and "for selecting and assessing a subservicer"; both parties must "provide copies of the subservicing agreement and the master servicer's audits and QC reviews of the subservicer's performance … upon request from Fannie Mae." **Selling Guide A4-1-01 (08/05/2026):** the seller/servicer must have "internal audit and management control procedures" that are "independent of all key functions" and "report directly to … senior management and/or board of directors," "effective and fully documented written policies and/or procedures," and "written procedures for the approval and management of vendors." **Selling Guide A2-4.1-02 (12/19/2017):** "Fannie Mae may examine and audit, at any reasonable time, all loan records"; the servicer must "document compliance with all Fannie Mae policies, procedures, and required timelines in the servicing loan file"; subservicers must be contractually bound to the same record-security measures. **A4-2.2-02 (11/12/2014):** law-firm oversight requires "periodic law firm compliance reviews" scaled to risk, with two-business-day escalation of fraud, data breaches, bar complaints and systemic issues to Fannie Mae Legal (Section 13 owns; 18.1 samples it).

**12 CFR 1024.38 (eCFR current Sept. 8, 2026):** "A servicer shall maintain policies and procedures that are reasonably designed to achieve the objectives set forth in paragraph (b)" — (b)(1) accurate and timely information, (b)(2) proper loss-mitigation evaluation, (b)(3) "facilitating oversight of, and compliance by, service providers" (periodic audits), (b)(4) transfers, (b)(5) informing borrowers of NoE/RFI procedures; (c)(1) retain records "until one year after" discharge or transfer; (c)(2) servicing file compilable "within five days." The CFPB's Compliance Management Review procedures (Aug. 30, 2017) examine board/management oversight, the compliance program (policies, training, monitoring/corrective action, complaint response), service-provider oversight and violations/consumer harm; the Mortgage Servicing Examination Procedures (Jan. 18, 2023) are organized in Modules 1–10 (transfers/escrow disclosures; payment processing; inquiries/complaints/error resolution; escrow/insurance; credit reporting; privacy; collections/bankruptcy; loss mitigation/early intervention/continuity; foreclosure; conclusions) — used here as the evidence taxonomy.

**LL-2026-04 (Apr. 8, 2026; effective Aug. 6, 2026).** Policies and procedures for AI/ML must "be transparent and communicated to appropriate personnel," "incorporate the characteristics of trustworthy and ethical AI/ML," "reflect an understanding of legal and regulatory requirements regarding AI/ML," "reflect risk management activities based on the lender's risk tolerance," "have an owner(s) that implements, maintains and reviews the policies and procedures at least annually," comply with the Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement, "manage risks and appropriate governance of subcontractor and vendor use of AI/ML that is no less protective," and the servicer must "promptly disclose the types of AI/ML used, the purpose and manner for such use, the safeguards … and such other information as Fannie Mae may require." The letter has no definitions, no bias-testing mandate and no remedies clause — breach is a Lender Contract breach (A1-4.1-01). **Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement (Sept. 2, 2025 edition):** annual threat risk assessment, NIST/ISO-aligned program, annual independent security assessment and penetration test, annual BCP test, vendor program "at least as protective," and an annual "written attestation executed by a duly authorized corporate officer." **State overlay (research/00a §5.6):** Colorado AI Act (eff. June 30, 2026) — risk-management program, impact assessments, pre-decision notice, adverse-decision explanation and human appeal for consequential financial/housing decisions; Utah/California disclosure norms. **Reg B:** a loss-mitigation denial to a *delinquent* borrower is not "adverse action" (12 CFR 1002.2(c)(2)(ii): "Any action or forbearance relating to an account taken in connection with inactivity, default, or delinquency"), but imminent-default (current-borrower) denials are, hence Fannie Mae **Form 183** (A4-2.1-06) certifying pre-approved adverse-action text — 18.1 samples both notice families. **CSBS Model Prudential Standards** (12 enacting states as of July 6, 2026, 00a §5.3): board oversight, internal audit and external audit, risk-management program for servicers with ≥2,000 loans in ≥2 states.

**Discrepancies with the blueprint row.** (1) Source is correct but incomplete — add A2-1-01, A2-1-07, Selling Guide A4-1-01/A2-4.1-02, Reg X 1024.38 and LL-2026-04. (2) "Per plan" understates the hard cadence: annual vendor QC tests are mandatory, and results/evidence must be producible on request at any time. (3) Automation class "b" is right for judgment reviews, but ~70% of QC tests here are class "a" deterministic re-derivations. (4) The SoR/Sub cell is not either/or: both run QC — Supermortgage over contracted activities, the partner over Supermortgage.

### Operational prerequisites
- **Written QC Plan v1** (Supermortgage; artifact `QC-PLAN-2026.1`, approved by the board/managing member and accepted by the partner under the subservicing agreement; 4–6 weeks). Must map to every A4-1-01 policy topic and to the CFPB Modules 1–9 and A1-1-03 categories.
- **QC officer** — a named Supermortgage individual independent of operations (Selling Guide A4-1-01 "independent of all key functions"), registered as `officer` with designation `qc_officer`; a **deputy** for segregation on appeals. **AI governance owner** (`officer`, designation `ai_governance_owner`) — LL-2026-04 "owner(s)". Both may be the same person only if the board accepts the concentration **[open decision 18.1-Q3]**.
- **Third line:** external internal-audit firm or the partner's internal audit function with charter access to the platform (read-only `ops-console` role `auditor`), plus the annual independent security assessment/pen-test vendor (Supplement).
- **Partner oversight agreement** — subservicing agreement clauses: QC plan acceptance, monthly QC report delivery, partner audit rights, A2-1-01 orderly-transfer plan (Section 17), Fannie Mae's right to copies (A2-1-07). Partner SoR; before boarding.
- **Fair-lending data** (A2-1-01) present on boarded loans (Section 1.1 field mapping) and access-controlled to the `qc-audit` fairness module.
- **AI governance program v1** (policy set, inventory, evaluation suites) approved before any Fannie Mae loan boards (baseline §11 "cross-cutting from day 1"); vendor flow-down clauses executed with Anthropic (API terms), the telephony/voice-AI provider, OCR/document-AI vendors, print/mail and any model-hosting vendor.
- **FHFA Suspended Counterparty Program / GSA SAM / HUD LDP screening** feed for employees and vendors (A4-1-01, A3-4-03) — `security-records` owns; QC tests it.

### Build spec
#### Inputs and triggers
- Schedules: `qc.cycle.monthly` (opens on the 3rd business day of each month for the prior month's population, after the investor-reporting close), `qc.cycle.quarterly` (adds jurisdiction and fairness tests), `qc.cycle.annual` (vendor QC tests, AI policy review, plan re-approval), `qc.continuous` (100% automated re-derivation tests run in the nightly job).
- Events that force targeted samples: `lossmit.decision.denied`, `foreclosure.referral.sent`, `bankruptcy.poc.filed`, `scra.status.detected`, `case.complaint.opened` (regulator/AG source), `case.fraud.opened`, `escrow.analysis.completed{shortage>threshold}`, `notice.sent{template in high-risk list}`, `arm.adjustment.completed{cap_test.applied≠none}` (7.2), `credit.furnish.file.sent` (8.3), `investor_event.rejected`, `timer.breached`, `agent_decision.overridden`, `agent_decision.low_confidence`.
- External: partner audit requests (`partner.audit.requested`), Fannie Mae requests for QC results (18.2), Guide/Lender Letter changes from `compliance-sentinel` (`ruleset.published`) which trigger rule re-mapping.

#### Data model
New tables (all `bigint` ids, `timestamptz` audit columns, append-only where stated; retention `life_of_loan_plus_4y` for loan-linked rows, `corporate_7y` for plan/report rows):
- `qc_plans`: `id`, `version` (e.g., `2026.1`), `effective_from`, `approved_by_officer_id`, `partner_accepted_at`, `document_id` (signed PDF), `scope_map` (jsonb: topic → rule codes), `status`.
- `qc_rules` (versioned, mirrors code in `packages/qc/rules/**`): `code` (e.g., `QC_ESCROW_ANALYSIS_RECOMPUTE`), `version`, `citation`, `taxonomy_nodes` (text[]: e.g., `cfpb.m4.escrow`, `fnma.a1103.escrow_admin`, `regab.1122.d4.x`), `population_selector` (parameterized SQL name), `test_kind` ∈ {rederive, checklist, judgment_llm, judgment_human, control_walkthrough}, `sampling` (jsonb: `method` ∈ {census, random, stratified, targeted}, `confidence`, `precision`, `min_n`, `strata`), `severity_default`, `owner_agent`, `remediation_owner_role`, `enabled_from`, `enabled_to`.
- `qc_cycles`: `id`, `kind` ∈ {monthly, quarterly, annual, adhoc}, `period_start`, `period_end`, `opened_at`, `closed_at`, `report_document_id`, `signed_by_officer_id`, `status`.
- `qc_samples`: `id`, `cycle_id`, `rule_code`, `rule_version`, `population_n`, `sample_n`, `selection_seed`, `selection_method`, `population_hash` (SHA-256 of the ordered id list, for reproducibility).
- `qc_tests` (append-only): `id`, `sample_id`, `subject_type` ∈ {loan, case, notice, payment, escrow_analysis, agent_decision, investor_event, ledger_entry, contact, vendor, control}, `subject_id`, `result` ∈ {pass, fail, na, inconclusive}, `expected` (jsonb), `observed` (jsonb), `variance_cents` (bigint, nullable), `reviewer_kind` ∈ {engine, llm, human}, `reviewer_model_version`, `agent_decision_id` (the `qc-audit` decision record), `human_reviewer_id`.

- **AI governance:** `ai_systems` (`code`, `name`, `kind` ∈ {agent, model, prompt_bundle, vendor_model, tool, deterministic_engine}, `purpose`, `risk_tier` ∈ {T1_consequential, T2_borrower_facing, T3_internal, T0_deterministic}, `owner_role`, `consumer_facing` bool, `consequential_decision` bool (Colorado), `fnma_data_access` bool, `vendor_id`, `deployed_at`, `retired_at`, `status`); `ai_system_versions` (`system_code`, `version`, `model_id` e.g. `claude-…`, `prompt_hash`, `rule_set_versions`, `eval_run_id`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `change_kind` ∈ {new, minor, major}); `ai_evaluations` (`version_id`, `suite_code`, `dataset_hash`, `metrics` jsonb, `pass` bool, `run_at`); `ai_monitoring_metrics` (daily rollups per system: decision volume, escalation rate, override rate, low-confidence rate, complaint rate, fairness stats); `ai_policy_documents` (policy code, version, owner, approved_at, next_review_due); `ai_disclosure_requests` (requester ∈ {fnma, partner, regulator, borrower}, received_at, package_document_id, signed_by, sent_at); `ai_vendor_attestations` (vendor, scope, attestation_document_id, equivalence_assessment, expires_at).
- Uses baseline `agent_decisions` (immutable; inputs, rule-set version, model version, prompt version, rationale, confidence, outcome, reviewer) as the primary QC subject for AI decisions.

#### State machine
`qc_cycles.status`: `planned → sampling → testing → findings_triage → reported → signed → closed`; `reopened` (new evidence) returns to `findings_triage`. `qc_findings` (`cases.status`): `open → validated (QC officer confirms) → capa_assigned → remediating → effectiveness_check → closed`; alternates `rejected` (false positive, with rationale) and `escalated_fnma` (self-report path, 18.5/18.2). `ai_system_versions.status`: `proposed → evaluated → approved → deployed → monitored → retired`; guard: no `deployed` without `ai_evaluations.pass = true` for every mandatory suite and an `approved_by` officer for T1/T2. Transitions by `qc-audit` (sampling/testing/triage), `officer:qc_officer` (validate, sign), `officer:ai_governance_owner` (approve versions), remediation owners (CAPA completion).

**B. Sampling parameters.** Census wherever a deterministic re-derivation exists (cost is compute). For judgment tests use a finite-population sample at 95% confidence, ±5% precision, p = 0.5: `n0 = 1.96² × 0.25 / 0.05² = 384.16`; `n = ceil(n0 / (1 + (n0 − 1)/N))`, floor 30, ceiling N. Worked example: N = 4,000 loss-mitigation decisions in a month → `n = ceil(384.16 / (1 + 383.16/4000)) = ceil(350.6) = 351`. Targeted (discretionary) additions on top of the random draw, always: 100% of denials, appeals, foreclosure referrals, SCRA-flagged loans, fraud cases, complaints from regulators/AGs, decisions with `confidence < 0.80`, overridden decisions, and any subject of a prior sev-1/2 finding for the next two cycles. Seeds are recorded so an examiner can regenerate the draw. Error-rate reporting per rule: `rate = fails / (pass + fails)` with a Wilson 95% interval; a rule breaches its **tolerance** (default 2% for rederive families, 5% for judgment families, 0% for sev-1 consumer-harm items) → automatic `qc_finding` at population level with root-cause analysis.

**C. Money remediation.** A failed rederive with `variance_cents ≠ 0` creates a reversing/correcting `ledger_entries` set (new entries, never edits), refunds within `SM_QC_CONSUMER_REMEDIATION_30`, and — if the error affected a notice — a corrected notice from the Notice Registry. Example: `QC_ESCROW_ANALYSIS_RECOMPUTE` finds a cushion computed at 1/4 of annual disbursements instead of 1/6 on a $4,800/yr escrow line: expected cushion 80,000¢, observed 120,000¢ → variance 40,000¢ over-collected → shortage spread recomputed, corrected annual statement, ledger credit 40,000¢ to `escrow`, finding severity sev-2 (no consumer harm if refunded before the payment change date) else sev-1.

**D. AI governance program (LL-2026-04) — program definition.**
1. *Policy set* (`ai_policy_documents`): `AI-GOV-001` AI Governance Policy (scope, principles: transparency, accountability, fairness, privacy/security, human oversight; owner; annual review); `AI-GOV-002` Inventory & Risk-Tiering Standard; `AI-GOV-003` Development & Change Management Standard (versioning of models/prompts/rule sets; eval gates; rollback); `AI-GOV-004` Evaluation & Testing Standard (golden datasets, accuracy thresholds per agent, adversarial/prompt-injection, fairness); `AI-GOV-005` Monitoring & Incident Standard (drift, escalation/override rates, complaint linkage, kill-switch); `AI-GOV-006` Vendor Flow-down Standard ("no less protective"; attestation; audit rights; Fannie Mae Data use limits per the Consolidated Technology Guide); `AI-GOV-007` Consumer Disclosure & Human Review Standard (baseline §8 items 5–6; Colorado/Utah/California; TCPA consent for AI voice); `AI-GOV-008` Data Governance for AI (PII minimization, no training on Fannie Mae Data, retention); `AI-GOV-009` Records & Disclosure Standard (decision records, Fannie Mae/regulator disclosure package).
2. *Inventory & tiers* (`ai_systems`): every baseline §8 agent, each Claude model id in use, each prompt bundle, the voice-AI vendor stack, OCR/document-extraction models, the collections prioritization model (Fannie Mae's "Behavioral Model Tool" — A4-1-01 requires periodic reviews and details available to Fannie Mae on request), and the deterministic engines (T0) they call. Tier drives controls: T1 (loss-mitigation eligibility/denial, foreclosure referral recommendation, fee assessment, credit-reporting derivation, fraud determinations) — mandatory human touchpoints per baseline §8, quarterly fairness tests, Colorado impact assessment, 100% decision-record completeness test; T2 (borrower-facing voice/chat/SMS/email, notice drafting) — disclosure test, transcript sampling, complaint linkage; T3 (internal triage) — accuracy sampling only.
3. *Evaluation suites* (`ai_evaluations`): per agent, a versioned golden dataset (≥300 cases for T1, ≥100 for T2/T3) with expected outcomes derived from the deterministic engines and human-adjudicated cases; pass thresholds: T1 outcome agreement ≥ 99.0% with zero unexplained adverse-outcome disagreements; T2 disclosure-given 100%, required-element coverage ≥ 98%; prompt-injection suite 100% blocked; regression on every model/prompt/rule-set change (`SM_AI_EVAL_GATE`).
4. *Fairness testing* (`QC_AI_FAIRNESS_LOSSMIT`, quarterly): using the A2-1-01 fair-lending elements (and BISG proxies only where elements are missing, flagged as proxy), compute per protected class the denial rate, offer-type mix, time-to-decision and appeal-reversal rate; report adverse-impact ratios (denial-rate ratio < 0.80 or a two-proportion z-test p < 0.05 triggers a finding and file-level review of matched pairs); the same test runs on fee assessment and foreclosure referral timing. Output is privileged-review candidate — route through counsel **[open decision 18.1-Q4]**.
5. *Monitoring* (`ai_monitoring_metrics`, daily; reviewed monthly): volume, escalation rate, human override rate (target band 2–15% for T1 — below 2% suggests rubber-stamping, above 15% suggests model or rule drift), low-confidence rate, complaint rate per 1,000 decisions, latency, tool-error rate, disclosure-given rate, borrower "ask for human" rate; automatic kill-switch (`feature flag <agent>.enabled=false` + human path) when a T1 metric breaches its band for 2 consecutive days.
6. *Disclosure package* (`ai.disclosure.package`): generated from the inventory — types of AI/ML, purpose and manner of use per system, safeguards (tiering, human touchpoints, evals, monitoring, vendor flow-down, security), current policy versions, last annual review — assembled by `qc-audit`, signed by `officer:ai_governance_owner` (and the partner's officer when Fannie Mae asks the master servicer), delivered by `fnma_portal_operator` through the channel Fannie Mae specifies.
7. *Annual review*: policy owner reviews each `AI-GOV-*` document against law/guidance changes tracked by `compliance-sentinel`, industry practice (NIST AI RMF 1.0 as the reference framework **[design choice]**), incident history and monitoring; board/partner receives the annual AI governance report with the inventory and evaluation summary.

#### Integrations
- **Partner (SoR):** outbound monthly QC report (PDF + machine-readable JSON of rule results and findings) via the partner SFTP/portal; inbound partner audit requests; the partner's own oversight QC may run against a read-only replica (`ops-console` role `partner_auditor`). Failure: report delivery retried; missed delivery → `SM_QC_REPORT_PARTNER_MONTHLY` breach.
- **Fannie Mae:** no API. Requests for QC results/policies/"examples of application" and for AI/ML disclosure arrive by letter/email/LQC and are logged as `fnma.request.received{kind}`; the package goes out as a `human_portal_task` (18.2 engine) with the officer signature.
- **Vendors:** annual QC test packages request vendor SOC reports, screening evidence and sample outputs via the vendor-management module (Section 19); AI vendors additionally supply `ai_vendor_attestations`.
- **Anthropic Messages API:** `qc-audit` uses a distinct model/prompt lineage (`ai_systems.code = 'qc-audit'`), never the producing agent's prompts; evaluation datasets are stored in `documents` with hashes.
- **Screening lists:** FHFA SCP, SAM.gov exclusions, HUD LDP — file/API pulls monthly (Section 19 owns the adapter; QC tests coverage).

#### Outputs and artifacts
- Monthly **QC Cycle Report** (template `QC-RPT-MONTHLY-v1`; contents: population/sample per rule, error rates with intervals, findings by severity and root cause, CAPA status, consumer remediation totals, AI monitoring summary, vendor tests completed, open exam items) — to senior management and the partner; quarterly board version; annual **Vendor QC Test Report** per vendor; annual **AI Governance Report**; the **Fannie Mae on-request package** (results + "evidence of correction actions"). No borrower notices originate here except corrected notices produced by the owning section after a finding; ledger postings only as remediation entries linked to the finding.
- Records: `qc_cycles`, `qc_samples` (with seeds), `qc_tests`, `qc_findings`/`cases`, `qc_corrective_actions`, `qc_reports`, `ai_*` tables, `agent_decisions` for every `qc-audit` judgment.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
- **Agent:** `qc-audit` (plus `compliance-sentinel` for timers). End-to-end: open the cycle → run population selectors → draw samples (seeded) → execute rederive/checklist tests via tools (`ledger.recompute`, `escrow.recompute_analysis`, `arm.second_engine`, `notice.checklist`, `timers.history`, `investor_events.replay`) → run judgment reviews (transcripts, decision rationales, notices' plain language) with a rubric per rule → cluster failures → draft findings with root cause and affected-population queries → propose CAPAs → assemble the cycle report → file the signature escalation.
- **Tools allowlist:** read-only SQL over projections and immutable logs; recompute engines; document retrieval; `cases.create{qc_finding}`; `escalations.create`; report rendering. **Never**: write to operational tables, post ledger entries (remediation entries are posted by the owning agent from the CAPA), send borrower communications, or change rule sets.
- **Decision record schema** (`agent_decisions` for `qc-audit`): `{rule_code, rule_version, subject_ref, inputs_hash, method, expected, observed, result, rationale, confidence, evidence_document_ids, model_version, prompt_version, reviewer}`.
- **Guardrails/independence:** separate model lineage and prompts; results of judgment tests below `confidence 0.85` go to `judgment_human`; QC officer (`officer:qc_officer`) validates every sev-1/sev-2 finding and signs the cycle; appeals of findings by operations owners are decided by the deputy QC officer (segregation).
- **Escalations:** `officer:qc_officer` (validate findings, sign reports); `officer` (Fannie Mae/partner packages; consumer remediation > $25,000 aggregate per finding); `officer:ai_governance_owner` (version approvals, disclosure package, annual review); `attorney` (fairness-test results and any finding suggesting a legal violation — privilege); `fnma_portal_operator` (LQC/email deliveries). Package for each: finding dossier (tests, evidence hashes, affected population, draft CAPA, draft remediation ledger set, draft notice list).
- **Disclosure/consent:** QC reviews of AI-voice transcripts use recordings captured under the call-recording consent (Section 4); no new borrower contact.
- **AI-off path:** the plan runs with engines-only tests (census rederives) and human reviewers work the judgment samples from the `ops-console` QC workbench; sample sizes unchanged.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Transfer-in/out mid-cycle:** transferred-out loans stay in the cycle for the pre-transfer period; transferred-in loans enter the boarding-accuracy census for 90 days (1.6); population hash includes transfer status.
- **Bankruptcy/SCRA/disaster overlays:** rules read the overlay flags so a "missed" notice suppressed by a bankruptcy rule is a pass with reason; SCRA-flagged loans are always targeted.
- **Successor in interest:** notices to confirmed successors are tested against 1024.32(c)/1024.38(b)(1)(vi) variants.
- **Partial data/rule-set version gaps:** if a subject's `rule_set` version has no matching QC rule version, the test returns `inconclusive` and a sev-3 finding on the QC plan itself.
- **Vendor outage:** vendor tests reschedule within the annual window; two consecutive failures to obtain vendor evidence → sev-1 vendor finding and partner notice.
- **Finding reversal:** findings are never deleted; a wrong finding gets `rejected` with rationale and the affected remediation is reversed with new entries.
- **Model change mid-cycle:** samples are tied to `agent_decisions.model_version`; a new version starts a new stratum so error rates are not blended.
- **Examiner reproducibility:** given `selection_seed` and `population_hash`, the draw must regenerate identically (tested).

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Tolerance thresholds** (2%/5%/0%) — default as stated; partner may impose tighter contract SLAs.
2. **Judgment-review model** — default: a different Claude model/prompt lineage; alternative: a second vendor model for T1 reviews (cost). Default: same vendor, separate lineage, plus human sub-sample.
3. **QC officer and AI governance owner as one person** — default: two people once headcount permits; one person at launch with board acknowledgment.
4. **Privilege for fairness analyses** — default: conducted at counsel's direction; summary statistics shared with the partner.
5. **Reference framework for AI-GOV** — default NIST AI RMF 1.0 mapping; Freddie Mac Bulletin 2025-16 controls adopted where the partner also services Freddie loans.
6. **Third line** — default: external internal-audit firm engaged annually, scope agreed with the partner's internal audit.
