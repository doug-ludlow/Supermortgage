### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Reg X §1024.20 homeownership counseling list (eCFR current as of 9/08/2026 via 00a-fed §6.4; CFPB eRegs read 2026-09-10) — VERIFIED.** (a)(1): "Except as otherwise provided in this section, not later than three business days after a lender, mortgage broker, or dealer receives an application, or information sufficient to complete an application, the lender must provide the loan applicant with a clear and conspicuous written list of homeownership counseling organizations that provide relevant counseling services in the loan applicant's location." "The list of homeownership counseling organizations distributed to each loan applicant under this section shall be obtained no earlier than 30 days prior to the time when the list is provided to the loan applicant from either: (i) The Web site maintained by the Bureau for lenders to use in complying with the requirements of this section; or (ii) Data made available by the Bureau or HUD for lenders to use in complying with the requirements of this section, provided that the data is used in accordance with instructions provided with the data." (a)(2): "The list of homeownership counseling organizations provided under this section may be combined and provided with other mortgage loan disclosures required pursuant to Regulation Z, 12 CFR part 1026, or this part unless prohibited by Regulation Z or this part." (a)(3): a mortgage broker or dealer may provide the list, and then "the lender is not required to provide an additional list" (not used — no broker channel). (a)(4): "If the lender, mortgage broker, or dealer does not provide the list of homeownership counseling organizations required under this section to the loan applicant in person, the lender must mail or deliver the list to the loan applicant by other means"; electronic delivery is subject to E-SIGN (00a-fed §6.4). (a)(5) (re-read 2026-09-11): the list is not required if, "before the end of the three-business-day period … the lender denies the application or the loan applicant withdraws the application." (c): reverse mortgages and timeshare plans are exempt. Business day: Reg X §1024.2(b) — "a day on which the offices of the business entity are open to the public for carrying on substantially all of the entity's business functions" (00a-fed §6.1) → unit `business_days_creditor`.

**CFPB Homeownership Counseling Organizations Lists and High-Cost Mortgage Counseling Interpretive Rule, 80 FR 22091 (April 21, 2015; effective April 21, 2015; federalregister.gov read 2026-09-10) — VERIFIED.** "lenders comply with this requirement when they provide a list of ten HUD-approved housing counseling agencies"; "lenders comply with § 1024.20(a)(1) when they use the loan applicant's five-digit zip code to generate a list of the ten closest HUD-approved housing counseling agencies to the centroid of the zip code of the loan applicant's current address"; "Lenders are also permitted to generate the list from a more precise geographic marker, such as a street address"; "The zip code of the loan applicant's current address generally is the default to be entered for list generation"; where "the applicant currently lives overseas, making it impossible to generate a list based on the zip code of the applicant's current address, the lender may use the five-digit zip code of the property securing the mortgage to generate the list"; "a lender using an applicant's mailing address instead of the current address to generate the list would be consistent with the requirement". Data fields "to the extent that they are available through the HUD API: Agency name, phone number, street address, street address continued, city, state, zip code, Web site URL, email address, counseling services provided, and languages spoken." Required accompanying language: "The counseling agencies on this list are approved by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), and they can offer independent advice about whether a particular set of mortgage loan terms is a good fit based on your objectives and circumstances, often at little or no cost to you. This list shows you several approved agencies in your area. You can find other approved counseling agencies at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's (CFPB) Web site: consumerfinance.gov/mortgagehelp or by calling 1-855-411-CFPB (2372). You can also access a list of nationwide HUD-approved counseling intermediaries at http://portal.hud.gov/hudportal/HUD?src=/ohc_nint." "The Bureau's tool, as discussed in § 1024.20(a)(1)(i), follows these data instructions." The rule is silent on multiple applicants (open question 2). The CFPB tool is at consumerfinance.gov/find-a-housing-counselor ("Lenders can use our find a housing counselor tool to meet RESPA requirements", 00b-orig N12); no CFPB API is documented — the HUD Housing Counselor API used by 21.1 (`data.hud.gov/Housing_Counselor/search`) is the machine path under (a)(1)(ii).

**Affiliated business arrangements — RESPA §3(7)–(8), 12 U.S.C. 2602 (uscode.house.gov, current through Sept 9, 2026) and Reg X §1024.15 (eCFR 9/08/2026 via 00a-fed §6.6; CFPB eRegs read 2026-09-10) — VERIFIED.** §2602(7): "an arrangement in which (A) a person who is in a position to refer business incident to or a part of a real estate settlement service involving a federally related mortgage loan, or an associate of such person, has either an affiliate relationship with or a direct or beneficial ownership interest of more than 1 percent in a provider of settlement services; and (B) either of such persons directly or indirectly refers such business to that provider or affirmatively influences the selection of that provider". §2602(8) "associate": spouse/parent/child; an entity that "controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with such person"; "an employer, officer, director, partner, franchisor, or franchisee"; or anyone with "an agreement, arrangement, or understanding... the purpose or substantial effect of which is to enable the person in a position to refer settlement business to benefit financially from the referrals". §1024.15(b)(1): the referring person must give "a written disclosure, in the format of the Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure Statement set forth in appendix D of this part, of the nature of the relationship" and "an estimated charge or range of charges", "on a separate piece of paper no later than the time of each referral or, if the lender requires use of a particular provider, the time of loan application", with the lender-referral alternative "at the time that the good faith estimate... is provided" (legacy GFE text). (b)(2): no required use of a particular provider, except a lender may require "an attorney, credit reporting agency, or real estate appraiser". (b)(3): the only thing of value received is a return on ownership/franchise interest. (d): "Any documents provided pursuant to this section shall be retained for 5 years after the date of execution." "Referral" is defined in §1024.14(f) (affirmative influence on the selection of a provider; required use is a referral) **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — §1024.14(f) not re-read today; (b), (c), (e), (g) read by the digest]**.

**Reg B §1002.14(a)(2) appraisal notice (CFPB eRegs read 2026-09-10; eCFR Part 1002 unreachable) and Appendix C form C-9 — VERIFIED.** "A creditor shall mail or deliver to an applicant, not later than the third business day after the creditor receives an application for credit that is to be secured by a first lien on a dwelling, a notice in writing of the applicant's right to receive a copy of all written appraisals developed in connection with the application." Appendix C: "Form C-9 is designed for use in notifying an applicant of the right to receive a copy of appraisals under § 1002.14"; C-9 text: "We may order an appraisal to determine the property's value and charge you for this appraisal. We will promptly give you a copy of any appraisal, even if your loan does not close. You can pay for an additional appraisal for your own use at your own cost." Comment 14(a)(2)-1: "Nothing in the text of the disclosure required by § 1002.14(a)(2) should be construed to affect, modify, limit, or supersede the operation of any legal, regulatory, or other requirements or standards relating to independence in the conduct of appraisers or the use of applicant-ordered appraisals by creditors." Comment 14(a)(1) (multiple applicants): the creditor need only provide the notice and copies "to one applicant", "the primary applicant where one is readily apparent" **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — fragments quoted by the extraction tool]**. **HPML appraisal statement — Reg Z §1026.35(c)(5) (LII mirror read 2026-09-10; eCFR 9/03/2026 via 00a-fed §5.7) — VERIFIED (statement text) / PARTIALLY VERIFIED (linking sentence wording).** (c)(5)(i) statement: "We may order an appraisal to determine the property's value and charge you for this appraisal. We will give you a copy of any appraisal, even if your loan does not close. You can pay for an additional appraisal for your own use at your own cost." The paragraph provides that compliance with Regulation B §1002.14(a)(2) satisfies it. (c)(5)(ii): delivered or placed in the mail "no later than the third business day after receipt of the consumer's application" and, for a loan that becomes an HPML after application, the third business day after the creditor determines that it is an HPML. General QMs are exempt from §1026.35(c) altogether ((c)(2)(i), 00a-fed §5.7), so the statement matters only for a non-QM HPML; the C-9/LE text satisfies it in every case.

**FCRA §609(g), 15 U.S.C. 1681g(g) (uscode.house.gov, current through Sept 9, 2026) — VERIFIED.** (g)(1)(A): any person that makes or arranges loans secured by one-to-four units of residential real property and uses a consumer credit score in connection with the application must provide the score information "as soon as reasonably practicable"; (g)(1)(B): "the information required under subsection (f)" — the score, the range, up to four key factors (five if inquiries are a factor), the date and the CRA; (g)(1)(C): "Any provision in a contract that prohibits the disclosure of a credit score" is void; (g)(1)(D): the "NOTICE TO THE HOME LOAN APPLICANT: In connection with your application for a home loan, the lender must disclose to you the score that a consumer reporting agency distributed to users and the lender used in connection with your home loan, and the key factors affecting your credit scores. The credit score is a computer generated summary calculated at the time of the request and based on information that a consumer reporting agency or lender has on file..." (the statute continues with the statements on score variability, the right to obtain and dispute the report, and the CRA/lender contact points; the notice is reproduced verbatim from the statute in the template); (g)(1)(E): not required to explain the information, to disclose information other than the score and key factors, to disclose post-closing scores, "to provide more than one disclosure per loan transaction", or to provide it when another person has done so; (g)(1)(F): no liability for the CRA's content. Timing language: "as soon as reasonably practicable" — no day count.

**Reg V §1022.74(d) credit-score-disclosure exception (CFPB eRegs read 2026-09-10; eCFR 9/03/2026 via 00a-fed §7) — VERIFIED.** (d)(1)(i): applies where "The consumer requests from the person an extension of credit that is or will be secured by one to four units of residential real property" and a credit score is used. (d)(1)(ii) content: (A) "A statement that a consumer report (or credit report) is a record of the consumer's credit history"; (B) "A statement that a credit score is a number that takes into account information in a consumer report"; (C) "A statement that the consumer's credit score can affect whether the consumer can obtain credit and what the cost of that credit will be"; (D) the information required under §609(g); (E) the distribution of credit scores under the same model "in the form of a bar graph containing a minimum of six bars" or a comparable graphic or statement; (F) "A statement that the consumer is encouraged to verify the accuracy of the information contained in the consumer report"; (G) "A statement that Federal law gives the consumer the right to obtain copies of his or her consumer reports directly"; (H) the centralized source for free annual reports; (I) the Bureau website reference. (d)(2): clear and conspicuous, segregated from other information except the §609(g) notice, in a retainable written form, and may be on or with the §609(g) disclosure. (d)(3): "must be provided to the consumer at the time the disclosure required by section 609(g) of the FCRA is provided to the consumer, but in any event at or before consummation". (d)(4): when multiple scores are used, disclose the score used (or one of them) with its information. (d)(5): model form H-3 — appropriate use is compliance. §1022.75(c): separate notices per consumer, each with only that consumer's score (00a-fed §7). Consequence: no risk-based pricing notice under §1022.72 is ever sent for these loans; the H-3 exception notice plus the §609(g) notice are the deliverable.

**Reg Z §1026.19(b)–(c) variable-rate disclosures (CFPB eRegs and eCFR 9/08/2026, read 2026-09-10) — VERIFIED.** (b): "Except as provided in paragraph (d) of this section, if the annual percentage rate may increase after consummation in a transaction secured by the consumer's principal dwelling with a term greater than one year, the following disclosures must be provided at the time an application form is provided or before the consumer pays a non-refundable fee, whichever is earlier (except that the disclosures may be delivered or placed in the mail not later than three business days following receipt of a consumer's application when the application reaches the creditor by telephone, or through an intermediary agent or broker):" (b)(1): "The booklet titled Consumer Handbook on Adjustable Rate Mortgages, or a suitable substitute." (b)(2): "A loan program disclosure for each variable-rate program in which the consumer expresses an interest" containing (i) "The fact that the interest rate, payment, or term of the loan can change"; (ii) "The index or formula used in making adjustments, and a source of information about the index or formula"; (iii) "An explanation of how the interest rate and payment will be determined, including an explanation of how the index is adjusted, such as by the addition of a margin"; (iv) "A statement that the consumer should ask about the current margin value and current interest rate"; (v) "The fact that the interest rate will be discounted, and a statement that the consumer should ask about the amount of the interest rate discount"; (vi) "The frequency of interest rate and payment changes"; (vii) "Any rules relating to changes in the index, interest rate, payment amount, and outstanding loan balance including, for example, an explanation of interest rate or payment limitations, negative amortization, and interest rate carryover"; (viii) either (A) "A historical example, based on a $10,000 loan amount, illustrating how payments and the loan balance would have been affected by interest rate changes implemented according to the terms of the loan program disclosure. The example shall reflect the most recent 15 years of index values..." or (B) "The maximum interest rate and payment for a $10,000 loan originated at the initial interest rate (index value plus margin, adjusted by the amount of any discount or premium) in effect as of an identified month and year for the loan program disclosure assuming the maximum periodic increases in rates and payments under the program; and the initial interest rate and payment for that loan and a statement that the periodic payment may increase or decrease substantially depending on changes in the rate"; (ix) how the consumer may calculate payments for the amount to be borrowed from (viii)(A) or (viii)(B); (x) "The fact that the loan program contains a demand feature"; (xi) "The type of information that will be provided in notices of adjustments and the timing of such notices"; (xii) "A statement that disclosure forms are available for the creditor's other variable-rate loan programs." (c): "For an application that is accessed by the consumer in electronic form, the disclosures required by paragraph (b) of this section may be provided to the consumer in electronic form on or with the application." (d): other Federal agencies' variable-rate regulations may be substituted (not applicable). **Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. "Reg X §1024.33(a)" (Servicing Disclosure Statement) is reverse-mortgage-only; for TRID loans the servicing intent is on the LE (§1026.37(m)(6)). `NTC_REGX_1024_33A_SDS` is retired for forward loans (00a-fed §17 item 4).
2. "§1024.6 (booklet)" is incomplete: for §1026.19(e) loans the operative duty is Reg Z §1026.19(g) — the *creditor's* duty, 3 business days on the Reg Z general definition, with the (g)(1)(iii) purchase-only scope (refinances exempt) and the (g)(2) no-alteration rule; and the booklet was re-issued in August 2026.
3. "Reg B §1002.14(a)(2)... 3 business days" — Reg B defines no business day; the anchor is the *Reg B* application (which on a TBD-property purchase precedes the TRID application); the LE's "Appraisal" block is the same C-9 text and ordinarily is the notice; one notice to the primary applicant suffices where applicants are multiple.
4. "FCRA §609(g)... and Reg V §1022.74 (risk-based pricing / credit-score exception notice)" — no risk-based pricing notice is ever sent; the §1022.74(d) exception notice (H-3) is delivered with the §609(g) notice "as soon as reasonably practicable" and "in any event at or before consummation", one per consumer with only that consumer's scores; the timing is not "3 business days".
5. "ARM disclosures 'before the consumer pays a non-refundable fee or the application is provided'" understates the rule: "at the time an application form is provided or before the consumer pays a non-refundable fee, whichever is earlier", with a 3-business-day exception only for applications that reach the creditor "by telephone, or through an intermediary agent or broker", and §1026.19(c) electronic delivery "on or with the application"; the program disclosure has twelve content items and the CHARM booklet is the June 2020 edition.
6. "3 business days (each rule's own definition)" — the definitions that exist (Reg X §1024.2(b); Reg Z general) describe the same "offices open" test; none of these notices uses the Reg Z specific definition; every timer here runs on `business_days_creditor`.
7. The counseling-list row omits the 30-day freshness rule, the two permitted sources, the ten-agency/data-field content standard and the accompanying-language standard of the 2015 interpretive rule, and the reverse/timeshare exemptions; "counseling-agency list API (CFPB)" does not exist as an API — the HUD API and data instructions are the machine path (00b-orig N12).
8. AfBA "at or before referral" omits the "time of loan application" branch for required providers, the statutory > 1 %/affiliate test and "associate" definition, the required-use prohibition and its three exceptions, and the 5-year retention.
9. GLBA: the trigger is the establishment of the customer relationship (origination), not the application; delivery with the application package is a policy choice (open question 7), and an oral description never suffices (§1016.9(d)).
10. "State disclosure matrices... within 3 business days" — NY 3 NYCRR 38.3 and NJ 3:1-16.3 must precede the application interview or any fee, MA §17B and TX §57.200 are due *at* application, CA §7114 at written application; none is a "within 3 business days" notice except CA §1632.5.
11. The HPML §1026.35(c)(5) statement is absent from the row but is carried by the same notice; it has its own trigger when a loan becomes an HPML after application.
12. "Per application within 3 business days" also misses that the counseling list must be regenerated if the applicant's current address changes before delivery, and that the score notices depend on 22.2's `credit.report.received`, which can be days after the application.

#### State machine

Per companion `disclosures` row: `planned` (rule evaluated; `required` and `due_at` set) → `generated` (content produced; counseling list/score data/ARM illustration snapshot attached) → `delivered` (in person/e-sign portal/e-mail with evidence) or `mailed` (print vendor proof) → `received` (evidence) → `acknowledged` (where the rule requires a signature/confirm) — or `satisfied_by_le` (Reg B appraisal notice/HPML statement carried on an LE delivered inside the window) — or `exempt` (with reason). Terminal: `acknowledged`/`received`/`satisfied_by_le`/`exempt`/`superseded` (re-generated list after an address change; re-issued ARM disclosure for a different program) / `cancelled` (application withdrawn or denied before the due date — §1026.19(g)(1)(i) and §1024.6(a)(1) permit skipping the booklet on denial within the period; the counseling list and Reg B notice are still sent if the application was received and not denied within the period). Package-level: `assembling` → `delivered` → `complete` when every `required=true` row is terminal. Pre-application gates (NY, NJ, ARM) are separate rows created at `lead.qualified`/`application.started` time (20.3/21.1) whose delivery is a guard on `application.received`/`impose_fee`.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Calendar and counting.** All companion timers use calendar `creditor` (Mon–Fri, federal legal public holidays closed, partner-published closures; 21.2 rule 1 and open question 21.2-1). Day 0 = the anchor day in the creditor's time zone; `due_at = end_of_day(nth_business_day(anchor_date, n, creditor))`. Same-day rules ("at the time of application") resolve to end of the anchor day. Delivery = in-person hand-off, authenticated portal delivery, or e-mail under E-SIGN consent; "placed in the mail" = the print vendor's mailing-date proof. No mailbox presumption applies to these rules (none has a "received" test) except the policy on the Reg B copy rule in 24.2.
2. **Three anchors.** `application_received_at` (Reg B) anchors the Reg B appraisal notice, the counseling list (policy), the privacy notice (policy) and the at-application state notices; `trid_received_at` anchors the Toolkit and CA §1632.5; `credit.report.received` anchors the score notices; `application.arm_interest.recorded` anchors the ARM gate; `referral.requested` anchors AfBA; `compliance.hpml.determined` anchors the HPML statement. Where the Reg B application precedes the TRID application (TBD purchase), the LE cannot be the Reg B notice unless it is delivered inside the Reg B window — rule 4.
3. **Counseling list generation.** `zip_used = current_address.zip5` of the applicant (mailing address permitted; property zip only when the current address has no five-digit zip, e.g., overseas); compute the centroid; query the HUD API for agencies sorted by distance; take exactly 10; render the eleven data fields in the order of the interpretive rule; append the accompanying language verbatim; record `hud_snapshot_at` (the moment the HUD data was obtained — this, not the render time, is the "obtained" time for the 30-day rule); freshness test at delivery: `delivered_at − hud_snapshot_at ≤ 30 calendar_days`, else regenerate. Multiple applicants: one list per application generated from the primary applicant's zip and delivered to all applicants; a second list when a co-applicant's current zip differs (open question 2). The list is combined with the LE package (§1024.20(a)(2)).
4. **Reg B appraisal notice satisfaction.** Let `le_out = min(disclosures.delivered_at, mailed_at)` for LE v1. If `le_out ≤ due_at(REGB_1002_14_APPRAISAL_NOTICE_3BD)` → set `satisfied_by_disclosure_id = le_disclosure_id`, state `satisfied_by_le`. Otherwise (Reg B application earlier than the TRID application by more than the slack, LE late, or an LE that will not exist — e.g., pre-qualification converted to an application and then withdrawn) → render `NTC_REGB_1002_14_APPRAISAL_NOTICE` and deliver by `due_at − 4h`. The notice goes to the primary applicant (readily apparent = borrower 1 on the 1003) and, by policy, to every applicant. The HPML (c)(5) text differs from C-9 only by the word "promptly"; the platform's standalone template uses the C-9 wording, which satisfies both (compliance with §1002.14(a)(2) satisfies (c)(5)).
5. **Toolkit.** Required iff `transaction_type = 'purchase'` and the property is a 1–4 family residential property (condo/PUD/manufactured titled as real property included); never for limited cash-out or cash-out refinances ((g)(1)(iii)(A)). Edition = the registry's current asset (`2026-08`); `language_edition = 'es'` in addition to English when `interview_language = 'es'` (the English edition is always delivered; the Spanish one is a courtesy copy). Denial before `due_at` cancels the timer with reason `denied_within_period`; a withdrawal before `due_at` cancels with `withdrawn`.
6. **Score notices.** On `credit.report.received` for a borrower, render one document per borrower containing the §609(g) notice text, every score obtained for that borrower (each with CRA, model, range, date, up to four key factors — five when inquiries are a factor — and the CRA contact), the representative/applicable score flagged, and the H-3 elements (A)–(I) including the bar graph from the CRA's distribution data; never include another borrower's data; deliver by the next creditor business day and always before consummation (gate). A re-pull (22.2 refresh/re-pull, e.g., after a freeze lift) produces a new notice only if a new score is *used* in the decision; §609(g)(1)(E)(iv) does not require more than one disclosure per transaction, but the platform re-issues when the score used changes (policy — open question 3).
7. **ARM disclosures.** When `application.arm_interest.recorded{plan}` arrives from an `electronic` channel, the borrower app shows the CHARM booklet and the program disclosure for that plan on the screen preceding the ARM-specific application fields (§1026.19(c)); the gate opens on the delivery event (authenticated view is receipt evidence; an acknowledgment is requested but not required). From a `telephone` channel, the agent sends the documents to the portal/e-mail under E-SIGN consent during the call (gate opens on delivery) or, failing consent, mails them the same day; `REGZ_1026_19B_ARM_DISCLOSURE_3BD` is the outer bound. A fee is "non-refundable" for this rule unless the fee agreement states it is refundable; under the SM economics no application fee is collected and the credit-report fee is refundable per the fee agreement — nevertheless 21.4's `impose_fee` consults the gate for every ARM application. Program disclosure content: (i)–(xii) per plan; (viii)(B) maximum-rate/payment illustration on a $10,000 loan at the plan's initial rate as of the identified month/year (refreshed monthly by the `pricing` agent from the rate sheet) — worked arithmetic for Plan 4927 (5/6 SOFR, caps 2/1/5) as of October 2026 with an illustrative initial rate of 5.875 %: initial P&I on $10,000/360 months = 10,000 × 0.0048958333 ÷ (1 − 1.0048958333⁻³⁶⁰) = $59.15 (rounded half-up at the cent); maximum rate = 5.875 % + 5.000 % = 10.875 %, reached at the earliest after the first change (+2 %) and three subsequent changes (+1 % each), i.e., 78 months after origination; maximum payment computed on the then-remaining balance over the remaining term at 10.875 %; the disclosure states the initial payment, the maximum rate and payment, and that "the periodic payment may increase or decrease substantially depending on changes in the rate". The disclosure also states the index ("30-day Average SOFR as published by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York"), the source of information, the 45-day lookback, rounding to the nearest one-eighth, the caps, that the initial rate may be discounted or a premium (with the "ask about the discount" statement when discounted), the adjustment-notice content and timing (§1026.20(c)/(d), serviced under 2.x), no demand feature, no negative amortization, and that forms for other programs are available. A single combined form for the four SOFR plans is permitted (comment 19(b)(2)-3); the platform delivers the plan of interest plus the combined form.
8. **AfBA.** `afba_required = ∃ affiliate_relationships(owner ∈ {partner, SM}, provider)`; the disclosure is generated in the Appendix D format with the relationship and charge range and delivered as a separate document no later than the referral (a required provider's disclosure at application). Required use is permitted only for `service ∈ {attorney, credit_reporting_agency, appraiser}`; the platform never requires use of any other affiliated provider and the provider list (21.2) marks affiliates (`affiliate=true`) so the tolerance engine treats their charges as zero-tolerance. Retention 5 years from execution (`respa_afba_5y`). Default inventory: none — SM and the partner are unaffiliated and SM holds no interest in any settlement-service provider (open question 4).
9. **Privacy notice.** Delivered in the package (portal acknowledgment as a step, or mail); never satisfied orally; joint delivery to co-applicants permitted (§1016.9(g)); the partner's notice edition is versioned; a consumer who already received the partner's initial notice as an existing servicing customer receives it again by policy (open question 7). 25.4's gate remains the legal backstop.
10. **State matrix execution.** `applies_by='property_state'` for NY, NJ, MA, TX, CA (§7114 and §1632.5), AZ, FL; NY/NJ pre-application gates are evaluated at `lead.qualified` when the property state is known and, at the latest, on `application.started`; the intake agent presents the NY/NJ disclosure and captures the acknowledgment before the 1003 interview begins; a voice-only borrower without electronic consent cannot satisfy NY's signature/confirm-button requirement during the call — the agent pauses the application (stays `started`) and mails the disclosure for signature or obtains E-SIGN consent (edge case below).
11. **SDS.** `NTC_REGX_1024_33A_SDS` is never issued; the `disclosure` agent writes an `exempt{reverse_only}` row on every application so exams see the affirmative decision and the LE's servicing block reference.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`disclosure` agent (shared package with 21.2/21.5; companion tools: `planCompanionPackage`, `evaluateExemptions`, `generateCounselingList`, `selectBookletEdition`, `renderScoreDisclosure`, `renderArmProgramDisclosure`, `evaluateAfBA`, `evaluateStateMatrix`, `renderNotice`, `assertConsent`, `deliver`, `recordReceipt`, `recordAcknowledgment`, `markSatisfiedByLE`, `setTimers`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end behavior: on `application.started`/`.received` it evaluates every rule and the state matrix, creates the rows and timers, opens pre-application gates where a state requires them, generates the counseling list and the privacy notice, waits for 22.2's score payloads and 21.2's LE render, assembles the package, delivers through the consented channel the same day as the LE (or earlier for at-application state notices), records evidence and acknowledgments, marks the Reg B notice satisfied by the LE when the dates allow and otherwise issues the standalone notice, and reacts to later triggers (ARM interest, referrals, HPML determination, address change, revised LE for CA §1632.5). Decision record (`agent_decisions`): `{application_id, package_id, rules[{rule_code, required, exemption_reason, anchor_event, anchor_at, due_at, calendar, satisfied_by, delivered_at/mailed_at, receipt_evidence, acknowledged_at, asset_version, language_edition}], counseling_list{zip_used, zip_source, hud_snapshot_at, agency_count}, score_disclosures[{borrower, report_id, scores_count}], arm{plan, channel, gate_opened_at}, afba{referrals_evaluated, disclosures_issued}, state_matrix{state, rows, confidence}, model_version, prompt_version, rationale}`. Guardrails (never alone): never mark a rule satisfied without a delivery/mailing/acknowledgment event or an LE row inside the window; never deliver a counseling list obtained more than 30 days earlier, with fewer than ten agencies, or generated from the property zip while a current-address zip exists; never include one borrower's score in another borrower's notice; never alter the Toolkit or CHARM beyond (g)(2) changes; never present consumer-specific ARM rates in the program disclosure; never require use of an affiliated provider outside the three §1024.15(b)(2) exceptions; never treat an oral explanation as delivery of the privacy notice; never issue an SDS on a forward loan; never deliver electronically without a prior valid consent; never let a state gate be bypassed by a `human_agent` without a logged reason. Escalations: `compliance-sentinel` on any breach, HUD outage past 4 hours, missing CRA distribution data, or a state with `confidence='unverified'`; partner `officer` for template/edition changes (Toolkit, privacy notice, AfBA inventory); `human_agent` on borrower request (may explain the documents' contents — App. A (b)(2)(i)); `licensed_specialist` where `jurisdiction_rules` require an individual to deliver a state notice (none identified in the eight states). Borrower-facing disclosure/consent: the AI disclosure (21.1) precedes every delivery conversation; E-SIGN scope confirmed; the counseling list's accompanying language, the C-9 text, the §609(g) notice, the H-3 statements and the CHARM booklet are delivered verbatim. Human path: the ops console shows the same package with manual generate/deliver/acknowledge controls, logged as human decisions.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- Applicant lives overseas (no five-digit zip): generate the list from the property zip (`zip_source='property_address_overseas'`) and record why.
- Co-applicant's current zip differs from the primary's: a second list from the co-applicant's zip is generated and delivered to that applicant; both lists ride the package.
- Applicant changes current address before delivery: regenerate (`superseded`) and deliver the new list; the timer is unchanged.
- Property TBD purchase: Reg B application Mon Oct 19 without a property; the Reg B appraisal notice is due Thu Oct 22 and no LE exists → standalone C-9 notice delivered by Oct 22 (with the privacy notice and the counseling list by policy); the Toolkit waits for the six items.
- LE late (21.2 breach): the companion notices still go out inside their own windows by separate delivery; the Reg B notice is issued standalone at `due_at − 4h`.
- E-SIGN consent revoked between render and delivery: print the whole package the same day; NY/NJ acknowledgments then require a signed copy.
- Voice-only borrower in NY with no e-mail: the application stays `started` until the signed 38.3 disclosure is returned or E-SIGN consent is captured; the borrower is told why; no fee is imposed.
- Product switch from fixed to ARM after the LE: `application.arm_interest.recorded` → ARM disclosures before the ARM application fields and before any further fee; 21.5 handles the revised LE (borrower-requested change).
- Interest in more than one variable-rate program: a program disclosure for each (or the combined form), one gate per program of interest.
- Loan becomes an HPML after application: `REGZ_1026_35C5` timer from the determination; satisfied by the earlier LE/notice.
- Score received after the LE (e.g., frozen file lifted Oct 7): score notice for that borrower by the next creditor business day; the consummation gate keeps closing until every scored borrower is covered.
- No score for a borrower (no credit file): no §609(g)/H-3 notice for that borrower; recorded as `exempt{no_score}`.
- Denial within the period: Toolkit cancelled (`denied_within_period`); counseling list, Reg B notice and privacy notice still delivered if not already; score notices still delivered (the §615(a) adverse-action notice is 21.6's).
- Affiliated provider introduced mid-stream (e.g., the partner acquires an interest in a title agency): `affiliate_relationships` effective date; every later referral is gated; earlier referrals need no retroactive disclosure but are reported.
- Toolkit edition change mid-pipeline: applications with the older edition already delivered are compliant (earlier versions usable per the CFPB page); new deliveries use the new asset.
- HUD API outage: cached dataset ≤ 30 days; else sev 1 and manual list via the CFPB tool before `due_at`.
- Interview conducted in Spanish for a non-CA property: no §1632.5 duty; the Spanish Toolkit/CHARM courtesy copies are still offered; binding documents remain English (21.1 open question 5).
- Reverse mortgage or timeshare: out of scope; the `exempt{reverse_only}` SDS row and `exempt` counseling-list row would apply only if the product scope ever changed.

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Reg B appraisal notice via the LE.** Default: an LE delivered or mailed inside the Reg B window (anchored on the Reg B application) is the notice; a standalone C-9 notice issues otherwise. Counsel to confirm comment 37(m)(1)-1's full text (only its opening words were readable today).
2. **Counseling list per applicant or per application.** Default: one list from the primary applicant's current zip delivered to every applicant, plus a separate list for any co-applicant with a different current zip.
3. **Score notice scope and re-issuance.** Default: every score obtained for the borrower with the applicable/representative score flagged; re-issue when a different score is used after a re-pull (§609(g)(1)(E)(iv) does not require it — policy).
4. **AfBA between SM and the partner.** Default: no affiliated business arrangement exists (no affiliate relationship, no > 1 % interest, no "associate" agreement whose substantial effect is to benefit the referrer); counsel to confirm against the term sheet and 12 U.S.C. 2602(7)–(8) before go-live; SM's processing services are compensated as services actually performed (§1024.14(g)).
5. **Business-day unit for Reg B, Reg X and §1026.19(b)/(g).** Default: `business_days_creditor` for all (consistent with 21.2 and 24.2); Saturdays and Columbus Day per the partner's `creditor_calendars`.
6. **"Application form is provided" in a conversational intake.** Default: the ARM gate sits in front of the ARM-specific interview fields and every fee; for voice, delivery during the call under E-SIGN consent, with the 3-business-day mail path as the outer bound.
7. **Privacy notice for existing servicing customers.** Default: deliver the partner's initial notice again with the package (harmless duplication) rather than rely on §1016.4(d) (text not read today).
8. **State-matrix ownership and the unverified states.** Default: 31.1 owns `jurisdiction_rules.early_disclosures`; 21.3 executes; a state may go live only with counsel sign-off; the eight-state matrix above is representative, not exhaustive (e.g., MA 209 CMR/attorney-choice notices beyond §17B, FL 494 lender items, OH OAC 1301:8-7 rules, CA §1785.20.2 credit-score disclosure are UNVERIFIED).
9. **Toolkit edition cut-over.** Default: the August 2026 edition from go-live; the older edition is never used; the CFPB's transition statement is relied on only if an earlier delivery is discovered.
10. **FL anti-coercion timing.** Resolved 2026-09-11: R. 69B-124.002 requires the notice "when negotiations for a loan begin, and prior to any formal application for loan or the payment of any fees," signed by the borrower and retained — so the statement is presented at intake start (21.1/20.3 hand-off) and the signed acknowledgment gates `application.received` and `impose_fee`, like the NY/NJ pre-application gates. Remaining question: whether a borrower who refuses to sign may still proceed after the statement is delivered (default: deliver, log the refusal, escalate to `licensed_specialist`; partner counsel to confirm).
11. **ARM illustration basis.** Default: (viii)(B) maximum-rate/payment illustration refreshed monthly (a 15-year SOFR history for (viii)(A) does not exist — 30-day Average SOFR publication began in 2020 — so (viii)(A) is unavailable for SOFR programs) **[design inference; the FRBNY publication start date was not verified today]**.
