### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-10)

**Reg B §1002.2(f) and Official Interpretations (CFPB eRegs; eCFR Part 1002 returned 503 — cited from eRegs).** "Application means an oral or written request for an extension of credit that is made in accordance with procedures used by a creditor for the type of credit requested"; "completed application" = the creditor "has received all the information that the creditor regularly obtains and considers in evaluating applications for the amount and type of credit requested." Comment 2(f)-1: "A creditor has the latitude under the regulation to establish its own application process and to decide the type and amount of information it will require from credit applicants." Comment 2(f)-2: "procedures" means "the actual practices followed by a creditor for making credit decisions as well as its stated application procedures." Comment 2(f)-3: "A creditor is encouraged to provide consumers with information about loan terms"; "If in giving information to the consumer the creditor also evaluates information about the consumer, decides to decline the request, and communicates this to the consumer, the creditor has treated the inquiry or prequalification request as an application and must then comply with the notification requirements under § 1002.9"; "Whether the inquiry or prequalification request becomes an application depends on how the creditor responds to the consumer, not on what the consumer says or asks"; example (i) (rate quoted after asking for the make, price and downpayment of a car) is an inquiry; example (ii) — a request to "preapprove" reviewed "under a program in which the institution, after a comprehensive analysis of her creditworthiness, issues a written commitment valid for a designated period of time to extend a loan up to a specified amount" — is an application. Comment 2(f)-6: "The creditor must act with reasonable diligence to collect information needed to complete the application." §1002.2(e): applicant = "any person who requests or who has received an extension of credit from a creditor, and includes any person who is or may become contractually liable." §1002.5 (00a-fed §3.4): no inquiry into race, color, religion, national origin or sex except §1002.13 monitoring; marital status only as married/unmarried/separated; no childbearing inquiry; §1002.13 monitoring information is requested for covered dwelling applications (at application, with the disclosure that the federal government requests it). **VERIFIED.**

**Reg C §1003.2 (eCFR up to date as of 9/08/2026).** (b)(1) application = "An oral or written request for a covered loan that is made in accordance with procedures used by a financial institution for the type of credit requested." (b)(2): "A request for preapproval for a home purchase loan … is an application under this section if the request is reviewed under a program in which the financial institution, after a comprehensive analysis of the creditworthiness of the applicant, issues a written commitment to the applicant valid for a designated period of time to extend a home purchase loan up to a specified amount," subject only to conditions "requiring identification of a suitable property," "no material change in the applicant's financial condition or creditworthiness prior to closing," and limited customary conditions. (p) refinancing = "a new, dwelling-secured debt obligation satisfies and replaces an existing, dwelling-secured debt obligation by the same borrower." Appendix B (00a-fed §4.5): demographic information must be requested "whether the application is taken in person, by mail or telephone, or on the internet"; for telephone/internet applications never inferred. **VERIFIED.**

**TRID application — §1026.2(a)(3) and comment 2(a)(3)-1 (CFPB eRegs; eCFR 9/04/2026).** "Application means the submission of a consumer's financial information for the purposes of obtaining an extension of credit"; for §1026.19(e)/(f) transactions it "consists of the submission of the consumer's name, the consumer's income, the consumer's social security number to obtain a credit report, the property address, an estimate of the value of the property, and the mortgage loan amount sought." Comment 2(a)(3)-1: "Once a creditor has received these six pieces of information, it has an application for purposes of the requirements of Regulation Z." Comment on SSN: "If a consumer does not have a social security number, the creditor may substitute whatever unique identifier the creditor uses to obtain a credit report on the consumer." §1026.19(e)(2)(iii): "The creditor or other person shall not require a consumer to submit documents verifying information related to the consumer's application before providing the disclosures"; §1026.19(e)(2)(ii): a written estimate specific to the consumer before the LE must carry "Your actual rate, payment, and costs could be higher. Get an official Loan Estimate before choosing a loan." (20.4). Reg X §1024.2(b) "application" adds "and any other information deemed necessary by the loan originator" (00a-fed §6.7). **VERIFIED.**

**E-SIGN — 15 U.S.C. 7001 (current through Sept 8, 2026; 00a-fed §8 plus today's fetch).** (c)(1)(A)–(D) consent elements (affirmative consent; prior statement of the paper option, scope — "(II) to identified categories of records", withdrawal procedure, paper-copy procedure and fees; hardware/software statement; consent "in a manner that reasonably demonstrates that the consumer can access information in the electronic form"; re-disclosure on material change). (c)(3): contract validity not denied "solely because of the failure to obtain electronic consent." (c)(4): withdrawal does not affect records "provided or made available … prior to implementation of the consumer's withdrawal." **(c)(6): "An oral communication or a recording of an oral communication shall not qualify as an electronic record for purposes of this subsection except as otherwise provided under applicable law."** (b): nothing requires any person "to agree to use or accept electronic records or electronic signatures." (d)(1): retained records must accurately reflect the information and remain accessible. **VERIFIED.** Servicing 7.4 already implements the consent choreography (`consents`, `consent_disclosure_versions`, demonstration test with link + PDF token, `ESIGN_7001C_CONSENT_GATE`); 20.3 adds the origination category.

**FCRA — 15 U.S.C. 1681b (00a-fed §7, VERIFIED).** (a)(3)(A): a consumer report may be furnished to a person who "intends to use the information in connection with a credit transaction involving the consumer on whom the information is to be furnished and involving the extension of credit to … the consumer"; (a)(3)(F): "legitimate business need" in connection with a consumer-initiated business transaction. A consumer's request for a prequalification/quote is consumer-initiated; a "soft" inquiry is still a consumer report obtained under (a)(3)(A)/(F) and may be used only for that transaction. §609(g) (Notice to the Home Loan Applicant) and Reg V §1022.74(d) credit-score disclosure attach once a score is used "in connection with an application" — delivered by 21.3 at application; freezes (§1681c-1(i)): a frozen file blocks a new-credit pull until lifted (00a-fed §7).

**GLBA §1016.4 (eCFR 9/04/2026; 00a-fed §14).** Initial notice to "An individual who becomes your customer, not later than when you establish a customer relationship"; to a *consumer* only "before you disclose any nonpublic personal information about the consumer to any nonaffiliated third party" outside the §1016.13–.15 exceptions; relationship established "when you originate or acquire the servicing rights to a loan." **VERIFIED.** A lead is a consumer; ordering a soft pull is a disclosure "necessary to effect … a transaction that a consumer requests" (§1016.14) — no notice trigger at lead stage; the partner's initial notice goes out with the application package (`NTC_GLBA_1016_4_PRIVACY_INITIAL`, 21.3) and no later than consummation (`GLBA_1016_4_INITIAL_PRIVACY_GATE`, 25.4).

**BSA/OFAC and Red Flags (00a-fed §9, §7).** 31 CFR 1029.220 (CIP) is "[Reserved]" — no CIP for non-bank RMLOs; identity is driven by the AML program's internal controls, Fannie Mae identity rules and the Red Flags ITPP (16 CFR 681: written program to identify/detect/respond to red flags for covered accounts, incl. mortgage loans). OFAC screening happens at application (22.6), not at lead. **VERIFIED.**

**SAFE Act / Reg H App. A (00a-fed §12, VERIFIED).** Not taking an application: forwarding "without review," "explaining the contents," "Generally describes … the loan application process without a discussion of particular loan products," collecting "only basic identifying information." Offering/negotiating = "Presenting for consideration by a borrower or prospective borrower particular loan terms, whether verbally, in writing, or otherwise" or "Communicating … for the purpose of reaching a mutual understanding about prospective residential mortgage loan terms"; not offering/negotiating: "Providing general explanations or descriptions in response to consumer queries." No regulator statement on AI "taking an application" exists (UNVERIFIED-negative). Design: the AI runs the interview and gives general explanations and rate-sheet information; the *personalized* quote is presented under the `mlo_of_record` (baseline §0/§8 default `assisted`).

**Colorado SB 26-189 (signed act, leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/116489, fetched 2026-09-10).** C.R.S. 6-1-1701 definitions: "covered ADMT" = "automated decision-making technology that is used to materially influence a consequential decision"; consequential decision = a decision "made about a consumer that relates to the provision of or a consumer's access to, eligibility for, selection for, or compensation for a covered domain," and "(d) a financial or lending service" is a covered domain; adverse outcome = a decision that "denies, terminates, revokes, or materially reduces or restricts a consumer's access to, eligibility for, selection for, compensation for, or the provision of an opportunity or service." 6-1-1704 (deployer disclosures): "Prior to a deployer using a covered ADMT to materially influence a consequential decision, the deployer shall provide a clear and conspicuous notice to a consumer that the deployer used or will use a covered ADMT in a consequential decision affecting the consumer and instructions regarding how the consumer may obtain the additional information described in this section," or alternatively "by maintaining a prominent public notice that is reasonably accessible at points of consumer interaction"; post-adverse-outcome disclosure within 30 days (name, version, developer of the ADMT, data categories); 6-1-1705 human review "to the extent commercially reasonable" and data correction; 6-1-1708: a creditor that "is required to provide and that provides a notice to a consumer pursuant to the federal 'Equal Credit Opportunity Act'" complies to the extent that notice satisfies the state content; 6-1-1703 records "for not less than three years after the date of a consequential decision"; 6-1-1706 AG enforcement under the CCPA with a 60-day cure before Jan 1, 2030; **"this act takes effect January 1, 2027" and "applies to consequential decisions made on or after January 1, 2027."** **VERIFIED.** Ownership: `NTC_CO_SB26_189_ADMT_NOTICE` and the Colorado timers belong to 21.6; 20.3 triggers the pre-use notice at the first interaction for Colorado consumers/properties (both the point-of-interaction line and the public notice page).

**Utah AI Policy Act, Utah Code §13-72-302 as amended by S.B. 226 (2025) — [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — statute site renders via JavaScript; Davis Polk client update Apr 4, 2025].** Disclosure of generative-AI use upon "a clear and unambiguous request"; mandatory up-front, clear and conspicuous disclosure for "high-risk artificial intelligence interactions" — interactions that collect "sensitive personal information" (financial, health, biometric) or provide personalized advice "that could reasonably be relied upon to make significant personal decisions," including financial advice; safe harbor for disclosure "at the outset and throughout the interaction"; effective May 7, 2025; sunset July 2027; Division of Consumer Protection enforcement, "$2,500 per violation." A mortgage intake collects financial information → treated as high-risk → disclosure at the outset.

**California CPPA ADMT regulations (approved text; 00a-fed §13.3) — [PARTIALLY VERIFIED — §7001 definitions read; §7200 compliance date and §7220 text not read].** ADMT = "any technology that processes personal information and uses computation to replace human decisionmaking or substantially replace human decisionmaking"; "significant decision" includes "financial or lending services" ("extension of credit or a loan"); §7220 pre-use notice and §7221 opt-out with a human-appeal exception. Applicability to GLBA-covered data and the compliance date are 31.2's questions; 20.3 delivers the pre-use notice text 31.2 approves at first interaction for California consumers. California's bot-disclosure statute (Bus. & Prof. Code §17940 et seq.) **[UNVERIFIED — not fetched]** is covered by the same disclosure line.

**Reg Z §1026.36(g) (00a-fed §12).** The MLO of record's name and NMLSR ID appear on the credit application, LE/CD, note and security instrument — the `mlo_of_record` is assigned at lead stage so the assignment exists before 21.1 renders the 1003.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**
1. The row cites FCRA "§604(a)(3)(F)" for soft pulls; the primary hook is §604(a)(3)(A) (credit transaction involving the consumer, extension of credit); (F) is the fallback. Either way the purpose is limited to the consumer-initiated transaction.
2. "LE clock starts only when the six items are received" is right, but for a subserviced borrower four items (name, SSN, address, an estimate of value) are effectively on file; the boundary must be defined by *confirmation for the new transaction* (rule 5) or the LE is late.
3. The row treats "Reg C §1003.2(b) preapproval program" as governing; SM's prequalification is deliberately **not** a preapproval program (no written commitment after comprehensive analysis), so purchase prequalifications are not HMDA preapproval requests — which is a design choice the row does not make.
4. The row's "state AI-disclosure statutes (CO, UT, CA)": Colorado's duty is a *pre-use notice* for consequential decisions on/after Jan 1, 2027 (SB 26-189), not the SB24-205 regime; Utah is a disclosure-on-request rule plus up-front disclosure for high-risk interactions; California's is a CCPA regulation with an unread compliance date.
5. The row omits the SAFE Act boundary that decides what the AI may say (general explanations vs particular terms).
6. The row omits Reg B §1002.13/HMDA App. B: demographic information is requested at application, never at lead; and the Reg B "evaluate + decline + communicate" trap at prequalification.
7. E-SIGN consent must be captured **before** the LE if the LE is to be e-delivered within 3 business days — the row lists E-SIGN but not this sequencing.

#### State machine

`new` → `disclosed` (AI disclosure delivered on the first AI channel; human channels skip) → `authenticated{L1|L2}` → `exploring` (information-giving; no six-item collection of income/loan amount unless the consumer chooses to proceed) → `prequal_requested` → `prequalified` (information provided or letter issued) → `terms_review` (MLO of record reviewing the personalized quote; `SM_MLO_PREAPP_TERMS_REVIEW_1BH`) → `terms_presented` → `applying` (consumer elects to proceed; income/loan amount collected) → `converted` (`application.received` + `application.trid_received` → 21.1/21.2) | `expired` (`SM_LEAD_INACTIVITY_EXPIRY_90`) | `closed_lost{reason}`. Any communicated negative evaluation from `exploring`/`prequalified` forces `converted` with `application.received` (Reg B) and routes to 21.6 — this transition is the only path the `communicateDecline` tool can take (gate below). Transitions are performed by the `intake` agent; `terms_review → terms_presented` requires the `mlo_of_record` approval event; `human_agent` may perform any transition from the ops console.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **First-contact disclosure.** Every AI channel opens with `NTC_SM_AI_INTERACTION_DISCLOSURE`: "You're speaking with [Partner]'s automated assistant, operated by Supermortgage. I'm an AI, not a person. You can ask for a person at any time and I'll connect you." Logged as `consents(kind=ai_disclosure_ack)` with version and timestamp; a direct question ("are you a bot/human?") is always answered truthfully (Utah request rule; UDAAP).
2. **Identity levels.** L0: contact captured only (no data revealed, no pull). L1: OTP to the email or phone entered (prospects). L2: existing borrower authenticates through the portal or the 4.x authentication script (on-file loan data may be discussed). L3/L4: 22.6 at application. Soft pull requires ≥ L1 and the consumer's own entry/confirmation of name, address, DOB and SSN/ITIN (on-file SSN for L2 borrowers may be used only after the consumer authorizes it in-session).
3. **Information-giving vs application (Reg B).** The agent may explain programs, criteria (max LTV 97% LCOR/80% cash-out, occupancy, DU-eligible products), general rate-sheet ranges, the 20.1 benefit numbers for a subserviced borrower, and the process. It may never say the consumer "does not qualify," "would be denied," or "cannot get" the loan. Every generated utterance passes a decline classifier; a positive classification blocks the utterance and rewrites it as a criteria statement ("this program allows a loan up to 97% of the home's value; with the value you gave, that would be about $X"). If the consumer asks for a decision, the agent offers to take an application (conversion) and explains that a written decision follows within 30 days (21.6).
4. **Preapproval program (Reg C).** Not offered. The prequalification letter states: it is based on unverified information and a soft credit inquiry, is "not a commitment to lend and not a preapproval," has no validity period as a commitment, and lists the conditions in general terms; MAP (q)/(r) checklist applied. A purchase lead who needs a preapproval letter for a seller is offered a full application after the property is identified (21.1) — open question 2.
5. **TRID six-item detector.** `trid_items[k].present=true` when: name — consumer-entered or confirmed at L2; income — stated by the consumer for the new transaction (never taken from the origination file); SSN — consumer-authorized for a *hard* credit pull for the new transaction (the soft-pull authorization does not count: it is "to obtain a credit report" for prequalification, not for the extension of credit — open question 1, default conservative: the soft-pull authorization **does** count once the property address, value and loan amount are also present, i.e., the platform treats the earlier date as the application date); property address — refinance: on-file address confirmed at L2; purchase: identified property; value estimate — stated by the consumer or the platform's estimate *accepted* by the consumer; loan amount sought — stated by the consumer (refinance: acceptance of the proposed payoff-based amount counts). `trid_application_at = max(at)` of the six once all are present; emits `application.trid_received`; 21.2 owns the LE timer. `regb_application_at` is set at the same moment (the consumer's request "in accordance with procedures used by a creditor") or earlier if the platform evaluated and communicated a decline.
6. **What may be collected pre-application.** Contact data; transaction goals; property state/address; occupancy intent; estimated value; existing-loan facts (L2); consents; soft-pull authorization; stated assets (optional); income *only* when the consumer proceeds (rule 5). Never before application: §1002.13 demographic information, marital status beyond married/unmarried/separated, alimony/child-support questions, citizenship (collected at application per B2-2-02 — 21.1), documents (§1026.19(e)(2)(iii)).
7. **SAFE Act phrasing.** General rate information: "today's 30-year fixed rates for this program range from X% to Y% (APR …) depending on credit and loan-to-value" (published rate sheet, not particular terms). Personalized terms ("your rate would be 6.125%, payment $3,402.62") are generated by 20.4 and presented only after `mlo.review.completed`; the presentation names the MLO ("reviewed by [MLO name], NMLSR ID ####"). The AI never negotiates (no counter-proposals on rate/points); requests to negotiate route to the `mlo_of_record`.
8. **E-SIGN capture.** The `NTC_SM_ESIGN_CONSENT` statement lists the two categories (origination disclosures; servicing communications) with the (c)(1)(B)/(C) elements; the consumer may consent to either or both; the demonstration test (link opened from the email + PDF token) completes consent; a chat/voice "yes" never counts ((c)(6)); withdrawal per 7.4. Consent per applicant; co-applicants consent individually.
9. **Soft-pull use and retention.** Use limited to: representative-score tier for pricing, freeze/fraud-alert detection, liability pre-fill offered to the consumer (never auto-submitted to DU — 22.2 orders the tri-merge). Retention: if an application follows, the soft report is part of the Reg B file (25 months, §1002.12); if not, deleted at lead expiry (90 days) except the authorization and a hash (FCRA audit).
10. **TCPA consents at lead.** Providing a phone number in the inquiry = prior express consent for *informational* calls/texts about that inquiry (FCC 2008/2012 rulings — **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED]**); marketing PEWC only through the 20.2 written flow.
11. **Privacy notice.** No consumer notice at lead stage (sharing only under §1016.14/.15); the partner's initial notice is delivered with the application package (21.3) and, for existing servicing customers, was delivered at boarding/annually (7.x).
12. **Human path.** "Person" at any time → warm transfer to `human_agent` (SLA 10 s to start, per 11.1); the human continues in the same lead record; SAFE Act: if terms are to be discussed, the human is the `mlo_of_record` or transfers to one.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)

`intake` agent with `borrower-comms`. Tools: `deliverDisclosure`, `authenticate` (OTP/portal), `captureConsent` (E-SIGN via 7.4 flow; TCPA via 20.2 flow; credit authorization), `orderSoftPull` (gated), `explainProgram` (criteria/general rates only), `getBenefit` (20.1 payload for L2 borrowers), `requestQuote` (20.4) → `requestTermsReview` (MLO), `presentTerms` (after review), `issuePrequalLetter`, `detectTridItems`, `convertToApplication`, `transferToHuman`, `writeDecision`. The `communicateDecline` tool exists only to fail loudly (gate) and route to conversion. Decision record per interaction: `{lead_id, interaction_id, disclosure_version, assurance_level, consents_captured, softpull_authorization_id, utterance_classifier_results, trid_items_snapshot, conversion_decision, rationale}`. Guardrails: no substantive exchange before disclosure; no on-file data before L2; no soft pull without authorization; no decline language; no particular terms before MLO review; no demographic/marital/childbearing questions; no document requests before the LE; no promises ("guaranteed," "pre-approved"); truthful answer to "are you human?"; state overlays enforced by `jurisdiction_rules`. Escalations: `mlo_of_record` (terms review, negotiation, any state requiring a licensed individual to take the application — `licensed_specialist`), `human_agent` on request, `compliance-sentinel` on classifier hits above threshold, `officer` for consent-text changes. Human path: ops console lead view with the same tools; humans see the same gates.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- **Consumer provides all six items in the first message** (e.g., an email with name, income, SSN, address, value, amount): `application.trid_received` fires on receipt; the LE clock starts even though no interview occurred; 21.1 completes the 1003 afterwards.
- **Existing borrower refuses the soft pull**: quote uses the on-file score tier with the estimate flag; the hard pull happens at application.
- **Frozen credit**: soft pull returns a freeze indicator; the consumer is told how to lift it (1 hour electronic — §1681c-1(i)); no adverse inference.
- **Co-borrower absent**: prequal proceeds for the present party; the application requires each applicant's own consents and authorization.
- **Lead in a state where the partner is unlicensed**: information about licensing only; no quote; 31.1 gate.
- **Consumer says "just tell me if I'll be approved"**: conversion offered; no prediction.
- **Voice consent to E-SIGN**: not accepted; link sent; the LE will be mailed if not completed by 21.2's channel decision (no breach).
- **Utah consumer asks mid-call**: disclosure repeated verbatim.
- **Colorado before Jan 1, 2027**: notice delivered anyway from Dec 1, 2026 (policy), no gate breach if omitted before Jan 1.
- **Minor/incapacitated caller or third party**: no data; suggest the borrower call; SII rules (4.4).
- **Duplicate leads** (same party, two channels): merged by `party_id`/email+phone; the earliest `trid_application_at` governs.
- **Lead expiry with partial six items**: no application ever existed; data purged per rule 9 except consents (retained per 7.4).

### Open questions / decisions
1. **Does the soft-pull SSN authorization count as "SSN to obtain a credit report" for the TRID six items?** Default: **yes (conservative)** — once the other five are present, the application is received and the LE clock starts; alternative (later clock) needs counsel's written position.
2. **Offer a Reg C preapproval program for purchase leads?** Default: **no** — prequalification letters only; revisit if purchase conversion suffers (a preapproval program makes denied preapproval requests HMDA-reportable and triggers Reg B notices).
3. **MLO review of personalized pre-application quotes** (SAFE Act). Default: **required** under `origination.ai_mlo_intake=assisted` with a 1-business-hour SLA; `autonomous` only with a written legal position (baseline §8).
4. **Colorado pre-use notice start date.** Default: deliver from **Dec 1, 2026** for all Colorado consumers (statutory duty for decisions on/after Jan 1, 2027).
5. **Video-call intake and the HMDA "in person" question.** Default: video is treated as telephone/internet (no visual-observation collection) pending the commentary check (00a-fed §4.5).
6. **Lead retention.** Default: 90-day inactivity expiry; consents and authorizations retained per 7.4; soft-pull data deleted unless an application exists.
