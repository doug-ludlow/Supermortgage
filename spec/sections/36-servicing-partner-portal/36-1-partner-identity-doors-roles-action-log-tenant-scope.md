### Blueprint row
The partner's people have no door. The book is loaded and reviewed by Supermortgage's own staff on `/ops` (34.3) and through the machine API `/v1/partner-book/*` under a bearer token; the partner learns what the platform concluded by e-mail and by asking. Section 34's README is explicit that the operator portal is internal only — no partner users, no tenancy — and the borrower app is the homeowner's. This process gives the partner's people real accounts of their own: a partner user bound to one `parties{servicer}` row, a door that copies 34.1's mechanics (a code to the e-mail, then a password, a passkey if they want one), three roles that are not staff roles, one action-log row per request with the person, the role and the result and never a homeowner's name, and a `/v1/partner/*` prefix on which every query is scoped to the session's `partner_party_id` and anything outside the tenant is `404 NOT_FOUND`. It does not import a tape (36.2), score a loan (33.2) or talk to a homeowner (33.1, 32.x).

### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-16)
**Access controls and MFA (16 CFR §314.4(c)(1), (c)(5)).** Customer information may be accessed only by authorized users, and any individual accessing any information system must use multi-factor authentication unless the qualified individual approves equivalent controls in writing. A partner user reads homeowner information the partner already holds as servicer, on Supermortgage's system: the session here is a possession factor (a code to the e-mail on the partner user row, or a registered passkey) plus a knowledge factor (the password) — two factors; a code alone never opens a partner session, and a password alone never does. **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — carried from 34.1's verification of the Safeguards Rule as amended 2021 (effective June 2023); the primary text was not re-read in this session.]**

**The partner's data is used for the partner's program (12 CFR §1016.13; brief §3 rule 10).** The portal is the partner's book, branded with the partner's legal name and NMLSR id; Supermortgage may not market its own products from the partner's servicing data, and nothing a partner user sees is a Supermortgage acquisition funnel. The three roles see the tenant's rows and no other tenant's; no view carries another partner's counts. **[PARTIALLY VERIFIED — §1016.13 as 33.1 and 34.3 cite it; the reuse and redisclosure limits of §1016.11 that forbid the acquisition funnel were not re-read against the primary source in this session.]**

**Every action is attributable (19.2; 18.1; 34.1 rule 4).** A command a partner user dispatches runs on the bus with `actor = {kind: human, id: <partner_user_id>, role: partner_admin | partner_ops | partner_auditor}`; the decision record carries it; the portal's own `partner_actions` row carries the request (the action, the subject, the role that acted, the result and the refusal code) so a read is logged too, with the authority it was read under. Nothing here bypasses a tool's guardrail: `book.import` runs for a partner user because the session's role is `partner_admin`, never because a header or a body field said so. **[VERIFIED — 34.1 rules 3–4 as built (src/runtime/staff/auth.ts, roles.ts); the pattern is copied, the tables are not shared.]**

**Existence is not a signal (34.2 rule 4; 34.1 rule 1's no-enumeration doors).** A loan, an import, a report or a partner user that is not the session's tenant's is `404 NOT_FOUND`, never `403`: a `403` would tell partner A that partner B's id exists. The doors answer an unknown address, a missing code and a wrong code alike, as the staff door does. **[VERIFIED — the policy of the brief §3 rule 5 and of 34.1's door as built (`OTP_INVALID` for every door failure; src/runtime/staff/roles.ts); no statute is claimed.]**

**No consumer report (FCRA §604).** Nothing in this section orders a report, and no partner role can: the partner's tape FICO stays a file fact for pricing after a homeowner's own Yes (20.4; 33.2). **[VERIFIED — as 33.1 and 33.2 verified it; this process adds no path to a bureau.]**

**The subservicing arrangement after board (Selling Guide A2-1-07; A2-7-03).** When the new loan Supermortgage originated boards (30.2 / 35.10), the partner remains master servicer, Fannie seller and MSR owner and Supermortgage is subservicer of the new loan; A2-1-07's conditions (both parties Fannie-approved, Form 101, separate 1013/1014 custodials, the master fully liable) then apply, and A2-7-03 if Supermortgage is later appointed onto already-delivered loans. V1 implements none of those forms and claims nothing under them; they are named so the roles and the banner never imply otherwise. **[UNVERIFIED — the Guide topics were not read in this session; the build treats them as V2 inputs to confirm before any serviced pane is built (36.6).]**

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**: (1) The brief §5.1 says `partner.user.invite` is a staff command "that only `ops_analyst` / `admin` may call", and its roles table and 36.1-T4 have a `partner_admin` inviting a `partner_ops`; the build follows both: staff `ops_analyst` / `admin` call it for any tenant (the first `partner_admin` — the only way a tenant gets a user), a `partner_admin` calls it for their own tenant only, with the tenant from the session. (2) The brief names no command that disables a partner user or changes its roles, and the roles table gives `partner_admin` "invite/disable"; the disable is DELTA-01 in docs/partner-portal/BACKEND-DELTAS.md, and a role change in V1 is a disable and a new invitation (Open questions). (3) 34.1 stores the staff e-mail as a hash beside an encrypted copy; the brief lists `email` (normalized) on `partner_users`; the build follows the brief, with the column encrypted at rest as the template requires and the lookup by normalized value. (4) 36.1-T6 asks the log row for a `view`; the brief's `partner_actions` has no such column, so the view is the row's `subject_kind` (rule 5). (5) No OIDC door in V1, as the brief says; the staff side has none either (34.1 discrepancy 2).

### Build spec
#### Inputs and triggers
- `POST /v1/partner/auth/code` `{email}` → a six-digit code by e-mail (FAKE outside production, `fake_code` echoed; the answer is the same whether or not the address is a partner user's); `POST /v1/partner/auth/verify` `{email, code}` → a short-lived enrol/step token (never a portal session); `POST /v1/partner/auth/password` `{token, password}` (set at enrolment, or reset); `POST /v1/partner/auth/signin` `{email, password}` (the password plus the recent possession the token proves) → a session; `POST /v1/partner/auth/passkey/*` (register on an enrolled session; sign in with a passkey as the possession factor in place of the code); `POST /v1/partner/auth/signout`; `GET /v1/partner/me` (the user, the tenant's legal name and NMLSR id, the roles held, the session's default role).
- Every `/v1/partner/*` request → the session from `Authorization: Bearer` (the app's cookie proxy turns `sm_partner_session` into it; 30-minute idle, 12-hour absolute) → the role for the route (rule 3) → one `partner_actions` row (rule 5) → the handler, every query of which carries `partner_party_id = session.partner_party_id` (rule 4). A request carrying a staff cookie, the `x-actor-id` / `x-actor-role` headers, `x-staff-role` or the machine `API_TOKEN` and no partner session is 401 — there is no staff fallback (36.1-T7); a partner session presented to `/ops`, `/ops/api/*`, `/v1/partner-book/*` or `/v1/borrower/*` is 401 — there is no partner fallback (36.1-T8).
- `POST /v1/partner/users/invite` `{email, name, roles}` (`partner_admin`; the tenant from the session) → `partner.user.invite`; from the operator portal, staff `ops_analyst` / `admin` dispatch the same command with a `partner_party_id` through the existing bus route (`POST /ops/api/tools/{process}/{name}`, 34.5 rule 11). `GET /v1/partner/users` (`partner_admin`; the tenant's users with roles and status; no other tenant's). A disable is DELTA-01.
- The routes 36.2–36.5 declare dispatch 33.1's `book.import` (36.2) and call 34.3's `book.history`, `book.loans`, `book.loan`, `book.day`, `book.daily_report` and 33.1's `book.report` through this wrapper: every call passes `filter.partner = session.partner_party_id` (never null) and the actor is the session's.
- No event of its own on the loan-event log: the record of this process is `partner_actions` (one row per request) and the decision record `partner.user.invite` writes; no clock arms on anything here.

#### Data model
New tables (append-only where noted; retention `security_logs_5y` for the log, `corporate_7y` for accounts; PII columns encrypted):
- **`partner_users`** (new): `id uuid pk`, `partner_party_id` → `parties` (`party_type = servicer`; 33.1's row), `email text` (normalized; unique per tenant; pii, encrypted at rest), `name text` (pii), `roles text[]` (⊆ {partner_admin, partner_ops, partner_auditor}), `status text` ∈ {invited, active, disabled}, `created_at`, `disabled_at`.
- **`partner_credentials`** (new): `id uuid pk`, `partner_user_id` → `partner_users`, `kind text` ∈ {password, passkey}, `secret_hash text` (the password hash as 34.1's door hashes it; the credential public key and id for a passkey), `label text`, `created_at`, `revoked_at` — the shape of 34.1's `staff_credentials`, never the staff table.
- **`partner_sessions`** (new): `id uuid pk`, `partner_user_id` → `partner_users`, `partner_party_id` → `parties`, `role text` (the acting role — the session's default, the least-privileged role held; rule 3), `created_at`, `last_seen_at`, `expires_at`, `revoked_at`. Cookie `sm_partner_session` on the app; 30 minutes idle, 12 hours absolute; revoked on sign-out, disable and role change.
- **`partner_actions`** (new; append-only; retention 5 years): `id uuid pk`, `at timestamptz`, `partner_user_id`, `partner_party_id`, `role text` (the role that acted; on a refusal the role that was asked for), `action text` (`partner_portal.viewed` for a read; the bus command name for an act — `book.import`, `partner.user.invite`), `subject_kind text` (the view for a read: `home`, `book`, `import`, `holds`, `eligibility`, `pipeline`, `pipeline.loan`, `loan`, `serviced`, `report`, `report.export`, `users`, `me` — the values 36.2–36.6 log; the subject's kind for an act), `subject_id text`, `result text` ∈ {ok, refused}, `refusal_code text`. No PII column: never a homeowner's name, e-mail or phone, never a money figure, never a filter's text.
- Baseline tables written: `auth_challenges` (the e-mail code and the passkey challenge, reused from 32.2 as 34.1 reused it, with `subject_kind = partner` — migration 0127's check lists `party` and `staff`, so the new migration adds `partner` to it and a `partner_user_id` reference beside 0127's `staff_user_id`; no third OTP table), `agent_decisions` (`partner.user.invite` — a human decision with the inviter's rationale), `notices` / `notice_deliveries` (`NTC_SM_PARTNER_USER_INVITE`), `escalations` (five failed sign-ins for one account within an hour, as 34.1 rule 1 — one `compliance` row). Never `staff_users`, `staff_credentials`, `staff_sessions` or `staff_actions` (36.1-T1).
- Baseline tables read: `parties` (the tenant's `servicer` row: legal name, NMLSR id) and — only through the tools the wrapper calls — 33.1–33.3's and 34.3's tables (36.2–36.5).

#### State machine
Per partner user (`partner_users.status`): `invited —(code verified + password set; a passkey optional)→ active —(DELTA-01, by a partner_admin of the tenant or by staff)→ disabled`; a disabled user's sessions are revoked in the same transaction and never reopen (36.1-T5). Per session (`partner_sessions`): `open —(30 minutes idle | 12 hours | sign-out | disable | role change)→ closed` (`revoked_at` set; the next request answers 401 `SESSION_EXPIRED`). Per request: `received → logged → answered | refused`, the log row completed with the result. A `partner_admin` performs the invitation; a person's own row is never changed by that person; no agent performs any transition.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Two factors, always; the mechanics are 34.1's.** A partner session opens only after a possession factor (the six-digit code to the e-mail on the partner user row — one open challenge per address, 10 minutes, `subject_kind = partner` on `auth_challenges` — or a registered passkey verified by the same WebAuthn verifier the borrower and staff doors use) and the password (at least 12 characters, checked against the breached-password list the borrower door uses, hashed as the staff door hashes; `PASSWORD_WEAK` otherwise). A code alone yields an enrol/step token good for 10 minutes that can set or reset the password and register a passkey and opens nothing (`GET /v1/partner/me` with it answers 401); a password alone opens nothing. Five failures (codes or passwords) lock the account for 15 minutes (`ACCOUNT_LOCKED` on the log; the wire answer stays the generic door refusal) and open one `compliance` escalation as 34.1 rule 1 does. No OIDC in V1.
2. **Roles are the only authority, and they are not staff roles.** `partner_admin` reads everything in the tenant and acts: `book.import` (36.2), `partner.user.invite`, the daily report export (36.5); `partner_ops` reads the book, eligibility, the pipeline, the loan page and home and acts on nothing in V1; `partner_auditor` reads home, eligibility, the pipeline, reports and loan pages — no upload, no admin — and exports the daily report. The three are the partner actor `{kind: human, id: partner_user_id, role}` and nothing else: never `ops_analyst`, `officer`, `compliance` or `admin`, never a `StaffRole`, never a row in `staff_users`. No partner role waives a money field, approves a campaign (20.2 stays `officer`), resolves a hold (`book.resolve` stays `ops_analyst` on `/ops`), sends a homeowner a message or orders a report. A role is never self-granted: `partner.user.invite` refuses the caller's own e-mail, and a tenant keeps at least one active `partner_admin` or staff re-provisions one.
3. **The role on a read, the role on an act (34.1 rule 3, three roles).** The session's `role` is its default acting role — the least-privileged role the user holds in the one declared order `partner_ops < partner_auditor < partner_admin` (ops acts on nothing in V1; the auditor exports; the admin acts on everything the tenant may). A request may name a held role (body `role` or `?role=`, 34.1's "Act as"): on a `GET`, a named role the route does not accept falls back to the least-privileged role the route accepts that the user holds, and the answer names the role that acted (`acted_as`); on a `POST`, the named (or default) role must be one the tool or route accepts, else 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, act_as: [the accepted roles the user holds]}` before any write — the screen offers "act as <role>" for each and re-sends with that role named (36.1-T2: a `partner_ops` session's `POST /v1/partner/book/imports` is `403 ROLE_REQUIRED` with `act_as: []`). A role the user does not hold is refused on either method; no route substitutes a greater authority for the one named. The `chooseRole` pattern of `src/runtime/staff/roles.ts` is copied into the partner runtime with the three roles only — never imported with the staff roles in it.
4. **Tenant or 404.** Every query the wrapper runs carries `partner_party_id = session.partner_party_id`, taken from the session and never from a header, a body field or a query string (36.2-T4); 34.3's read tools are called with `filter.partner` set to it, never null. A loan, import, report, user or pipeline item whose `partner_party_id` is another tenant's answers `404 NOT_FOUND` — never `403`, never an empty list beside an error — and the log row reads `result = refused`, `refusal_code = NOT_FOUND` with the requested id and no homeowner field (36.1-T3). A list route answers the tenant's rows only; another tenant's are absent, not refused (36.3-T5, 36.4-T5).
5. **Everything is logged, nothing sensitive is.** One `partner_actions` row per request, written before the handler runs and completed with the result and the role that acted: `action` is `partner_portal.viewed` for every read (the view in `subject_kind`, the row's id in `subject_id` — 36.1-T6's `view`) and the bus command name for every act; the row carries ids and the role and never a homeowner's name, e-mail or phone, a money figure or a filter's text. Retained five years; exported by staff through 34.4's evidence pack; no PII in any payload the portal writes.
6. **Sessions end.** 30 minutes idle, 12 hours absolute, sign-out, a disable or a change of the user's roles — each revokes (`revoked_at`) and answers 401 `SESSION_EXPIRED` on the next request; the app returns to `/partners/sign-in` with the return path kept. A disabled user presenting a valid code and password is refused with the generic door answer and no session row is written (36.1-T5). Expiry is two session columns compared on every request, as 34.1's; no timer.
7. **Two surfaces, no fallback.** `/v1/partner/*` resolves only a `partner_sessions` bearer: a staff cookie, the header actor, `x-staff-role` and the machine `API_TOKEN` are ignored there and the request is 401 (36.1-T7; the same for `/partners`, whose proxy forwards only to `/v1/partner/*`). `/ops`, `/ops/api/*`, `/v1/partner-book/*` and `/v1/borrower/*` resolve no partner session (36.1-T8). Mount order in the server: the existing `/v1/partner-book/*` unchanged, then `/v1/partner/*`, then `/ops/api/*` unchanged.
8. **The first admin, then the tenant runs itself.** `partner.user.invite{partner_party_id, email, name, roles}`: by staff `ops_analyst` / `admin` (from `/ops` through the existing bus route) for any tenant — the only way a tenant gets its first user; by a `partner_admin` for their own tenant only, `partner_party_id` from the session and never from the body. It writes `partner_users{status = invited}` with the roles named (any subset of the three; the inviter's attestation that the invitee is a distinct natural person in the decision's rationale), renders `NTC_SM_PARTNER_USER_INVITE` to the e-mail, and refuses a second row for the same normalized e-mail in the tenant (the earlier row is re-invited instead). The invitee's `verify` + password enrolment moves the row to `active` (36.1-T4: an invited `partner_ops` signs in and cannot upload). In non-production the demo partner's first `partner_admin` is seeded; the seed runs the same command with the system actor.

No money figure is computed here.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`security-records` agent (tools: `partner.user.invite`). End-to-end: `partner.user.invite` writes the partner user row and sends the invitation, under staff `ops_analyst` / `admin` for any tenant or a `partner_admin` for their own; it is the only tool this process owns. The doors are not tools — they are the partner runtime's door service, the pattern of the staff door, and open the only path to a `partner_sessions` row. Everything a partner user does after the door is a read through 34.3's `book.history`, `book.loans`, `book.loan`, `book.day`, `book.daily_report` and 33.1's `book.report`, or the one write, 33.1's `book.import` (36.2) — each dispatched with the partner actor and the tenant from the session, never re-declared here. Decision record schema `{partner_user_id, partner_party_id, action: invite, roles, rationale, by, rule_set_version: partner_portal.access.v1, model_version: deterministic, prompt_version: 36.1-v1, confidence: 1}`. Guardrails: `TWO_FACTORS` (34.1's; no session on one factor), `NOT_FOUND` for anything outside the tenant (rule 4; every query carries the session's `partner_party_id`), `ROLE_REQUIRED` with `act_as` on an act (rule 3; never a silent substitution), `NO_PII_IN_LOG` (34.1's; the action log carries ids and the role only), `NO_SELF_ROLE_CHANGE` (34.1's), `LOAN_MONITORED` (33.1's; no partner role reaches a servicing command), `ANALYST_NEVER_DECIDES` (33.2's; no partner role changes a verdict); a staff session, the header actor and the machine token open nothing on `/v1/partner/*` and a partner session opens nothing on `/ops` (rule 7); the three roles are never `ops_analyst`, `officer`, `compliance` or `admin`. Escalations: `ops_analyst` or `admin` (staff) to provision a tenant's first `partner_admin` or re-provision one when the tenant has no active admin; `compliance` on five failed sign-ins for one partner account within an hour (the same escalation the staff door opens); `officer` for nothing — no money field moves here.

#### Edge cases and failure modes
- The invitation e-mail bounces → `partner_users.status` stays `invited`; the `partner_admin` (or staff) re-invites to a corrected address by the same command; the old row is superseded (disabled through DELTA-01 once accepted), never edited in place.
- A partner user with no passkey loses e-mail access → re-invited (a new code path); a password alone never opens a session.
- The tenant's last active `partner_admin` is disabled or locked → the tenant keeps reading under its other roles; only staff `ops_analyst` / `admin` can provision a new `partner_admin`; nothing self-heals.
- The same person works at two partners → two `partner_users` rows, two tenants, two sign-ins; a session is bound to one `partner_party_id` and never switches.
- A partner user's e-mail is also a homeowner party's e-mail → unrelated identities: `partner_users` is not `parties`; no link, no merge, no borrower session.
- A staff member with a staff session opens `/partners` → the door; their staff cookie is not read (36.1-T7). A partner user opens `/ops` → the staff door; their partner cookie is not read (36.1-T8).
- The multipart or JSON body names a `partner_party_id`, a `partner`, a `profile` or an `x-staff-role` → ignored; the session decides (36.2-T4).
- A request for another tenant's id → `404 NOT_FOUND` and a refused log row; ten such requests in a minute are ten rows — the log is the detector and 34.4 shows it to staff; no lockout on reads in V1.
- The session expires mid-upload → 401 `SESSION_EXPIRED`; 33.1's idempotency (the file hashes) means the retry after sign-in is `already_loaded` if the first request committed, or a fresh import if it did not.
- The FAKE e-mail port outside production echoes `fake_code` on the code route and holds the invitation; in production neither is echoed.

#### Audit and evidence
What an examiner is shown: the tenant's partner users with roles, status, enrolment and disable dates and factors; the action log filtered by tenant, person, subject or date, each row with the role that acted and the result — no name, e-mail, phone or figure on any row; the invitation's rendered text against its checklist; the lock escalations; the decision record of every invitation. Exported by staff through 34.4's evidence pack; the partner sees its own users on the Admin area and, in V1, no log view (Open questions).

### Open questions / decisions
1. Does `partner_sessions` carry the bearer's hash and the factors that opened it, as `staff_sessions` does (`token_hash`, `factors`)? **Default: yes — the same shape as 34.1's session row; the brief's column list is what the tests read and is kept exactly.**
2. How does the bus accept the partner actor on `book.import`, whose role gates check `hasRole` against `HUMAN_ROLES` in `src/app/roles.ts`, where `partner_admin` is not a `HumanRole`? **Default: Session 1's decision, recorded there; the rule stands — the actor is `{human, partner_user_id, partner_admin}`, `partner_party_id` from the session, and no staff role is substituted.**
3. Should the door and the invitation also append `loan_events` rows as 34.1's `staff.*` events do? **Default: not in V1 — the action log and the decision record are the record and no clock arms on a partner event; if the evidence pack needs them, they are named through a delta.**
4. Should a partner user's roles be changeable in place? **Default: not in V1 — a role change is a disable (DELTA-01) and a new invitation, so every session under the old roles is revoked as rule 6 requires.**
5. Should the partner see its own action log? **Default: not in V1; the Admin area lists users only; the log is staff evidence (34.4).**
