### Blueprint row
The operator portal knows four roles (`ops_analyst`, `officer`, `compliance`, `admin` — src/runtime/staff/roles.ts:15) while the command bus gates on twenty-two (`HUMAN_ROLES`, src/app/roles.ts:10-12): every `allow.humanRoles` list in a command spec is checked by `hasRole` (src/app/commands.ts:77), and at HEAD the tool specs name fifteen distinct kernel roles (`officer` 23 specs, `ops_analyst` 18, `fnma_portal_operator` 8, `human_agent` 6, `funding_approver` 5, `attorney` 5, `underwriting_reviewer` 4, `mlo_of_record` 4, `licensed_specialist` 3, `bsa_officer` 3, `compliance` 3, `lossmit_reviewer` 2, `settlement_agent` 1, `counsel` 1; `admin` 4) and the domain code checks eleven more by literal (`officer` 111 sites, `attorney` 14, `fnma_portal_operator` 13, `qc_officer` 8, `ciso` 8, `underwriting_reviewer` 6, `signing_officer` 6, `funding_approver` 6, `mlo_of_record` 4, `lossmit_reviewer` 3, `counsel` 2 — grep of `requireRole(`/`role === ` over src/app and src/domain on 2026-09-15). A person can act under those roles in exactly two ways today. (1) Under `INTEGRATIONS=fake` — what infra/terraform/run.tf:10 deploys and the only value src/runtime/config.ts:36-37 admits — the FAKE reviewers fill six roles (`mlo_of_record`, `underwriting_reviewer`, `qc_officer`, `signing_officer`, `funding_approver`, `human_agent`; src/infra/integrations/reviewers.ts:40) after 20 s through the owning sections' own tools as `{kind: human, id: "FAKE:<role>", role}` (reviewers.ts:78, :166-169), switched off only by `FAKE_REVIEWERS=off` or a non-fake `INTEGRATIONS` (reviewers.ts:53-57) — and, unlike the partner-book FAKE officer (src/runtime/partner-book-offers.ts:90), never by `ENVIRONMENT=production`. (2) On `/v1` the acting human is whatever JSON the caller sends behind one shared bearer: `actorOf(b["actor"])` (src/runtime/server.ts:145-149) and `approvedBy: actorOf(b["approvedBy"])` (server.ts:214, :228-236) are trusted as sent, `API_TOKEN` is a single secret (config.ts:8, :34-35; server.ts:46-48), and when no approver is sent the decision record's approver defaults to the actor itself (commands.ts:94-96) — so `requireDualControl`'s "two distinct officers" (roles.ts:35-44) is satisfied by two asserted ids. The portal already verifies the actor from rows for its own routes (src/runtime/controls/common.ts:48-52 `requireStaffRole`; src/console/server.ts:5-15 and :265-274 the session actor through `chooseRole` — since 34.1's 2026-09-15 amendment the role the request names on an act, `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, held, act_as}` when the route does not accept it, never a silent substitution; :218-221 the header actor only with the ops bearer outside production) and marks a queue row a FAKE will fill (src/console/pg-store.ts:25-29); 26.x's `deliverLoanEstimate` demands a real `mlo_of_record` (src/runtime/origination.ts:405-408) that nobody can sign in as. With `FAKE_REVIEWERS=off` the queue therefore goes to a person who cannot exist. This process gives every kernel role a per-person identity in the staff directory, makes the `/v1` actor and every dual-control approver a verified principal, keeps the independence roles disjoint on one person, measures each role's queue and who can drain it, switches the FAKE reviewers off one role and one environment at a time by two people, and puts all of it on one handover board — the gap the assessment lists under "People and roles — the spec's humans cannot sign in" and "API authorization for people and partners on /v1".

### Verified requirement (as of 2026-09-15)
**Roles are the only authority; the actor is the session's (34.1 rules 2–4 as amended 2026-09-15; src/runtime/staff/roles.ts:47-83 `ROLE_ORDER` and `chooseRole`; src/console/server.ts:218-274).** A portal request acts as `{kind: human, id: staff_user_id, role}` for a role the account holds: on an act, the role the request names (`x-staff-role`, body `role`) when the route or tool accepts it, else 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, held, act_as: [the accepted roles the account holds]}` before any write — never a silent substitution of a greater held role; on a read, a held role the route does not accept falls back to the least-privileged accepted held role and the answer names it (`acted_as`); a route that needs a role the account lacks answers 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role}` before any read; a role is never self-granted (`NO_SELF_ROLE_CHANGE`) and at least one active `admin` remains (`LAST_ADMIN_STAYS`); one staff account per natural person, every two-person check on the platform comparing staff account ids, and `compliance` ⟂ `officer` on one row once a second active account exists (`STAFF_ROLE_DISJOINT`, rule 2); the header actor is honoured only with the ops bearer token and `ENVIRONMENT ≠ production` (rule 3; console/server.ts:218-221). Every request leaves a `staff_actions` row with ids and the role that acted — `staff_actions.role`, migration 0139; on a refusal the role asked for (rule 4). This process extends the same rules to the other eighteen roles and to `/v1`; it changes none of them.

**The kernel's role vocabulary and dual control (src/app/roles.ts; docs/ARCHITECTURE.md convention 6).** `HUMAN_ROLES` is the closed list of twenty-two human roles the registry's breach column and every guardrail name; `requireRole` is the only way a command checks one, so the AI path and the console path are held to identical rules; `requireDualControl(actors, role)` needs two actors with distinct ids holding the role (roles.ts:43-47, citing 5.2's single transfer > $250,000 or daily > $1,000,000, 3.7's payee change > $10,000, 15.4/16.2's write-offs); money fields are never agent-corrected and a waiver needs an `officer`. Convention 7: the ops console and the agent call the same service.

**QC independence (Selling Guide D1-1-02, as 28.1 verified it).** The `qc_officer` who reviews tier-1/tier-2 findings, samples the agent's no-defect conclusions and signs the monthly report is independent of production; "production agents never touch QC records" (28.1 automation class); the FAKE `qc_officer` closes the prefunding hold under that identity precisely because 28.1's independence check accepts it (reviewers.ts:120-131). The disjointness this process enforces on one person follows from that rule; the pairs beyond `qc_officer` are policy and are marked so below.

**Dual control on the wire release (26.3 — policy, "not a Fedwire or regulatory requirement").** The only human step in funding is the `funding_approver`'s release of the outbound wire; the releaser is never the preparer; `SM_O73_DUAL_CONTROL_RELEASE_1H` escalates to a backup `funding_approver` and never auto-releases. The approver is therefore a second, distinct person — which the runtime today cannot tell from a second asserted id.

**Identities, privileged access and credential reset (19.2; db/migrations/0022_data_security.sql:197-220).** `identities` mirrors the identity provider (`kind ∈ {human, system, fnma_system_id, vendor}`, `privileged`, `mfa_method`, `last_credential_reset_at`, `entitlements`, `last_certified_at`); `SM_PRIV_ACCESS_REVIEW_90` reviews privileged access quarterly **[policy]**, `FNMA_SUPP_ACCESS_CERT_365` certifies every human and system account annually, `FNMA_TECHGUIDE_CREDENTIAL_RESET_90D` resets Fannie Mae credentials every 90 days. The staff holders this process creates are rows in that mirror, so 19.2's reviews see them; this process defines no review clock of its own for them. NYDFS 500.7 (access privileges limited and periodically reviewed) is cited through 19.2's frameworks map only **[UNVERIFIED — the 500.7 text was not re-read for this file; 19.2 verified 500.12 MFA and the review cadence]**.

**FAKE reviewers (DELTA-30; reviewers.ts).** Every approval a FAKE gives runs through the owning section's own tool, carries the actor id `FAKE:<role>`, the name "FAKE reviewer" and a note saying so, is idempotent by the item's own record, and never closes a timer-breach escalation ("a timer breach is a person's finding, never auto-closed", reviewers.ts:176). `FAKE_REVIEWERS=off` leaves the queue to a person. The console shows "— FAKE reviewer" on a pending row a FAKE will fill.

**Discrepancies vs blueprint**: (1) The §35 plan (d1238cc) limited `staff_users.reviewer_roles` to the six FAKE roles; at HEAD 35.4 needs a human `officer` and `qc_officer` who can sign in, 35.6 needs `fnma_portal_operator` and `settlement_agent` holders, and the domain code gates `attorney`, `ciso`, `counsel`, `lossmit_reviewer`, `bsa_officer`, `licensed_specialist` and `signing_officer` — the column holds any of the twenty-two kernel roles; the four staff roles stay in `roles` and `admin` never appears in `reviewer_roles`. (2) The plan named `admin` as a breach target; `admin` is a console role, not a kernel `HUMAN_ROLE` (roles.ts:10-12), so every clock here escalates to `compliance` and `admin` only acts. (3) The plan gave this process one table and one timer; the `/v1` gap (server.ts:145-149, :214, :228-236) is closed here too, so principals, break-glass and the queue snapshots are added. (4) commands.ts:94 lets the approver default to the actor; this process makes a dual-control approval a record written by a distinct verified principal and refuses the default for commands that declare dual control. (5) 34.1 is at 100% of its units (docs/audit/COVERAGE.md row `34.1 | 9/9 | 5/5 | 1/1 | 1/1 | 5/5 | 0/0 | 21/21 | 100.0`) and its 2026-09-15 amendment already leaves room for this process — migration 0139 adds `staff_actions.role` and 34.1's data model says "35.7's later extension adds `principal_id`, `surface` and `source` beside it" — so its file is not amended here: the column and the constraints arrive in this process's own append-only migration and every 34.1 T-id (`34.1-T1` … `34.1-T9`) passes unchanged (T1). (6) `fakeReviewerRolesFromEnv` (reviewers.ts:53-57) does not read `ENVIRONMENT`; `fakeOfficerFromEnv` (partner-book-offers.ts:90) does — this process makes production the empty FAKE set everywhere (rule 6).

### Build spec
#### Inputs and triggers
- `POST /ops/api/roles/grants` `{staff_user_id, role, environment, rationale}` (admin; `role ∈ HUMAN_ROLES`) → `roles.grant`; for `qc_officer`, `funding_approver`, `ciso` and `bsa_officer` the grant is pending until a `compliance` session (a different person) names the same `request_id` within 10 minutes (34.4's kill-switch pattern) — `POST /ops/api/roles/grants/{request_id}/confirm`. `POST /ops/api/roles/grants/{id}/revoke` `{rationale}` (admin) → `roles.revoke`.
- `GET /ops/api/roles/queue?environment=&role=` (ops_analyst, compliance, admin) → `roles.queue`; `GET /ops/api/handover/board?environment=` → `handover.board`.
- `POST /ops/api/roles/approvals` `{request_id}` (the second person, under their own session or principal) → `roles.approve` — the only way a dual-control approval record comes to exist.
- `POST /ops/api/roles/breakglass` `{role, subject: {loan_id | application_id}, reason}` (an active `compliance` or `officer` account) → `roles.breakglass`; `POST /ops/api/roles/breakglass/{id}/review` `{disposition ∈ {justified, unjustified}, reason}` (compliance, a different person).
- `POST /ops/api/principals` `{kind ∈ {staff, service, partner}, staff_user_id?, party_id?, name, scopes, expires_at}` (admin) → `principals.issue` (the secret is returned once and never stored); `POST /ops/api/principals/{id}/revoke` `{rationale}` (admin) → `principals.revoke`.
- Every `/v1/*` request (tools, applications, fund, transfers, sweep, demo): `Authorization: Bearer` is resolved to an `api_principals` row before the route; a staff principal acts as `{human, staff_user_id, role}` for a role the person holds (`x-staff-role` / body `role` — the role the request names, honoured when the person holds it and the command accepts it, else 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, held, act_as}`; a request naming no role acts under the person's default role, the least-privileged held; 34.1 rule 3 as amended 2026-09-15 — `chooseRole` in act mode, never a silent substitution); a service principal acts as `{agent | system, <name>}` and can carry no human role; the body's `actor` and `approvedBy` are refused when they disagree with the principal (rule 2). The shared `API_TOKEN` is accepted only outside production for the deploy workflow's smoke and the demo routes, logged as `source = shared_token`.
- `POST /ops/api/handover/plan` `{environment}` (compliance | admin) → `handover.plan`; `POST /ops/api/handover/enable` `{environment, role, request_id?}` (compliance requests, admin confirms within 10 minutes) → `handover.enable` — the FAKE for that role in that environment is off from the confirmation; `POST /ops/api/handover/revert` `{environment, role, rationale}` (nonprod only, same two people).
- The sweep: `roles.queue_scan` as a 35.3 cycle unit once per calendar day (`cycles.run_unit`), and a light re-scan of roles with open items inside every breach pass, so `role.queue.unstaffed` is raised within one sweep of the item that caused it.
- 34.1's own events: `staff.disabled{staff_user_id}` and `staff.role.changed` cascade here (rule 9); `staff.signed_in` feeds the "signed in within 30 days" holder test; `staff.access_review.completed` carries the dormant-grant rationales.
- Events (global; none carries an e-mail, a name or a token): `role.granted{grant_id, staff_user_id, role, environment, by, confirmed_by?}`, `role.revoked{grant_id, staff_user_id, role, environment, by, cause ∈ {revoke, disabled, access_review, breakglass_expired}}`, `role.exercised{grant_id, staff_user_id, role, command, subject}` (the first commit under a grant), `role.queue.scan_completed{scan_run_id, environment, as_of_date, roles: [{role, open_items, holders, fake, status}]}`, `role.queue.unstaffed{environment, role, detected_at, open_items, oldest_opened_at}`, `role.staffed{environment, role, staff_user_id, cause ∈ {grant, exercised, fake_on}}`, `role.breakglass.used{breakglass_id, grant_id, staff_user_id, role, subject, used_at, expires_at}`, `role.breakglass.reviewed{breakglass_id, reviewed_by, disposition}`, `principal.issued{principal_id, kind, staff_user_id?, scopes, expires_at, by}`, `principal.revoked{principal_id, by, cause}`, `principal.refused{principal_id?, code, count}` (the anomaly row, once per hour per token hash), `dual_control.approved{request_id, command, subject, requested_by, approved_by, approved_role}`, `handover.planned{environment, roles: [...], by}`, `handover.enabled{environment, role, by, confirmed_by, holders, pending_items}`, `handover.reverted{environment, role, by, confirmed_by}`, `handover.request.expired{request_id}`.

#### Data model
New tables (append-only where noted; retention `security_logs_5y` for the ledgers and snapshots, `corporate_7y` for grants and principals; no borrower PII anywhere in this process):
- **`role_grants`** (new; append-only): `id uuid pk`, `staff_user_id` → `staff_users`, `role text` (∈ `HUMAN_ROLES`; CHECK against the twenty-two literals, asserted equal to `src/app/roles.ts` by db.test), `environment text` (nonprod | production | the `ENVIRONMENT` value), `action text` ∈ {grant, revoke, breakglass, breakglass_expired}, `request_id uuid`, `granted_by uuid` → `staff_users` (null when the cause is 34.1's disable or access review, which name their own actor on the decision row), `confirmed_by uuid` → `staff_users` (the `compliance` confirmation for the independence roles), `cause text`, `rationale text`, `decision_id uuid` → `agent_decisions`, `effective_at timestamptz`, `expires_at timestamptz` (break-glass: `used_at + 4 hours`), `created_at`. The ledger of every change to `staff_users.reviewer_roles`; a constraint trigger refuses an UPDATE of that column with no matching `role_grants` row in the same transaction (`ROLE_CHANGE_WITHOUT_GRANT`).
- **`role_queue_snapshots`** (new; append-only): `id uuid pk`, `scan_run_id uuid` (35.3's `cycle_runs.id` or the sweep's `sweep_runs.id`), `environment text`, `as_of_date date`, `role text`, `open_items int`, `oldest_opened_at timestamptz`, `holders int` (active accounts holding the role), `holders_signed_in_30d int`, `fake bool` (a FAKE fills it in this environment now), `status text` ∈ {staffed, fake, unstaffed, idle}, `created_at`. One row per role per scan — twenty-two rows a day per environment; the board reads the latest scan.
- **`role_handovers`** (new; append-only): `id uuid pk`, `environment text`, `role text`, `action text` ∈ {planned, requested, enabled, reverted, expired}, `request_id uuid`, `requested_by uuid` → `staff_users`, `confirmed_by uuid` → `staff_users`, `holders jsonb` (the `staff_user_id`s holding the role at that instant), `pending_items int` (queue items open at the switch), `rationale text`, `decision_id uuid`, `effective_at timestamptz`, `created_at`. The current FAKE set of an environment is the environment's default (rule 6) minus the roles whose latest row is `enabled` — read by the reviewers from Postgres, so every instance and the sweep job agree.
- **`breakglass_uses`** (new; append-only): `id uuid pk`, `grant_id` → `role_grants`, `staff_user_id`, `role text`, `subject_kind text` ∈ {loan, application}, `subject_id uuid`, `reason text`, `used_at timestamptz`, `expires_at timestamptz`, `created_at`. The review is a `role.breakglass.reviewed` event and an `agent_decisions` row by a different `compliance` member, never an update.
- **`api_principals`** (new): `id uuid pk`, `kind text` ∈ {staff, service, partner}, `staff_user_id` → `staff_users` (staff), `party_id` → `parties` (partner), `name text`, `token_hash text unique` (sha-256; the token itself is never stored, shown once at issue), `scopes jsonb` (`{loans: "all" | [ids], applications: "all" | [ids], partner_id?, processes: [prefixes]}`), `issued_by uuid`, `issued_at`, `expires_at timestamptz` (≤ 365 days for service and partner, ≤ 90 days for staff), `last_used_at timestamptz` (the one mutable column, touched at most once a minute), `revoked_at`, `revoked_by uuid`, `refusals_in_hour int` (the anomaly counter for a revoked or expired token), `created_at`.
- **`staff_users`** (extended): + `reviewer_roles text[] not null default '{}'` (⊆ `HUMAN_ROLES`, never `admin`); + CHECK `NOT ('qc_officer' = ANY(reviewer_roles) AND ('officer' = ANY(roles) OR reviewer_roles && '{mlo_of_record, underwriting_reviewer, funding_approver, settlement_agent, signing_officer}'))`; + CHECK `NOT ('funding_approver' = ANY(reviewer_roles) AND ('officer' = ANY(roles) OR 'settlement_agent' = ANY(reviewer_roles)))`; + CHECK `NOT ('underwriting_reviewer' = ANY(reviewer_roles) AND 'mlo_of_record' = ANY(reviewer_roles))` — the matrix of rule 3, enforced where a hand-written UPDATE cannot bypass it.
- **`staff_actions`** (extended; append-only): + `principal_id uuid` → `api_principals`, + `surface text` ∈ {ops, v1} default `ops`, + `source text` ∈ {session, principal, shared_token, header} — one row per `/v1` request as well as per portal request (34.1 rule 4: ids and the role that acted — `staff_actions.role` from migration 0139, beside which 34.1's data model places these three columns; never a name).
- Baseline tables written: `identities` (19.2's mirror — one row per staff holder, `kind = human`, `privileged = true` for `officer`, `compliance`, `ciso`, `funding_approver`, `qc_officer`, `bsa_officer`, `entitlements = {roles, reviewer_roles, principals}`, `last_certified_at` from the latest 34.1 access review; one `kind = system` row per service principal), `loan_events` (the events above), `agent_decisions` (every action tool; `approved_by`/`approved_role` from the approval record, never from the body), `escalations` (the breaches and the `ciso` anomaly), `timers` (the four clocks), `cycle_runs` (35.3, the daily scan), `staff_sessions` (revoked on a revoke, as on a role change — 34.1 rule 5).

#### State machine
Per grant (`role_grants`, latest row per `(staff_user_id, role, environment)`): `requested —(admin `roles.grant`; not the caller's own row; `staff_users.status = active`; the disjointness matrix; an independence role waits for a distinct `compliance` confirmation within 10 minutes, else `expired`)→ active —(`roles.revoke` | 34.1 `staff.disabled` | 34.1 access review `change`/`disable`)→ revoked`; `active` with no `role.exercised` for 30 calendar days is `dormant` (a flag on the board and a `compliance` escalation; the grant stays active until revoked or kept with a rationale). Break-glass: `used —(4 hours)→ expired —(a different compliance member reviews)→ reviewed` (the review may arrive before the expiry; the grant expires regardless). Per principal: `active —(revoke | expires_at | 34.1 disable of its staff user)→ revoked`. Per role per environment (the handover): `fake —(`handover.plan`: ≥ 1 active holder signed in within 30 days)→ planned —(`handover.enable`: compliance requests, a different admin confirms within 10 minutes)→ person —(`handover.revert`, nonprod only, the same two people)→ fake`; in production the only state is `person` (rule 6) and `handover.enable` is a no-op that records the holders. Terminal: `revoked`, `reviewed`, `expired`.

Jurisdiction overrides: none.

#### Business rules and calculations
1. **Twenty-two roles, one grammar.** A person may act under a role only if it is in `HUMAN_ROLES` (src/app/roles.ts) and the account holds it: the four staff roles in `staff_users.roles` (34.1) and any of the twenty-two in `staff_users.reviewer_roles`; a session's actable set is `roles ∪ reviewer_roles` minus `admin` (34.1 rule 2: `admin` touches no borrower). `ops_analyst`, `officer` and `compliance` are the same words on both sides and are held once, in `roles`. A grant of `admin` or of a word outside the list is refused `UNKNOWN_ROLE{role}` before any read; a migration that adds a role to the CHECK without the same word in roles.ts fails db.test (`ROLE_LIST_DRIFT`).
2. **Never self-asserted.** On `/v1` the actor is the principal's, not the body's: a staff principal → `{human, staff_user_id, role}` with the role the request names (`x-staff-role` / body `role`) when the person holds it and the command accepts it — 34.1 rule 3's `chooseRole` in act mode, which refuses 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED{role, held, act_as}` otherwise and never substitutes a greater held role; a request naming no role acts under the person's default role (the least-privileged held, 34.1 rule 3); a service or partner principal → `{agent | system, name}` and never a human role (`NO_HUMAN_ROLE_ON_SERVICE_PRINCIPAL`); a body `actor` that names another id, kind or role is refused 403 `NO_SELF_ASSERTED_ACTOR` and logged; a body `approvedBy` is refused `APPROVER_NOT_SELF_ASSERTED` whatever it says. A command whose spec declares dual control (`allow.dualControl: {role, threshold}` — the `requireDualControl` sites) runs only when an approval record exists: `roles.approve{request_id}` by a distinct verified principal holding the role, written as `dual_control.approved` and an `agent_decisions` row; the runtime passes that record as `approvedBy` and commands.ts:94's default (the actor as its own approver) is refused for such commands (`APPROVER_DISTINCT`). The shared `API_TOKEN` opens `/v1` only with `ENVIRONMENT ≠ production` (the deploy smoke and the demo routes), acts as the header actor exactly as 34.1 rule 3 allows on the console, and is refused 403 `SHARED_TOKEN_REFUSED_IN_PRODUCTION` otherwise.
3. **The disjointness matrix.** On one person: `qc_officer` ⟂ `officer`, `mlo_of_record`, `underwriting_reviewer`, `funding_approver`, `settlement_agent`, `signing_officer` (28.1, D1-1-02: independent of production — verified); `funding_approver` ⟂ `officer` and `settlement_agent` (26.3's releaser-never-preparer, extended to the person who waives money fields and the person who disburses — **policy, the plan's design; 26.3 names no such pair [UNVERIFIED as a rule]**); `underwriting_reviewer` ⟂ `mlo_of_record` (21.6's reviewer of the adverse decision is not the originator — policy, 21.6 names the reviewer role only). A grant that would break a pair is refused `ROLE_DISJOINT{role, conflicts_with}` by the tool and by the CHECK (rule 3 of the data model), so a hand-written UPDATE fails too. `admin` and `compliance` may hold no reviewer role that lets them approve their own grants: the independence roles (`qc_officer`, `funding_approver`, `ciso`, `bsa_officer`) need a `compliance` confirmation by a person other than the granting `admin` within 10 minutes; the grant of a role to the confirming person is refused `CONFIRMER_IS_HOLDER`. 34.1's own pair — `compliance` ⟂ `officer` on one row once a second active staff account exists (`STAFF_ROLE_DISJOINT`, 34.1 rule 2 as amended 2026-09-15) — is enforced by `staff.role.set` and the access review, not here; this matrix adds the kernel-role pairs beside it, and every pair compares staff account ids, one account per natural person (34.1 rule 2).
4. **Dual-control thresholds are the sections' and are asserted by the cent.** The kernel's `requireDualControl` (roles.ts:35-44) applies at 5.2's single transfer > $250,000 or daily > $1,000,000 (5.2's guardrail and 5.2-T8), 3.7's payee remittance change > $10,000 or a new payee (3.7's guardrail), and the write-offs its comment attributes to 15.4/16.2 (15.4's `officer` write-off authority; 16.2's `officer` acts — absorbed shortages > $500 and the post-close funding decision — name no dual-control threshold of their own); this process supplies the second person, never the threshold. Precision: the comparison is on bigint cents; "greater than" is strict.

**Worked example A (5.2 single transfer).** A custodial advance transfer of **$260,000.00** (26,000,000 cents) is greater than the **$250,000.00** threshold (25,000,000 cents) → the command needs an approval record by a second, distinct `officer`; the same transfer for **$249,999.99** (24,999,999 cents) is not greater → one `officer` commits it alone and the decision row's `approved_by` is that officer.

**Worked example B (3.7 payee change).** A changed remittance instruction for an existing payee of **$10,000.01** (1,000,001 cents) exceeds the **$10,000.00** threshold (1,000,000 cents) → two distinct officers; an instruction for exactly $10,000.00 (1,000,000 cents, not greater) → one officer, unless the payee is new, in which case two whatever the amount.

5. **The role queue.** For a role in an environment the open items are: `escalations` rows with `owner_role = role` and `completed_at IS NULL`; 35.6 orchestration steps in `waiting_human{role}`; the section work items a FAKE would fill (a `terms.presentation.requested` with no `mlo.review.completed` for `mlo_of_record`; a `qc_reviews` row selected or in review for `qc_officer` — reviewers.ts:92-131's own selectors, reused). Holders are active `staff_users` holding the role; `fake` is rule 6's answer for the role. Status: `unstaffed` when `open_items > 0 ∧ holders = 0 ∧ ¬fake`; `fake` when `fake` (holders or not); `staffed` when `holders > 0 ∧ ¬fake`; `idle` when `open_items = 0 ∧ holders = 0 ∧ ¬fake`. `role.queue.unstaffed` is emitted once per `(environment, role)` transition into `unstaffed` (never again while it stays there) and `role.staffed` when it leaves it. Timer-breach escalations (`kind ∈ sev1..sev4`) count as open items for their `owner_role` — they are a person's finding (34.4 rule 1) and the person must exist.
6. **The FAKE set is per environment and empty in production.** The environment's default is `FAKE_REVIEWER_ROLES` (the six) when `INTEGRATIONS = fake` and `FAKE_REVIEWERS ≠ off`, else `∅`; `ENVIRONMENT = production` forces `∅` whatever the other two say (`NO_FAKE_IN_PRODUCTION`, the partner-book-offers.ts:90 rule applied to reviewers.ts:51-55); the current set is the default minus the roles whose latest `role_handovers` row in this environment is `enabled`. The reviewers and the console's queue marking read the current set from Postgres (`handover.board`'s own query), so 1..N instances and the sweep job agree. Every FAKE act keeps DELTA-30's markers; the day's count of FAKE approvals is read by 35.11's daily report and is 0 in production by construction.
7. **The handover is two people's decision, one role at a time.** `handover.plan` lists, per role: holders, holders signed in within 30 days, open items and the oldest, whether a FAKE fills it, and what is missing (`HANDOVER_NEEDS_HOLDER{role}` when no holder has signed in within 30 days); `handover.enable` for a role is requested by `compliance` and confirmed by a different `admin` within 10 minutes, refused `TWO_PERSON_HANDOVER` when the confirmer is the requester and `HANDOVER_NEEDS_HOLDER` when the plan's test fails at confirmation time; from the confirmation the FAKE stops filling that role (an item the FAKE had not yet approved stays for the person and is counted as `pending_items` on the row); `handover.revert` exists only outside production. Enabling a role in an environment satisfies nothing and arms nothing; the queue scan does the measuring.
8. **Break-glass is loud, short and reviewed.** An active `compliance` or `officer` account may assume a role it does not hold for one subject (a loan or an application) and 4 hours with a reason: a `role_grants{action: breakglass}` row, a `breakglass_uses` row, `role.breakglass.used`, `SM_ROLE_BREAKGLASS_REVIEW_1BD`; the acts under it are ordinary acts of that role on that subject only (`ROLE_DENIED` elsewhere); `qc_officer`, `funding_approver`, `ciso` and `bsa_officer` cannot be broken into (`NO_BREAKGLASS_INDEPENDENT_ROLE`), nor can a role whose pair the person already holds (rule 3); the review is by a different `compliance` member.
9. **Revocation cascades, dormancy is flagged.** 34.1's `staff.disabled` revokes every reviewer role and every principal of the person in the same transaction (a `role_grants{action: revoke, cause: disabled}` row each); 34.1's access review `change` that drops a reviewer role does the same with `cause: access_review`; a grant with no `role.exercised` in 30 calendar days breaches `SM_ROLE_GRANT_DORMANT_30D` and the next access review's `keep` on that user must carry a `rationale` naming the grant (`DORMANT_GRANT_NEEDS_RATIONALE`); the `identities` mirror row follows every change in the same transaction.
10. **Nothing here changes a money field.** The tools write their own rows and the mirror; a waiver stays the owning section's `officer` command; this process changes who may act, never what a section computes.

#### AI agent design (AI-first)
`security-records` agent (tools: `roles.grant`, `roles.revoke`, `roles.queue`, `roles.approve`, `roles.breakglass`, `principals.issue`, `principals.revoke`, `handover.plan`, `handover.enable`, `handover.board`, `writeDecision`). End-to-end: the agent runs the daily queue scan and the in-sweep re-scan unprompted, writes the snapshots, raises `role.queue.unstaffed` and `role.staffed`, keeps the `identities` mirror current on every grant, revoke, principal and access review, expires break-glass grants and unconfirmed requests, counts refused presentations of dead tokens and opens the `ciso` anomaly; it proposes nothing about people — every grant, revoke, confirmation, approval, break-glass and handover is a named person's command through the portal or a staff principal. Decision record schema `{subject: {kind: grant | principal | handover | breakglass | approval | scan, id}, action, environment, role?, by, by_role, confirmed_by?, reason, rule_set_version: roles.v1, model_version: deterministic, prompt_version: 35.7-v1, confidence: 1}`. #### Edge cases and failure modes
- Two admins grant the same role to the same person at once → the second sees the first's row under the `staff_users` row lock and answers the existing grant (idempotent; one `role.granted`).
- An `admin` grants a reviewer role to themselves → `NO_SELF_ROLE_CHANGE` (34.1's invariant applies to `reviewer_roles` too).
- The confirming `compliance` member for an independence-role grant is the person being granted → `CONFIRMER_IS_HOLDER`; the confirmation arrives after 10 minutes → `expired`, the grant never activates, logged.
- A FAKE role is handed over in nonprod while an item is pending past the delay → the FAKE's next tick does not approve it (the set is read from Postgres per tick); the item stays for the person and appears in `pending_items`.
- Production started with `FAKE_REVIEWERS` unset and `INTEGRATIONS=fake` → the FAKE set is `∅`; every role with open work and no holder is `unstaffed` within one sweep and `compliance` hears about each within 1 servicer business day; nothing is approved by anyone who is not a person.
- A deploy-smoke call with the shared token and `x-actor-role: officer` in production → 403 `SHARED_TOKEN_REFUSED_IN_PRODUCTION`; the smoke is expected to use a service principal there (35.12).
- A staff principal whose person loses the role mid-flight → the next request under that role is 403 `ROLE_REQUIRED` (the held set is read from rows per request); the person's other roles keep working.
- A service principal presented with a body `actor` naming a human role → `NO_HUMAN_ROLE_ON_SERVICE_PRINCIPAL`; nothing runs.
- `roles.approve` for a request whose command already committed or expired → `APPROVAL_STALE{request_id}`; an approval by the requester → `APPROVER_DISTINCT`.
- A break-glass act on the wrong subject → `ROLE_DENIED` by the owning command (the grant's `subject` is on the actor context); a second break-glass by the same person for the same subject inside the 4 hours → the existing grant is returned, no second clock.
- The queue scan finds an `escalations.owner_role` outside `HUMAN_ROLES` (an agent name or `partner_user`) → reported on the board under `not_a_human_role` with the count; no `role.queue.unstaffed` (the owning section's registry row is the defect).
- 34.1's access review drops a reviewer role while its holder has an open session under it → the session is revoked in the same transaction (34.1 rule 5) and the grant row says `cause: access_review`.
- More than 100 open items for one role → the snapshot stores the count and the oldest; the board pages the items through `roles.queue`.
